### Key Vocabulary
- **Hypotenuse ($c$)**: The longest side of a right-angled triangle, always located directly opposite the $90^\circ$ right angle.
- **Pythagoras' theorem**: In any right-angled triangle with shorter sides (legs) $a$ and $b$ and hypotenuse $c$, the sum of the squares of the two shorter sides equals the square of the hypotenuse: $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$.
- **Pythagorean triad**: A set of three positive integers $(a, b, c)$ that satisfy $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, such as $(3, 4, 5)$, $(5, 12, 13)$, $(8, 15, 17)$, and $(7, 24, 25)$.

---

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Pythagoras' Theorem and Finding the Hypotenuse ($c$)

In any right-angled triangle, if squares are drawn on each of the three sides, the combined area of the squares on the two shorter sides ($a^2 + b^2$) equals the area of the square on the hypotenuse ($c^2$). To find the hypotenuse $c$, square the two shorter sides, add them together, and take the positive square root.

$$c^2 = a^2 + b^2 \implies c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$$

> **💡 Hypotenuse Opposite $90^\circ$ is Always the Longest Side**
> Draw an arrow from the $90^\circ$ right-angle box straight across the triangle — it always points to the hypotenuse $c$. Your answer for $c$ must be greater than both $a$ and $b$.

### Finding a Shorter Side ($a$ or $b$)

When the hypotenuse $c$ and one shorter side $b$ are known, rearrange $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ by subtracting $b^2$ from both sides: $a^2 = c^2 - b^2$. Then take the positive square root: $a = \sqrt{c^2 - b^2}$. Leave the answer in exact surd form (e.g., $\sqrt{45}\text{ cm}$) unless asked to round to a specified number of decimal places.

$$a^2 = c^2 - b^2 \implies a = \sqrt{c^2 - b^2}$$

> **💡 Add Squares for the Hypotenuse; Subtract Squares for a Shorter Side**
> Finding the longest side ($c$)? ADD the squares ($a^2 + b^2$). Finding a shorter side ($a$)? SUBTRACT the squares ($c^2 - b^2$).

### Pythagorean Triads and the Converse of Pythagoras' Theorem

A set of three positive integers $(a, b, c)$ satisfying $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ is called a Pythagorean triad (e.g., $(3, 4, 5)$, $(5, 12, 13)$, $(8, 15, 17)$, $(7, 24, 25)$, and their integer multiples like $(6, 8, 10)$). By the converse of Pythagoras' theorem, if the three side lengths of a triangle satisfy $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ (where $c$ is the longest side), then the triangle is guaranteed to contain a $90^\circ$ right angle opposite side $c$.

$$\text{If } a^2 + b^2 = c^2, \text{ then the angle opposite side } c \text{ is } 90^\circ$$

> **💡 Multiples of Triads Are Also Triads**
> Multiplying any Pythagorean triad by a positive integer $k$ produces another triad: $3 \times (3, 4, 5) = (9, 12, 15)$ since $9^2 + 12^2 = 81 + 144 = 225 = 15^2$.

## Worked Examples

### WE8_1_1: Finding the Length of the Hypotenuse

**Problem**: Find the length of the hypotenuse $c$ in a right-angled triangle with shorter sides: (a) $a = 8\text{ cm}$ and $b = 15\text{ cm}$, and (b) $a = 5\text{ m}$ and $b = 9\text{ m}$ (give both the exact surd and the value rounded to $2$ decimal places).

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), substitute $a = 8$ and $b = 15$ into $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$ and take the square root. | $$c^2 = 8^2 + 15^2 = 64 + 225 = 289 \implies c = \sqrt{289} = 17\text{ cm}$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), substitute $a = 5$ and $b = 9$ into $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$. Since $106$ is not a perfect square, write $\sqrt{106}\text{ m}$ and approximate to $2$ d.p. | $$c^2 = 5^2 + 9^2 = 25 + 81 = 106 \implies c = \sqrt{106}\text{ m} \approx 10.30\text{ m}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $17\text{ cm}$, (b) $\sqrt{106}\text{ m} \approx 10.30\text{ m}$

### WE8_1_3: Applying Pythagoras' Theorem to a Firefighting Ladder

**Problem**: A Country Fire Authority (CFA) crew places a $13\text{ m}$ extension ladder against a vertical brick wall so that the foot of the ladder is $5\text{ m}$ from the base of the wall on horizontal ground. (a) How high up the wall does the ladder reach? (b) If the foot of the ladder is pulled a further $2.8\text{ m}$ away from the wall (to $7.8\text{ m}$ from the wall), how far down the wall does the top of the ladder slide?

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), the wall, ground, and ladder form a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse $c = 13\text{ m}$ and base $b = 5\text{ m}$. Solve $h^2 = 13^2 - 5^2$. | $$h^2 = 13^2 - 5^2 = 169 - 25 = 144 \implies h = \sqrt{144} = 12\text{ m}$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), the new base is $7.8\text{ m}$ while the ladder length remains $c = 13\text{ m}$. Find the new height $h_{\text{new}}$ and subtract from $12\text{ m}$. | $$h_{\text{new}}^2 = 13^2 - 7.8^2 = 169 - 60.84 = 108.16 \implies h_{\text{new}} = \sqrt{108.16} = 10.4\text{ m}$$
$$\text{Distance slid down} = 12 - 10.4 = 1.6\text{ m}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $12\text{ m}$, (b) $1.6\text{ m}$ (to a new height of $10.4\text{ m}$)

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Adding Squares When Finding a Shorter Side
- **The Misconception**: Blindly calculating $a^2 = c^2 + b^2$ even when the hypotenuse $c$ is already known.
- **The Correct Concept**: The hypotenuse $c$ is the longest side, so any shorter side must be less than $c$. You must subtract the known shorter side squared from the hypotenuse squared: $a^2 = c^2 - b^2$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\text{Hypotenuse } 10, \text{ leg } 6: \quad a^2 = 10^2 + 6^2 = 136 \implies a \approx 11.66 \quad \text{(Incorrect — longer than hypotenuse!)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$a^2 = 10^2 - 6^2 = 100 - 36 = 64 \implies a = 8$$
- **Counter-Example**: A shorter side can never exceed the hypotenuse ($10$).

### Simplifying $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$ as $a + b$
- **The Misconception**: Taking the square root of each term separately across an addition sign ($\sqrt{a^2 + b^2} = a + b$).
- **The Correct Concept**: Square roots do NOT distribute over addition or subtraction: $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2} \neq a + b$. You must evaluate $a^2 + b^2$ as a single sum before taking the square root.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 3 + 4 = 7 \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5$$
- **Counter-Example**: If the hypotenuse of a $(3, 4, 5)$ triangle had length $3 + 4 = 7$, the triangle would collapse into a flat straight line.

Find the length of the hypotenuse in a right-angled triangle with shorter sides of $9\text{ cm}$ and $12\text{ cm}$.

**Final Answer**: $15\text{ cm}$

#### Question `8_1_Q2` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Find the unknown shorter side $a$ in a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse $17\text{ m}$ and other shorter side $15\text{ m}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Rearrange Pythagoras' theorem to $a^2 = c^2 - b^2$: $a^2 = 17^2 - 15^2 = 289 - 225 = 64$.

**Final Answer**: $8\text{ m}$

#### Question `8_1_Q4` [Medium] *(Understanding)*
Determine whether a triangle with side lengths $10\text{ cm}, 24\text{ cm}$, and $26\text{ cm}$ is right-angled.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Sum the squares of the two shorter sides: $10^2 + 24^2 = 100 + 576 = 676$.
2. Square the longest side: $26^2 = 676$. Since $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, the triangle is right-angled.

**Final Answer**: Yes, because $10^2 + 24^2 = 100 + 576 = 676 = 26^2$
</details>

#### Question `8_1_Q5` [Medium] *(Understanding)*
An isosceles triangle has two equal sides of length $13\text{ cm}$ and a base of length $10\text{ cm}$. Find (a) its perpendicular height $h$ and (b) its area.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The altitude $h$ bisects the $10\text{ cm}$ base into two $5\text{ cm}$ segments, forming two right-angled triangles with hypotenuse $13\text{ cm}$ and base $5\text{ cm}$.
2. By Pythagoras' theorem, $h = \sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{144} = 12\text{ cm}$, so $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 12 = 60\text{ cm}^2$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $h = 12\text{ cm}$, (b) $\text{Area} = 60\text{ cm}^2$
</details>

#### Question `8_1_Q6` [Challenging] *(Coordinate Geometry)*
Find the distance between the points $A(1, 2)$ and $B(7, 10)$ on the Cartesian plane.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find the horizontal run $\Delta x = 7 - 1 = 6$ and vertical rise $\Delta y = 10 - 2 = 8$.
2. Apply Pythagoras' theorem: $d = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10\text{ units}$.

**Final Answer**: $10\text{ units}$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `8_1_Q7` [Easy] *(Australian Bushwalking Navigation)*
A bushwalker in Wilson's Promontory walks $4.5\text{ km}$ due south from Tidal River and then $6.0\text{ km}$ due east. What is her direct straight-line distance back to Tidal River?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Due south and due east meet at a $90^\circ$ right angle, so the direct return path is the hypotenuse $d$.
2. Calculate $d = \sqrt{4.5^2 + 6.0^2} = \sqrt{20.25 + 36} = \sqrt{56.25} = 7.5\text{ km}$.

**Final Answer**: $7.5\text{ km}$

#### Question `8_1_Q8` [Medium] *(Building & Carpentry)*
A carpenter in Geelong is checking whether a rectangular deck frame measuring $3.6\text{ m}$ wide by $4.8\text{ m}$ long is 'square' (has true $90^\circ$ corners). What should the diagonal measurement from corner to opposite corner be if the corners are exactly $90^\circ$?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. If the corner is $90^\circ$, the diagonal $c$ satisfies $c^2 = 3.6^2 + 4.8^2 = 12.96 + 23.04 = 36$.
2. Taking the square root gives $c = \sqrt{36} = 6.0\text{ m}$ (a $1.2 \times (3, 4, 5)$ triangle).

**Final Answer**: $6.0\text{ m}$
</details>

#### Question `8_1_Q9` [Challenging] *(Telecommunications Engineering)*
A vertical radio mast on Mount Dandenong is supported by a taut steel guy wire anchored to horizontal ground $16\text{ m}$ from the base of the mast and attached $30\text{ m}$ up the mast. If four such identical guy wires are needed (each with an extra $1.5\text{ m}$ for clamps), what total length of steel wire is required?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find the taut length of one wire using Pythagoras' theorem: $L = \sqrt{16^2 + 30^2} = \sqrt{256 + 900} = \sqrt{1156} = 34\text{ m}$.
2. Add $1.5\text{ m}$ for clamps ($35.5\text{ m}$ per wire) and multiply by $4$: $4 \times 35.5 = 142\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: $142\text{ m}$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `8_1_Q10` [Challenging] *(Converse & Angle Classification)*
A triangle has side lengths $8\text{ cm}, 11\text{ cm}$, and $14\text{ cm}$. By comparing $8^2 + 11^2$ with $14^2$, determine whether the triangle is right-angled, acute-angled, or obtuse-angled, and explain why.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate $8^2 + 11^2 = 64 + 121 = 185$, and compare with $14^2 = 196$.
2. Because $14^2 > 8^2 + 11^2$, the $14\text{ cm}$ side has been stretched wider than the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle ($\sqrt{185} \approx 13.60\text{ cm}$), meaning the angle opposite the $14\text{ cm}$ side is obtuse ($> 90^\circ$).

**Final Answer**: Obtuse-angled, because $8^2 + 11^2 = 185 < 196 = 14^2$ (the longest side is longer than a $90^\circ$ hypotenuse, so the angle opposite $14\text{ cm}$ is greater than $90^\circ$)
</details>

#### Question `8_1_Q11` [Extension] *(Plato's Triad Generator)*
Verify algebraically that for any integer $n > 1$, the three expressions $a = 2n$, $b = n^2 - 1$, and $c = n^2 + 1$ form a Pythagorean triad by expanding $a^2 + b^2$ and comparing with $c^2$. What triad is generated when $n = 4$?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Note that $(2n)^2 + (n^2 - 1)^2 = 4n^2 + (n^4 - 2n^2 + 1) = n^4 + 2n^2 + 1 = (n^2 + 1)^2 = c^2$.
2. Substituting $n = 4$ gives $a = 2(4) = 8$, $b = 4^2 - 1 = 15$, and $c = 4^2 + 1 = 17$, which is the triad $(8, 15, 17)$.

**Final Answer**: When $n = 4$, $(8, 15, 17)$ is generated ($8^2 + 15^2 = 64 + 225 = 289 = 17^2$)
</details>

#### Question `8_1_Q12` [Extension] *(3D Pythagoras)*
A rectangular pencil box measures $24\text{ cm}$ long, $8\text{ cm}$ wide, and $6\text{ cm}$ high. By applying Pythagoras' theorem twice (first across the rectangular floor, then through the 3D interior), find the length of the longest straight pencil that can fit diagonally inside the box.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. First find the diagonal $d_{\text{floor}}$ of the $24\text{ cm} \times 8\text{ cm}$ base: $d_{\text{floor}}^2 = 24^2 + 8^2 = 576 + 64 = 640$.
2. Next use the right-angled triangle formed by $d_{\text{floor}}$ and the vertical height $6\text{ cm}$: $D^2 = d_{\text{floor}}^2 + 6^2 = 640 + 36 = 676 \implies D = \sqrt{676} = 26\text{ cm}$.

**Final Answer**: $26\text{ cm}$
</details>
