**Prerequisite Skills**:

### Key Vocabulary
- **Congruent figures ($\cong$)**: Geometric figures that have the exact same shape and size; one can be mapped onto the other by rigid transformations (translations, rotations, reflections). Corresponding sides and corresponding angles are equal.
- **Similar figures ($\sim$)**: Geometric figures that have the exact same shape but possibly different sizes; corresponding angles are equal and corresponding side lengths are in a constant ratio called the scale factor $k$.
- **Included angle**: The angle located directly between (enclosed by) two specified sides of a triangle, required for the $\text{SAS}$ congruence test.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### The Four Triangle Congruence Tests ($\text{SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS}$)

Two triangles are congruent ($\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$) if they satisfy any one of four minimum conditions: (1) $\text{SSS}$ (Side–Side–Side): all three pairs of corresponding sides are equal; (2) $\text{SAS}$ (Side–Angle–Side): two pairs of sides and the included angle between them are equal; (3) $\text{ASA}$ or $\text{AAS}$ (Angle–Side–Angle): two pairs of angles and a corresponding side are equal; (4) $\text{RHS}$ (Right angle–Hypotenuse–Side): both have a $90^\circ$ angle, equal hypotenuses, and one equal pair of shorter sides.

$$\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF \quad (\text{by SSS, SAS, ASA, or RHS})$$

> **💡 Write Congruent Vertices in Matching Order**
> In a congruence statement $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$, vertex $A$ must correspond to $D$, $B$ to $E$, and $C$ to $F$. Note that $\text{AAA}$ proves similarity (not congruence), and $\text{SSA}$ (non-included angle) is not a valid congruence test.

### Similar Figures and Scale Factor ($k$)

When a figure is enlarged or reduced to form a similar figure ($\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$), all corresponding angles remain equal and every linear measurement is multiplied by the constant scale factor $k = \frac{\text{image length}}{\text{original length}}$. If $k > 1$, the image is an enlargement; if $0 < k < 1$, it is a reduction.

$$k = \frac{DE}{AB} = \frac{EF}{BC} = \frac{DF}{AC}$$

> **💡 Angles Never Multiply by the Scale Factor**
> When a triangle is enlarged by scale factor $k = 3$, its side lengths triple, but its interior angles stay unchanged (their sum must still be $180^\circ$).

### Hierarchy and Diagonal Properties of Quadrilaterals

The interior angles of any quadrilateral sum to $360^\circ$. Using congruent triangles cut by a diagonal, we can prove the properties of the parallelogram family: in any parallelogram, opposite sides are parallel and equal, opposite angles are equal, co-interior angles sum to $180^\circ$, and diagonals bisect each other. Special parallelograms inherit these properties plus extra ones: a rectangle has $90^\circ$ angles and equal diagonals; a rhombus has $4$ equal sides and perpendicular diagonals ($90^\circ$) that bisect the vertex angles; a square is both a rectangle and a rhombus.

$$\sum \text{Interior angles of quadrilateral} = (4 - 2) \times 180^\circ = 360^\circ$$

> **💡 Every Square is a Rhombus and a Rectangle**
> A square has all properties of a parallelogram, a rectangle (equal diagonals, $90^\circ$ corners), and a rhombus ($4$ equal sides, diagonals bisecting at $90^\circ$).

## Worked Examples

### WE8_2_1: Identifying Congruence Tests and Finding Unknown Values

**Problem**: In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle PQR$, $AB = PQ = 7\text{ cm}$, $BC = QR = 10\text{ cm}$, and $\angle ABC = \angle PQR = 52^\circ$. (a) State the congruence test that proves $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR$. (b) If $AC = 8.1\text{ cm}$ and $\angle BAC = 83^\circ$, find the length $PR$ and the size of $\angle QRP$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | The $52^\circ$ angle is at vertex $B$ (between sides $AB$ and $BC$) and vertex $Q$ (between sides $PQ$ and $QR$), so it is the included angle between the two equal pairs of sides. | $$\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR \quad (\text{SAS})$$ |
| 2 | Since the triangles are congruent, corresponding sides are equal ($PR = AC = 8.1\text{ cm}$) and corresponding angles are equal ($\angle QPR = \angle BAC = 83^\circ$). Use the angle sum of $\triangle PQR$ ($180^\circ$) to find $\angle QRP$. | $$PR = AC = 8.1\text{ cm}$$
$$\angle QRP = 180^\circ - (52^\circ + 83^\circ) = 180^\circ - 135^\circ = 45^\circ$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $\text{SAS}$, (b) $PR = 8.1\text{ cm}$, $\angle QRP = 45^\circ$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Right-angled triangles $\triangle DEF$ ($\angle E = 90^\circ$) and $\triangle XYZ$ ($\angle Y = 90^\circ$) have hypotenuses $DF = XZ = 13\text{ cm}$ and sides $EF = YZ = 5\text{ cm}$. State the congruence test and find $XY$ if $DE = 12\text{ cm}$.
> *Hint*: Both triangles have a Right angle, equal Hypotenuses, and one equal shorter Side.
> **Working**: Congruence test is $\text{RHS}$; corresponding side $XY = DE = 12\text{ cm}$.
> **Answer**: $\text{RHS}$; $XY = 12\text{ cm}$

### WE8_2_2: Using Scale Factors in Similar Triangles

**Problem**: Triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle DEF$ are similar ($\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$). In $\triangle ABC$, $AB = 6\text{ cm}$, $BC = 8\text{ cm}$, and $AC = 9\text{ cm}$. In $\triangle DEF$, the side corresponding to $AB$ is $DE = 15\text{ cm}$. Find (a) the scale factor $k$ from $\triangle ABC$ to $\triangle DEF$, and (b) the lengths $EF$ and $DF$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Divide the image side $DE = 15\text{ cm}$ by the corresponding original side $AB = 6\text{ cm}$ to find the scale factor $k$. | $$k = \frac{DE}{AB} = \frac{15}{6} = 2.5$$ |
| 2 | Multiply each remaining side of $\triangle ABC$ by $k = 2.5$. | $$EF = 2.5 \times BC = 2.5 \times 8 = 20\text{ cm}$$
$$DF = 2.5 \times AC = 2.5 \times 9 = 22.5\text{ cm}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $k = 2.5$, (b) $EF = 20\text{ cm}$, $DF = 22.5\text{ cm}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Given $\triangle PQR \sim \triangle STU$ with $PQ = 8\text{ cm}$, $QR = 12\text{ cm}$, and $ST = 6\text{ cm}$, find the scale factor $k$ and the length $TU$.
> *Hint*: $k = \frac{ST}{PQ} = \frac{6}{8} = 0.75$.
> **Working**: $k = 0.75$; $TU = 0.75 \times 12 = 9\text{ cm}$.
> **Answer**: $k = 0.75$, $TU = 9\text{ cm}$

### WE8_2_3: Applying Properties of a Rhombus and Parallelogram

**Problem**: A rhombus $ABCD$ has diagonals $AC = 16\text{ cm}$ and $BD = 12\text{ cm}$ intersecting at point $M$, and interior angle $\angle BAD = 74^\circ$. Find: (a) the obtuse interior angle $\angle ABC$, and (b) the perimeter of the rhombus.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Since a rhombus is a parallelogram, adjacent (co-interior) angles between parallel sides are supplementary (sum to $180^\circ$). | $$\angle ABC = 180^\circ - 74^\circ = 106^\circ$$ |
| 2 | The diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other at $90^\circ$, forming four congruent right-angled triangles with legs $AM = \frac{16}{2} = 8\text{ cm}$ and $BM = \frac{12}{2} = 6\text{ cm}$. Use Pythagoras' theorem to find side $AB$, then multiply by $4$. | $$AB = \sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{64 + 36} = \sqrt{100} = 10\text{ cm}$$
$$\text{Perimeter} = 4 \times 10 = 40\text{ cm}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $\angle ABC = 106^\circ$, (b) $\text{Perimeter} = 40\text{ cm}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: A rhombus has diagonals of lengths $10\text{ cm}$ and $24\text{ cm}$. Find the side length and perimeter of the rhombus.
> *Hint*: Half-diagonals are $5\text{ cm}$ and $12\text{ cm}$ meeting at $90^\circ$.
> **Working**: Side $= \sqrt{5^2 + 12^2} = 13\text{ cm}$; Perimeter $= 4 \times 13 = 52\text{ cm}$.
> **Answer**: Side $= 13\text{ cm}$, Perimeter $= 52\text{ cm}$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Using $\text{SAS}$ When the Angle is Not Included Between the Two Sides
- **The Misconception**: Claiming two triangles are congruent by $\text{SAS}$ when the equal angle is opposite one of the two sides ($\text{SSA}$) rather than between them.
- **The Correct Concept**: In $\text{SAS}$, the 'A' stands for the included angle located at the vertex where the two known sides meet. A non-included angle ($\text{SSA}$) only proves congruence when the angle is $90^\circ$ ($\text{RHS}$).
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$AB = DE = 8, \; BC = EF = 6, \; \angle A = \angle D = 40^\circ \implies \text{Congruent by SAS} \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\text{For sides } AB \text{ and } BC, \text{ the included angle must be } \angle B \text{ (not } \angle A\text{)}$$
- **Counter-Example**: With $AB = 8$, $BC = 6$, and $\angle A = 40^\circ$, side $BC$ can swing to two different positions on line $AC$, forming one acute triangle and one obtuse triangle.

### Assuming the Diagonals of Any Parallelogram Are Equal or Meet at $90^\circ$
- **The Misconception**: Assuming all parallelograms have equal diagonals (only true for rectangles and squares) or perpendicular diagonals (only true for rhombuses, squares, and kites).
- **The Correct Concept**: In a general parallelogram, diagonals bisect each other, but the diagonal across the acute corners is longer than the diagonal across the obtuse corners, and they do not meet at $90^\circ$ unless all four sides are equal (a rhombus).
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\text{In any parallelogram } ABCD, \; AC = BD \text{ and } AC \perp BD \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\text{In a parallelogram, diagonals bisect each other } (AM = MC, BM = MD); \; AC = BD \iff \text{rectangle}; \; AC \perp BD \iff \text{rhombus}$$
- **Counter-Example**: Tilt a rectangle sideways into a slanted parallelogram: one diagonal stretches longer while the other compresses shorter.

#### Question `8_2_Q1` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Three angles of a quadrilateral measure $78^\circ, 112^\circ$, and $85^\circ$. Find the size of the fourth interior angle.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The sum of the four interior angles of any quadrilateral is $360^\circ$.
2. Subtract the three known angles from $360^\circ$: $360^\circ - (78^\circ + 112^\circ + 85^\circ) = 360^\circ - 275^\circ = 85^\circ$.

**Final Answer**: $85^\circ$
</details>

#### Question `8_2_Q2` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
In a parallelogram $ABCD$, $\angle DAB = 68^\circ$. Find the sizes of $\angle BCD$, $\angle ABC$, and $\angle CDA$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal, so $\angle BCD = \angle DAB = 68^\circ$.
2. Co-interior angles between parallel sides sum to $180^\circ$, so $\angle ABC = \angle CDA = 180^\circ - 68^\circ = 112^\circ$.

**Final Answer**: $\angle BCD = 68^\circ$, $\angle ABC = 112^\circ$, $\angle CDA = 112^\circ$
</details>

#### Question `8_2_Q3` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle DEF$, $\angle A = \angle D = 48^\circ$, $\angle B = \angle E = 65^\circ$, and $AB = DE = 11\text{ cm}$. State the congruence test that proves $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$ and find $\angle F$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Two angles and the included side between them are equal in both triangles, so $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$ by $\text{ASA}$.
2. By the angle sum of $\triangle DEF$, $\angle F = 180^\circ - (48^\circ + 65^\circ) = 67^\circ$.

**Final Answer**: $\text{ASA}$; $\angle F = 67^\circ$
</details>

#### Question `8_2_Q4` [Medium] *(Understanding)*
Two triangles are similar with scale factor $k = 3.5$. If the perimeter of the smaller triangle is $24\text{ cm}$, what is the perimeter of the larger triangle?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Every side of the smaller triangle is multiplied by $k = 3.5$, so the total perimeter is also multiplied by $3.5$.

</details>

#### Question `8_2_Q5` [Medium] *(Understanding)*
The four interior angles of a quadrilateral are $(2x)^\circ, (3x - 10)^\circ, (2x + 30)^\circ$, and $(3x - 20)^\circ$. Solve for $x$ and find the largest angle of the quadrilateral.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Sum the four angles to $360^\circ$: $2x + (3x - 10) + (2x + 30) + (3x - 20) = 360 \implies 10x = 360 \implies x = 36$.
2. Evaluate each angle: $2(36) = 72^\circ$, $3(36) - 10 = 98^\circ$, $2(36) + 30 = 102^\circ$, and $3(36) - 20 = 88^\circ$. The largest angle is $102^\circ$.

**Final Answer**: $x = 36$; largest angle $= 102^\circ$
</details>

#### Question `8_2_Q6` [Medium] *(Understanding)*
In a kite $ABCD$ with $AB = AD$ and $CB = CD$, the two non-equal angles are $\angle A = 104^\circ$ and $\angle C = 46^\circ$. Find the size of the two equal opposite angles $\angle B$ and $\angle D$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. In a kite, the pair of opposite angles between the unequal sides are equal ($\angle B = \angle D$).
2. Use the quadrilateral angle sum: $2(\angle B) + 104^\circ + 46^\circ = 360^\circ \implies 2(\angle B) = 210^\circ \implies \angle B = \angle D = 105^\circ$.

**Final Answer**: $\angle B = \angle D = 105^\circ$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `8_2_Q7` [Medium] *(Shadow Reckoning & Similarity)*
At $2{:}00\text{ pm}$ in Mildura, a vertical $1.8\text{ m}$ fence post casts a horizontal shadow of length $1.2\text{ m}$. At the exact same moment, a nearby river red gum tree casts a horizontal shadow of length $14.0\text{ m}$. Using similar right-angled triangles, calculate the height of the river red gum tree.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Because the sun's rays strike the ground at the same angle, the post-and-shadow triangle is similar to the tree-and-shadow triangle.
2. Equate ratios of height to shadow length: $\frac{h}{14.0} = \frac{1.8}{1.2} = 1.5 \implies h = 1.5 \times 14.0 = 21\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: $21\text{ m}$
</details>

#### Question `8_2_Q8` [Challenging] *(Surveying with Congruent Triangles)*
To measure the width $AB$ of the Yarra River without crossing it, a surveyor marks $C$ on the bank so that $\angle ABC = 90^\circ$ and $BC = 20\text{ m}$, continues another $20\text{ m}$ along the bank to $D$ ($CD = 20\text{ m}$), and walks perpendicular to the bank to $E$ until $E, C, A$ are in a straight line. If $DE = 34\text{ m}$, prove $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle EDC$ and state the width of the river.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle EDC$: $\angle ABC = \angle EDC = 90^\circ$ (given), $BC = DC = 20\text{ m}$ (given), and $\angle ACB = \angle ECD$ (vertically opposite angles). Thus $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle EDC$ by $\text{ASA}$.
2. Because corresponding sides of congruent triangles are equal, the river width is $AB = ED = 34\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle EDC$ by $\text{ASA}$; river width $AB = 34\text{ m}$
</details>

#### Question `8_2_Q9` [Challenging] *(Structural Engineering)*
A rectangular gate frame in a Victorian country paddock is $2.4\text{ m}$ wide and $1.0\text{ m}$ high. A diagonal steel brace is bolted from one corner to the opposite corner to triangulate the gate. Find the length of the brace and explain using triangle congruence why a triangle is rigid whereas an unbraced rectangle can sag into a parallelogram.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. By Pythagoras' theorem, the diagonal brace has length $\sqrt{2.4^2 + 1.0^2} = \sqrt{5.76 + 1.0} = \sqrt{6.76} = 2.6\text{ m}$.
2. Without a diagonal brace, four fixed side lengths can form infinitely many parallelograms of different angles; adding the diagonal splits the frame into two triangles whose three side lengths are fixed, locking their angles uniquely by $\text{SSS}$.

**Final Answer**: Brace length $= 2.6\text{ m}$; by $\text{SSS}$, fixing all three side lengths of a triangle locks its angles rigidly
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `8_2_Q10` [Challenging] *(Deductive Geometric Proof)*
Let $ABCD$ be a parallelogram ($AB \parallel DC$ and $AD \parallel BC$) with diagonal $AC$ drawn. Prove that $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle CDA$ using the $\text{ASA}$ test, and hence deduce that opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal ($AB = CD$ and $BC = DA$).

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Since $AB \parallel DC$, alternate angles $\angle BAC = \angle DCA$. Since $BC \parallel AD$, alternate angles $\angle BCA = \angle DAC$. Diagonal $AC$ is a common side to both triangles, so $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle CDA$ by $\text{ASA}$.
2. Because corresponding sides of congruent triangles are equal, $AB = CD$ and $BC = DA$.

**Final Answer**: $\angle BAC = \angle DCA$ (alternate angles, $AB \parallel DC$), $AC = CA$ (common side), $\angle BCA = \angle DAC$ (alternate angles, $AD \parallel BC$) $\implies \triangle ABC \cong \triangle CDA$ ($\text{ASA}$)
</details>

#### Question `8_2_Q11` [Challenging] *(Geometric Classification Algorithm)*
Write a 3-step decision algorithm (using yes/no questions about side lengths and angles) to classify any parallelogram as a **square**, a **rectangle** (non-square), a **rhombus** (non-square), or a **general parallelogram**.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Question 1: Are two adjacent sides equal (so all $4$ sides are equal)? Question 2: Is one interior angle equal to $90^\circ$ (so all $4$ angles are $90^\circ$)?
2. Decision rule: (Q1 Yes, Q2 Yes) $\to$ Square; (Q1 Yes, Q2 No) $\to$ Rhombus; (Q1 No, Q2 Yes) $\to$ Rectangle; (Q1 No, Q2 No) $\to$ General parallelogram.

**Final Answer**: Step 1: Are all 4 sides equal? Step 2: Is there a $90^\circ$ interior angle? (Yes/Yes = Square; Yes/No = Rhombus; No/Yes = Rectangle; No/No = General parallelogram)
</details>

#### Question `8_2_Q12` [Extension] *(Isosceles Triangle Theorem Proof)*
In an isosceles triangle $\triangle ABC$ with $AB = AC$, let $M$ be the midpoint of the base $BC$ ($BM = CM$). Prove that $\triangle ABM \cong \triangle ACM$, and hence prove both that the base angles are equal ($\angle B = \angle C$) and that the median $AM$ meets the base $BC$ at right angles ($90^\circ$).

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. In $\triangle ABM$ and $\triangle ACM$: $AB = AC$ (given), $BM = CM$ ($M$ is midpoint), and $AM = AM$ (common side), so $\triangle ABM \cong \triangle ACM$ by $\text{SSS}$.
2. Corresponding angles are equal, so $\angle B = \angle C$ and $\angle AMB = \angle AMC$. Since $\angle AMB$ and $\angle AMC$ form a straight line ($180^\circ$), each equals $90^\circ$.

**Final Answer**: $\triangle ABM \cong \triangle ACM$ by $\text{SSS}$, so $\angle B = \angle C$ and $\angle AMB = \angle AMC = \frac{180^\circ}{2} = 90^\circ$
</details>
