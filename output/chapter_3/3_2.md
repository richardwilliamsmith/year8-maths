### Key Vocabulary
- **Cost Price (CP) and Selling Price (SP)**: The Cost Price is what a business pays to acquire or produce an item; the Selling Price is what the customer pays to buy it.
- **Profit and Loss**: If $\text{SP} > \text{CP}$, the business makes a $\text{Profit} = \text{SP} - \text{CP}$. If $\text{SP} < \text{CP}$, it incurs a $\text{Loss} = \text{CP} - \text{SP}$.
- **Simple Interest ($I = Prt$)**: Interest calculated as a fixed percentage $r$ of the initial principal $P$ for each time period $t$.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Profit, Loss, and Percentage Profit/Loss on Cost Price

Unless stated otherwise, percentage profit and percentage loss are always calculated as a percentage of the original Cost Price ($\text{CP}$).

$$\%\text{ Profit} = \frac{\text{SP} - \text{CP}}{\text{CP}} \times 100\%, \quad \%\text{ Loss} = \frac{\text{CP} - \text{SP}}{\text{CP}} \times 100\%$$

> **💡 Cost Price in the Denominator**
> Whether calculating percentage profit or percentage loss, always divide the dollar profit or loss by the COST PRICE ($\text{CP}$), not the selling price.

### Successive Discounts

When a store offers a second discount on an already discounted sale price (e.g., 'take a further $10\%$ off sale items'), the second percentage is applied to the intermediate sale price, not added to the first percentage. Multiply the individual discount multipliers together.

$$\text{Final Price} = \text{Original} \times \left(1 - \frac{d_1}{100}\right) \times \left(1 - \frac{d_2}{100}\right)$$

> **💡 Never Add Successive Discount Percentages**
> A $20\%$ discount followed by a further $10\%$ discount is $0.80 \times 0.90 = 0.72$ ($28\%$ off in total), NOT $30\%$ off!

### Simple Interest and Total Amount ($I = Prt$ and $A = P + I$)

When money is invested or borrowed at a simple interest rate of $r$ per annum (p.a.) for $t$ years on an initial principal $P$, the interest earned or charged is $I = Prt$ (where $r = \frac{R}{100}$ is written as a decimal or fraction). The total amount at the end of the term is $A = P + I$.

$$I = P \times r \times t = \frac{P \times R \times t}{100}, \quad A = P + I$$

> **💡 Match Time Units to the Interest Rate**
> If the interest rate is given 'per annum' (per year) and the time is given in months, divide the number of months by $12$ to convert $t$ into years (e.g., $18\text{ months} = \frac{18}{12} = 1.5\text{ years}$).

## Worked Examples

### WE3_2_1: Calculating Percentage Profit on Cost Price

**Problem**: A Melbourne bicycle shop buys helmets for a cost price of $\$48$ each and sells them for $\$66$ each. Find (a) the profit per helmet and (b) the percentage profit on the cost price.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Subtract the cost price ($\$48$) from the selling price ($\$66$) to find the dollar profit. | $$\text{Profit} = 66 - 48 = \$18$$ |
| 2 | Divide the profit ($\$18$) by the cost price ($\$48$) and multiply by $100\%$. | $$\text{Percentage Profit} = \frac{18}{48} \times 100\% = \frac{3}{8} \times 100\% = 37.5\%$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $\$18$, (b) $37.5\%$

> **✏️ Now You Try**

> *Hint*: Divide the profit by $\$40$ and multiply by $100\%$.
> **Working**: (a) $\text{Profit} = 54 - 40 = \$14$; (b) $\frac{14}{40} \times 100\% = 35\%$.
> **Answer**: (a) $\$14$, (b) $35\%$

### WE3_2_2: Simple Interest with Time in Months

**Problem**: Noah invests $\$4000$ in an Australian term deposit paying $4.5\%$ per annum simple interest for $18\text{ months}$. Calculate (a) the simple interest earned and (b) the total value of the investment at the end of the $18\text{ months}$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Identify $P = 4000$, $r = \frac{4.5}{100} = 0.045$, and convert $18\text{ months}$ to years: $t = \frac{18}{12} = 1.5\text{ years}$. | $$I = P \times r \times t = 4000 \times 0.045 \times 1.5 = \$270$$ |
| 2 | Add the interest $I = \$270$ to the principal $P = \$4000$ to find the total final amount $A$. | $$A = P + I = 4000 + 270 = \$4270$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $\$270$, (b) $\$4270$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Chloe invests $\$6000$ at $5\%$ p.a. simple interest for $30\text{ months}$. Find (a) the interest earned and (b) the total final amount.
> *Hint*: Convert $30\text{ months}$ into $t = \frac{30}{12} = 2.5\text{ years}$.
> **Working**: (a) $I = 6000 \times 0.05 \times 2.5 = \$750$; (b) $A = 6000 + 750 = \$6750$.
> **Answer**: (a) $\$750$, (b) $\$6750$

### WE3_2_3: Rearranging the Simple Interest Formula to Find Principal or Time

**Problem**: A small business in Dandenong borrows $\$12\,000$ at a simple interest rate of $6\%$ p.a. How many years did the loan run if the total simple interest paid was $\$2160$?

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Substitute $I = 2160$, $P = 12000$, and $r = 0.06$ into $I = Prt$. | $$2160 = 12000 \times 0.06 \times t \implies 2160 = 720t$$ |
| 2 | Divide both sides by $720$ (the annual interest charge) to find $t$. | $$t = \frac{2160}{720} = 3\text{ years}$$ |

**Answer**: $3\text{ years}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: An investor earns $\$960$ in simple interest over $4\text{ years}$ at a rate of $3\%$ p.a. What was the initial principal $P$ invested?
> *Hint*: Solve $960 = P \times 0.03 \times 4$.
> **Working**: $960 = 0.12P \implies P = 960 \div 0.12 = \$8000$.
> **Answer**: $\$8000$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Forgetting to Convert Months into Years in $I = Prt$
- **The Misconception**: Substituting $t = 9$ directly into $I = Prt$ for a $9$-month investment when $r$ is an annual ('per annum') interest rate.
- **The Correct Concept**: Because 'per annum' means per year, $t$ must be expressed in years: $9\text{ months} = \frac{9}{12} = 0.75\text{ years}$. Substituting $t = 9$ overstates the interest by a factor of $12$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$I = 2000 \times 0.04 \times 9 = \$720 \quad (\text{Incorrect: treats } 9\text{ months as } 9\text{ years})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$I = 2000 \times 0.04 \times \frac{9}{12} = \$60 \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: $9\text{ months}$ is less than $1\text{ year}$ ($\$80$ interest), so the interest must be less than $\$80$.

### Adding Successive Discount Percentages Directly
- **The Misconception**: Claiming that a $30\%$ discount followed by an extra $20\%$ off equals a $50\%$ discount.
- **The Correct Concept**: The second discount of $20\%$ applies only to the remaining $70\%$ of the price ($0.20 \times 70\% = 14\%$), so the combined discount is $30\% + 14\% = 44\%$ (multiplier $0.70 \times 0.80 = 0.56$).
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$30\% + 20\% = 50\% \text{ total discount} \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$1 - (0.70 \times 0.80) = 1 - 0.56 = 0.44 = 44\% \text{ total discount} \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: If two successive $50\%$ discounts added to $100\%$, the item would be free instead of costing $0.5 \times 0.5 = 25\%$ of the original price!

#### Question `3_2_Q3` [Easy] *(Simple Interest)*
Calculate the simple interest earned on $\$5000$ invested at $4\%$ p.a. for $3\text{ years}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute $P = 5000$, $r = 0.04$, and $t = 3$ into $I = Prt$.
2. $I = 5000 \times 0.04 \times 3 = \$600$.

#### Question `3_2_Q4` [Medium] *(Fractional Years in Simple Interest)*
Calculate the simple interest on a loan of $\$8400$ at $5.5\%$ p.a. for $8\text{ months}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Convert $8\text{ months}$ to years: $t = \frac{8}{12} = \frac{2}{3}\text{ years}$.
2. $I = 8400 \times 0.055 \times \frac{2}{3} = 462 \times \frac{2}{3} = \$308$.

**Final Answer**: $\$308$
</details>

#### Question `3_2_Q5` [Medium] *(Successive Discounts)*
A coat originally priced at $\$250$ is discounted by $20\%$, and then discounted by a further $15\%$ for VIP members. Find the final VIP price.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Apply both discount multipliers ($0.80$ and $0.85$): $250 \times 0.80 \times 0.85$.
2. $200 \times 0.85 = \$170$.

**Final Answer**: $\$170$
</details>

#### Question `3_2_Q6` [Challenging] *(Finding Interest Rate)*
An investment of $\$7500$ grows to a total amount of $\$8850$ after $4\text{ years}$ of simple interest. Find the annual simple interest rate $R\%$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find the total interest earned: $I = 8850 - 7500 = \$1350$.
2. Substitute into $1350 = 7500 \times \frac{R}{100} \times 4 \implies 1350 = 300R$.
3. Divide by $300$: $R = \frac{1350}{300} = 4.5\%\text{ p.a.}$

**Final Answer**: $4.5\%\text{ p.a.}$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `3_2_Q7` [Medium] *(Queen Victoria Market Commerce)*
A fruit vendor at the Queen Victoria Market buys $12$ crates of mangoes ($20$ mangoes per crate) for a total cost of $\$480$. Unfortunately, $15$ mangoes are bruised and cannot be sold. She sells the remaining mangoes for $\$3.00$ each. Calculate her overall percentage profit on the batch.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Total mangoes purchased: $12 \times 20 = 240$. Saleable mangoes: $240 - 15 = 225$.
2. Total revenue (Selling Price): $225 \times 3 = \$675$.
3. Profit: $675 - 480 = \$195$.
4. Percentage profit: $\frac{195}{480} \times 100\% = 40.625\%$.

**Final Answer**: $40.625\%$
</details>

#### Question `3_2_Q8` [Medium] *(Australian Retail Comparison)*
Two Melbourne department stores sell the same $\$400$ espresso machine. Store A offers a single flat discount of $35\%$. Store B offers '$25\%$ off, plus a further $12\%$ off the discounted price'. Which store offers the lower final price, and by how much?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Store A price: $400 \times (1 - 0.35) = 400 \times 0.65 = \$260$.
2. Store B price: $400 \times 0.75 \times 0.88 = 300 \times 0.88 = \$264$.
3. Store A is cheaper by $264 - 260 = \$4$.

**Final Answer**: Store A is cheaper by $\$4$
</details>

#### Question `3_2_Q9` [Challenging] *(Car Loan Repayment Modelling)*
Liam takes out a simple interest car loan of $\$18\,000$ at $6.5\%$ p.a. over $5\text{ years}$, to be repaid in $60$ equal monthly instalments covering both principal and interest. Calculate (a) the total amount to be repaid and (b) the monthly repayment.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate total simple interest: $I = 18000 \times 0.065 \times 5 = \$5850$.
2. Total amount to repay: $A = 18000 + 5850 = \$23\,850$.
3. Monthly repayment over $60$ months: $23850 \div 60 = \$397.50$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $\$23\,850$, (b) $\$397.50$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `3_2_Q10` [Medium] *(Equivalent Single Discount)*
Find the single percentage discount that is equivalent to two successive discounts of $20\%$ and $25\%$. Show why the order in which the two discounts are applied does not matter.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The combined multiplier is $0.80 \times 0.75 = 0.60 = 60\%$.
2. By the commutative property of multiplication, $0.80 \times 0.75 = 0.75 \times 0.80 = 0.60$.
3. Since $60\%$ of the original price remains, the equivalent single discount is $100\% - 60\% = 40\%$.

**Final Answer**: $40\%$
</details>

#### Question `3_2_Q11` [Challenging] *(Doubling Time Independence)*
How many years does it take for any positive principal $P$ to double in value under simple interest at $8\%$ per annum? Explain why the answer does not depend on the starting amount $P$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. For the investment to double ($A = 2P$), the simple interest earned must equal the principal ($I = P$).
2. Substitute $I = P$ into $I = Prt$: $P = P \times 0.08 \times t$.
3. Dividing both sides by $P > 0$ cancels $P$ completely: $1 = 0.08t \implies t = \frac{1}{0.08} = 12.5\text{ years}$.

**Final Answer**: $12.5\text{ years}$
</details>

#### Question `3_2_Q12` [Extension] *(Mark-up & Discount Proof)*
A shopkeeper marks up the cost price of an item by $40\%$, and then offers customers a $25\%$ discount off the marked price. Does the shopkeeper make a profit or a loss, and of what percentage on the cost price? Prove your answer algebraically.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Let $C$ be the cost price. After a $40\%$ mark-up, the marked price is $1.40C$.
2. After a $25\%$ discount, the selling price is $0.75 \times 1.40C = 1.05C$.
3. Since $1.05C = C + 0.05C$, the shopkeeper makes a $5\%$ profit on the cost price.

**Final Answer**: $5\%$ profit on cost price
</details>
