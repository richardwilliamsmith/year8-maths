### Key Vocabulary
- **Percentage (%)**: A rate or proportion 'per hundred': $p\% = \frac{p}{100}$.
- **Multiplier (scale factor)**: The single decimal or fraction by which an original amount is multiplied to apply a percentage increase ($1 + \frac{r}{100}$) or decrease ($1 - \frac{r}{100}$).
- **Goods and Services Tax (GST)**: Australia's federal tax of $10\%$ added to the pre-tax price of most goods and services, making the GST-inclusive price $110\%$ ($1.1$ times) the pre-GST price.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Expressing One Quantity as a Percentage of Another and Finding a Percentage

To express quantity $A$ as a percentage of quantity $B$ (ensuring both are in the same units), form the fraction $\frac{A}{B}$ and multiply by $100\%$. Conversely, to find $p\%$ of a quantity $Q$, multiply $\frac{p}{100} \times Q$.

$$A \text{ as a \% of } B = \frac{A}{B} \times 100\%, \quad p\% \text{ of } Q = \frac{p}{100} \times Q$$

> **💡 Match the Units First**
> Before forming the fraction $\frac{A}{B}$, always convert $A$ and $B$ to the exact same unit (e.g., convert $\$2.50$ to $250\text{ cents}$ when comparing with $50\text{ cents}$).

### The Multiplier Method for Percentage Increase and Decrease

The original amount represents $100\%$. Increasing a quantity by $r\%$ gives $(100 + r)\%$ of the original amount, corresponding to a decimal multiplier of $1 + \frac{r}{100}$. Decreasing a quantity by $r\%$ gives $(100 - r)\%$ of the original amount, corresponding to a decimal multiplier of $1 - \frac{r}{100}$.

$$\text{New Value (Increase)} = \text{Original} \times \left(1 + \frac{r}{100}\right), \quad \text{New Value (Decrease)} = \text{Original} \times \left(1 - \frac{r}{100}\right)$$

> **💡 One-Step Multiplier Rule**
> To increase by $15\%$, multiply by $1.15$. To decrease by $15\%$, multiply by $0.85$. To reverse a percentage change and find the original amount, DIVIDE by the multiplier.

### Percentage Change (Percentage Increase or Decrease)

To find the percentage change when a quantity changes from an original value to a new value, divide the actual change ($\text{New} - \text{Original}$) by the original value and multiply by $100\%$.

$$\text{Percentage Change} = \frac{\text{New Value} - \text{Original Value}}{\text{Original Value}} \times 100\%$$

> **💡 Always Divide by the Original Value**
> The denominator of a percentage change calculation is ALWAYS the starting (original) value, never the final value.

### Australian 10% Goods and Services Tax (GST)

In Australia, GST is $10\%$ of the pre-tax price. Therefore, the GST-inclusive price equals $110\%$ ($1.1$ or $\frac{11}{10}$) of the pre-GST price. Given the GST-inclusive price, you find the pre-GST price by dividing by $1.1$ (or multiplying by $\frac{10}{11}$), and you find the GST component by dividing the GST-inclusive price by $11$.

$$\text{Price}_{\text{inc GST}} = 1.1 \times \text{Price}_{\text{ex GST}}, \quad \text{Price}_{\text{ex GST}} = \frac{\text{Price}_{\text{inc GST}}}{1.1}, \quad \text{GST} = \frac{\text{Price}_{\text{inc GST}}}{11}$$

> **💡 The Divide-by-11 Rule for Australian GST**
> A GST-inclusive receipt total consists of $10$ parts pre-tax price plus $1$ part GST ($11$ parts in total). So the GST included in a final receipt price is $\frac{1}{11}$ of the final price, NOT $10\%$ of the final price!

## Worked Examples

### WE3_1_1: Applying Multipliers for Percentage Increase and Decrease

**Problem**: (a) Increase $\$240$ by $15\%$. (b) Decrease $450\text{ L}$ by $24\%$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | For part (a), a $15\%$ increase gives $100\% + 15\% = 115\% = 1.15$. Multiply $\$240$ by $1.15$. | $$\$240 \times 1.15 = \$276$$ |
| 2 | For part (b), a $24\%$ decrease leaves $100\% - 24\% = 76\% = 0.76$. Multiply $450\text{ L}$ by $0.76$. | $$450 \times 0.76 = 342\text{ L}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $\$276$, (b) $342\text{ L}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**

### WE3_1_2: Australian 10% GST Calculations

**Problem**: An electrician in Melbourne quotes $\$440$ (including $10\%$ GST) to install a ceiling fan. Find (a) the pre-GST price of the job and (b) the exact amount of GST included in the bill.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | The GST-inclusive price ($\$440$) is $110\% = 1.1$ times the pre-GST price. Divide $\$440$ by $1.1$ to find the pre-GST price. | $$\text{Pre-GST price} = \frac{440}{1.1} = \$400$$ |
| 2 | Find the GST component either by subtracting $\$440 - \$400$ or by dividing the GST-inclusive price by $11$. | $$\text{GST} = \frac{440}{11} = \$40$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $\$400$, (b) $\$40$

> **✏️ Now You Try**

> **Working**: (a) $1320 \div 1.1 = \$1200$; (b) $1320 \div 11 = \$120$.
> **Answer**: (a) $\$1200$, (b) $\$120$

### WE3_1_3: Finding the Original Amount After a Percentage Change

**Problem**: After a $20\%$ winter clearance discount at a Geelong surf shop, a wetsuit sells for $\$224$. What was the original marked price of the wetsuit before the discount?

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | A $20\%$ discount means the sale price is $100\% - 20\% = 80\% = 0.8$ of the original price. | $$0.8 \times \text{Original Price} = 224$$ |
| 2 | Divide both sides by the multiplier $0.8$ (or $\frac{4}{5}$) to recover the original price. | $$\text{Original Price} = \frac{224}{0.8} = 224 \times \frac{5}{4} = \$280$$ |

**Answer**: $\$280$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: After an $8\%$ annual pay rise, a nurse in Ballarat earns $\$1890$ per fortnight. What was her fortnightly pay before the pay rise?
> *Hint*: Divide $\$1890$ by the multiplier $1.08$.
> **Working**: $1890 \div 1.08 = \$1750$.
> **Answer**: $\$1750$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Calculating 10% of the GST-Inclusive Price to Find the GST
- **The Misconception**: When an item costs $\$110$ including GST, students often take $10\%$ of $\$110$ ($\$11$) and claim the pre-GST price is $\$110 - \$11 = \$99$.
- **The Correct Concept**: The $10\%$ GST was calculated on the pre-GST price ($\$100$), not on the final $\$110$ price. Always divide the GST-inclusive price by $11$ to find the GST ($\$110 \div 11 = \$10$).
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\text{GST} = 0.10 \times 110 = \$11, \quad \text{Pre-GST} = \$99 \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\text{GST} = \frac{110}{11} = \$10, \quad \text{Pre-GST} = \frac{110}{1.1} = \$100 \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: If the pre-GST price were $\$99$, adding $10\%$ GST would give $\$99 \times 1.1 = \$108.90$, not $\$110$.

### Assuming Equal Percentage Increase and Decrease Cancel Out
- **The Misconception**: Believing that increasing a price by $20\%$ and then decreasing the new price by $20\%$ returns the price to its original value.
- **The Correct Concept**: The $20\%$ decrease applies to the larger increased price ($120\%$), not the original $100\%$. Multiplying the multipliers gives $1.20 \times 0.80 = 0.96 = 96\%$, a net $4\%$ decrease.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$+20\% \text{ then } -20\% = 0\% \text{ net change} \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$1.20 \times 0.80 = 0.96 = 96\% \implies 4\% \text{ net decrease} \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: Starting with $\$100$: $+20\%$ gives $\$120$; then $-20\%$ of $\$120$ subtracts $\$24$, leaving $\$96 \neq \$100$.

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `3_1_Q7` [Medium] *(Melbourne Water Storage)*
Thomson River Dam in Victoria held $640\text{ GL}$ (gigalitres) of water at the start of winter and $800\text{ GL}$ at the end of spring. Calculate the percentage increase in water storage.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find the increase in volume: $800 - 640 = 160\text{ GL}$.
2. Divide by the initial volume ($640\text{ GL}$) and multiply by $100\%$: $\frac{160}{640} \times 100\% = \frac{1}{4} \times 100\% = 25\%$.

**Final Answer**: $25\%$
</details>

#### Question `3_1_Q8` [Medium] *(Retail Mark-up & GST)*
A Melbourne furniture store advertises a sofa with a pre-GST wholesale price of $\$1500$. The store first applies a $20\%$ retail mark-up, and then adds $10\%$ Australian GST to the marked-up price. What is the final GST-inclusive price paid by the customer?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Apply the $20\%$ mark-up multiplier ($1.20$): $1500 \times 1.20 = \$1800$.
2. Apply the $10\%$ GST multiplier ($1.10$): $1800 \times 1.10 = \$1980$.

**Final Answer**: $\$1980$
</details>

#### Question `3_1_Q9` [Challenging] *(Australian Boxing Day Sales)*
An electronics retailer increases the price of a $\$500$ monitor by $20\%$, and later offers a 'Boxing Day Sale' discount of $25\%$ off the increased price. Find the final Boxing Day price and the overall percentage change from the original $\$500$ price.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Multiply the original price by both multipliers: $500 \times 1.20 \times 0.75 = \$450$.
2. Since $1.20 \times 0.75 = 0.90 = 90\%$, this represents an overall decrease of $100\% - 90\% = 10\%$.

**Final Answer**: $\$450$ (a net decrease of $10\%$)
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `3_1_Q10` [Challenging] *(Inverse Percentage Multipliers)*
If the price of an item is increased by $25\%$, by what percentage must the new price be decreased to return it to its original price? Justify your answer using fractions.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Increasing by $25\%$ multiplies the original price by $1 + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{5}{4}$.
2. To return to the original price, we must multiply the new price by the reciprocal $\frac{4}{5} = 0.80 = 80\%$.
3. Reducing a quantity to $80\%$ of its value is a decrease of $100\% - 80\% = 20\%$.

**Final Answer**: $20\%$
</details>

#### Question `3_1_Q11` [Medium] *(Commutativity of Multipliers)*
Show that applying a $10\%$ discount first and then adding $10\%$ GST gives the exact same final price as adding $10\%$ GST first and then applying a $10\%$ discount.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Let $P$ be the initial pre-GST price.
2. Discount first, then GST: $(P \times 0.90) \times 1.10 = 0.99P$.
3. GST first, then discount: $(P \times 1.10) \times 0.90 = 0.99P$.
4. By the commutative law of multiplication, $0.90 \times 1.10 = 1.10 \times 0.90 = 0.99$, so the final price is identical.

**Final Answer**: Both orders multiply the original price by $0.90 \times 1.10 = 0.99$
</details>

#### Question `3_1_Q12` [Extension] *(Algebraic Percentage Proof)*
Prove that for any positive percentage $r\%$, increasing a positive price $P$ by $r\%$ and then decreasing the result by $r\%$ always results in a net loss of $\left(\dfrac{r}{10}\right)^2\%$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The combined multiplier is $\left(1 + \frac{r}{100}\right)\left(1 - \frac{r}{100}\right) = 1 - \left(\frac{r}{100}\right)^2$.
2. Since $\left(\frac{r}{100}\right)^2 = \frac{r^2 / 100}{100} = \frac{(r/10)^2}{100}$, the new value is $\left(\frac{r}{10}\right)^2\%$ less than $100\%$.

**Final Answer**: Combined multiplier is $\left(1 + \frac{r}{100}\right)\left(1 - \frac{r}{100}\right) = 1 - \frac{r^2}{10000}$
</details>
