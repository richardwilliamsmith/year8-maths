**Prerequisite Skills**:

- Area of rectangles ($A = lw$) and triangles ($A = \frac{1}{2}bh$)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Hectare ($\text{ha}$)**: A metric unit of land area equal to a square of side length $100\text{ m}$, so $1\text{ ha} = 100\text{ m} \times 100\text{ m} = 10\,000\text{ m}^2$.
- **Perpendicular height ($h$)**: The shortest distance between the base of a shape and its opposite vertex or parallel side, measured at right angles ($90^\circ$) to the base.
- **Composite shape**: A geometric figure that can be partitioned into (or formed by subtracting) two or more standard shapes such as rectangles, triangles, and trapeziums.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Converting Metric Units of Area and Hectares

Because area is two-dimensional ($\text{length} \times \text{width}$), the conversion factor between square units is the square of the linear conversion factor. Since $1\text{ cm} = 10\text{ mm}$, $1\text{ cm}^2 = 10^2\text{ mm}^2 = 100\text{ mm}^2$. Similarly, $1\text{ m}^2 = 100^2\text{ cm}^2 = 10\,000\text{ cm}^2$, $1\text{ ha} = 10\,000\text{ m}^2$, and $1\text{ km}^2 = 1000^2\text{ m}^2 = 1\,000\,000\text{ m}^2 = 100\text{ ha}$.

$$1\text{ cm}^2 = 100\text{ mm}^2, \quad 1\text{ m}^2 = 10\,000\text{ cm}^2, \quad 1\text{ ha} = 10\,000\text{ m}^2, \quad 1\text{ km}^2 = 100\text{ ha}$$

> **💡 Square the Linear Conversion Factor**
> To convert between square units, multiply or divide by the linear factor squared ($10^2 = 100$, $100^2 = 10\,000$, $1000^2 = 1\,000\,000$).

### Area of Parallelograms, Rhombuses, and Kites

A parallelogram with base $b$ and perpendicular height $h$ can be rearranged into a rectangle of the same base and height, giving $A = bh$. A kite or a rhombus has diagonals of lengths $x$ and $y$ that intersect at right angles ($90^\circ$), enclosing exactly half the area of a surrounding $x \times y$ rectangle, giving $A = \frac{1}{2}xy$.

$$A_{\text{parallelogram}} = bh, \qquad A_{\text{rhombus/kite}} = \frac{1}{2}xy$$

> **💡 Never Use Slant Edge as Height**
> In $A = bh$, $h$ must be the perpendicular height at $90^\circ$ to the base $b$, never the slanted side length.

### Area of a Trapezium and Composite Shapes

A trapezium has one pair of parallel sides of lengths $a$ and $b$ separated by a perpendicular height $h$. Its area equals the average of the two parallel sides multiplied by the perpendicular height: $A = \frac{1}{2}(a + b)h$. For composite shapes, split the figure into non-overlapping standard shapes (addition method) or subtract cut-out regions from an enclosing rectangle (subtraction method).

$$A_{\text{trapezium}} = \frac{1}{2}(a + b)h = \left(\frac{a + b}{2}\right)h$$

> **💡 Average of Parallel Sides Times Height**
> Add the two parallel sides $a$ and $b$ in brackets first, divide by $2$, and multiply by the perpendicular distance $h$ between them.

## Worked Examples

### WE7_1_1: Converting Metric Units of Area

**Problem**: Convert: (a) $4.5\text{ m}^2$ to $\text{cm}^2$, and (b) $36\,000\text{ m}^2$ to hectares ($\text{ha}$).

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), since $1\text{ m} = 100\text{ cm}$, $1\text{ m}^2 = 100^2\text{ cm}^2 = 10\,000\text{ cm}^2$. Multiply $4.5$ by $10\,000$. | $$4.5\text{ m}^2 = 4.5 \times 10\,000\text{ cm}^2 = 45\,000\text{ cm}^2$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), since $1\text{ ha} = 10\,000\text{ m}^2$, divide $36\,000$ by $10\,000$. | $$36\,000\text{ m}^2 = \frac{36\,000}{10\,000}\text{ ha} = 3.6\text{ ha}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $45\,000\text{ cm}^2$, (b) $3.6\text{ ha}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**

> **Working**: (a) $850 \div 100 = 8.5\text{ cm}^2$; (b) $2.4 \times 10\,000 = 24\,000\text{ m}^2$.
> **Answer**: (a) $8.5\text{ cm}^2$, (b) $24\,000\text{ m}^2$

### WE7_1_2: Calculating the Area of a Trapezium and a Kite

**Problem**: Calculate the area of: (a) a trapezium with parallel sides $a = 11\text{ cm}$ and $b = 17\text{ cm}$ and perpendicular height $h = 8\text{ cm}$, and (b) a kite with diagonals of lengths $x = 14\text{ cm}$ and $y = 9\text{ cm}$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), substitute $a = 11$, $b = 17$, and $h = 8$ into $A = \frac{1}{2}(a + b)h$. | $$A = \frac{1}{2}(11 + 17) \times 8 = \frac{1}{2}(28) \times 8 = 14 \times 8 = 112\text{ cm}^2$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), substitute $x = 14$ and $y = 9$ into $A = \frac{1}{2}xy$. | $$A = \frac{1}{2} \times 14 \times 9 = 7 \times 9 = 63\text{ cm}^2$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $112\text{ cm}^2$, (b) $63\text{ cm}^2$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Calculate the area of: (a) a trapezium with parallel sides $9\text{ m}$ and $15\text{ m}$ and height $6\text{ m}$, and (b) a rhombus with diagonals $12\text{ m}$ and $16\text{ m}$.
> *Hint*: Use $A = \frac{1}{2}(a + b)h$ and $A = \frac{1}{2}xy$.
> **Working**: (a) $\frac{1}{2}(24)(6) = 72\text{ m}^2$; (b) $\frac{1}{2}(12)(16) = 96\text{ m}^2$.
> **Answer**: (a) $72\text{ m}^2$, (b) $96\text{ m}^2$

### WE7_1_3: Perimeter and Area of an L-Shaped Composite Deck

**Problem**: An L-shaped timber deck has outer length $10\text{ m}$ and outer width $8\text{ m}$, with a rectangular corner of $4\text{ m}$ by $3\text{ m}$ cut out. Find (a) the perimeter of the deck and (b) the area of the deck.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | The two inner step edges of an L-shape have the same total horizontal and vertical lengths as the corner removed from the $10\text{ m} \times 8\text{ m}$ bounding rectangle, or we can sum all $6$ sides: $10 + 8 + (10 - 4) + 3 + 4 + (8 - 3)$. | $$\text{Perimeter} = 10 + 8 + 6 + 3 + 4 + 5 = 36\text{ m}$$ |
| 2 | Use the subtraction method for the area: subtract the $4\text{ m} \times 3\text{ m}$ cut-out rectangle from the full $10\text{ m} \times 8\text{ m}$ rectangle. | $$\text{Area} = (10 \times 8) - (4 \times 3) = 80 - 12 = 68\text{ m}^2$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $36\text{ m}$, (b) $68\text{ m}^2$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: A rectangular lawn measuring $15\text{ m}$ by $12\text{ m}$ has a square garden bed of side $3\text{ m}$ dug out of its centre. Find the area of the remaining lawn.
> *Hint*: Subtract the area of the square ($3 \times 3$) from the area of the rectangle ($15 \times 12$).
> **Working**: $15 \times 12 - 3^2 = 180 - 9 = 171\text{ m}^2$.
> **Answer**: $171\text{ m}^2$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Using Linear Conversion Factors for Square Units ($1\text{ m}^2 \neq 100\text{ cm}^2$)
- **The Misconception**: Multiplying by $100$ instead of $10\,000$ when converting $\text{m}^2$ to $\text{cm}^2$.
- **The Correct Concept**: A square metre is $100\text{ cm}$ long AND $100\text{ cm}$ wide, so it contains $100 \times 100 = 10\,000$ square centimetres.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$3\text{ m}^2 = 3 \times 100 = 300\text{ cm}^2 \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$3\text{ m}^2 = 3 \times 100^2 = 30\,000\text{ cm}^2$$
- **Counter-Example**: A small $30\text{ cm} \times 10\text{ cm}$ ruler has area $300\text{ cm}^2$, which is clearly tiny compared to $3\text{ m}^2$.

### Multiplying Base by Slanted Side in a Parallelogram
- **The Misconception**: Using the slanted side length instead of the perpendicular height to find the area of a parallelogram.
- **The Correct Concept**: The slant side is only used for perimeter; area always requires the perpendicular height $h$ measured at $90^\circ$ to the base $b$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\text{Base } 12\text{ cm}, \text{ slant side } 7\text{ cm}, \text{ height } 5\text{ cm}: \quad A = 12 \times 7 = 84\text{ cm}^2 \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$A = bh = 12 \times 5 = 60\text{ cm}^2$$
- **Counter-Example**: If you shear a parallelogram until it is nearly flat, its slant side stays $7\text{ cm}$ while its area approaches $0$ as its perpendicular height $h$ approaches $0$.

### Fluency & Understanding

#### Question `7_1_Q1` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Calculate the perimeter and area of a parallelogram with base $14\text{ cm}$, slanted side $9\text{ cm}$, and perpendicular height $7\text{ cm}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The perimeter is the sum of the four outer edges: $P = 2(14 + 9) = 46\text{ cm}$.
2. The area uses the base and perpendicular height: $A = bh = 14 \times 7 = 98\text{ cm}^2$.

**Final Answer**: Perimeter $= 46\text{ cm}$, Area $= 98\text{ cm}^2$
</details>

#### Question `7_1_Q2` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Convert $6.8\text{ ha}$ into (a) $\text{m}^2$ and (b) $\text{km}^2$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Since $1\text{ ha} = 10\,000\text{ m}^2$, multiply by $10\,000$: $6.8 \times 10\,000 = 68\,000\text{ m}^2$.
2. Since $1\text{ km}^2 = 100\text{ ha}$, divide by $100$: $6.8 \div 100 = 0.068\text{ km}^2$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $68\,000\text{ m}^2$, (b) $0.068\text{ km}^2$
</details>

#### Question `7_1_Q3` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Find the area of a trapezium with parallel sides of $13\text{ m}$ and $21\text{ m}$ and perpendicular height $9\text{ m}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute $a = 13$, $b = 21$, and $h = 9$ into $A = \frac{1}{2}(a + b)h$.
2. Calculate: $A = \frac{1}{2}(34) \times 9 = 17 \times 9 = 153\text{ m}^2$.

**Final Answer**: $153\text{ m}^2$
</details>

#### Question `7_1_Q4` [Medium] *(Understanding)*
A rhombus has an area of $120\text{ cm}^2$ and one diagonal of length $15\text{ cm}$. Find the length of its other diagonal.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute $A = 120$ and $x = 15$ into $A = \frac{1}{2}xy$: $120 = \frac{1}{2}(15)y = 7.5y$.
2. Solve for $y$: $y = \frac{120}{7.5} = 16\text{ cm}$.

**Final Answer**: $16\text{ cm}$
</details>

#### Question `7_1_Q5` [Medium] *(Understanding)*
A trapezium has an area of $108\text{ cm}^2$, a perpendicular height of $8\text{ cm}$, and one parallel side of length $11\text{ cm}$. Find the length of the other parallel side $b$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute into $A = \frac{1}{2}(a + b)h$: $108 = \frac{1}{2}(11 + b) \times 8 = 4(11 + b)$.
2. Divide both sides by $4$ and subtract $11$: $27 = 11 + b \implies b = 16\text{ cm}$.

**Final Answer**: $16\text{ cm}$
</details>

#### Question `7_1_Q6` [Medium] *(Understanding)*
A gable wall is a composite shape consisting of a rectangle $12\text{ m}$ wide by $4\text{ m}$ high topped by an isosceles triangle of base $12\text{ m}$ and perpendicular height $2.5\text{ m}$. Find the total area of the wall.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate the area of the rectangular section: $A_{\text{rect}} = 12 \times 4 = 48\text{ m}^2$.
2. Calculate the area of the triangular roof gable: $A_{\text{tri}} = \frac{1}{2} \times 12 \times 2.5 = 15\text{ m}^2$.
3. Add the two areas: $48 + 15 = 63\text{ m}^2$.

**Final Answer**: $63\text{ m}^2$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `7_1_Q7` [Medium] *(Australian Backyard Design)*
A rectangular swimming pool in Mildura measures $10\text{ m}$ by $5\text{ m}$ and is surrounded on all four sides by a uniform paved path of width $1.5\text{ m}$. Find the area of the paved path.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Adding a $1.5\text{ m}$ border on both ends increases each dimension by $2 \times 1.5 = 3\text{ m}$, giving outer dimensions $13\text{ m}$ by $8\text{ m}$.
2. Subtract the pool area from the outer rectangle area: $A_{\text{path}} = (13 \times 8) - (10 \times 5) = 104 - 50 = 54\text{ m}^2$.

**Final Answer**: $54\text{ m}^2$
</details>

#### Question `7_1_Q8` [Challenging] *(Victorian Agriculture)*
A Gippsland dairy paddock is shaped like a trapezium with parallel fences of lengths $450\text{ m}$ and $750\text{ m}$ separated by a perpendicular width of $500\text{ m}$. (a) Find the area of the paddock in hectares ($\text{ha}$). (b) If fertiliser is applied at $120\text{ kg}$ per hectare, how many tonnes of fertiliser are required?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate the area in square metres: $A = \frac{1}{2}(450 + 750) \times 500 = 600 \times 500 = 300\,000\text{ m}^2$.
2. Convert to hectares by dividing by $10\,000$: $300\,000 \div 10\,000 = 30\text{ ha}$.
3. Multiply by $120\text{ kg/ha}$ and convert to tonnes: $30 \times 120 = 3600\text{ kg} = 3.6\text{ tonnes}$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $30\text{ ha}$, (b) $3.6\text{ tonnes}$
</details>

#### Question `7_1_Q9` [Challenging] *(Renovation Modelling)*
A bathroom floor measuring $3.6\text{ m}$ by $2.4\text{ m}$ is to be tiled with square ceramic tiles of side length $30\text{ cm}$. Allowing an extra $10\%$ of tiles for cutting and wastage, how many tiles should be purchased?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Convert floor dimensions to centimetres: $360\text{ cm}$ by $240\text{ cm}$. Area of floor $= 360 \times 240 = 86\,400\text{ cm}^2$, and area of one tile $= 30 \times 30 = 900\text{ cm}^2$.
2. Exact number of tiles $= 86\,400 \div 900 = 96$ tiles.
3. Include $10\%$ wastage: $96 \times 1.10 = 105.6$, which rounds up to $106$ whole tiles.

**Final Answer**: $106\text{ tiles}$ ($96$ exact tiles $+ 10\% = 105.6$, rounded up to $106$)
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `7_1_Q10` [Medium] *(Geometric Proof)*
Explain geometrically why the area of a rhombus or kite with perpendicular diagonals $x$ and $y$ is $A = \frac{1}{2}xy$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Draw a rectangle around the kite or rhombus with sides parallel to the two perpendicular diagonals $x$ and $y$; this bounding rectangle has area $xy$.
2. The diagonals divide the kite/rhombus into four right-angled triangles that are congruent to the four right-angled triangles outside it, so the kite/rhombus occupies exactly half of the rectangle's area: $A = \frac{1}{2}xy$.

**Final Answer**: A bounding rectangle drawn through the four vertices has dimensions $x$ by $y$ (area $xy$), and the four right-angled triangles inside the kite/rhombus are congruent to the four outer corner triangles
</details>

#### Question `7_1_Q11` [Challenging] *(Connecting Quadrilaterals)*
A square has diagonals of length $10\text{ cm}$. Since a square is a special rhombus whose diagonals meet at right angles, use the rhombus formula $A = \frac{1}{2}xy$ to find the area of the square, and deduce its side length as an exact surd.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Both diagonals of the square have length $10\text{ cm}$ and intersect at $90^\circ$, so $A = \frac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 10 = 50\text{ cm}^2$.
2. Since the area of a square of side $s$ is $s^2 = 50$, its exact side length is $s = \sqrt{50}\text{ cm}$.

**Final Answer**: $\text{Area} = 50\text{ cm}^2$, side length $= \sqrt{50}\text{ cm}$
</details>

#### Question `7_1_Q12` [Extension] *(Unifying Area Formulas)*
Show how the trapezium area formula $A = \frac{1}{2}(a + b)h$ reduces to (a) the parallelogram/rectangle formula when $a = b$, and (b) the triangle formula when $a = 0$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. In a parallelogram or rectangle, both parallel sides are equal ($a = b$); substituting $a = b$ gives $A = \frac{1}{2}(2b)h = bh$.
2. In a triangle, the top parallel side shrinks to a single vertex ($a = 0$); substituting $a = 0$ gives $A = \frac{1}{2}(0 + b)h = \frac{1}{2}bh$.

**Final Answer**: (a) When $a = b$, $A = \frac{1}{2}(b + b)h = bh$; (b) when $a = 0$, $A = \frac{1}{2}(0 + b)h = \frac{1}{2}bh$
</details>
