### Key Vocabulary
- **24-Hour Time**: A four-digit time notation (`HHMM` or `HH:MM`) running from `0000` (midnight) to `2359` ($11\text{:59 pm}$), eliminating the need for am and pm labels.
- **Coordinated Universal Time (UTC)**: The primary global time standard (formerly Greenwich Mean Time, GMT) from which all world time zones are measured as positive (ahead/east) or negative (behind/west) offsets.
- **Australian Standard Time Zones**: Australia has three main standard time zones: Australian Western Standard Time (AWST, $\text{UTC}+8$), Australian Central Standard Time (ACST, $\text{UTC}+9.5$), and Australian Eastern Standard Time (AEST, $\text{UTC}+10$).
- **Daylight Saving Time (ACDT and AEDT)**: Between the first Sunday in October and the first Sunday in April, clocks in SA create ACDT ($\text{UTC}+10.5$) and clocks in VIC, NSW, TAS, and ACT move $1\text{ hour}$ forward to AEDT ($\text{UTC}+11$), while WA, QLD, and NT stay on standard time.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Converting Between 12-Hour and 24-Hour Time

In 12-hour time, the day is split into two $12$-hour periods: **am** (*ante meridiem*, before noon) and **pm** (*post meridiem*, after noon). In 24-hour time, hours are numbered continuously from $00$ to $23$. For morning times from $1\text{:00 am}$ to $11\text{:59 am}$, write a leading zero (`0100` to `1159`). For afternoon/evening times from $1\text{:00 pm}$ to $11\text{:59 pm}$, add $12$ to the hours (`1300` to `2359`). Special cases: $12\text{:00 am}$ (midnight) is `0000` and $12\text{:00 pm}$ (noon) is `1200`.

$$\text{pm hour } (1\text{ to }11) + 12 = 24\text{-hour value}; \qquad 12\text{:XX am} \to \texttt{00XX}, \quad 12\text{:XX pm} \to \texttt{12XX}$$

> **💡 Watch Out for the 12s: Midnight vs Noon**
> $12\text{:30 am}$ is $30$ minutes after midnight, so it is `0030` (NOT `1230`). $12\text{:30 pm}$ is $30$ minutes after noon, so it stays `1230` (do NOT add $12$ to $12\text{ pm}$).

### Calculating Elapsed Duration and Crossing Midnight

Because time uses base $60$ ($1\text{ hour} = 60\text{ minutes}$), never subtract times as ordinary base-$10$ decimals! The safest strategy to find the duration between a start time and an end time is **bridging (chunking) to the next whole hour**, then counting whole hours, or borrowing $1\text{ hour} = 60\text{ minutes}$ when subtracting in 24-hour time. When an event crosses midnight (`24:00` / `00:00`), find the time from start to midnight first, then add the time after midnight.

$$\text{Duration across midnight} = (24\text{:00} - t_{\text{start}}) + t_{\text{end}}$$

> **💡 Borrow 60 Minutes, Never 100**
> When subtracting `16:20 - 13:45`, rewrite `16:20` as $15\text{ hours } 80\text{ minutes}$ ($60 + 20 = 80$), then subtract: $(15 - 13)\text{ h } (80 - 45)\text{ min} = 2\text{ h } 35\text{ min}$.

### Australian Time Zones: Standard Time and Daylight Saving Time

During winter (Standard Time), Australia uses three zones: **AWST** (WA, $\text{UTC}+8$), **ACST** (SA and NT, $\text{UTC}+9.5$, which is $1.5\text{ h}$ ahead of WA and $0.5\text{ h}$ behind the east), and **AEST** (QLD, NSW, VIC, TAS, ACT, $\text{UTC}+10$, which is $2\text{ h}$ ahead of WA). During summer (Daylight Saving), VIC, NSW, TAS, and ACT advance to **AEDT** ($\text{UTC}+11$) and SA advances to **ACDT** ($\text{UTC}+10.5$), while WA ($\text{UTC}+8$), NT ($\text{UTC}+9.5$), and QLD ($\text{UTC}+10$) do not change their clocks.

$$\text{Time Difference } \Delta t = \text{Offset}_{\text{destination}} - \text{Offset}_{\text{origin}}$$

> **💡 East is Ahead (+), West is Behind (-)**
> Moving east towards a larger UTC offset means local clock time is later (add the offset difference); moving west towards a smaller UTC offset means local clock time is earlier (subtract the offset difference).

### International Flights and Multi-Zone Travel Problems

To solve flight arrival or duration problems across different time zones, always convert the departure and arrival times into the **same time zone** (either the departure city's local time, the arrival city's local time, or UTC) before adding or subtracting flight duration.

$$\text{Arrival (Local Dest)} = \text{Departure (converted to Dest Time)} + \text{Flight Duration}$$

> **💡 Convert to One Common Time Zone First**
> Never subtract local departure time from local arrival time directly! Convert the departure time into the destination's time zone first, and then add or subtract the flight time.

### WE7_4_1: Converting 12-Hour and 24-Hour Times and Finding Duration Across Midnight

**Problem**: An overnight V/Line coach departs Southern Cross Station in Melbourne at $9\text{:45 pm}$ on Friday and arrives in Mildura at $4\text{:20 am}$ on Saturday. (a) Write both the departure and arrival times in 24-hour time. (b) Calculate the total duration of the journey in hours and minutes.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | For $9\text{:45 pm}$, add $12$ to the hours ($9 + 12 = 21$) to get `2145` (or `21:45`). For $4\text{:20 am}$, include a leading zero to get `0420` (or `04:20`). | $$9\text{:45 pm} \to \texttt{2145} \; (21\text{:45}), \qquad 4\text{:20 am} \to \texttt{0420} \; (04\text{:20})$$ |
| 2 | Bridge across midnight (`24:00`): find the duration from $9\text{:45 pm}$ to midnight, then add the duration from midnight to $4\text{:20 am}$. | $$9\text{:45 pm to } 10\text{:00 pm} = 15\text{ min}; \quad 10\text{:00 pm to midnight} = 2\text{ h} \implies 2\text{ h } 15\text{ min before midnight}$$
$$\text{Total duration} = 2\text{ h } 15\text{ min} + 4\text{ h } 20\text{ min} = 6\text{ hours } 35\text{ minutes}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) Departure `2145`, Arrival `0420`; (b) $6\text{ hours } 35\text{ minutes}$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Subtracting Times as Base-10 Decimals Instead of Base-60 Hours and Minutes
- **The Misconception**: Treating `15:20 - 12:45` as ordinary decimal subtraction $15.20 - 12.45 = 2.75$ and claiming the duration is $2\text{ hours } 75\text{ minutes}$ (or $2\text{ hours } 45\text{ minutes}$).
- **The Correct Concept**: There are $60\text{ minutes}$ in $1\text{ hour}$, not $100$. When borrowing $1\text{ hour}$ from the hours column, add $60\text{ minutes}$ to the minutes column ($20 + 60 = 80\text{ minutes}$).
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$15.20 - 12.45 = 2.75 \implies 2\text{ h } 75\text{ min} \quad \text{(Incorrect — borrowed 100 instead of 60)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$15\text{ h } 20\text{ min} = 14\text{ h } 80\text{ min}; \quad (14 - 12)\text{ h } (80 - 45)\text{ min} = 2\text{ h } 35\text{ min}$$
- **Counter-Example**: From $12\text{:45 pm}$ to $1\text{:00 pm}$ is $15\text{ min}$, and from $1\text{:00 pm}$ to $3\text{:20 pm}$ is $2\text{ h } 20\text{ min}$, giving $2\text{ h } 35\text{ min}$.

### Confusing 12:00 am (Midnight) and 12:00 pm (Noon) in 24-Hour Time
- **The Misconception**: Writing $12\text{:15 am}$ as `1215` or adding $12$ to $12\text{:15 pm}$ to get `2415`.
- **The Correct Concept**: The new day begins at $12\text{:00 am}$ (midnight), which has $00$ hours (`0000`), so $12\text{:15 am}$ is `0015`. Midday ($12\text{:00 pm}$) is already $12$ hours into the day, so $12\text{:15 pm}$ is `1215`.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$12\text{:15 am} \to \texttt{1215} \quad \text{and} \quad 12\text{:15 pm} \to \texttt{2415} \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$12\text{:15 am} \to \texttt{0015} \quad \text{and} \quad 12\text{:15 pm} \to \texttt{1215}$$
- **Counter-Example**: One minute after $11\text{:59 am}$ (`1159`) is $12\text{:00 pm}$ (`1200`), and one minute after $11\text{:59 pm}$ (`2359`) is $12\text{:00 am}$ (`0000`).

#### Question `7_4_Q11` [Challenging] *(Base-10 vs Base-60 Reasoning)*
Liam calculates the number of minutes between `09:50` and `12:10` by computing $1210 - 950 = 260\text{ minutes}$. Explain mathematically why subtracting four-digit 24-hour integers always overestimates the duration by $40\text{ minutes}$ for every hour boundary crossed, and use this rule to correct Liam's answer.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. In four-digit 24-hour notation, $1\text{ hour}$ increases the number by $100$, whereas $1\text{ hour}$ is actually $60\text{ minutes}$ — an excess of $100 - 60 = 40$ per hour boundary.
2. From `09:50` to `12:10` crosses $12 - 9 = 3$ hour boundaries, so subtracting $3 \times 40 = 120$ from $260$ gives the true duration: $260 - 120 = 140\text{ minutes}$ ($2\text{ hours } 20\text{ minutes}$).

**Final Answer**: Each hour boundary jumps by $100 - 60 = 40$ in four-digit notation (e.g. `0959` to `1000` jumps by $41$ instead of $1$). Since `09:50` to `12:10` crosses $3$ hour boundaries ($10\text{:00}, 11\text{:00}, 12\text{:00}$), the true duration is $260 - 3 \times 40 = 140\text{ minutes}$ ($2\text{ h } 20\text{ min}$).
</details>

#### Question `7_4_Q12` [Extension] *(Astronomy & Solar Time Zones)*
Earth rotates through $360^\circ$ of longitude every $24\text{ hours}$. (a) Through how many degrees of longitude does Earth rotate in $1\text{ hour}$, and how many minutes of time correspond to $1^\circ$ of longitude? (b) Melbourne lies near longitude $145^\circ\text{ E}$ and Perth lies near $116^\circ\text{ E}$. Based purely on solar longitude, what is the true solar time difference (in hours and minutes) between Melbourne and Perth?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. In $1\text{ hour}$, Earth rotates $\frac{360^\circ}{24} = 15^\circ$. Each $1^\circ$ of longitude corresponds to $\frac{60\text{ min}}{15} = 4\text{ minutes}$ of solar time.
2. The longitude difference is $145^\circ - 116^\circ = 29^\circ$. Multiplying by $4\text{ minutes/degree}$ gives $29 \times 4 = 116\text{ minutes} = 1\text{ hour } 56\text{ minutes}$ (which is why WA and the eastern states have a $2$-hour standard time difference).

**Final Answer**: (a) $15^\circ$ per hour ($4\text{ minutes}$ per degree of longitude); (b) $1\text{ hour } 56\text{ minutes}$ ($116\text{ minutes}$)
</details>
