**VCAA Version 2.0 Alignment**: `VC2M8M03` (Measurement)
> Solve problems involving the volume and capacity of right prisms using appropriate units; solve problems involving duration and time zones.

**Target Mastery**: Calculate the volume of right prisms using $V = Ah$, convert between volume ($\text{cm}^3, \text{m}^3$) and liquid capacity ($\text{mL}, \text{L}, \text{kL}$), and solve duration and Australian/global time zone problems.

**Prerequisite Skills**:
- Area of rectangles, triangles, and trapeziums (Section 7A)
- 24-hour time and elapsed time

### Key Vocabulary
- **Right prism and uniform cross-section**: A 3D solid with two identical, parallel polygon bases (its uniform cross-section of area $A$) joined by rectangular side faces perpendicular to the base.
- **Volume vs Capacity**: Volume is the amount of 3D space an object occupies (measured in $\text{mm}^3, \text{cm}^3, \text{m}^3$). Capacity is the amount of fluid a container can hold (measured in $\text{mL}, \text{L}, \text{kL}, \text{ML}$).
- **Australian Time Zones**: Standard time zones across Australia relative to Coordinated Universal Time (UTC): AWST (UTC$+8$), ACST (UTC$+9.5$), and AEST (UTC$+10$), plus Daylight Saving AEDT (UTC$+11$) and ACDT (UTC$+10.5$).

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Volume of Any Right Prism ($V = Ah$)

Every right prism has a uniform cross-section (the shape you see if you slice the prism parallel to its base anywhere along its length). The volume $V$ of any right prism equals the area $A$ of its uniform cross-section multiplied by the perpendicular length or height $h$ connecting the two identical bases.

$$V = Ah \quad \left(V_{\text{rect prism}} = lwh, \quad V_{\text{triangular prism}} = \frac{1}{2}bh \times L\right)$$

> **💡 Identify the Uniform Cross-Section First**
> The base $A$ of a prism is NOT always the face resting on the ground — it is the two-dimensional face that stays identical when sliced parallel to the ends.

### Converting Volume Units and Linking Volume to Capacity

Because volume is three-dimensional, linear conversion factors are cubed: $1\text{ cm}^3 = 10^3\text{ mm}^3 = 1000\text{ mm}^3$ and $1\text{ m}^3 = 100^3\text{ cm}^3 = 1\,000\,000\text{ cm}^3$. By metric definition, $1\text{ cm}^3$ holds exactly $1\text{ mL}$ of liquid, a $10\text{ cm} \times 10\text{ cm} \times 10\text{ cm}$ cube ($1000\text{ cm}^3$) holds $1\text{ L}$, and $1\text{ m}^3$ holds $1000\text{ L} = 1\text{ kilolitre (kL)}$.

$$1\text{ cm}^3 = 1\text{ mL}, \qquad 1000\text{ cm}^3 = 1\text{ L}, \qquad 1\text{ m}^3 = 1000\text{ L} = 1\text{ kL}$$

> **💡 Three Key Bridges Between Volume and Capacity**
> Memorise: $1\text{ cm}^3 = 1\text{ mL}$ and $1\text{ m}^3 = 1000\text{ L} = 1\text{ kL}$.

### 24-Hour Time, Duration, and Australian Time Zones

In standard winter time, Australia has three main time zones: Australian Western Standard Time (AWST, UTC$+8$: WA), Australian Central Standard Time (ACST, UTC$+9.5$: SA, NT), and Australian Eastern Standard Time (AEST, UTC$+10$: VIC, NSW, QLD, TAS, ACT). During Daylight Saving (October–April), VIC, NSW, TAS, and ACT move $1$ hour forward to AEDT (UTC$+11$), and SA moves to ACDT (UTC$+10.5$), while WA, NT, and QLD do not observe Daylight Saving.

$$\text{Standard: AWST (UTC}+8\text{)} \xrightarrow{+1.5\text{ h}} \text{ACST (UTC}+9.5\text{)} \xrightarrow{+0.5\text{ h}} \text{AEST (UTC}+10\text{)}$$

> **💡 East is Ahead; West is Behind**
> When converting from a western city (e.g., Perth) to an eastern city (e.g., Melbourne), ADD the time difference. Always convert flight departure and arrival to the SAME time zone before finding flight duration.

## Worked Examples

### WE7_3_1: Calculating the Volume of a Triangular Prism

**Problem**: A right triangular prism has a triangular cross-section with base $b = 12\text{ cm}$ and perpendicular triangle height $h_t = 9\text{ cm}$, and a prism length of $L = 20\text{ cm}$. Find (a) the cross-sectional area $A$, and (b) the volume $V$ of the prism in $\text{cm}^3$ and in litres ($\text{L}$).

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Calculate the area $A$ of the triangular cross-section using $A = \frac{1}{2}bh_t$. | $$A = \frac{1}{2} \times 12 \times 9 = 54\text{ cm}^2$$ |
| 2 | Multiply $A$ by the prism length $L = 20\text{ cm}$ to find the volume in $\text{cm}^3$, then divide by $1000$ to convert $\text{cm}^3$ to litres. | $$V = Ah = 54 \times 20 = 1080\text{ cm}^3 = \frac{1080}{1000}\text{ L} = 1.08\text{ L}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $A = 54\text{ cm}^2$, (b) $V = 1080\text{ cm}^3 = 1.08\text{ L}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: A triangular prism has a triangular cross-section of base $8\text{ cm}$ and height $5\text{ cm}$, and a prism length of $15\text{ cm}$. Find its volume in $\text{cm}^3$ and in $\text{mL}$.
> *Hint*: Find $A = \frac{1}{2}(8)(5) = 20\text{ cm}^2$, multiply by $15$, and use $1\text{ cm}^3 = 1\text{ mL}$.
> **Working**: $A = 20\text{ cm}^2$; $V = 20 \times 15 = 300\text{ cm}^3 = 300\text{ mL}$.
> **Answer**: $300\text{ cm}^3$ ($300\text{ mL}$)

### WE7_3_2: Volume and Capacity of a Trapezoidal Swimming Pool

**Problem**: A rectangular swimming pool is $25\text{ m}$ long and $10\text{ m}$ wide. Its depth slopes uniformly from $1.2\text{ m}$ at the shallow end to $2.4\text{ m}$ at the deep end, so its side profile is a trapezium. Find (a) the volume of the pool in $\text{m}^3$ and (b) its capacity in litres ($\text{L}$).

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | The uniform cross-section is the trapezoidal side wall with parallel vertical depths $a = 1.2\text{ m}$ and $b = 2.4\text{ m}$ separated by the $25\text{ m}$ length. | $$A = \frac{1}{2}(1.2 + 2.4) \times 25 = 1.8 \times 25 = 45\text{ m}^2$$ |
| 2 | Multiply the cross-sectional area $A = 45\text{ m}^2$ by the pool width $h = 10\text{ m}$, then multiply by $1000$ since $1\text{ m}^3 = 1000\text{ L}$. | $$V = 45 \times 10 = 450\text{ m}^3 = 450 \times 1000\text{ L} = 450\,000\text{ L}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $450\text{ m}^3$, (b) $450\,000\text{ L}$ ($450\text{ kL}$)

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: A water trough has a trapezoidal cross-section with parallel top and bottom widths $60\text{ cm}$ and $40\text{ cm}$, perpendicular depth $30\text{ cm}$, and length $200\text{ cm}$. Find its capacity in litres.
> *Hint*: Find $A = \frac{1}{2}(60 + 40) \times 30\text{ cm}^2$, multiply by $200\text{ cm}$, and divide by $1000$.
> **Working**: $A = 1500\text{ cm}^2$; $V = 1500 \times 200 = 300\,000\text{ cm}^3 = 300\text{ L}$.
> **Answer**: $300\text{ L}$

### WE7_3_3: Calculating Flight Arrivals Across Australian Time Zones

**Problem**: In January (during Daylight Saving), Melbourne is on AEDT (UTC$+11$) and Perth is on AWST (UTC$+8$). A Qantas flight departs Melbourne at $10{:}45\text{ am}$ AEDT and takes $4\text{ hours } 20\text{ minutes}$. At what local time (AWST) does the flight land in Perth?

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | First find the arrival time in Melbourne time (AEDT) by adding the flight duration of $4\text{ h } 20\text{ min}$ to $10{:}45\text{ am}$. | $$10{:}45 + 4\text{ h } 20\text{ min} = 15{:}05\text{ (}3{:}05\text{ pm AEDT)}$$ |
| 2 | Perth (UTC$+8$) is $11 - 8 = 3$ hours behind Melbourne (UTC$+11$). Subtract $3$ hours from $15{:}05$. | $$15{:}05 - 3\text{ hours} = 12{:}05\text{ pm AWST}$$ |

**Answer**: $12{:}05\text{ pm}$ AWST

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: On the same January day, a return flight departs Perth at $1{:}30\text{ pm}$ AWST (UTC$+8$) and flies for $3\text{ hours } 45\text{ minutes}$ to Melbourne (AEDT, UTC$+11$). What is the local arrival time in Melbourne?
> *Hint*: Add $3\text{ h } 45\text{ min}$ to $13{:}30$, then add $3\text{ hours}$ for the time zone change.
> **Working**: $13{:}30 + 3\text{ h } 45\text{ min} = 17{:}15\text{ AWST}$; $+ 3\text{ h} = 20{:}15 = 8{:}15\text{ pm AEDT}$.
> **Answer**: $8{:}15\text{ pm}$ AEDT ($20{:}15$)

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Equating $1\text{ m}^3$ to $100\text{ L}$ Instead of $1000\text{ L}$
- **The Misconception**: Thinking $1\text{ m}^3 = 100\text{ L}$ because $1\text{ m} = 100\text{ cm}$.
- **The Correct Concept**: A $1\text{ m} \times 1\text{ m} \times 1\text{ m}$ cube holds $10 \times 10 \times 10 = 1000$ one-litre cubes (each $10\text{ cm} \times 10\text{ cm} \times 10\text{ cm}$), so $1\text{ m}^3 = 1000\text{ L} = 1\text{ kL}$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$2.5\text{ m}^3 = 2.5 \times 100 = 250\text{ L} \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$2.5\text{ m}^3 = 2.5 \times 1000 = 2500\text{ L}$$
- **Counter-Example**: $1\text{ m}^3 = 1\,000\,000\text{ cm}^3 = 1\,000\,000\text{ mL} = 1000\text{ L}$.

### Treating Minutes as Base-100 Decimals ($1.5\text{ hours} \neq 1\text{ h } 50\text{ min}$)
- **The Misconception**: Interpreting $2.5\text{ hours}$ as $2\text{ hours } 50\text{ minutes}$ or subtracting times as standard base-$10$ numbers.
- **The Correct Concept**: There are $60$ minutes in $1$ hour, so $0.5\text{ hours} = 0.5 \times 60 = 30\text{ minutes}$, and $0.25\text{ hours} = 15\text{ minutes}$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$3.5\text{ hours} = 3\text{ hours } 50\text{ minutes} \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$3.5\text{ hours} = 3\text{ hours } + (0.5 \times 60)\text{ minutes} = 3\text{ hours } 30\text{ minutes}$$
- **Counter-Example**: Half an hour is $30$ minutes, not $50$ minutes.

### Fluency & Understanding

#### Question `7_3_Q1` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Calculate the volume in $\text{cm}^3$ and capacity in litres ($\text{L}$) of a rectangular fish tank measuring $60\text{ cm}$ long, $30\text{ cm}$ wide, and $40\text{ cm}$ high.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Multiply the three dimensions: $V = lwh = 60 \times 30 \times 40 = 72\,000\text{ cm}^3$.
2. Divide by $1000$ to convert $\text{cm}^3$ to litres: $72\,000 \div 1000 = 72\text{ L}$.

**Final Answer**: $72\,000\text{ cm}^3 = 72\text{ L}$
</details>

#### Question `7_3_Q2` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Convert: (a) $4.2\text{ m}^3$ to litres ($\text{L}$), and (b) $8500\text{ cm}^3$ to litres ($\text{L}$).

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Since $1\text{ m}^3 = 1000\text{ L}$, $4.2\text{ m}^3 = 4.2 \times 1000 = 4200\text{ L}$.
2. Since $1000\text{ cm}^3 = 1\text{ L}$, $8500\text{ cm}^3 = 8500 \div 1000 = 8.5\text{ L}$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $4200\text{ L}$, (b) $8.5\text{ L}$
</details>

#### Question `7_3_Q3` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
A right prism has an L-shaped cross-sectional area of $42\text{ cm}^2$ and a length of $15\text{ cm}$. Calculate its volume.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Use the prism volume formula $V = Ah$ with $A = 42\text{ cm}^2$ and $h = 15\text{ cm}$.
2. Multiply: $V = 42 \times 15 = 630\text{ cm}^3$.

**Final Answer**: $630\text{ cm}^3$
</details>

#### Question `7_3_Q4` [Medium] *(Understanding)*
A right triangular prism has a volume of $540\text{ cm}^3$ and a length of $18\text{ cm}$. If the base of its triangular cross-section is $10\text{ cm}$, find the perpendicular height $h_t$ of the triangle.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. First find the cross-sectional area $A = \frac{V}{L} = \frac{540}{18} = 30\text{ cm}^2$.
2. Substitute into the triangle area formula $30 = \frac{1}{2}(10)h_t = 5h_t \implies h_t = 6\text{ cm}$.

**Final Answer**: $6\text{ cm}$
</details>

#### Question `7_3_Q5` [Medium] *(Australian Time Zones)*
In June (standard time), Adelaide is on ACST (UTC$+9.5$) and Brisbane is on AEST (UTC$+10$). When a live AFL match kicks off in Adelaide at $7{:}10\text{ pm}$ ACST, what is the local time in (a) Brisbane (AEST) and (b) Perth (AWST, UTC$+8$)?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Brisbane (UTC$+10$) is $0.5$ hours ($30$ minutes) ahead of Adelaide (UTC$+9.5$), giving $7{:}10\text{ pm} + 30\text{ min} = 7{:}40\text{ pm}$ AEST.
2. Perth (UTC$+8$) is $1.5$ hours ($1\text{ h } 30\text{ min}$) behind Adelaide (UTC$+9.5$), giving $7{:}10\text{ pm} - 1\text{ h } 30\text{ min} = 5{:}40\text{ pm}$ AWST.

**Final Answer**: (a) $7{:}40\text{ pm}$ AEST, (b) $5{:}40\text{ pm}$ AWST
</details>

#### Question `7_3_Q6` [Easy] *(Victorian Rail Travel)*
A V/Line train departs Southern Cross Station at $08{:}48$ and arrives in Warrnambool at $12{:}23$ on the same day. How long is the journey in hours and minutes?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Count forward from $08{:}48$ to $09{:}00$ ($12\text{ minutes}$), then from $09{:}00$ to $12{:}00$ ($3\text{ hours}$), plus $23\text{ minutes}$ to $12{:}23$.
2. Add the parts: $3\text{ hours} + (12 + 23)\text{ minutes} = 3\text{ hours } 35\text{ minutes}$.

**Final Answer**: $3\text{ hours } 35\text{ minutes}$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `7_3_Q7` [Medium] *(Australian Rainwater Harvesting)*
A rectangular house roof in Ballarat has a horizontal footprint area of $180\text{ m}^2$. During a storm, $25\text{ mm}$ ($0.025\text{ m}$) of rain falls uniformly across the roof and drains into a rainwater tank with no loss. How many litres of water enter the tank?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate the volume of water in cubic metres: $V = Ah = 180\text{ m}^2 \times 0.025\text{ m} = 4.5\text{ m}^3$.
2. Convert $\text{m}^3$ to litres by multiplying by $1000$: $4.5 \times 1000 = 4500\text{ L}$.

**Final Answer**: $4500\text{ L}$
</details>

#### Question `7_3_Q8` [Medium] *(Construction Modelling)*
A concrete driveway slab is $12\text{ m}$ long, $3.5\text{ m}$ wide, and $15\text{ cm}$ ($0.15\text{ m}$) thick. Ready-mix concrete costs $\$280$ per cubic metre. Calculate (a) the volume of concrete required in $\text{m}^3$ and (b) the total cost of the concrete.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Convert all dimensions to metres and multiply: $V = 12 \times 3.5 \times 0.15 = 6.3\text{ m}^3$.
2. Multiply volume by $\$280/\text{m}^3$: $6.3 \times 280 = \$1764$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $6.3\text{ m}^3$, (b) $\$1764$
</details>

#### Question `7_3_Q9` [Challenging] *(International Aviation)*
An international flight departs Melbourne (AEDT, UTC$+11$) at $11{:}30\text{ pm}$ on Tuesday and arrives in Singapore (SGT, UTC$+8$) at $4{:}15\text{ am}$ SGT on Wednesday. Find the flight duration in hours and minutes.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Convert the departure time to Singapore time (UTC$+8$ is $3$ hours behind UTC$+11$): $11{:}30\text{ pm} - 3\text{ hours} = 8{:}30\text{ pm}$ SGT on Tuesday.
2. Find the elapsed time from $8{:}30\text{ pm}$ Tuesday to $4{:}15\text{ am}$ Wednesday: $3\text{ h } 30\text{ min}$ to midnight plus $4\text{ h } 15\text{ min}$ after midnight $= 7\text{ hours } 45\text{ minutes}$.

**Final Answer**: $7\text{ hours } 45\text{ minutes}$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `7_3_Q10` [Medium] *(Identifying Cross-Sections)*
A triangular tent is $2.4\text{ m}$ long, with a triangular front opening of base $1.8\text{ m}$ and height $1.5\text{ m}$. A student says the 'base' of the tent is the $2.4\text{ m} \times 1.8\text{ m}$ rectangular floor and multiplies that area by $1.5\text{ m}$. Explain why this is wrong and calculate the correct volume.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Slices parallel to the rectangular floor shrink as you move upwards towards the top ridge, whereas slices parallel to the triangular front stay identical along the $2.4\text{ m}$ length.
2. Using the triangular cross-section $A = \frac{1}{2}(1.8)(1.5) = 1.35\text{ m}^2$ gives the true volume $V = 1.35 \times 2.4 = 3.24\text{ m}^3$ (half the student's answer of $6.48\text{ m}^3$).

**Final Answer**: The rectangular floor is not a uniform cross-section because the tent narrows to a ridge at the top; correct volume is $V = \left(\frac{1}{2} \times 1.8 \times 1.5\right) \times 2.4 = 3.24\text{ m}^3$
</details>

#### Question `7_3_Q11` [Easy] *(Australian Geography & Time)*
During Daylight Saving in January, why is Brisbane (QLD) $1$ hour behind Sydney (NSW) even though both cities lie at nearly the same longitude on Australia's east coast?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Time zones follow state/territory legislation as well as longitude.
2. In summer, NSW observes Daylight Saving Time (AEDT, UTC$+11$) while Queensland stays on Australian Eastern Standard Time (AEST, UTC$+10$), creating a $1$-hour difference.

**Final Answer**: New South Wales advances its clocks by $1$ hour to AEDT (UTC$+11$) in summer, whereas Queensland remains on standard time AEST (UTC$+10$) all year round
</details>

#### Question `7_3_Q12` [Extension] *(3D Scaling Proof)*
If every linear dimension (length, width, and height) of a rectangular prism is multiplied by a scale factor of $k = 3$, by what factor does (a) its surface area increase and (b) its volume increase? Prove your answer algebraically.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Replacing $l, w, h$ with $3l, 3w, 3h$ in each rectangular face area multiplies every face area by $3 \times 3 = 9$.
2. In the volume formula, $V_{\text{new}} = (3l)(3w)(3h) = 27lwh = 27V$, so the volume increases by $3^3 = 27$.

**Final Answer**: (a) Surface area increases by a factor of $3^2 = 9$; (b) volume increases by a factor of $3^3 = 27$
</details>
