### Key Vocabulary
- **Prime number**: A whole number greater than $1$ that has exactly two positive factors: $1$ and itself (e.g., $2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, \dots$).
- **Prime factorisation**: Expressing a composite number as a product of prime numbers only, usually written in ascending order with exponent (index) notation.
- **Highest Common Factor (HCF)**: The largest positive integer that divides evenly into two or more given integers without remainder.
- **Lowest Common Multiple (LCM)**: The smallest positive integer that is a multiple of two or more given integers.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic and Index Form

Every integer greater than $1$ is either prime itself or can be factorised uniquely as a product of prime numbers (up to the order of the factors). Using a factor tree or repeated division by primes, we collect repeated prime factors using exponent (index) notation in ascending order of prime bases.

$$360 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 5 = 2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5$$

> **💡 Factor Tree Rule**
> Keep splitting composite branches until every branch ends at a prime number ($2, 3, 5, 7, 11, \dots$). Never include $1$ in a prime factorisation, as $1$ is neither prime nor composite.

### Finding the Highest Common Factor (HCF) Using Prime Factors

To find the Highest Common Factor (HCF) of two or more large numbers, write each number in prime factor exponent form. Identify only the prime bases that are common to every number, and take the lowest power (minimum exponent) of each shared prime factor.

$$\text{HCF}(2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5,\; 2^2 \times 3^3 \times 7) = 2^{\min(3,2)} \times 3^{\min(2,3)} = 2^2 \times 3^2 = 36$$

> **💡 HCF = Shared Primes, Lowest Powers**
> Even though 'Highest' is in the name HCF, you select the LOWEST exponent of each prime factor that appears in ALL of the numbers so that the factor can divide into each of them.

### Finding the Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) Using Prime Factors

To find the Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) of two or more numbers from their prime factorisations, include every prime base that appears in at least one of the numbers, raised to the highest power (maximum exponent) with which it appears in any single number.

$$\text{LCM}(2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5,\; 2^2 \times 3^3 \times 7) = 2^3 \times 3^3 \times 5 \times 7 = 7560$$

> **💡 LCM = All Primes, Highest Powers**
> Even though 'Lowest' is in the name LCM, you select the HIGHEST exponent of every prime factor across the numbers so that every original number divides evenly into the multiple.

### The Product Identity for Two Positive Integers

For any two positive integers $a$ and $b$, the product of their Highest Common Factor and their Lowest Common Multiple is always equal to the product of the two integers themselves.

$$\text{HCF}(a, b) \times \text{LCM}(a, b) = a \times b$$

> **💡 HCF-LCM Product Check**
> Once you know $\text{HCF}(a, b)$, you can quickly find $\text{LCM}(a, b)$ using $\text{LCM}(a, b) = \frac{a \times b}{\text{HCF}(a, b)}$.

## Worked Examples

### WE1_4_1: Expressing a Composite Number in Prime Factor Index Form

**Problem**: Express $504$ as a product of prime factors in exponent (index) form.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Repeatedly divide by the smallest prime factor $2$ until the quotient is odd: $504 \div 2 = 252$, $252 \div 2 = 126$, $126 \div 2 = 63$. | $$504 = 2^3 \times 63$$ |
| 2 | Factorise $63$ by dividing by the prime $3$: $63 = 9 \times 7 = 3^2 \times 7$. | $$63 = 3^2 \times 7$$ |
| 3 | Combine all prime factors in ascending order of base using exponent notation. | $$504 = 2^3 \times 3^2 \times 7$$ |

**Answer**: $2^3 \times 3^2 \times 7$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Express $396$ as a product of prime factors in exponent form.
> *Hint*: Divide by $2$ twice first ($396 = 4 \times 99$), then factorise $99$.
> **Working**: $396 = 4 \times 99 = 2^2 \times 9 \times 11 = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 11$.
> **Answer**: $2^2 \times 3^2 \times 11$

### WE1_4_2: Finding HCF and LCM via Prime Factorisation

**Problem**: Use prime factorisation to find (a) the Highest Common Factor (HCF) and (b) the Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) of $72$ and $120$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Write both $72$ and $120$ as products of prime factors in index form. | $$72 = 8 \times 9 = 2^3 \times 3^2 \quad \text{and} \quad 120 = 8 \times 3 \times 5 = 2^3 \times 3^1 \times 5^1$$ |
| 2 | For the HCF, take the shared prime bases ($2$ and $3$) raised to their lowest exponents: $\min(3,3) = 3$ for base $2$, and $\min(2,1) = 1$ for base $3$. | $$\text{HCF}(72, 120) = 2^3 \times 3^1 = 8 \times 3 = 24$$ |
| 3 | For the LCM, take all prime bases ($2$, $3$, and $5$) raised to their highest exponents: $2^3$, $3^2$, and $5^1$. | $$\text{LCM}(72, 120) = 2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5^1 = 8 \times 9 \times 5 = 360$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $\text{HCF} = 24$, (b) $\text{LCM} = 360$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Use prime factorisation to find (a) the HCF and (b) the LCM of $84$ and $140$.
> *Hint*: First show that $84 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 7$ and $140 = 2^2 \times 5 \times 7$.
> **Working**: $84 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 7$ and $140 = 2^2 \times 5 \times 7$. $\text{HCF} = 2^2 \times 7 = 28$; $\text{LCM} = 2^2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 7 = 420$.
> **Answer**: (a) $\text{HCF} = 28$, (b) $\text{LCM} = 420$

### WE1_4_3: Melbourne Tram Synchronisation (Applied LCM)

**Problem**: At Flinders Street Station in Melbourne, a Route 70 tram departs every $12\text{ minutes}$ and a Route 75 tram departs every $15\text{ minutes}$. If both trams depart together at $8\text{:00 am}$, at what time will they next depart together from Flinders Street Station?

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | The number of minutes until both trams depart together again is the Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) of $12$ and $15$. | $$12 = 2^2 \times 3 \quad \text{and} \quad 15 = 3 \times 5$$ |
| 2 | Compute the LCM by taking the highest power of each prime factor ($2^2$, $3$, and $5$). | $$\text{LCM}(12, 15) = 2^2 \times 3 \times 5 = 4 \times 15 = 60\text{ minutes}$$ |
| 3 | Convert $60\text{ minutes}$ to $1\text{ hour}$ and add to $8\text{:00 am}$. | $$8\text{:00 am} + 60\text{ minutes} = 9\text{:00 am}$$ |

**Answer**: $9\text{:00 am}$ (after $60\text{ minutes}$)

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: At Southern Cross Station, three regional V/Line trains depart for Geelong, Ballarat, and Bendigo every $20\text{ minutes}$, $24\text{ minutes}$, and $30\text{ minutes}$ respectively. If all three depart simultaneously at $9\text{:00 am}$, how many minutes pass before all three depart together again, and at what time does this occur?
> *Hint*: Find $\text{LCM}(20, 24, 30)$.
> **Working**: $20 = 2^2 \times 5$, $24 = 2^3 \times 3$, $30 = 2 \times 3 \times 5$. $\text{LCM} = 2^3 \times 3 \times 5 = 120\text{ minutes}$ ($2\text{ hours}$), so $11\text{:00 am}$.
> **Answer**: $120\text{ minutes}$ ($11\text{:00 am}$)

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Stopping a Factor Tree at a Composite Factor or Including 1
- **The Misconception**: Writing the prime factorisation of $72$ as $2^3 \times 9$ (leaving $9$ unfactorised) or writing $1 \times 2^3 \times 3^2$.
- **The Correct Concept**: Every base in a prime factorisation must be a prime number. Since $9 = 3^2$ is composite and $1$ is not prime, the unique prime factorisation of $72$ is $2^3 \times 3^2$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$72 = 2^3 \times 9 \quad (\text{Incorrect: } 9 \text{ is composite})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$72 = 2^3 \times 3^2 \quad (\text{Correct: } 2 \text{ and } 3 \text{ are prime})$$
- **Counter-Example**: If composite factors were allowed, factorisations would not be unique (e.g., $72 = 8 \times 9 = 6 \times 12$).

### Confusing HCF and LCM in Word Problems
- **The Misconception**: Using LCM when asked to cut lengths of timber into the largest possible equal pieces, or using HCF when asked when two repeating events will next coincide.
- **The Correct Concept**: Splitting/dividing items into smaller equal groups or lengths requires a factor (HCF $\le$ the given numbers). Repeating cycles meeting in the future requires a multiple (LCM $\ge$ the given numbers).
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\text{Equal cut length of } 18\text{ m and } 24\text{ m} = \text{LCM}(18, 24) = 72\text{ m} \quad (\text{Impossible!})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\text{Equal cut length of } 18\text{ m and } 24\text{ m} = \text{HCF}(18, 24) = 6\text{ m} \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: You cannot cut $72\text{ m}$ pieces out of an $18\text{ m}$ plank of timber; each piece must be at most $18\text{ m}$ long.

#### Question `1_4_Q1` [Easy] *(Prime Factorisation)*
Write $180$ as a product of prime factors in exponent (index) form, and verify its value.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Split $180 = 18 \times 10$.
2. Factorise $18 = 2 \times 3^2$ and $10 = 2 \times 5$.
3. Collect like prime bases: $180 = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 5$.

**Final Answer**: $2^2 \times 3^2 \times 5$

#### Question `1_4_Q2` [Easy] *(Highest Common Factor)*
Find the Highest Common Factor (HCF) of $96$ and $144$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Write each number in prime factor form: $96 = 2^5 \times 3$ and $144 = 2^4 \times 3^2$.
2. Take the lowest power of each common prime factor: $\text{HCF}(96, 144) = 2^4 \times 3^1 = 16 \times 3 = 48$.

**Final Answer**: $48$
</details>

#### Question `1_4_Q3` [Easy] *(Lowest Common Multiple)*
Find the Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) of $18$ and $30$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Write each number in prime factor form: $18 = 2 \times 3^2$ and $30 = 2 \times 3 \times 5$.
2. Take the highest power of each prime factor: $\text{LCM}(18, 30) = 2^1 \times 3^2 \times 5^1 = 2 \times 9 \times 5 = 90$.

**Final Answer**: $90$
</details>

#### Question `1_4_Q4` [Medium] *(Three-Integer HCF & LCM)*
Find both the HCF and the LCM of $45$, $60$, and $150$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Prime factorise all three numbers: $45 = 3^2 \times 5$, $60 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 5$, and $150 = 2 \times 3 \times 5^2$.
2. Shared primes across all three numbers are $3$ and $5$, with lowest powers $3^1$ and $5^1$: $\text{HCF} = 3 \times 5 = 15$.
3. Highest powers of all primes ($2, 3, 5$) are $2^2, 3^2, 5^2$: $\text{LCM} = 4 \times 9 \times 25 = 900$.

**Final Answer**: $\text{HCF} = 15$, $\text{LCM} = 900$
</details>

#### Question `1_4_Q5` [Medium] *(HCF-LCM Product Identity)*
Two numbers have a Highest Common Factor of $14$ and a Lowest Common Multiple of $210$. If one of the numbers is $42$, find the other number.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Use the identity $a \times b = \text{HCF}(a, b) \times \text{LCM}(a, b)$.
2. Substitute $42 \times b = 14 \times 210 = 2940$.
3. Divide by $42$: $b = 2940 \div 42 = 70$.

**Final Answer**: $70$
</details>

#### Question `1_4_Q6` [Challenging] *(Prime Factorisation & Perfect Squares)*
Find the smallest positive integer $k$ by which $252$ must be multiplied so that $252k$ is a perfect square.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find the prime factorisation of $252$: $252 = 4 \times 63 = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 7^1$.
2. A positive integer is a perfect square if and only if every exponent in its prime factorisation is even.
3. The prime factors $2$ and $3$ already have even exponents ($2$), while $7$ has exponent $1$. Multiplying by $k = 7$ makes the factorisation $2^2 \times 3^2 \times 7^2 = (2 \times 3 \times 7)^2 = 42^2 = 1764$.

**Final Answer**: $7$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `1_4_Q7` [Medium] *(Australian Construction (Bunnings Timber))*
A builder in Geelong buys two lengths of Merbau decking timber from Bunnings measuring $240\text{ cm}$ and $336\text{ cm}$. She wants to cut both planks into shorter pieces of equal length with no timber wasted. (a) What is the greatest possible length of each piece? (b) How many pieces of this length will she obtain in total?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find the HCF of $240$ and $336$: $240 = 2^4 \times 3 \times 5$ and $336 = 2^4 \times 3 \times 7$.
2. The shared prime factors are $2^4 \times 3 = 16 \times 3 = 48\text{ cm}$.
3. Number of pieces from the $240\text{ cm}$ plank: $240 \div 48 = 5$.
4. Number of pieces from the $336\text{ cm}$ plank: $336 \div 48 = 7$.
5. Total number of pieces: $5 + 7 = 12\text{ pieces}$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $48\text{ cm}$, (b) $12\text{ pieces}$
</details>

#### Question `1_4_Q8` [Medium] *(Australian Community Sausage Sizzle)*
A school Fundraising Committee in Ballarat is preparing sausage sizzle packs. Sausages are sold in packs of $24$ and bread rolls are sold in packs of $16$. What is the minimum number of packs of sausages and packs of bread rolls the committee must buy so that there is an equal number of sausages and bread rolls with none left over?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find the Lowest Common Multiple of $24$ and $16$: $24 = 2^3 \times 3$ and $16 = 2^4$, so $\text{LCM}(24, 16) = 2^4 \times 3 = 48$.
2. Number of sausage packs needed: $48 \div 24 = 2\text{ packs}$.
3. Number of bread roll packs needed: $48 \div 16 = 3\text{ packs}$.

**Final Answer**: $2\text{ packs of sausages}$ and $3\text{ packs of bread rolls}$ ($48$ of each)
</details>

#### Question `1_4_Q9` [Challenging] *(Victorian Maritime Navigation)*
Two lighthouses on the Victorian coast near Cape Otway flash at regular intervals. Lighthouse A flashes every $18\text{ seconds}$ and Lighthouse B flashes every $28\text{ seconds}$. Both lighthouses flash together at exactly $9\text{:00 pm}$. How many additional times will they flash simultaneously between $9\text{:00 pm}$ and $10\text{:00 pm}$ (inclusive)?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find $\text{LCM}(18, 28)$: $18 = 2 \times 3^2$ and $28 = 2^2 \times 7$, so $\text{LCM}(18, 28) = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 7 = 252\text{ seconds}$.
2. Convert $1\text{ hour}$ ($9\text{:00 pm}$ to $10\text{:00 pm}$) into seconds: $60 \times 60 = 3600\text{ seconds}$.
3. Divide $3600$ by $252$ and take the integer floor: $\lfloor 3600 \div 252 \rfloor = \lfloor 14.2857\dots \rfloor = 14$ additional simultaneous flashes.

**Final Answer**: $14\text{ times}$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `1_4_Q10` [Challenging] *(Divisor Pairing & Square Numbers)*
Explain why any number whose prime factorisation has only even exponents (such as $2^4 \times 3^2$) must have an odd number of positive factors in total.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. A number $n$ whose prime factorisation has only even exponents is a perfect square, say $n = m^2$.
2. Every positive factor $d$ of $n$ with $d < m$ pairs uniquely with a distinct factor $n/d > m$.
3. The factor $d = m = \sqrt{n}$ pairs with itself ($m \times m = n$), adding $1$ unpaired factor to the paired factors, making the total number of factors odd.
4. For $2^4 \times 3^2 = 144$, the total number of factors is $(4 + 1)(2 + 1) = 5 \times 3 = 15$, which is odd.

**Final Answer**: All factors pair up as $(d, n/d)$ except the square root $\sqrt{n}$, which pairs with itself, yielding an odd total count
</details>

#### Question `1_4_Q11` [Medium] *(Coprime Integers Proof)*
Two positive integers $a$ and $b$ are called coprime (or relatively prime) if $\text{HCF}(a, b) = 1$. State a simplified formula for $\text{LCM}(a, b)$ when $a$ and $b$ are coprime, and explain using prime factorisation why this formula always holds.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Since $\text{HCF}(a, b) = 1$, $a$ and $b$ share no common prime factors.
2. Taking the highest power of every prime factor across $a$ and $b$ simply multiplies all prime factors of $a$ with all prime factors of $b$.
3. Equivalently, from $\text{HCF}(a, b) \times \text{LCM}(a, b) = a \times b$, substituting $\text{HCF}(a, b) = 1$ gives $\text{LCM}(a, b) = a \times b$.

**Final Answer**: $\text{LCM}(a, b) = a \times b$
</details>

#### Question `1_4_Q12` [Extension] *(Remainder & LCM Reasoning)*
Find the smallest positive integer $n > 2$ that leaves a remainder of $2$ when divided by $6$, $8$, and $9$. Justify each step of your reasoning.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. If $n$ leaves a remainder of $2$ when divided by $6$, $8$, and $9$, then $(n - 2)$ must be exactly divisible by $6$, $8$, and $9$.
2. Therefore, the smallest positive value of $(n - 2)$ is $\text{LCM}(6, 8, 9)$.
3. Using prime factorisation, $6 = 2 \times 3$, $8 = 2^3$, and $9 = 3^2$, so $\text{LCM}(6, 8, 9) = 2^3 \times 3^2 = 72$.
4. Since $n - 2 = 72$, we obtain $n = 72 + 2 = 74$.

**Final Answer**: $74$
</details>
