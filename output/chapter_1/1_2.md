### Key Vocabulary
- **Product**: The result of multiplying two or more numbers together.
- **Quotient**: The result of dividing one number (the dividend) by another non-zero number (the divisor).
- **Base and Exponent (Index)**: In the expression $a^n$, the base $a$ is the repeated factor and the exponent (or index) $n$ counts how many times $a$ is multiplied by itself.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Sign Rules for Multiplying Two Integers

Multiplication can be understood as repeated addition. For instance, $3 \times (-4) = (-4) + (-4) + (-4) = -12$. Taking the opposite of that product shows that $(-3) \times (-4) = +12$. In general, when multiplying two non-zero integers, if both integers have the same sign, their product is positive; if they have different signs, their product is negative.

$$(+a) \times (+b) = +ab, \quad (-a) \times (-b) = +ab, \quad (+a) \times (-b) = -ab, \quad (-a) \times (+b) = -ab$$

> **💡 Multiplication Sign Rule**
> When multiplying two numbers: SAME signs give a POSITIVE result; DIFFERENT signs give a NEGATIVE result. Multiplying any integer by $0$ always gives $0$.

### Sign Rules for Dividing Two Integers

Because division is the inverse operation of multiplication, the exact same sign rules apply to quotients. If the dividend and divisor have the same sign, the quotient is positive; if they have opposite signs, the quotient is negative. Division by zero ($a \div 0$) is always undefined.

$$\frac{-a}{-b} = +\frac{a}{b}, \quad \frac{-a}{+b} = \frac{+a}{-b} = -\frac{a}{b} \quad (b \neq 0)$$

> **💡 Fraction Sign Placement Rule**
> For any fraction, a single negative sign can be written in the numerator, in the denominator, or in front of the fraction bar: $\frac{-a}{b} = \frac{a}{-b} = -\frac{a}{b}$. Two negative signs in a fraction cancel to give a positive: $\frac{-a}{-b} = \frac{a}{b}$.

### Products of Three or More Integers

By the associative and commutative laws of multiplication, factors can be multiplied in any order. When multiplying a chain of non-zero integers, every pair of negative factors multiplies to produce a positive value.

$$(-1)^{2k} = +1 \quad \text{and} \quad (-1)^{2k+1} = -1$$

> **💡 Counting Negative Factors Rule**
> In a product or quotient of non-zero numbers: an EVEN number of negative factors produces a POSITIVE answer; an ODD number of negative factors produces a NEGATIVE answer.

### Powers of Negative Integers: $(-a)^n$ versus $-a^n$

Brackets determine whether the negative sign is part of the base being raised to the power. In $(-a)^n$, the base is $(-a)$, so the negative sign is repeated $n$ times. In $-a^n$, the exponent applies only to $a$, meaning the opposite of $a^n$, which is always negative for $a > 0$.

$$(-a)^2 = (-a) \times (-a) = +a^2 \quad \text{whereas} \quad -a^2 = -(a \times a) = -a^2$$

> **💡 Bracket Base Warning**
> Watch the brackets carefully! $(-5)^2 = (-5) \times (-5) = +25$, because the negative is inside the brackets. Without brackets, $-5^2 = -(5 \times 5) = -25$.

## Worked Examples

### WE1_2_1: Multiplying and Dividing Directed Numbers

**Problem**: Evaluate: (a) $-8 \times 7$, (b) $-9 \times (-12)$, and (c) $-84 \div (-7)$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), $-8$ and $7$ have different signs, so the product is negative. Multiply the magnitudes $8 \times 7 = 56$. | $$-8 \times 7 = -56$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), $-9$ and $-12$ have the same sign (both negative), so the product is positive. Multiply $9 \times 12 = 108$. | $$-9 \times (-12) = +108 = 108$$ |
| 3 | In part (c), $-84$ and $-7$ have the same sign, so the quotient is positive. Divide $84 \div 7 = 12$. | $$-84 \div (-7) = +12 = 12$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $-56$, (b) $108$, (c) $12$

> **✏️ Now You Try**

> **Answer**: (a) $-66$, (b) $104$, (c) $-12$

### WE1_2_2: Multi-Factor Products of Integers

**Problem**: Evaluate $-4 \times (-3) \times (-5) \times 2$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Count the number of negative factors: there are $3$ negative factors ($-4$, $-3$, and $-5$). Since $3$ is odd, the overall product must be negative. | $$\text{Sign of product} = (-)\times(-)\times(-)\times(+) = -$$ |
| 2 | Multiply the magnitudes in a convenient order using the associative law: $(4 \times 3) \times (5 \times 2) = 12 \times 10 = 120$, and attach the negative sign. | $$-4 \times (-3) \times (-5) \times 2 = -(4 \times 3 \times 5 \times 2) = -120$$ |

**Answer**: $-120$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Evaluate $-2 \times (-6) \times 5 \times (-3)$.
> *Hint*: Count the negative factors first, then pair $2 \times 5 = 10$ to make mental multiplication easier.
> **Working**: Three negative factors give a negative result: $-(2 \times 5 \times 6 \times 3) = -(10 \times 18) = -180$.
> **Answer**: $-180$

### WE1_2_3: Evaluating Powers of Negative Integers

**Problem**: Evaluate: (a) $(-6)^2$, (b) $-6^2$, and (c) $(-4)^3$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), the negative sign is inside the brackets, so the base is $-6$. Multiply $-6$ by itself. | $$(-6)^2 = (-6) \times (-6) = 36$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), there are no brackets around $-6$, so only the $6$ is squared and the negative sign remains in front. | $$-6^2 = -(6 \times 6) = -36$$ |
| 3 | In part (c), the base is $-4$ raised to an odd exponent ($3$). Multiply three factors of $-4$. | $$(-4)^3 = (-4) \times (-4) \times (-4) = 16 \times (-4) = -64$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $36$, (b) $-36$, (c) $-64$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Evaluate: (a) $(-9)^2$, (b) $-9^2$, and (c) $(-3)^3$.
> *Hint*: Check whether the negative sign is inside the brackets and whether the exponent is even or odd.
> **Working**: (a) $(-9)^2 = (-9) \times (-9) = 81$; (b) $-9^2 = -(9 \times 9) = -81$; (c) $(-3)^3 = (-3) \times (-3) \times (-3) = -27$.
> **Answer**: (a) $81$, (b) $-81$, (c) $-27$

### WE1_2_4: Submersible Descent Rate in the Bass Strait

**Problem**: A marine research ROV (remotely operated vehicle) in the Bass Strait starts at the ocean surface ($0\text{ m}$) and descends at a constant rate of $-14\text{ m}$ per minute for $8\text{ minutes}$. It then ascends at $+9\text{ m}$ per minute for $6\text{ minutes}$. Find the ROV's final depth relative to sea level.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Calculate the change in elevation during the $8$-minute descent by multiplying the rate ($-14\text{ m/min}$) by the time ($8\text{ min}$). | $$\text{Descent change} = 8 \times (-14) = -112\text{ m}$$ |
| 2 | Calculate the change in elevation during the $6$-minute ascent by multiplying the rate ($+9\text{ m/min}$) by the time ($6\text{ min}$). | $$\text{Ascent change} = 6 \times 9 = +54\text{ m}$$ |
| 3 | Add the two elevation changes to find the final position relative to sea level. | $$\text{Final position} = -112 + 54 = -58\text{ m}$$ |

**Answer**: $-58\text{ m}$ ($58\text{ m}$ below sea level)

> **✏️ Now You Try**

> *Hint*: Compute $5 \times (-18) + 4 \times 12$.
> **Working**: $5 \times (-18) + 4 \times 12 = -90 + 48 = -42\text{ m}$.

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Treating $-a^2$ as Equivalent to $(-a)^2$
- **The Misconception**: Students frequently write $-4^2 = 16$, assuming the minus sign is squared along with the $4$.
- **The Correct Concept**: By the order of operations, indices bind more tightly than negation (which is multiplication by $-1$). Thus $-4^2 = -1 \times 4^2 = -16$, whereas $(-4)^2 = (-4) \times (-4) = +16$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$-4^2 = (-4) \times (-4) = 16 \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$-4^2 = -(4 \times 4) = -16 \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: Consider $20 - 4^2$. We know $4^2 = 16$, so $20 - 4^2 = 20 - 16 = 4$. If $-4^2$ were $+16$, then $20 - 4^2$ would absurdly equal $36$.

### Multiplying Base and Exponent Instead of Repeated Multiplication
- **The Misconception**: Calculating $(-2)^3$ as $(-2) \times 3 = -6$ instead of multiplying three factors of $(-2)$.
- **The Correct Concept**: An exponent of $3$ means multiplying the base by itself three times: $(-2)^3 = (-2) \times (-2) \times (-2) = -8$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$(-2)^3 = -2 \times 3 = -6 \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$(-2)^3 = (-2) \times (-2) \times (-2) = 4 \times (-2) = -8 \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: A cube of side length $2\text{ cm}$ has volume $2^3 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8\text{ cm}^3$, not $6\text{ cm}^3$.

### Assuming Three Negative Factors Multiply to a Positive
- **The Misconception**: Thinking that because all factors in $(-2) \times (-3) \times (-4)$ have the same negative sign, the answer must be positive ($+24$).
- **The Correct Concept**: The 'same signs give a positive' rule applies strictly to a pair of factors at a time. The first two negatives multiply to give a positive: $(-2) \times (-3) = +6$. Multiplying $+6$ by the third negative factor $(-4)$ gives $-24$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$(-2) \times (-3) \times (-4) = +24 \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$((-2) \times (-3)) \times (-4) = (+6) \times (-4) = -24 \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: Any odd number of negative factors leaves one negative factor unpaired after pairing off negatives into positives.

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `1_2_Q7` [Medium] *(Australian Financial Context (ASX))*
An investor in Sydney holds $450$ shares in an Australian mining company listed on the ASX. Over five consecutive trading days, the share price changes by $-12\text{ cents}$ per share each day. Express the total change in the value of the investor's holding in dollars.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate the change per share over the $5$ days: $5 \times (-12) = -60\text{ cents}$ per share.
2. Multiply by the $450$ shares: $450 \times (-60) = -27000\text{ cents}$.
3. Convert cents to Australian dollars by dividing by $100$: $-27000 \div 100 = -\$270$ (a decrease of $\$270$).

**Final Answer**: $-\$270$
</details>

#### Question `1_2_Q8` [Medium] *(Competition Scoring)*
In a Year 8 Australian Mathematics Competition practice quiz with $25$ questions, a student receives $+4$ marks for each correct answer, $-2$ marks for each incorrect answer, and $-1$ mark for each unanswered question. Priya answers $17$ questions correctly, gets $5$ questions wrong, and leaves the remaining questions unanswered. Calculate Priya's total score.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find the number of unanswered questions: $25 - 17 - 5 = 3$.
2. Calculate marks from correct answers: $17 \times 4 = 68$.
3. Calculate marks from incorrect answers: $5 \times (-2) = -10$.
4. Calculate marks from unanswered questions: $3 \times (-1) = -3$.
5. Sum the parts: $68 + (-10) + (-3) = 68 - 13 = 55$.

**Final Answer**: $55$
</details>

#### Question `1_2_Q9` [Challenging] *(Australian Weather Modelling)*
Over a $6$-hour overnight period in Canberra during July, the temperature fell steadily from $+4^\circ\text{C}$ at $11\text{:00 pm}$ to $-8^\circ\text{C}$ at $5\text{:00 am}$. (a) Find the average rate of temperature change in degrees Celsius per hour. (b) Assuming this constant rate began at $11\text{:00 pm}$, what was the temperature at $3\text{:00 am}$?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find the total temperature change over the $6$ hours: $-8 - 4 = -12^\circ\text{C}$.
2. Divide the total change by $6$ hours to find the hourly rate: $-12 \div 6 = -2^\circ\text{C}$ per hour.
3. From $11\text{:00 pm}$ to $3\text{:00 am}$ is $4$ hours, so the change is $4 \times (-2) = -8^\circ\text{C}$.
4. Add this change to the initial temperature: $4 + (-8) = -4^\circ\text{C}$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $-2^\circ\text{C/h}$, (b) $-4^\circ\text{C}$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `1_2_Q10` [Medium] *(Pattern & Index Reasoning)*
Explain why $(-1)^{99} + (-1)^{100} = 0$ without performing $99$ or $100$ multiplications.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. When $-1$ is raised to an odd positive integer exponent ($99$), pairs of $(-1) \times (-1) = +1$ leave one factor of $-1$, so $(-1)^{99} = -1$.
2. When $-1$ is raised to an even positive integer exponent ($100$), all factors pair up into positives, so $(-1)^{100} = +1$.
3. Adding the two results gives $-1 + 1 = 0$.

**Final Answer**: $(-1)^{99} = -1$ and $(-1)^{100} = +1$, so $-1 + 1 = 0$
</details>

#### Question `1_2_Q11` [Challenging] *(Sign Analysis & Proof)*
Three non-zero integers $p$, $q$, and $r$ satisfy $p \times q \times r > 0$ and $p + q + r < 0$. Explain how many of the three integers must be negative, and provide a concrete example of values for $p$, $q$, and $r$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Since the product $p \times q \times r > 0$ is positive, the number of negative integers among $p, q, r$ must be even: either $0$ negatives or $2$ negatives.
2. If there were $0$ negatives, all three integers would be positive, which would make $p + q + r > 0$, contradicting $p + q + r < 0$.
3. Therefore, exactly $2$ of the integers must be negative and $1$ must be positive, with the sum of the two negative integers having a larger magnitude than the positive integer.
4. Example: Let $p = -5$, $q = -4$, and $r = 2$. Then $p \times q \times r = (-5)(-4)(2) = 40 > 0$ and $p + q + r = -5 - 4 + 2 = -7 < 0$.

**Final Answer**: Exactly two of the integers must be negative (e.g., $p = -5, q = -4, r = 2$)
</details>

#### Question `1_2_Q12` [Extension] *(Number Theory & Factor Pairs)*
Find all pairs of integers $(a, b)$ with $a \le b$ such that $a \times b = -18$ and $a + b = -3$. Show how you systematically test or deduce the solution.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Since $a \times b = -18 < 0$, one integer is negative and the other is positive. Because $a \le b$, $a$ is negative and $b$ is positive.
2. Since $a + b = -3 < 0$, the negative integer $a$ has a larger magnitude than $b$.
3. List the factor pairs $(a, b)$ of $-18$ where $|a| > |b|$: $(-18, 1)$, $(-9, 2)$, and $(-6, 3)$.
4. Check the sums: $-18 + 1 = -17$, $-9 + 2 = -7$, and $-6 + 3 = -3$. Thus $(a, b) = (-6, 3)$ is the unique solution.

**Final Answer**: $(a, b) = (-6, 3)$
</details>
