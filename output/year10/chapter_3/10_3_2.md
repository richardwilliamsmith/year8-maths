### Key Vocabulary
- **General quadratic equation**: A second-degree polynomial equation written in standard form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, where $a \neq 0$.
- **Discriminant ($\Delta$)**: The quantity $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$ under the square root sign in the Quadratic Formula, which determines the number and nature of the real solutions.
- **Quadratic Formula**: The universal formula $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$ giving the exact solutions of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ when $\Delta \ge 0$.
- **Repeated (double) root**: The single real solution $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$ that occurs when $\Delta = 0$, corresponding geometrically to a parabola whose vertex touches the $x$-axis tangentially.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### The Discriminant and the Nature of Roots

When we complete the square on the general quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ ($a \neq 0$), we obtain $\left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = \frac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a^2}$. Whether we can take the real square root of the right-hand side depends entirely on the sign of the numerator $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$, called the discriminant: if $\Delta > 0$, there are two distinct real solutions (rational if $\Delta$ is a perfect square, irrational surds otherwise); if $\Delta = 0$, there is exactly one repeated real solution; and if $\Delta < 0$, there are no real solutions.

$$\Delta = b^2 - 4ac: \quad \begin{cases} \Delta > 0 & \text{2 distinct real roots} \\ \Delta = 0 & \text{1 repeated real root} \\ \Delta < 0 & \text{0 real roots} \end{cases}$$

> **💡 Calculate $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$ First!**
> Evaluating $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$ before writing the full Quadratic Formula immediately tells you if real solutions exist and prevents sign errors inside the square root.

### Solving Equations with the Quadratic Formula

Before reading off the coefficients $a$, $b$, and $c$, always rearrange the quadratic equation into standard form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ with zero on one side. Then substitute $a$, $b$, and $c$ (including their signs!) into the Quadratic Formula and simplify any surds in the numerator before dividing both terms by $2a$.

$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}$$

> **💡 Put Negative $b$ in Brackets When Squaring**
> If $b = -5$, then $-b = -(-5) = +5$, and $b^2 = (-5)^2 = +25$ (ALWAYS positive!). Never type $-5^2$ into a calculator without brackets, as the calculator evaluates $-5^2 = -25$.

### Quadratic Modelling in Geometry and Kinematics

Many real-world problems involving rectangular areas, Pythagoras' theorem, or projectile motion ($h(t) = -5t^2 + vt + h_0$) lead to quadratic equations. After solving the quadratic equation algebraically, always check both mathematical roots against physical constraints: reject negative lengths, widths, or negative times ($t < 0$) when they lie outside the practical domain of the model.

$$\text{Area / Pythagoras / Projectile} \implies ax^2 + bx + c = 0 \implies \text{select physically valid root } (x > 0)$$

> **💡 Check Domain Restrictions in Applied Problems**
> An algebraic equation doesn't know that side lengths and time elapsed must be positive—always state why you reject an extraneous negative root in your conclusion.

## Worked Examples

### WE10_3_2_1: Using the Discriminant to Determine the Number of Real Roots

**Problem**: Calculate the discriminant $\Delta$ and state the number and nature of the real solutions for: (a) $2x^2 - 5x + 4 = 0$ and (b) $9x^2 - 12x + 4 = 0$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), identify $a = 2, b = -5, c = 4$ and evaluate $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$. | $$\Delta = (-5)^2 - 4(2)(4) = 25 - 32 = -7 < 0 \implies \text{no real solutions}$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), identify $a = 9, b = -12, c = 4$ and evaluate $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$. | $$\Delta = (-12)^2 - 4(9)(4) = 144 - 144 = 0 \implies \text{1 repeated rational solution}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $\Delta = -7$ ($0$ real solutions); (b) $\Delta = 0$ ($1$ repeated rational solution)

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Calculate the discriminant $\Delta$ and state the number of real solutions for $3x^2 - 7x + 2 = 0$.
> *Hint*: Substitute $a = 3, b = -7, c = 2$ into $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$.
> **Working**: $\Delta = (-7)^2 - 4(3)(2) = 49 - 24 = 25 > 0$ (a perfect square), so there are $2$ distinct rational solutions.
> **Answer**: $\Delta = 25$; $2$ distinct rational solutions

### WE10_3_2_2: Finding Exact Surd Solutions Using the Quadratic Formula

**Problem**: Solve $x^2 - 6x + 2 = 0$, giving your answers in simplest exact surd form.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Identify $a = 1, b = -6, c = 2$ and calculate the discriminant $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$. | $$\Delta = (-6)^2 - 4(1)(2) = 36 - 8 = 28$$ |
| 2 | Substitute into the Quadratic Formula and simplify $\sqrt{28} = \sqrt{4 \times 7} = 2\sqrt{7}$. | $$x = \frac{-(-6) \pm \sqrt{28}}{2(1)} = \frac{6 \pm 2\sqrt{7}}{2}$$ |
| 3 | Factor $2$ out of the numerator and cancel with the denominator. | $$x = \frac{2(3 \pm \sqrt{7})}{2} = 3 \pm \sqrt{7}$$ |

**Answer**: $x = 3 - \sqrt{7}$ or $x = 3 + \sqrt{7}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Solve $x^2 + 4x - 1 = 0$, giving your answers in simplest exact surd form.
> *Hint*: $\Delta = 4^2 - 4(1)(-1) = 20$ and $\sqrt{20} = 2\sqrt{5}$.
> **Working**: $x = \frac{-4 \pm 2\sqrt{5}}{2} = -2 \pm \sqrt{5}$.
> **Answer**: $x = -2 \pm \sqrt{5}$

### WE10_3_2_3: Modelling Projectile Motion with a Quadratic Equation

**Problem**: A ball is thrown upwards from the top of a $15\text{ m}$ platform. Its height $h$ metres above the ground after $t$ seconds is modelled by $h = -5t^2 + 10t + 15$. Find the time $t$ when the ball hits the ground.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | The ball hits the ground when $h = 0$. Set $-5t^2 + 10t + 15 = 0$ and divide both sides by $-5$ to simplify. | $$-5t^2 + 10t + 15 = 0 \xrightarrow{\div (-5)} t^2 - 2t - 3 = 0$$ |
| 2 | Factorise or apply the Quadratic Formula to solve for $t$. | $$(t - 3)(t + 1) = 0 \implies t = 3 \text{ or } t = -1$$ |
| 3 | Since time after launch must be non-negative ($t \ge 0$), reject $t = -1$. | $$t = 3\text{ seconds (since } t \ge 0\text{)}$$ |

**Answer**: $3\text{ seconds}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: A diver jumps from a $10\text{ m}$ platform so that her height above the water after $t$ seconds is $h = -5t^2 + 5t + 10$. When does she enter the water ($h = 0$)?
> *Hint*: Set $-5t^2 + 5t + 10 = 0$, divide by $-5$ to get $t^2 - t - 2 = 0$, and select $t > 0$.
> **Working**: $(t - 2)(t + 1) = 0 \implies t = 2\text{ s}$ (rejecting $t = -1$).
> **Answer**: $2\text{ seconds}$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Evaluating $b^2$ as Negative When $b < 0$
- **The Misconception**: Calculating $\Delta$ for $x^2 - 4x - 5 = 0$ as $-4^2 - 4(1)(-5) = -16 + 20 = 4$ instead of $(-4)^2 - 4(1)(-5) = 16 + 20 = 36$.
- **The Correct Concept**: The square of any non-zero real number is strictly positive: $(-4)^2 = (-4) \times (-4) = +16$. Always enclose negative values of $b$ in brackets when squaring.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\Delta = -4^2 - 4(1)(-5) = -16 + 20 = 4 \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\Delta = (-4)^2 - 4(1)(-5) = 16 + 20 = 36 \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: The equation $x^2 - 4x - 5 = (x - 5)(x + 1) = 0$ has solutions $x = 5$ and $x = -1$, which require $\sqrt{\Delta} = 6$, so $\Delta = 36$, not $4$.

### Dividing Only the First Term of the Numerator by $2a$
- **The Misconception**: Simplifying $\frac{6 \pm 2\sqrt{5}}{2}$ as $3 \pm 2\sqrt{5}$, dividing $6$ by $2$ while leaving $2\sqrt{5}$ untouched.
- **The Correct Concept**: The denominator $2a$ divides the ENTIRE numerator, so you must divide both $-b$ and $\sqrt{\Delta}$ by $2a$: $\frac{6 \pm 2\sqrt{5}}{2} = \frac{6}{2} \pm \frac{2\sqrt{5}}{2} = 3 \pm \sqrt{5}$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\frac{6 \pm 2\sqrt{5}}{2} = 3 \pm 2\sqrt{5} \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\frac{6 \pm 2\sqrt{5}}{2} = \frac{2(3 \pm \sqrt{5})}{2} = 3 \pm \sqrt{5} \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: With numbers, $\frac{6 + 4}{2} = \frac{10}{2} = 5 = 3 + 2$, whereas $3 + 4 = 7 \neq 5$.

### Fluency & Understanding

#### Question `10_3_2_Q1` [Easy] *(Rational Roots via Quadratic Formula)*
Find the discriminant $\Delta$ of $2x^2 + 7x - 4 = 0$ and hence solve the equation using the Quadratic Formula.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate $\Delta = 7^2 - 4(2)(-4) = 49 + 32 = 81$.
2. Substitute into the Quadratic Formula: $x = \frac{-7 \pm \sqrt{81}}{2(2)} = \frac{-7 \pm 9}{4}$, giving $x = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$ or $x = \frac{-16}{4} = -4$.

**Final Answer**: $\Delta = 81$; $x = \frac{1}{2}$ or $x = -4$
</details>

#### Question `10_3_2_Q2` [Medium] *(Simplifying Surd Roots)*
Solve $x^2 - 8x + 11 = 0$, expressing your answers in simplest exact surd form.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate $\Delta = (-8)^2 - 4(1)(11) = 64 - 44 = 20$.
2. $x = \frac{8 \pm \sqrt{20}}{2} = \frac{8 \pm 2\sqrt{5}}{2} = 4 \pm \sqrt{5}$.

**Final Answer**: $x = 4 \pm \sqrt{5}$
</details>

#### Question `10_3_2_Q3` [Medium] *(Rearranging Before Applying the Formula)*
Rearrange $3x^2 = 4x + 2$ into standard form and solve for $x$ in exact surd form.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Rearrange to $3x^2 - 4x - 2 = 0$, so $a = 3, b = -4, c = -2$.
2. Calculate $\Delta = (-4)^2 - 4(3)(-2) = 16 + 24 = 40$, so $\sqrt{\Delta} = 2\sqrt{10}$.
3. $x = \frac{4 \pm 2\sqrt{10}}{6} = \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{10}}{3}$.

**Final Answer**: $x = \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{10}}{3}$
</details>

#### Question `10_3_2_Q4` [Medium] *(Finding Parameter for Repeated Root)*
Find the positive value of $k$ for which $x^2 + kx + 16 = 0$ has exactly one repeated real solution.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. For one repeated real solution, set $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac = 0$: $k^2 - 4(1)(16) = 0$.
2. $k^2 = 64 \implies k = 8$ (taking $k > 0$).

**Final Answer**: $k = 8$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `10_3_2_Q5` [Medium] *(Rectangular Area Modelling)*
The length of a rectangular solar panel is $3\text{ m}$ longer than its width $w\text{ m}$, and its area is $28\text{ m}^2$. Write and solve a quadratic equation to find its width and length.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Area equation: $w(w + 3) = 28 \implies w^2 + 3w - 28 = 0$.
2. Factorise or use the Quadratic Formula: $(w + 7)(w - 4) = 0 \implies w = 4$ (rejecting $w = -7$ since $w > 0$).
3. Width is $4\text{ m}$ and length is $4 + 3 = 7\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: Width $4\text{ m}$, length $7\text{ m}$
</details>

#### Question `10_3_2_Q6` [Medium] *(Pythagoras Quadratic Problem)*
A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse $13\text{ cm}$, and one of its shorter sides is $7\text{ cm}$ longer than the other shorter side $x\text{ cm}$. Use Pythagoras' theorem to form a quadratic equation and find $x$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. By Pythagoras' theorem, $x^2 + (x + 7)^2 = 13^2 \implies x^2 + x^2 + 14x + 49 = 169$.
2. Simplify and divide by $2$: $2x^2 + 14x - 120 = 0 \implies x^2 + 7x - 60 = 0$.
3. $(x + 12)(x - 5) = 0 \implies x = 5\text{ cm}$ (since $x > 0$).

**Final Answer**: $x = 5\text{ cm}$ (sides are $5\text{ cm}, 12\text{ cm}, 13\text{ cm}$)
</details>

#### Question `10_3_2_Q7` [Challenging] *(Uniform Border Area Problem)*
A rectangular photograph measuring $12\text{ cm}$ by $8\text{ cm}$ is surrounded by a border of uniform width $x\text{ cm}$. If the combined area of the photograph and border is $140\text{ cm}^2$, find the width $x$ of the border.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The outer dimensions are $(12 + 2x)$ and $(8 + 2x)$, so $(12 + 2x)(8 + 2x) = 140$.
2. Expand and rearrange: $96 + 40x + 4x^2 = 140 \implies 4x^2 + 40x - 44 = 0 \implies x^2 + 10x - 11 = 0$.
3. $(x + 11)(x - 1) = 0 \implies x = 1\text{ cm}$ (rejecting $x = -11$).

**Final Answer**: $x = 1\text{ cm}$
</details>

#### Question `10_3_2_Q8` [Medium] *(Number Modelling)*
Two positive numbers differ by $2$ and the sum of their squares is $34$. Find the two numbers.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Let the numbers be $x$ and $x + 2$ ($x > 0$). Then $x^2 + (x + 2)^2 = 34 \implies 2x^2 + 4x - 30 = 0 \implies x^2 + 2x - 15 = 0$.
2. $(x + 5)(x - 3) = 0 \implies x = 3$ (since $x > 0$), so the numbers are $3$ and $5$.

**Final Answer**: $3$ and $5$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `10_3_2_Q9` [Challenging] *(Guaranteed Real Roots When ac < 0)*
Prove that for any non-zero real numbers $a$ and $c$ with opposite signs ($ac < 0$), the quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ ALWAYS has two distinct real solutions regardless of the value of $b$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Consider the discriminant $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$.
2. For any real number $b$, $b^2 \ge 0$. Since $a$ and $c$ have opposite signs, $ac < 0$, which means $-4ac > 0$.
3. Therefore $\Delta = b^2 + (-4ac) > 0$, guaranteeing two distinct real solutions.

**Final Answer**: Since $b^2 \ge 0$ and $ac < 0 \implies -4ac > 0$, the discriminant $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac > 0$ is strictly positive for all real $b$
</details>

#### Question `10_3_2_Q10` [Challenging] *(Vieta's Sum of Roots Proof)*
Using the Quadratic Formula $x_1 = \frac{-b + \sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}$ and $x_2 = \frac{-b - \sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}$, prove Vieta's sum-of-roots formula $x_1 + x_2 = -\frac{b}{a}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Add the two roots: $x_1 + x_2 = \frac{-b + \sqrt{\Delta}}{2a} + \frac{-b - \sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}$.
2. The $+\sqrt{\Delta}$ and $-\sqrt{\Delta}$ terms cancel out, leaving $\frac{-2b}{2a} = -\frac{b}{a}$.

**Final Answer**: $x_1 + x_2 = \frac{(-b + \sqrt{\Delta}) + (-b - \sqrt{\Delta})}{2a} = \frac{-2b}{2a} = -\frac{b}{a}$
</details>

#### Question `10_3_2_Q11` [Extension] *(Vieta's Product of Roots Proof)*
Using the Quadratic Formula, prove Vieta's product-of-roots formula $x_1 x_2 = \frac{c}{a}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Multiply the two roots using DOTS in the numerator: $x_1 x_2 = \left(\frac{-b + \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\right)\left(\frac{-b - \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\right) = \frac{(-b)^2 - (b^2 - 4ac)}{4a^2}$.
2. Simplify the numerator: $\frac{b^2 - b^2 + 4ac}{4a^2} = \frac{4ac}{4a^2} = \frac{c}{a}$.

**Final Answer**: $x_1 x_2 = \frac{(-b)^2 - (\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac})^2}{4a^2} = \frac{b^2 - (b^2 - 4ac)}{4a^2} = \frac{4ac}{4a^2} = \frac{c}{a}$
</details>

#### Question `10_3_2_Q12` [Extension] *(Tangency of Line and Parabola via Discriminant)*
Find the value of $m$ for which the straight line $y = 2x + m$ is tangent to the parabola $y = x^2 + 6x + 7$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Equate the two equations to find intersections: $x^2 + 6x + 7 = 2x + m \implies x^2 + 4x + (7 - m) = 0$.
2. A tangent touches the parabola at a single repeated point, so set $\Delta = 0$: $4^2 - 4(1)(7 - m) = 0$.
3. $16 - 28 + 4m = 0 \implies 4m = 12 \implies m = 3$.

**Final Answer**: $m = 3$
</details>
