### Key Vocabulary
- **Unit circle**: The circle of radius $1$ centred at the origin $(0, 0)$ with equation $x^2 + y^2 = 1$; for any angle $\theta$ measured anticlockwise from the positive $x$-axis, the point $P(\theta)$ on the circle has coordinates $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$.
- **Reference angle (related acute angle $\alpha$)**: The acute angle ($0^\circ < \alpha < 90^\circ$) formed between the terminal ray of $\theta$ and the horizontal $x$-axis: $\alpha = 180^\circ - \theta$ (Q2), $\alpha = \theta - 180^\circ$ (Q3), or $\alpha = 360^\circ - \theta$ (Q4).
- **CAST quadrant diagram**: A memory aid showing which trigonometric ratios are positive in each quadrant: All in Quadrant 1 ($0^\circ$–$90^\circ$), Sine in Quadrant 2 ($90^\circ$–$180^\circ$), Tangent in Quadrant 3 ($180^\circ$–$270^\circ$), and Cosine in Quadrant 4 ($270^\circ$–$360^\circ$).
- **Amplitude and period**: For $y = a\sin(bx) + c$ or $y = a\cos(bx) + c$, the amplitude $|a|$ is the vertical half-height from the centre line $y = c$ to a peak or trough, and the period $T = \frac{360^\circ}{b}$ (or $\frac{2\pi}{b}$ in radians) is the horizontal length of one complete wave cycle.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Exact Values for Special Angles ($30^\circ, 45^\circ, 60^\circ$) and Boundary Angles

Two special triangles generate all standard exact trigonometric ratios: an isosceles right-angled triangle with legs $1, 1$ and hypotenuse $\sqrt{2}$ gives the ratios for $45^\circ$, and an equilateral triangle of side $2$ bisected into two right-angled triangles with legs $1, \sqrt{3}$ and hypotenuse $2$ gives the ratios for $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$. On the unit circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$, the four axis intercepts $(1,0), (0,1), (-1,0), (0,-1)$ immediately give the exact values at the boundary angles $0^\circ, 90^\circ, 180^\circ, 270^\circ, 360^\circ$.

$$\begin{array}{c|ccc} \theta & 30^\circ & 45^\circ & 60^\circ \\ \hline \sin\theta & \frac{1}{2} & \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\ \cos\theta & \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & \frac{1}{2} \\ \tan\theta & \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} & 1 & \sqrt{3} \end{array}$$

> **💡 The $\frac{\sqrt{0}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{1}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{4}}{2}$ Pattern**
> For $\theta = 0^\circ, 30^\circ, 45^\circ, 60^\circ, 90^\circ$, $\sin\theta$ simply counts up $\frac{\sqrt{0}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{1}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{4}}{2}$ while $\cos\theta$ counts down in reverse!

### The Unit Circle and Four-Quadrant Symmetry (CAST)

For any angle $\theta$, let $P(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ be the point where the terminal ray meets the unit circle, with $\tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$ equal to the gradient of the ray $OP$. By Pythagoras' theorem on the unit circle, $\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$ for every angle $\theta$. To evaluate $\sin\theta, \cos\theta$, or $\tan\theta$ in Quadrants 2, 3, or 4: (1) identify the quadrant and determine the sign ($+$ or $-$) using CAST (All, Sine, Tangent, Cosine), and (2) find the acute reference angle $\alpha$ measured to the horizontal $x$-axis ($180^\circ \pm \alpha$ or $360^\circ - \alpha$).

$$\text{Q2: } 180^\circ - \alpha \ (+\sin), \quad \text{Q3: } 180^\circ + \alpha \ (+\tan), \quad \text{Q4: } 360^\circ - \alpha \ (+\cos)$$

> **💡 Always Measure the Reference Angle to the Horizontal $x$-Axis**
> Never use $90^\circ \pm \alpha$ or $270^\circ \pm \alpha$ for reference angles — always measure $\alpha$ to the horizontal $180^\circ$ or $360^\circ$ axis so the trigonometric function name stays the same.

### Graphs of Sine and Cosine Functions ($y = a\sin(bx) + c$ and $y = a\cos(bx) + c$)

As $\theta$ rotates around the unit circle, plotting $y = \sin x$ and $y = \cos x$ against $x$ produces smooth, periodic wave curves oscillating between $-1$ and $1$ with a fundamental period of $360^\circ$ (or $2\pi$ radians). For the transformed functions $y = a\sin(bx) + c$ and $y = a\cos(bx) + c$: the vertical mean (equilibrium) line is $y = c$, the amplitude is $|a|$ (so the range is $[c - |a|,\, c + |a|]$), and the period is $T = \frac{360^\circ}{b}$. Dividing one period $T$ into $4$ equal quarter-steps of length $\frac{T}{4}$ locates all peaks, troughs, and centre-line intercepts.

$$\text{Amplitude} = |a|, \qquad \text{Period } T = \frac{360^\circ}{b}, \qquad \text{Range} = [c - |a|,\, c + |a|]$$

> **💡 Sine Starts at the Centre Line; Cosine Starts at the Peak**
> At $x = 0^\circ$, $\sin 0^\circ = 0$ (starts on the centre line heading up for $a > 0$), whereas $\cos 0^\circ = 1$ (starts at the maximum peak $c + a$ for $a > 0$).

### WE10_6_3_1: Evaluating Exact Trigonometric Ratios Across All Four Quadrants

**Problem**: Without using a calculator, find the exact value of: (a) $\cos 150^\circ$, (b) $\tan 225^\circ$, and (c) $\sin 300^\circ$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In (a), $150^\circ$ lies in Quadrant 2 ($90^\circ < 150^\circ < 180^\circ$), where cosine is negative. The acute reference angle to $180^\circ$ is $\alpha = 180^\circ - 150^\circ = 30^\circ$. | $$\cos 150^\circ = -\cos(180^\circ - 150^\circ) = -\cos 30^\circ = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$ |
| 2 | In (b), $225^\circ$ lies in Quadrant 3 ($180^\circ < 225^\circ < 270^\circ$), where tangent is positive. The acute reference angle to $180^\circ$ is $\alpha = 225^\circ - 180^\circ = 45^\circ$. | $$\tan 225^\circ = +\tan(225^\circ - 180^\circ) = \tan 45^\circ = 1$$ |
| 3 | In (c), $300^\circ$ lies in Quadrant 4 ($270^\circ < 300^\circ < 360^\circ$), where sine is negative. The acute reference angle to $360^\circ$ is $\alpha = 360^\circ - 300^\circ = 60^\circ$. | $$\sin 300^\circ = -\sin(360^\circ - 300^\circ) = -\sin 60^\circ = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, (b) $1$, (c) $-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$

### WE10_6_3_3: Amplitude, Period, Range, and Tidal Modelling with Cosine Graphs

**Problem**: The water depth $d(t)$ (in metres) at a pier on the Bass Coast $t$ hours after high tide ($t = 0$) is modelled by $d(t) = 3\cos(30t^\circ) + 5$ for $0 \le t \le 24$. Find: (a) the amplitude, period (in hours), and range (minimum and maximum depths), (b) the water depth at $t = 4$ hours, and (c) the first time $t > 0$ when low tide occurs.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Compare $d(t) = 3\cos(30t^\circ) + 5$ with $y = a\cos(bt^\circ) + c$, where $a = 3, b = 30$, and $c = 5$. | $$\text{Amplitude} = \|3\| = 3\text{ m}, \qquad \text{Period } T = \frac{360}{30} = 12\text{ hours}$$

**Answer**: (a) Amplitude $= 3\text{ m}$, period $= 12\text{ hours}$, range $= [2\text{ m}, 8\text{ m}]$; (b) $3.5\text{ m}$; (c) $t = 6\text{ hours}$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Measuring Reference Angles to the Vertical $y$-Axis ($90^\circ$ or $270^\circ$)
- **The Misconception**: Writing $150^\circ = 90^\circ + 60^\circ$ and concluding $\sin 150^\circ = \sin 60^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.
- **The Correct Concept**: Reference angles MUST be measured to the horizontal $x$-axis ($180^\circ$ or $360^\circ$). Since $150^\circ = 180^\circ - 30^\circ$, the reference angle is $30^\circ$, so $\sin 150^\circ = \sin 30^\circ = \frac{1}{2}$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\sin 150^\circ = \sin(90^\circ + 60^\circ) = \sin 60^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \quad \text{(Incorrect — used } 90^\circ \text{ axis!)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\sin 150^\circ = \sin(180^\circ - 30^\circ) = \sin 30^\circ = \frac{1}{2}$$
- **Counter-Example**: At $150^\circ$, the ray is only $30^\circ$ above the negative $x$-axis, so its $y$-coordinate ($\sin 150^\circ$) is $\frac{1}{2}$, not $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 0.866$.

### Reporting a Negative Amplitude or Confusing Amplitude with Full Peak-to-Trough Height
- **The Misconception**: Saying that $y = -3\sin(x)$ has amplitude $-3$, or that a wave oscillating between $-3$ and $3$ has amplitude $6$.
- **The Correct Concept**: Amplitude is a distance (half the peak-to-trough vertical span): $\text{Amplitude} = |a| = \frac{y_{\max} - y_{\min}}{2} > 0$. A negative sign in $a = -3$ reflects the wave vertically across the centre line, but the amplitude is still $|-3| = 3$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$y = -3\sin(x) \implies \text{Amplitude} = -3 \text{ or } 6 \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$y = -3\sin(x) \implies \text{Amplitude} = |-3| = 3$$
- **Counter-Example**: A water wave with crests $3\text{ m}$ above mean sea level and troughs $3\text{ m}$ below mean sea level has a $3\text{ m}$ amplitude and a $6\text{ m}$ total tidal range.

#### Question `10_6_3_Q8` [Challenging] *(Electrical Engineering)*

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `10_6_3_Q9` [Medium] *(Unit Circle Identities)*
Use the unit circle definition $P(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ to prove that for any angle $\theta$: (a) $\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$, and (b) $\cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta$ while $\sin(-\theta) = -\sin\theta$ (even and odd symmetry).

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. By Pythagoras' theorem, every point $(x, y) = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ on the unit circle of radius $1$ satisfies $x^2 + y^2 = 1 \implies \cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$.
2. Rotating clockwise by $\theta$ (angle $-\theta$) reflects the point $P(x, y)$ across the horizontal $x$-axis to $(x, -y)$, preserving the $x$-coordinate ($\cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta$) and negating the $y$-coordinate ($\sin(-\theta) = -\sin\theta$).

**Final Answer**: (a) $(x, y) = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ lies on $x^2 + y^2 = 1$; (b) reflecting across the $x$-axis maps $(x, y) \mapsto (x, -y)$, so $\cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta$ and $\sin(-\theta) = -\sin\theta$
</details>

#### Question `10_6_3_Q10` [Medium] *(Asymptotes of Tangent)*
Explain using the unit circle why $\tan 90^\circ$ and $\tan 270^\circ$ are undefined, whereas $\tan 0^\circ = \tan 180^\circ = \tan 360^\circ = 0$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. By definition, $\tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} = \frac{y}{x}$. At $90^\circ$ and $270^\circ$, the point on the unit circle is $(0, \pm 1)$, so $x = \cos\theta = 0$ and division by zero is undefined.
2. At $0^\circ, 180^\circ, 360^\circ$, the point is $(\pm 1, 0)$, so $y = \sin\theta = 0$ and $\tan\theta = \frac{0}{\pm 1} = 0$.

**Final Answer**: Since $\tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$, at $90^\circ$ and $270^\circ$ we have $\cos\theta = 0$ (division by zero, vertical line gradient), whereas at $0^\circ, 180^\circ, 360^\circ$ we have $\sin\theta = 0$ and $\cos\theta = \pm 1$ (zero gradient)
</details>

#### Question `10_6_3_Q11` [Challenging] *(Compressed Period Equations)*
How many solutions does the equation $\sin(2x) = \frac{1}{2}$ have on the interval $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$? Find all of them.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Let $u = 2x$, so as $x$ ranges over $[0^\circ, 360^\circ]$, $u = 2x$ ranges over two full revolutions $[0^\circ, 720^\circ]$.
2. Solve $\sin u = \frac{1}{2}$ on $[0^\circ, 720^\circ]$: $u = 30^\circ, 150^\circ, 390^\circ, 510^\circ$, and divide by $2$ to get $x = 15^\circ, 75^\circ, 195^\circ, 255^\circ$.

**Final Answer**: $4$ solutions: $x = 15^\circ, 75^\circ, 195^\circ, 255^\circ$
</details>

#### Question `10_6_3_Q12` [Extension] *(Co-Function Phase Shift)*
Show by comparing exact values at $x = 0^\circ, 30^\circ, 60^\circ, 90^\circ$ and using right-angled triangle co-functions that $\sin(90^\circ - \theta) = \cos\theta$ for all acute angles $\theta$, and explain what this means graphically for the curves $y = \sin x$ and $y = \cos x$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. In any right-angled triangle with acute angles $\theta$ and $90^\circ - \theta$, $\sin(90^\circ - \theta) = \frac{\text{Opp}(90^\circ - \theta)}{\text{Hyp}} = \frac{\text{Adj}(\theta)}{\text{Hyp}} = \cos\theta$.
2. Graphically, $\cos x = \sin(x + 90^\circ)$, meaning the cosine wave is identical in shape to the sine wave translated $90^\circ$ horizontally to the left.

**Final Answer**: In a right-angled triangle, the side opposite $90^\circ - \theta$ is adjacent to $\theta$, so $\sin(90^\circ - \theta) = \cos\theta$; graphically, $y = \cos x$ is the curve $y = \sin x$ shifted $90^\circ$ to the left
</details>
