### Key Vocabulary
- **True bearing ($\theta^\circ\text{T}$)**: A direction measured clockwise from True North ($000^\circ\text{T}$), written using three digits from $000^\circ\text{T}$ to $360^\circ\text{T}$ (e.g., due East is $090^\circ\text{T}$ and South-West is $225^\circ\text{T}$).
- **Back-bearing (return bearing)**: The true bearing from point $B$ back to point $A$, which differs from the forward bearing from $A$ to $B$ by exactly $180^\circ$ (add $180^\circ$ if $\theta < 180^\circ$; subtract $180^\circ$ if $\theta \ge 180^\circ$).
- **Angle of elevation and depression**: The acute angle measured upward (elevation) or downward (depression) from the horizontal line of sight to an object; by alternate interior angles between parallel horizontals, the angle of elevation from $A$ to $B$ equals the angle of depression from $B$ to $A$.
- **Angle between a line and a plane in 3D**: The acute angle formed between a slanted 3D line segment and its orthogonal projection (shadow) on the horizontal base plane.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Three-Figure True Bearings and Back-Bearings

In aviation, bushwalking, and marine navigation, directions are specified using three-figure true bearings measured clockwise from True North ($000^\circ\text{T}$). The four cardinal directions are North ($000^\circ\text{T}$ or $360^\circ\text{T}$), East ($090^\circ\text{T}$), South ($180^\circ\text{T}$), and West ($270^\circ\text{T}$). Because the North-South lines drawn through any two locations $A$ and $B$ are parallel, co-interior and alternate angles allow us to find the back-bearing from $B$ to $A$ by adding or subtracting $180^\circ$.

$$\text{Back-bearing} = \begin{cases} \theta + 180^\circ & \text{if } 0^\circ \le \theta < 180^\circ \\ \theta - 180^\circ & \text{if } 180^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ \end{cases}$$

> **💡 Always Draw a Compass Cross at the 'FROM' Point**
> For 'the bearing of $B$ FROM $A$', draw a North-East-South-West cross at $A$ and sweep clockwise from North to the ray $AB$. Include leading zeros for angles under $100^\circ$ (e.g., $060^\circ\text{T}$).

### Navigation Components (East-West and North-South)

When a vessel travels a distance $d$ on a true bearing $\theta$, its path forms the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle against the North-South and East-West axes. By finding the acute angle $\alpha$ against either the North-South line or East-West line, you can resolve the journey into a North/South component and an East/West component using $\sin\alpha$ and $\cos\alpha$. Multi-leg journeys are solved by summing the net East/West and North/South displacements.

$$\sin\theta = \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Hypotenuse}}, \quad \cos\theta = \frac{\text{Adjacent}}{\text{Hypotenuse}}, \quad \tan\theta = \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Adjacent}}$$

> **💡 Extract a Clean 2D Right-Angled Triangle**
> Sketch the compass diagram first, shade the right-angled triangle formed with the North-South axis, and label its acute interior angle before choosing $\sin$, $\cos$, or $\tan$.

### 3D Pythagoras and Right-Angled Trigonometry in Space

To solve a 3D spatial problem (such as finding the space diagonal $AG$ of a cuboid of length $l$, width $w$, and height $h$, or the angle that a pyramid edge makes with its horizontal base), work in two 2D stages. First, apply Pythagoras' theorem or trigonometry in the horizontal base plane to find the floor diagonal $d_{\text{floor}} = \sqrt{l^2 + w^2}$. Second, use the vertical right-angled triangle containing $d_{\text{floor}}$ and the vertical height $h$ to find the 3D hypotenuse $D = \sqrt{l^2 + w^2 + h^2}$ and the angle of elevation $\theta$ satisfying $\tan\theta = \frac{h}{d_{\text{floor}}}$.

$$d_{\text{floor}} = \sqrt{l^2 + w^2}, \qquad D_{\text{space}} = \sqrt{l^2 + w^2 + h^2}, \qquad \tan\theta = \frac{h}{d_{\text{floor}}}$$

> **💡 Draw Each 2D Triangle Flat on Your Page**
> Right angles in 3D perspective drawings rarely look like $90^\circ$. Redraw the horizontal base triangle and the vertical elevation triangle separately as flat 2D right-angled triangles.

## Worked Examples

### WE10_6_1_1: True Bearings, Back-Bearings, and Navigation Components

**Problem**: A rescue yacht leaves Sorrento ($S$) and sails $24\text{ km}$ on a true bearing of $060^\circ\text{T}$ to a buoy ($B$). Find: (a) how far North and how far East the buoy $B$ is from Sorrento $S$ (give exact values), and (b) the true bearing of Sorrento $S$ from the buoy $B$ (the back-bearing).

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | A bearing of $060^\circ\text{T}$ makes an angle of $60^\circ$ clockwise from due North. In the right-angled triangle with the North axis, the North component is Adjacent ($24\cos 60^\circ$) and the East component is Opposite ($24\sin 60^\circ$). | $$\text{North distance} = 24\cos 60^\circ = 24 \times \frac{1}{2} = 12\text{ km}$$
$$\text{East distance} = 24\sin 60^\circ = 24 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 12\sqrt{3}\text{ km} \approx 20.78\text{ km}$$ |
| 2 | Since the forward bearing from $S$ to $B$ is $060^\circ\text{T} < 180^\circ$, add $180^\circ$ to obtain the back-bearing from $B$ to $S$. | $$\text{Back-bearing} = 060^\circ + 180^\circ = 240^\circ\text{T}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $12\text{ km}$ North and $12\sqrt{3}\text{ km}$ ($\approx 20.78\text{ km}$) East; (b) $240^\circ\text{T}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**

> **Working**: South $= 10\cos 30^\circ = 5\sqrt{3}\text{ km}$, East $= 10\sin 30^\circ = 5\text{ km}$; back-bearing $= 150^\circ + 180^\circ = 330^\circ\text{T}$.

### WE10_6_1_2: 3D Cuboid Space Diagonal and Angle with the Base Plane

**Problem**: A rectangular shipping container $ABCDEFGH$ has a horizontal floor $ABCD$ of length $AB = 12\text{ m}$ and width $BC = 9\text{ m}$, and a vertical height of $CG = 8\text{ m}$ (with $G$ directly above $C$). Find: (a) the length of the floor diagonal $AC$, (b) the length of the 3D internal space diagonal $AG$, and (c) the angle of elevation $\theta = \angle GAC$ that the space diagonal $AG$ makes with the horizontal floor, correct to $2$ decimal places.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In the horizontal rectangular floor $\triangle ABC$ (right-angled at $B$), use Pythagoras' theorem to find $AC$. | $$AC = \sqrt{AB^2 + BC^2} = \sqrt{12^2 + 9^2} = \sqrt{144 + 81} = \sqrt{225} = 15\text{ m}$$ |
| 2 | In the vertical right-angled triangle $\triangle ACG$ (right-angled at $C$), use $AC = 15\text{ m}$ and $CG = 8\text{ m}$ to find the 3D hypotenuse $AG$. | $$AG = \sqrt{AC^2 + CG^2} = \sqrt{15^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{225 + 64} = \sqrt{289} = 17\text{ m}$$ |
| 3 | In $\triangle ACG$, use $\tan\theta = \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Adjacent}} = \frac{CG}{AC}$ to find $\theta = \angle GAC$. | $$\tan\theta = \frac{8}{15} \implies \theta = \arctan\left(\frac{8}{15}\right) \approx 28.07^\circ$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $AC = 15\text{ m}$, (b) $AG = 17\text{ m}$, (c) $\theta \approx 28.07^\circ$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: A rectangular room is $4\text{ m}$ long, $3\text{ m}$ wide, and $5\text{ m}$ high. Find (a) the floor diagonal $d_{\text{floor}}$, (b) the exact 3D space diagonal $D$, and (c) the exact angle of elevation $\theta$ of the space diagonal from the floor.
> *Hint*: Find $d_{\text{floor}} = \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2}$ first, then compare $d_{\text{floor}}$ with the vertical height $5\text{ m}$.
> **Working**: $d_{\text{floor}} = 5\text{ m}$; $D = \sqrt{5^2 + 5^2} = 5\sqrt{2}\text{ m}$; $\tan\theta = \frac{5}{5} = 1 \implies \theta = 45^\circ$.
> **Answer**: (a) $5\text{ m}$, (b) $5\sqrt{2}\text{ m}$, (c) $45^\circ$

### WE10_6_1_3: 3D Surveying Problem — Vertical Tower Viewed from Two Bearings

**Problem**: A vertical communications tower $OT$ of height $h = 60\text{ m}$ stands on flat horizontal ground with its base at $O$. Observer $A$ is due South of $O$ and measures the angle of elevation to the top $T$ as $45^\circ$. Observer $B$ is due East of $O$ and measures the angle of elevation to $T$ as $30^\circ$. Find: (a) the exact horizontal distances $OA$ and $OB$, and (b) the straight-line horizontal distance $AB$ between the two observers.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In the vertical right-angled triangle $\triangle OAT$, $\angle OAT = 45^\circ$ and $OT = 60\text{ m}$, so $OA = \frac{60}{\tan 45^\circ}$. | $$\tan 45^\circ = \frac{60}{OA} \implies OA = \frac{60}{1} = 60\text{ m}$$ |
| 2 | In the vertical right-angled triangle $\triangle OBT$, $\angle OBT = 30^\circ$ and $OT = 60\text{ m}$, so $OB = \frac{60}{\tan 30^\circ}$. | $$\tan 30^\circ = \frac{60}{OB} \implies OB = \frac{60}{1 / \sqrt{3}} = 60\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$$ |
| 3 | On the horizontal ground, $A$ is due South of $O$ and $B$ is due East of $O$, so $\angle AOB = 90^\circ$. Apply Pythagoras' theorem in $\triangle AOB$. | $$AB = \sqrt{OA^2 + OB^2} = \sqrt{60^2 + (60\sqrt{3})^2} = \sqrt{3600 + 10\,800} = \sqrt{14\,400} = 120\text{ m}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $OA = 60\text{ m}$ and $OB = 60\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$, (b) $AB = 120\text{ m}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Using the distances $OA = 60\text{ m}$ (due South of $O$) and $OB = 60\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$ (due East of $O$) from the example above, find the true bearing of observer $B$ from observer $A$.
> *Hint*: At point $A$ (which is due South of $O$), the ray $AO$ points due North ($000^\circ\text{T}$). Find $\angle OAB$ in right-angled $\triangle AOB$.
> **Working**: $\tan(\angle OAB) = \frac{OB}{OA} = \frac{60\sqrt{3}}{60} = \sqrt{3} \implies \angle OAB = 60^\circ$, so the true bearing of $B$ from $A$ is $060^\circ\text{T}$.
> **Answer**: $060^\circ\text{T}$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Measuring Bearings from the East/West Axis or Anticlockwise
- **The Misconception**: Measuring a true bearing from the East axis (like standard unit-circle polar angles) or measuring anticlockwise from North.
- **The Correct Concept**: True bearings are ALWAYS measured clockwise from True North ($000^\circ\text{T}$). A direction $30^\circ$ West of North is $360^\circ - 30^\circ = 330^\circ\text{T}$, never $030^\circ\text{T}$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\text{Direction } 30^\circ \text{ West of North} \implies 030^\circ\text{T} \quad \text{(Incorrect — swept anticlockwise)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$360^\circ - 30^\circ = 330^\circ\text{T}$$
- **Counter-Example**: $030^\circ\text{T}$ points North-North-East, which is $60^\circ$ away from North-North-West ($330^\circ\text{T}$).

### Placing the Angle of Depression Against the Vertical Cliff Instead of the Horizontal
- **The Misconception**: Labelling the angle of depression $\theta$ between the line of sight and the vertical cliff wall or tower.
- **The Correct Concept**: Both angles of elevation and angles of depression are measured from a HORIZONTAL line of sight. If the angle of depression from a cliff top is $\theta$, the interior angle at the top of the vertical cliff triangle is the complement $90^\circ - \theta$ (while the alternate interior angle at the bottom horizontal is $\theta$).
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\text{Height } h, \text{ horizontal distance } d, \text{ depression } \theta: \quad \tan\theta = \frac{d}{h} \quad \text{(Incorrect — put } \theta \text{ against vertical)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\tan\theta = \frac{\text{vertical height } h}{\text{horizontal distance } d}$$
- **Counter-Example**: Looking almost straight ahead ($\theta \approx 1^\circ$ depression) corresponds to a very shallow drop, not looking nearly straight down at the cliff base.

Find the three-figure back-bearing (the bearing of $A$ from $B$) when the true bearing of $B$ from $A$ is: (a) $047^\circ\text{T}$, and (b) $295^\circ\text{T}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. For (a), since $047^\circ < 180^\circ$, add $180^\circ$: $47^\circ + 180^\circ = 227^\circ\text{T}$.
2. For (b), since $295^\circ \ge 180^\circ$, subtract $180^\circ$: $295^\circ - 180^\circ = 115^\circ\text{T}$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $227^\circ\text{T}$, (b) $115^\circ\text{T}$

#### Question `10_6_1_Q2` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
A bushwalker hikes $8\text{ km}$ due North from trailhead $O$ to point $A$, then turns and walks $8\sqrt{3}\text{ km}$ due East to point $B$. Find (a) the straight-line distance $OB$ and (b) the true bearing of $B$ from $O$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. In right-angled $\triangle OAB$, $OB = \sqrt{8^2 + (8\sqrt{3})^2} = \sqrt{64 + 192} = \sqrt{256} = 16\text{ km}$.
2. Find $\angle AOB$ from North: $\tan(\angle AOB) = \frac{8\sqrt{3}}{8} = \sqrt{3} \implies \angle AOB = 60^\circ$, so the true bearing is $060^\circ\text{T}$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $16\text{ km}$, (b) $060^\circ\text{T}$
</details>

#### Question `10_6_1_Q3` [Medium] *(Fluency)*
From the top of a vertical lighthouse $45\text{ m}$ above sea level, the angle of depression to a fishing boat is $30^\circ$. Find the exact horizontal distance from the base of the lighthouse to the boat.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. By alternate interior angles, the angle of elevation from the boat to the top of the lighthouse is also $30^\circ$.
2. Solve $\tan 30^\circ = \frac{45}{d} \implies d = \frac{45}{1/\sqrt{3}} = 45\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: $45\sqrt{3}\text{ m} \approx 77.94\text{ m}$
</details>

#### Question `10_6_1_Q4` [Medium] *(Understanding)*
A rectangular prism $ABCDEFGH$ has base length $AB = 8\text{ cm}$, base width $BC = 6\text{ cm}$, and vertical height $CG = 10\text{ cm}$. Find (a) the base diagonal $AC$, (b) the exact 3D space diagonal $AG$, and (c) the exact angle of elevation $\angle GAC$.

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `10_6_1_Q5` [Medium] *(Aviation Navigation)*
A light aircraft departs Mildura Airport and flies $120\text{ km}$ on a bearing of $300^\circ\text{T}$. How far North and how far West of Mildura Airport is the aircraft?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. A bearing of $300^\circ\text{T}$ is $360^\circ - 300^\circ = 60^\circ$ West of True North.
2. North component $= 120\cos 60^\circ = 120 \times \frac{1}{2} = 60\text{ km}$; West component $= 120\sin 60^\circ = 60\sqrt{3}\text{ km}$.

**Final Answer**: $60\text{ km}$ North and $60\sqrt{3}\text{ km}$ ($\approx 103.92\text{ km}$) West

#### Question `10_6_1_Q6` [Challenging] *(Surveying in 2D)*
A surveyor stands on flat ground due South of a building and measures the angle of elevation to the roof as $30^\circ$. After walking $40\text{ m}$ due North directly towards the building, the angle of elevation to the roof is $60^\circ$. Find the exact vertical height $h$ of the building.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Let $x$ be the horizontal distance from the second observation point to the building, so $h = x\tan 60^\circ = x\sqrt{3}$.
2. From the first point ($x + 40\text{ m}$ away), $\tan 30^\circ = \frac{h}{x + 40} \implies \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{x\sqrt{3}}{x + 40} \implies x + 40 = 3x \implies x = 20\text{ m}$.
3. Substitute $x = 20$ to obtain $h = 20\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: $h = 20\sqrt{3}\text{ m} \approx 34.64\text{ m}$
</details>

#### Question `10_6_1_Q7` [Challenging] *(3D Architectural Geometry)*
A right square-based pyramid $ABCDV$ (modelling a glass skylight) has a horizontal square base $ABCD$ of side length $10\sqrt{2}\text{ m}$ and vertical apex height $OV = 10\text{ m}$ directly above the base centre $O$. Find (a) the distance $OA$ from the centre $O$ to a base corner $A$, (b) the sloping edge length $VA$, and (c) the angle of elevation $\angle VAO$ of a sloping edge from the base.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The full diagonal of the square base is $AC = \sqrt{(10\sqrt{2})^2 + (10\sqrt{2})^2} = \sqrt{400} = 20\text{ m}$, so $OA = \frac{1}{2}AC = 10\text{ m}$.
2. In vertical right-angled $\triangle AOV$, $VA = \sqrt{OA^2 + OV^2} = \sqrt{10^2 + 10^2} = 10\sqrt{2}\text{ m}$ and $\tan(\angle VAO) = \frac{OV}{OA} = \frac{10}{10} = 1 \implies \angle VAO = 45^\circ$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $OA = 10\text{ m}$, (b) $VA = 10\sqrt{2}\text{ m}$, (c) $\angle VAO = 45^\circ$
</details>

#### Question `10_6_1_Q8` [Challenging] *(Marine Navigation)*
Ship $P$ leaves port $O$ at $09{:}00$ and sails on a bearing of $030^\circ\text{T}$ at $12\text{ km/h}$. Ship $Q$ leaves the same port $O$ at $09{:}00$ and sails on a bearing of $120^\circ\text{T}$ at $16\text{ km/h}$. Find the straight-line distance between the two ships at $11{:}00$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. After $2$ hours, Ship $P$ has travelled $OP = 2 \times 12 = 24\text{ km}$ and Ship $Q$ has travelled $OQ = 2 \times 16 = 32\text{ km}$.
2. The angle between bearings $030^\circ\text{T}$ and $120^\circ\text{T}$ is $120^\circ - 30^\circ = 90^\circ$, so $\triangle POQ$ is right-angled at $O$: $PQ = \sqrt{24^2 + 32^2} = \sqrt{1600} = 40\text{ km}$.

**Final Answer**: $40\text{ km}$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `10_6_1_Q9` [Challenging] *(3D Cube Geometry)*
In a cube of side length $a$, let $\alpha$ be the angle that a face diagonal makes with an edge ($45^\circ$), and let $\beta$ be the angle that the 3D space diagonal makes with the horizontal base face. Prove that $\tan\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ and hence explain why $\beta < 45^\circ$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The horizontal base diagonal of a cube of side $a$ has length $d_{\text{floor}} = \sqrt{a^2 + a^2} = a\sqrt{2}$, while the vertical height is $a$.
2. Therefore $\tan\beta = \frac{a}{a\sqrt{2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} < 1 = \tan 45^\circ$; because $\tan\theta$ is strictly increasing for $0^\circ < \theta < 90^\circ$, $\beta < 45^\circ$.

**Final Answer**: $\tan\beta = \frac{a}{a\sqrt{2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.7071 < 1 = \tan 45^\circ$, so $\beta \approx 35.26^\circ < 45^\circ$
</details>

#### Question `10_6_1_Q10` [Medium] *(Geometric Proof of Back-Bearings)*
Prove using co-interior and reflex angles that if the true bearing of $B$ from $A$ is $\theta$ (where $0^\circ < \theta < 180^\circ$), then the true bearing of $A$ from $B$ is always $\theta + 180^\circ$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Draw parallel North rays $N_A$ at $A$ and $N_B$ at $B$ cut by the transversal segment $AB$.
2. The co-interior angle between $N_B$ and $BA$ (measured anticlockwise from North at $B$) is $180^\circ - \theta$, so the clockwise angle from $N_B$ around to $BA$ is $360^\circ - (180^\circ - \theta) = \theta + 180^\circ$.

**Final Answer**: The North rays at $A$ and $B$ are parallel; the co-interior angle to $\theta$ is $180^\circ - \theta$ (anticlockwise from North at $B$), so the clockwise bearing is $360^\circ - (180^\circ - \theta) = \theta + 180^\circ$
</details>

#### Question `10_6_1_Q11` [Challenging] *(3D Courtyard Elevation)*
A vertical flagpole of height $h$ stands at the corner $C$ of a horizontal rectangular courtyard $ABCD$ of length $AB = 40\text{ m}$ and width $BC = 30\text{ m}$. If the angle of elevation of the top of the flagpole from the opposite corner $A$ is $30^\circ$, find the exact height $h$ of the flagpole.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. First find the horizontal diagonal distance across the courtyard: $AC = \sqrt{40^2 + 30^2} = 50\text{ m}$.
2. In the vertical right-angled triangle at $C$, $h = AC\tan 30^\circ = 50 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} = \frac{50\sqrt{3}}{3}\text{ m}$.

</details>
