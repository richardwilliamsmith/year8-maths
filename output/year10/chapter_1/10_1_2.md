### Key Vocabulary
- **Surd**: An irrational root of a rational number, such as $\sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{5}$, or $\sqrt[3]{7}$, whose decimal expansion neither terminates nor recurs.
- **Like surds**: Surds that have the exact same simplified radicand (number under the root sign), such as $3\sqrt{5}$ and $-7\sqrt{5}$, which can be combined by adding or subtracting their coefficients.
- **Conjugate surds**: A pair of binomial surd expressions of the form $a\sqrt{x} + b\sqrt{y}$ and $a\sqrt{x} - b\sqrt{y}$ whose product is always rational by the Difference of Two Squares identity.
- **Rationalising the denominator**: Rewriting a fraction containing a surd in its denominator as an equivalent fraction with a rational (integer) denominator.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Simplifying, Adding, and Subtracting Surds

A square root $\sqrt{n}$ is simplified by extracting its largest perfect square factor using $\sqrt{a^2 b} = a\sqrt{b}$ (for $a > 0$). Once all surds in an expression are written in simplest form, like surds behave just like algebraic like terms: add or subtract their rational coefficients while keeping the surd factor unchanged.

$$\sqrt{a^2 b} = a\sqrt{b}, \quad m\sqrt{k} \pm n\sqrt{k} = (m \pm n)\sqrt{k}$$

> **💡 Simplify Every Surd Before Looking for Like Terms**
> At first glance $\sqrt{50} + \sqrt{18}$ look unlike, but simplifying gives $5\sqrt{2} + 3\sqrt{2} = 8\sqrt{2}$. Never add the numbers under the root sign: $\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b} \neq \sqrt{a + b}$.

### Multiplying Surds and Expanding Binomial Products

When multiplying two surd terms, multiply the rational coefficients together and multiply the radicands together using $\sqrt{a}\sqrt{b} = \sqrt{ab}$, then simplify any perfect square factors that arise. Binomial expansions involving surds follow the exact same distributive, perfect square, and Difference of Two Squares (DOTS) identities as algebraic binomials.

$$(a + b\sqrt{c})^2 = a^2 + 2ab\sqrt{c} + b^2 c, \quad (a + \sqrt{b})(a - \sqrt{b}) = a^2 - b$$

> **💡 Conjugate Pairs Eliminate the Surd**
> When you multiply conjugate binomial surds $(\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b})(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b})$, the middle surd terms cancel out completely, leaving the rational number $a - b$.

### Rationalising Monomial and Binomial Denominators

By mathematical convention, exact answers are written with a rational denominator. If the denominator is a single surd $c\sqrt{b}$, multiply the numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{b}$. If the denominator is a binomial surd such as $a + \sqrt{b}$ or $\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b}$, multiply both numerator and denominator by its conjugate ($a - \sqrt{b}$ or $\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$) so that the denominator becomes a rational difference of two squares.

$$\frac{a}{\sqrt{b}} = \frac{a\sqrt{b}}{b}, \quad \frac{c}{\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b}} \times \frac{\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}}{\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}} = \frac{c(\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b})}{a - b}$$

> **💡 Multiply Top and Bottom by the Conjugate**
> Multiplying numerator and denominator by the same non-zero conjugate is equivalent to multiplying by $1$, which changes the form of the fraction without changing its value.

## Worked Examples

### WE10_1_2_1: Simplifying and Combining Like Surds

**Problem**: Simplify $3\sqrt{12} + 2\sqrt{27} - \sqrt{48}$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Factor each radicand to extract its largest perfect square factor ($4$, $9$, and $16$). | $$3\sqrt{4 \times 3} + 2\sqrt{9 \times 3} - \sqrt{16 \times 3}$$ |
| 2 | Simplify each surd using $\sqrt{a^2 b} = a\sqrt{b}$. | $$= 3(2\sqrt{3}) + 2(3\sqrt{3}) - 4\sqrt{3} = 6\sqrt{3} + 6\sqrt{3} - 4\sqrt{3}$$ |
| 3 | Combine the coefficients of the like surds $\sqrt{3}$. | $$= (6 + 6 - 4)\sqrt{3} = 8\sqrt{3}$$ |

**Answer**: $8\sqrt{3}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Simplify $2\sqrt{50} - 3\sqrt{8} + \sqrt{32}$.
> *Hint*: Rewrite each surd as a multiple of $\sqrt{2}$ using $50 = 25 \times 2$, $8 = 4 \times 2$, and $32 = 16 \times 2$.
> **Working**: $2(5\sqrt{2}) - 3(2\sqrt{2}) + 4\sqrt{2} = 10\sqrt{2} - 6\sqrt{2} + 4\sqrt{2} = 8\sqrt{2}$.
> **Answer**: $8\sqrt{2}$

### WE10_1_2_2: Expanding Binomial Products Involving Surds

**Problem**: Expand and simplify: (a) $(3 - \sqrt{5})^2$ and (b) $(2\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2})(2\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2})$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), apply the perfect square expansion $(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$ with $a = 3$ and $b = \sqrt{5}$. | $$(3 - \sqrt{5})^2 = 3^2 - 2(3)(\sqrt{5}) + (\sqrt{5})^2 = 9 - 6\sqrt{5} + 5 = 14 - 6\sqrt{5}$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), apply the Difference of Two Squares identity $(A + B)(A - B) = A^2 - B^2$ with $A = 2\sqrt{3}$ and $B = \sqrt{2}$. | $$(2\sqrt{3})^2 - (\sqrt{2})^2 = (4 \times 3) - 2 = 12 - 2 = 10$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $14 - 6\sqrt{5}$, (b) $10$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Expand and simplify: (a) $(2 + \sqrt{3})^2$ and (b) $(3\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{5})(3\sqrt{2} - \sqrt{5})$.
> *Hint*: Note that $(3\sqrt{2})^2 = 9 \times 2 = 18$.
> **Working**: (a) $4 + 4\sqrt{3} + 3 = 7 + 4\sqrt{3}$; (b) $18 - 5 = 13$.
> **Answer**: (a) $7 + 4\sqrt{3}$, (b) $13$

### WE10_1_2_3: Rationalising Monomial and Binomial Denominators

**Problem**: Rationalise the denominator and simplify: (a) $\frac{10}{\sqrt{5}}$ and (b) $\frac{4}{\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{3}}$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), multiply numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{5}$ and simplify. | $$\frac{10}{\sqrt{5}} \times \frac{\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{5}} = \frac{10\sqrt{5}}{5} = 2\sqrt{5}$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator, which is $\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3}$. | $$\frac{4}{\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{3}} \times \frac{\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3}} = \frac{4(\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3})}{(\sqrt{7})^2 - (\sqrt{3})^2}$$ |
| 3 | Evaluate the denominator $7 - 3 = 4$ and cancel the common factor of $4$. | $$= \frac{4(\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3})}{4} = \sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $2\sqrt{5}$, (b) $\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Rationalise the denominator and simplify: (a) $\frac{12}{\sqrt{6}}$ and (b) $\frac{6}{\sqrt{5} + \sqrt{2}}$.
> *Hint*: For (b), multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt{5} - \sqrt{2}$.
> **Working**: (a) $\frac{12\sqrt{6}}{6} = 2\sqrt{6}$; (b) $\frac{6(\sqrt{5} - \sqrt{2})}{5 - 2} = 2(\sqrt{5} - \sqrt{2})$.
> **Answer**: (a) $2\sqrt{6}$, (b) $2\sqrt{5} - 2\sqrt{2}$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Adding Radicands Directly When Adding Surds
- **The Misconception**: Writing $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3} = \sqrt{5}$ or $\sqrt{9} + \sqrt{16} = \sqrt{25}$.
- **The Correct Concept**: Square roots do NOT distribute over addition or subtraction: $\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b} \neq \sqrt{a + b}$. Surds can only be combined when they have identical simplified radicands.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\sqrt{9} + \sqrt{16} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5 \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\sqrt{9} + \sqrt{16} = 3 + 4 = 7 \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: For $a = 9$ and $b = 16$, $\sqrt{9} + \sqrt{16} = 3 + 4 = 7$, whereas $\sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5 \neq 7$.

### Square-Rooting Only One Term in a Binomial Denominator
- **The Misconception**: Attempting to rationalise $\frac{1}{3 + \sqrt{2}}$ by multiplying top and bottom by $\sqrt{2}$ instead of the conjugate $3 - \sqrt{2}$.
- **The Correct Concept**: Multiplying $(3 + \sqrt{2})$ by $\sqrt{2}$ gives $3\sqrt{2} + 2$, which still contains a surd! Only multiplying by the conjugate $(3 - \sqrt{2})$ eliminates the surd via $(3 + \sqrt{2})(3 - \sqrt{2}) = 9 - 2 = 7$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\frac{1}{3 + \sqrt{2}} \times \frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3\sqrt{2} + 2} \quad (\text{Denominator is still irrational})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\frac{1}{3 + \sqrt{2}} \times \frac{3 - \sqrt{2}}{3 - \sqrt{2}} = \frac{3 - \sqrt{2}}{9 - 2} = \frac{3 - \sqrt{2}}{7} \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: In $3\sqrt{2} + 2$, the term $3\sqrt{2}$ remains irrational, so the denominator has not been rationalised.

### Fluency & Understanding

#### Question `10_1_2_Q1` [Easy] *(Adding and Subtracting Surds)*
Simplify $\sqrt{75} - \sqrt{27} + \sqrt{12}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Simplify each surd: $\sqrt{75} = 5\sqrt{3}$, $\sqrt{27} = 3\sqrt{3}$, and $\sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}$.
2. Combine like surds: $(5 - 3 + 2)\sqrt{3} = 4\sqrt{3}$.

**Final Answer**: $4\sqrt{3}$
</details>

#### Question `10_1_2_Q2` [Easy] *(Binomial Surd Expansion)*
Expand and simplify $(4 + \sqrt{7})(2 - \sqrt{7})$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Expand using FOIL: $4(2) - 4\sqrt{7} + 2\sqrt{7} - (\sqrt{7})^2 = 8 - 2\sqrt{7} - 7$.
2. Collect rational terms: $1 - 2\sqrt{7}$.

**Final Answer**: $1 - 2\sqrt{7}$
</details>

#### Question `10_1_2_Q3` [Medium] *(Monomial Denominator)*
Rationalise the denominator and simplify $\frac{15}{2\sqrt{5}}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Multiply numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{5}$: $\frac{15\sqrt{5}}{2 \times 5} = \frac{15\sqrt{5}}{10}$.
2. Divide numerator and denominator by $5$: $\frac{3\sqrt{5}}{2}$.

**Final Answer**: $\frac{3\sqrt{5}}{2}$
</details>

#### Question `10_1_2_Q4` [Medium] *(Conjugate Binomial Denominator)*
Rationalise the denominator and simplify $\frac{8}{3 - \sqrt{5}}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate $3 + \sqrt{5}$: $\frac{8(3 + \sqrt{5})}{3^2 - (\sqrt{5})^2} = \frac{8(3 + \sqrt{5})}{9 - 5}$.
2. Simplify: $\frac{8(3 + \sqrt{5})}{4} = 2(3 + \sqrt{5}) = 6 + 2\sqrt{5}$.

**Final Answer**: $6 + 2\sqrt{5}$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `10_1_2_Q5` [Medium] *(Exact Geometry with Surds)*
A rectangle has length $(\sqrt{18} + \sqrt{5})\text{ cm}$ and width $(\sqrt{18} - \sqrt{5})\text{ cm}$. Find the exact perimeter and the exact area of the rectangle in simplest form.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Note that $\sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}$. Perimeter $P = 2(3\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{5} + 3\sqrt{2} - \sqrt{5}) = 2(6\sqrt{2}) = 12\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}$.
2. Area $A = (\sqrt{18} + \sqrt{5})(\sqrt{18} - \sqrt{5}) = (\sqrt{18})^2 - (\sqrt{5})^2 = 18 - 5 = 13\text{ cm}^2$.

**Final Answer**: Perimeter $12\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}$; area $13\text{ cm}^2$
</details>

#### Question `10_1_2_Q6` [Challenging] *(Triangle Height with Surds)*
A right-angled triangle has area $6\text{ cm}^2$ and base $(\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3})\text{ cm}$. Find its exact perpendicular height $h$ with a rational denominator.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. From $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$, we have $h = \frac{2A}{b} = \frac{12}{\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3}}$.
2. Multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{3}$: $h = \frac{12(\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{3})}{7 - 3} = \frac{12(\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{3})}{4} = 3\sqrt{7} - 3\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}$.

**Final Answer**: $(3\sqrt{7} - 3\sqrt{3})\text{ cm}$
</details>

#### Question `10_1_2_Q7` [Challenging] *(Sum of Conjugate Fractions)*
Express $\frac{1}{\sqrt{3} - 1} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{3} + 1}$ as a single simplified number.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Rationalise each fraction using its conjugate denominator: $( \sqrt{3} - 1 )(\sqrt{3} + 1) = 3 - 1 = 2$.
2. Thus $\frac{\sqrt{3} + 1}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{3} - 1}{2} = \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{2} = \sqrt{3}$.

**Final Answer**: $\sqrt{3}$
</details>

#### Question `10_1_2_Q8` [Challenging] *(Rationalising Quotient of Conjugates)*
Rationalise the denominator of $\frac{\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2}}$ and write the result in the form $a + b\sqrt{6}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Multiply numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2}$: $\frac{(\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2})^2}{(\sqrt{3})^2 - (\sqrt{2})^2}$.
2. The denominator is $3 - 2 = 1$, and the numerator expands to $3 + 2\sqrt{6} + 2 = 5 + 2\sqrt{6}$.

**Final Answer**: $5 + 2\sqrt{6}$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `10_1_2_Q9` [Challenging] *(Golden Ratio Identity)*
The Golden Ratio is $\phi = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2}$. Prove algebraically that $\phi^2 - \phi - 1 = 0$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Expand $\phi^2 = \left(\frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1 + 2\sqrt{5} + 5}{4} = \frac{6 + 2\sqrt{5}}{4} = \frac{3 + \sqrt{5}}{2}$.
2. Notice that $\phi + 1 = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} + 1 = \frac{3 + \sqrt{5}}{2}$, so $\phi^2 - \phi - 1 = 0$.

**Final Answer**: $\phi^2 = \frac{3 + \sqrt{5}}{2} = \phi + 1$, so $\phi^2 - \phi - 1 = 0$
</details>

#### Question `10_1_2_Q10` [Medium] *(Closure of Rationals Under Conjugate Product)*
Explain why $(a + b\sqrt{p})(a - b\sqrt{p})$ is always a rational number whenever $a$, $b$, and $p$ are rational numbers ($p > 0$).

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Expanding gives $a^2 - ab\sqrt{p} + ab\sqrt{p} - b^2(\sqrt{p})^2 = a^2 - b^2 p$.
2. Because $a$, $b$, and $p$ are rational, $a^2 - b^2 p$ is a difference of products of rational numbers, which is always rational.

**Final Answer**: By DOTS it equals $a^2 - b^2 p$, and the rational numbers are closed under multiplication and subtraction
</details>

#### Question `10_1_2_Q11` [Extension] *(Comparing Surd Sums by Squaring)*
Without using a calculator, prove that $\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2} > \sqrt{5} + \sqrt{3}$ by squaring both positive expressions.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Square both positive expressions: $(\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2})^2 = 6 + 2\sqrt{12} + 2 = 8 + \sqrt{48}$, while $(\sqrt{5} + \sqrt{3})^2 = 5 + 2\sqrt{15} + 3 = 8 + \sqrt{60}$.
2. Since $\sqrt{60} > \sqrt{48}$, we see that $\sqrt{5} + \sqrt{3} > \sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}$ (disproving the claim that $\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}$ is larger).

**Final Answer**: $(\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2})^2 = 8 + 2\sqrt{12} < 8 + 2\sqrt{15} = (\sqrt{5} + \sqrt{3})^2$ is false; actually $\sqrt{5} + \sqrt{3} > \sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}$, let us verify: $( \sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2} )^2 = 8 + 4\sqrt{3} = 8 + \sqrt{48}$, whereas $(\sqrt{5} + \sqrt{3})^2 = 8 + 2\sqrt{15} = 8 + \sqrt{60}$, so $\sqrt{5} + \sqrt{3} > \sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}$!
</details>

#### Question `10_1_2_Q12` [Extension] *(Telescoping Surd Series)*
Use rationalising the denominator to evaluate the telescoping sum $S = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1} + \sqrt{2}} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{4}}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. For each term, $\frac{1}{\sqrt{k} + \sqrt{k+1}} = \frac{\sqrt{k+1} - \sqrt{k}}{(k + 1) - k} = \sqrt{k+1} - \sqrt{k}$.
2. Summing for $k = 1, 2, 3$ gives $(\sqrt{2} - 1) + (\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2}) + (\sqrt{4} - \sqrt{3}) = \sqrt{4} - 1 = 2 - 1 = 1$.

**Final Answer**: $1$
</details>
