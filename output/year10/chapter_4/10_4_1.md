### Key Vocabulary
- **Turning-point form**: A quadratic equation written as $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$, whose parabola has vertex (turning point) at $(h, k)$ and axis of symmetry $x = h$.
- **Horizontal asymptote**: A horizontal line $y = k$ that an exponential curve $y = a \cdot b^x + k$ approaches ever more closely as $x \to -\infty$ (for $b > 1$) or $x \to +\infty$ (for $0 < b < 1$) without ever crossing.
- **Circle relation**: The set of all points $(x, y)$ at a constant distance $r$ (the radius) from a fixed centre $(h, k)$, given by $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$.
- **Axis of symmetry**: The vertical line $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$ (or $x = \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}$ midway between the $x$-intercepts) that divides a parabola into two mirror-image halves.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Sketching Parabolas in Turning-Point, Intercept, and General Forms

A quadratic graph can be presented in three main algebraic forms, each directly revealing key geometric features: (1) Turning-point form $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$ gives the vertex $(h, k)$ and axis of symmetry $x = h$; (2) Intercept (factorised) form $y = a(x - p)(x - q)$ gives the $x$-intercepts $(p, 0)$ and $(q, 0)$, with the axis of symmetry halfway between them at $x = \frac{p + q}{2}$; and (3) General form $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ gives the $y$-intercept $(0, c)$ and axis of symmetry $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$. In all forms, $a > 0$ gives an upright parabola (minimum turning point) and $a < 0$ gives an inverted parabola (maximum turning point).

$$y = a(x - h)^2 + k \implies \text{Vertex } (h, k); \quad y = ax^2 + bx + c \implies x_v = -\frac{b}{2a}$$

> **💡 Opposite Sign Inside the Bracket, Same Sign Outside**
> In $y = 2(x - 3)^2 - 5$, the $(x - 3)$ shifts the parabola RIGHT to $h = +3$, while $-5$ shifts it DOWN to $k = -5$, giving vertex $(3, -5)$.

### Exponential Graphs and Horizontal Asymptotes

An exponential relation has the form $y = a \cdot b^x + k$ ($b > 0, b \neq 1$). Because $b^x > 0$ for every real number $x$, the term $a \cdot b^x$ can never equal $0$, which means $y$ can never equal $k$. Thus the horizontal line $y = k$ is a horizontal asymptote (always drawn as a dashed line and labelled with its equation $y = k$). The $y$-intercept is found by substituting $x = 0$, giving $y = a \cdot b^0 + k = a + k$. When $b > 1$ (and $a > 0$), the graph shows exponential growth; when $0 < b < 1$ (or when the exponent is $-x$), it shows exponential decay.

$$y = a \cdot b^x + k: \quad \text{Horizontal asymptote } y = k, \quad y\text{-intercept } (0,\, a + k)$$

> **💡 Always Draw and Label the Dashed Asymptote $y = k$**
> On any exponential graph sketch, draw the horizontal line $y = k$ as a dashed line and label its equation explicitly, alongside the $y$-intercept and any $x$-intercept.

### Circles in the Cartesian Plane

By Pythagoras' theorem (the distance formula), a point $P(x, y)$ lies at distance $r$ from the centre $C(h, k)$ if and only if $\sqrt{(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2} = r$. Squaring both sides gives the standard equation of a circle with centre $(h, k)$ and radius $r$. When the centre is at the origin $(0, 0)$, the equation simplifies to $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$, with axis intercepts $(\pm r, 0)$ and $(0, \pm r)$.

$$(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2 \quad (\text{Centre } (h, k),\; \text{Radius } r > 0)$$

> **💡 Take the Square Root of the Right-Hand Side to Find the Radius**
> In $(x + 2)^2 + (y - 5)^2 = 16$, the right-hand side is $r^2 = 16$, so the radius is $r = \sqrt{16} = 4$ (NOT $16$), and the centre is $(-2, 5)$.

### WE10_4_1_1: Finding Key Features of a Parabola in Turning-Point Form

**Problem**: For the parabola $y = (x - 2)^2 - 9$, find: (a) the coordinates of the turning point, (b) the $y$-intercept, and (c) the $x$-intercepts.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Read $(h, k)$ directly from $y = (x - 2)^2 - 9$: $h = 2$ and $k = -9$ (a minimum since $a = 1 > 0$). | $$\text{Turning point (minimum)} = (2, -9)$$ |
| 2 | Substitute $x = 0$ to find the $y$-intercept. | $$y = (0 - 2)^2 - 9 = 4 - 9 = -5 \implies (0, -5)$$ |
| 3 | Substitute $y = 0$ and solve $(x - 2)^2 = 9$ to find the $x$-intercepts. | $$(x - 2)^2 = 9 \implies x - 2 = \pm 3 \implies x = 5 \text{ or } x = -1$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $(2, -9)$, (b) $(0, -5)$, (c) $(-1, 0)$ and $(5, 0)$

> **✏️ Now You Try**

> **Working**: Vertex $(-1, -4)$; $y$-intercept at $x = 0$ is $1 - 4 = -3$; $x$-intercepts from $(x + 1)^2 = 4 \implies x = 1$ or $x = -3$.
> **Answer**: Turning point $(-1, -4)$, $y$-intercept $(0, -3)$, $x$-intercepts $(-3, 0)$ and $(1, 0)$

### WE10_4_1_2: Finding Key Features of an Exponential Graph

**Problem**: For the exponential relation $y = 2^x - 4$, find the equation of its horizontal asymptote, its $y$-intercept, and its $x$-intercept.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | As $x \to -\infty$, $2^x \to 0$, so $y \to -4$. The horizontal asymptote is $y = -4$. | $$\text{Horizontal asymptote: } y = -4$$ |
| 2 | Substitute $x = 0$ (using $2^0 = 1$) to find the $y$-intercept. | $$y = 2^0 - 4 = 1 - 4 = -3 \implies (0, -3)$$ |
| 3 | Substitute $y = 0$ and solve $2^x - 4 = 0$ to find the $x$-intercept. | $$2^x = 4 = 2^2 \implies x = 2 \implies (2, 0)$$ |

**Answer**: Asymptote $y = -4$, $y$-intercept $(0, -3)$, $x$-intercept $(2, 0)$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: For the exponential relation $y = 3^x - 9$, find the equation of the horizontal asymptote, the $y$-intercept, and the $x$-intercept.
> *Hint*: Set $x = 0$ ($3^0 = 1$) for the $y$-intercept and $3^x = 9$ for the $x$-intercept.
> **Working**: Asymptote $y = -9$; $y$-intercept $(0, 1 - 9) = (0, -8)$; $x$-intercept from $3^x = 9 \implies x = 2$, so $(2, 0)$.
> **Answer**: Asymptote $y = -9$, $y$-intercept $(0, -8)$, $x$-intercept $(2, 0)$

### WE10_4_1_3: Identifying the Centre, Radius, and Intercepts of a Circle

**Problem**: Find the centre and radius of the circle $(x - 3)^2 + (y + 4)^2 = 25$, and determine whether the circle passes through the origin $(0, 0)$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Compare $(x - 3)^2 + (y - (-4))^2 = 5^2$ with $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$. | $$\text{Centre } (h, k) = (3, -4), \quad \text{Radius } r = \sqrt{25} = 5$$ |
| 2 | Substitute $(x, y) = (0, 0)$ into the left-hand side of the equation to check if it equals $25$. | $$(0 - 3)^2 + (0 + 4)^2 = 9 + 16 = 25 \implies \text{Yes, } (0, 0) \text{ lies on the circle.}$$ |

**Answer**: Centre $(3, -4)$, radius $r = 5$; yes, the circle passes through $(0, 0)$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Assuming the $y$-Intercept of $y = b^x$ is $0$ Instead of $1$
- **The Misconception**: Substituting $x = 0$ into $y = 2^x$ and writing $y = 2^0 = 0$.
- **The Correct Concept**: By the zero index law, any non-zero base raised to the power $0$ equals $1$ ($b^0 = 1$). Therefore, every basic exponential curve $y = b^x$ passes through $(0, 1)$, never $(0, 0)$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$x = 0 \implies y = 2^0 = 0 \implies (0, 0) \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$x = 0 \implies y = 2^0 = 1 \implies (0, 1) \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: The line $y = 0$ is the horizontal asymptote of $y = 2^x$, so $2^x$ is strictly positive and never touches $(0, 0)$.

### Forgetting to Take the Square Root of $r^2$ in a Circle Equation
- **The Misconception**: Stating that the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 49$ has radius $49$.
- **The Correct Concept**: In $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$, the constant on the right-hand side is the SQUARE of the radius ($r^2$), so the radius is $r = \sqrt{49} = 7$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$x^2 + y^2 = 49 \implies r = 49 \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$x^2 + y^2 = 49 \implies r = \sqrt{49} = 7 \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: When $y = 0$, $x^2 = 49 \implies x = \pm 7$, so the circle crosses the $x$-axis at $7$ units from the origin, not $49$.

#### Question `10_4_1_Q1` [Easy] *(Parabola Vertex from General Form)*
Find the axis of symmetry and the coordinates of the turning point of the parabola $y = x^2 - 6x + 5$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Use $x = -\frac{b}{2a} = -\frac{-6}{2(1)} = 3$.
2. Substitute $x = 3$ into the equation: $y = 3^2 - 6(3) + 5 = 9 - 18 + 5 = -4$, giving $(3, -4)$.

**Final Answer**: Axis of symmetry $x = 3$; turning point $(3, -4)$
</details>

#### Question `10_4_1_Q2` [Easy] *(Parabola in Intercept Form)*
Find the $x$-intercepts and the coordinates of the turning point of $y = -(x - 1)(x - 5)$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Setting $y = 0$ gives $x$-intercepts at $x = 1$ and $x = 5$.
2. The axis of symmetry is midway at $x = \frac{1 + 5}{2} = 3$. Substituting $x = 3$ gives $y = -(3 - 1)(3 - 5) = -2(-2) = 4$, so the vertex is $(3, 4)$.

**Final Answer**: $x$-intercepts $(1, 0)$ and $(5, 0)$; maximum turning point $(3, 4)$
</details>

#### Question `10_4_1_Q3` [Medium] *(Exponential Graph Features)*
Find the horizontal asymptote, $y$-intercept, and $x$-intercept of the exponential graph $y = 2 \times 3^x - 6$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. As $x \to -\infty$, $3^x \to 0$, so the horizontal asymptote is $y = -6$.
2. At $x = 0$, $y = 2(3^0) - 6 = 2 - 6 = -4 \implies (0, -4)$.
3. At $y = 0$, $2 \times 3^x = 6 \implies 3^x = 3^1 \implies x = 1 \implies (1, 0)$.

**Final Answer**: Asymptote $y = -6$, $y$-intercept $(0, -4)$, $x$-intercept $(1, 0)$
</details>

#### Question `10_4_1_Q4` [Easy] *(Writing a Circle Equation)*
Write the equation of the circle with centre $(-3, 2)$ and radius $4$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute $h = -3$, $k = 2$, and $r = 4$ into $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$.
2. $(x - (-3))^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 4^2 \implies (x + 3)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 16$.

**Final Answer**: $(x + 3)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 16$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `10_4_1_Q5` [Medium] *(Parabolic Bridge Arch)*
A parabolic bridge arch is modelled by $y = -\frac{1}{4}(x - 10)^2 + 25$, where $x$ is the horizontal distance in metres and $y$ is the height in metres above the river. Find: (a) the maximum height of the arch, and (b) the horizontal span of the arch at river level ($y = 0$).

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The vertex of $y = -\frac{1}{4}(x - 10)^2 + 25$ is $(10, 25)$, so the maximum height is $25\text{ m}$.
2. Set $y = 0$: $\frac{1}{4}(x - 10)^2 = 25 \implies (x - 10)^2 = 100 \implies x - 10 = \pm 10 \implies x = 0$ or $x = 20$.
3. The horizontal span is $20 - 0 = 20\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: (a) Maximum height $25\text{ m}$ (at $x = 10\text{ m}$); (b) span $20\text{ m}$ (from $x = 0$ to $x = 20$)
</details>

#### Question `10_4_1_Q6` [Medium] *(Newton's Law of Cooling)*
A hot cup of tea placed in a room with ambient temperature $20^\circ\text{C}$ has temperature $T(^\circ\text{C})$ after $t$ minutes modelled by $T = 70(2)^{-t/5} + 20$. Find: (a) the initial temperature of the tea, (b) its temperature after $10$ minutes, and (c) the long-term temperature it approaches.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. At $t = 0$, $T = 70(2)^0 + 20 = 70 + 20 = 90^\circ\text{C}$.
2. At $t = 10$, $T = 70(2)^{-2} + 20 = \frac{70}{4} + 20 = 17.5 + 20 = 37.5^\circ\text{C}$.
3. As $t \to \infty$, $2^{-t/5} \to 0$, so $T \to 20^\circ\text{C}$ (the room's ambient temperature).

**Final Answer**: (a) $90^\circ\text{C}$, (b) $37.5^\circ\text{C}$, (c) $20^\circ\text{C}$ (asymptote $T = 20$)
</details>

#### Question `10_4_1_Q7` [Challenging] *(Completing the Square for a Circle)*
Complete the square for both $x$ and $y$ to find the centre and radius of the circle $x^2 - 6x + y^2 + 8y = 0$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Add $(-3)^2 = 9$ and $4^2 = 16$ to both sides: $(x^2 - 6x + 9) + (y^2 + 8y + 16) = 9 + 16$.
2. Rewrite as $(x - 3)^2 + (y + 4)^2 = 25$, giving centre $(3, -4)$ and radius $r = \sqrt{25} = 5$.

**Final Answer**: Centre $(3, -4)$, radius $r = 5$
</details>

#### Question `10_4_1_Q8` [Challenging] *(Intersection of a Line and a Circle)*
Find the two points of intersection of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ and the straight line $y = x + 1$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute $y = x + 1$ into $x^2 + y^2 = 25$: $x^2 + (x + 1)^2 = 25 \implies 2x^2 + 2x - 24 = 0 \implies x^2 + x - 12 = 0$.
2. Factorise: $(x + 4)(x - 3) = 0 \implies x = 3$ or $x = -4$.
3. Substitute back into $y = x + 1$: for $x = 3, y = 4$; for $x = -4, y = -3$. Points are $(3, 4)$ and $(-4, -3)$.

**Final Answer**: $(3, 4)$ and $(-4, -3)$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `10_4_1_Q9` [Medium] *(Parabola Above the x-Axis)*
Explain why the parabola $y = 2(x - 3)^2 + 5$ has no $x$-intercepts, using both algebraic reasoning and geometric reasoning.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Geometrically, the vertex is $(3, 5)$, which lies above the $x$-axis, and since $a = 2 > 0$ the parabola opens upwards, never crossing $y = 0$.
2. Algebraically, setting $y = 0$ gives $(x - 3)^2 = -\frac{5}{2}$, which has no real solutions because a square is never negative.

**Final Answer**: Geometrically its minimum vertex is $(3, 5)$ above the $x$-axis and it opens upwards ($a = 2 > 0$); algebraically $2(x - 3)^2 = -5$ has no real solutions since $(x - 3)^2 \ge 0$
</details>

#### Question `10_4_1_Q10` [Medium] *(Exponential Reflection in the y-Axis)*
Show algebraically that the graphs of $y = 2^{-x}$ and $y = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^x$ are identical, and explain how their graph is related to the graph of $y = 2^x$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. By the index laws, $2^{-x} = (2^{-1})^x = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^x$ for all real $x$.
2. Replacing $x$ with $-x$ reflects every point $(x, y)$ on $y = 2^x$ to $(-x, y)$ across the $y$-axis, turning exponential growth into exponential decay.

**Final Answer**: $2^{-x} = (2^{-1})^x = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^x$; replacing $x$ with $-x$ reflects the graph of $y = 2^x$ in the $y$-axis
</details>

#### Question `10_4_1_Q11` [Challenging] *(Point Position Relative to a Circle)*
Determine whether the point $P(4, 5)$ lies inside, on, or outside the circle $(x - 1)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 16$, and justify your conclusion.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate the squared distance from the centre $(1, 2)$ to $P(4, 5)$: $d^2 = (4 - 1)^2 + (5 - 2)^2 = 9 + 9 = 18$.
2. Since $d^2 = 18 > r^2 = 16$ (so $d = 3\sqrt{2} \approx 4.24 > 4$), $P(4, 5)$ lies strictly outside the circle.

**Final Answer**: Outside the circle, because $(4 - 1)^2 + (5 - 2)^2 = 18 > 16$ (distance from centre is $\sqrt{18} > 4$)
</details>

#### Question `10_4_1_Q12` [Extension] *(Determining a Quadratic Rule from Vertex and Point)*
A parabola has vertex $(2, -8)$ and passes through the point $(4, 0)$. Find its quadratic equation in turning-point form and general form.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute vertex $(h, k) = (2, -8)$ into $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$: $y = a(x - 2)^2 - 8$.
2. Substitute $(4, 0)$: $0 = a(4 - 2)^2 - 8 \implies 4a = 8 \implies a = 2$.
3. Thus $y = 2(x - 2)^2 - 8 = 2(x^2 - 4x + 4) - 8 = 2x^2 - 8x$.

**Final Answer**: $y = 2(x - 2)^2 - 8 = 2x^2 - 8x$
</details>
