### Key Vocabulary
- **Linear inequality**: A mathematical statement comparing two linear expressions using an inequality symbol ($<$, $>$, $\le$, or $\ge$).
- **Strict vs inclusive inequality**: A strict inequality ($<$ or $>$) excludes the boundary value (drawn with an open circle or dashed boundary line), whereas an inclusive inequality ($\le$ or $\ge$) includes the boundary value (closed circle or solid line).
- **Compound inequality**: Two inequalities joined into a single three-part statement, such as $-3 \le 2x + 1 < 7$, defining a bounded interval on the real number line.
- **Half-plane**: The region of the Cartesian plane lying on one side of a boundary straight line $ax + by = c$, representing all points $(x, y)$ that satisfy a two-variable linear inequality.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Properties of Inequalities and Reversing the Sign

Solving a linear inequality in one variable uses the same inverse operations as solving a linear equation, with one critical exception: multiplying or dividing both sides of an inequality by a negative number reverses the order of the real number line, so the inequality symbol must be flipped ($<$ becomes $>$, and $\le$ becomes $\ge$). Adding or subtracting any number, or multiplying/dividing by a positive number, leaves the inequality symbol unchanged.

$$\text{If } a < b \text{ and } c < 0, \text{ then } ac > bc \text{ and } \frac{a}{c} > \frac{b}{c}$$

> **💡 Multiply or Divide by a Negative? Flip the Sign!**
> Notice that $2 < 5$, but multiplying both sides by $-1$ gives $-2 > -5$. Whenever the coefficient of $x$ that you divide by is negative, reverse the inequality direction immediately.

### Number Line Graphs and Compound Inequalities

The solution set of a one-variable linear inequality is an infinite interval of real numbers. On a number line, an open circle ($\circ$) at $a$ indicates that $a$ is excluded ($x < a$ or $x > a$), while a filled/closed circle ($\bullet$) indicates that $a$ is included ($x \le a$ or $x \ge a$). A three-part compound inequality $a \le mx + c < b$ is solved by performing the same inverse operations on all three parts simultaneously.

$$a \le mx + c < b \iff \frac{a - c}{m} \le x < \frac{b - c}{m} \quad (m > 0)$$

> **💡 Apply Every Operation to All Three Parts**
> When solving $-5 < 2x - 1 \le 7$, add $1$ to the left, middle, AND right parts ($-4 < 2x \le 8$), then divide all three parts by $2$ ($-2 < x \le 4$).

### Graphing Linear Inequalities in Two Variables (Half-Planes)

A linear inequality in two variables, such as $y \le 2x + 3$ or $2x + 3y > 6$, divides the Cartesian plane into two half-planes separated by the boundary line $y = 2x + 3$ or $2x + 3y = 6$. Draw the boundary line as a dashed line for strict inequalities ($<$ or $>$) or a solid line for inclusive inequalities ($\le$ or $\ge$). Then substitute a convenient test point not on the line—usually the origin $(0, 0)$—to determine which half-plane satisfies the inequality.

$$y > mx + c \text{ (above dashed line)}, \quad y \le mx + c \text{ (on or below solid line)}$$

> **💡 Test the Origin $(0, 0)$ to Pick the Correct Half-Plane**
> If the boundary line does not pass through $(0, 0)$, substitute $x = 0, y = 0$ into the inequality. If the statement is true, $(0, 0)$ lies in the required region; if false, the required region is on the opposite side of the line.

## Worked Examples

### WE10_2_1_1: Solving a Linear Inequality with a Negative Coefficient

**Problem**: Solve the inequality $7 - 3x \ge 19$ and describe its graph on a number line.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Subtract $7$ from both sides of the inequality (this does not change the inequality direction). | $$-3x \ge 19 - 7 \implies -3x \ge 12$$ |
| 2 | Divide both sides by $-3$ and reverse the inequality sign from $\ge$ to $\le$. | $$x \le \frac{12}{-3} \implies x \le -4$$ |
| 3 | Describe the number line representation. | $$\text{Closed circle at } x = -4 \text{ with an arrow pointing left.}$$ |

**Answer**: $x \le -4$ (closed circle at $-4$, arrow pointing left)

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Solve the inequality $11 - 4x < 27$.
> *Hint*: Subtract $11$ first, then divide by $-4$ and flip the $<$ sign to $>$.
> **Working**: $-4x < 16 \implies x > -4$.
> **Answer**: $x > -4$

### WE10_2_1_2: Solving a Three-Part Compound Inequality

**Problem**: Solve the compound inequality $-5 \le 2x + 3 < 11$ and list all integer values of $x$ that satisfy it.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Subtract $3$ from all three parts of the compound inequality. | $$-5 - 3 \le 2x < 11 - 3 \implies -8 \le 2x < 8$$ |
| 2 | Divide all three parts by $+2$ (positive, so the inequality signs remain in the same direction). | $$-4 \le x < 4$$ |
| 3 | List the integers from $-4$ (inclusive) up to $4$ (strictly excluded). | $$x \in \{-4, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3\}$$ |

**Answer**: $-4 \le x < 4$; integers: $-4, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3$ ($8$ integers)

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Solve the compound inequality $-7 < 3x - 1 \le 8$.
> *Hint*: Add $1$ to all three parts, then divide all three parts by $3$.
> **Working**: $-6 < 3x \le 9 \implies -2 < x \le 3$.
> **Answer**: $-2 < x \le 3$

### WE10_2_1_3: Identifying a Half-Plane Using Intercepts and a Test Point

**Problem**: Describe how to sketch the half-plane defined by $2x + 3y < 12$, including the boundary line type, axis intercepts, and whether the origin $(0, 0)$ is included in the solution region.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Find the axis intercepts of the boundary line $2x + 3y = 12$ by setting $y = 0$ and $x = 0$. | $$y = 0 \implies 2x = 12 \implies x = 6; \quad x = 0 \implies 3y = 12 \implies y = 4$$ |
| 2 | Because the inequality $<$ is strict, the boundary line through $(6, 0)$ and $(0, 4)$ is drawn dashed. | $$\text{Dashed boundary line through } (6, 0) \text{ and } (0, 4)$$ |
| 3 | Test $(0, 0)$ in $2x + 3y < 12$: $2(0) + 3(0) = 0 < 12$, which is true. | $$0 < 12 \text{ (True)} \implies \text{the half-plane containing } (0, 0) \text{ below the line is the solution region.}$$ |

**Answer**: Dashed line through $(6, 0)$ and $(0, 4)$; the region below the line containing $(0, 0)$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Find the axis intercepts of the boundary line for $4x - y \ge 8$ and determine whether $(0, 0)$ satisfies the inequality.
> *Hint*: Substitute $(0, 0)$ into $4x - y \ge 8$ to check whether $0 \ge 8$ is true or false.
> **Working**: Intercepts $(2, 0)$ and $(0, -8)$ (solid line); at $(0, 0)$, $0 \ge 8$ is false, so $(0, 0)$ is NOT in the region.
> **Answer**: Solid line through $(2, 0)$ and $(0, -8)$; $(0, 0)$ does not satisfy the inequality

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Flipping the Inequality Sign Whenever a Negative Number Appears on the Right-Hand Side
- **The Misconception**: Reversing the inequality sign when solving $2x < -10$ to get $x > -5$, simply because $-10$ is negative.
- **The Correct Concept**: The inequality sign reverses ONLY when you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number. In $2x < -10$, you divide by $+2$ (which is positive), so the sign stays $<$: $x < -5$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$2x < -10 \implies x > \frac{-10}{2} \implies x > -5 \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$2x < -10 \implies x < \frac{-10}{2} \implies x < -5 \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: Test $x = -6$ (which satisfies $x < -5$): $2(-6) = -12 < -10$ (True). Testing $x = 0$ (which satisfies $x > -5$) gives $0 < -10$ (False).

### Using a Solid Boundary Line for a Strict Inequality
- **The Misconception**: Drawing a solid boundary line for $y > 2x - 1$ instead of a dashed line.
- **The Correct Concept**: Points on the line $y = 2x - 1$ make the two sides equal, so they do NOT satisfy the strict inequality $y > 2x - 1$. A dashed line visually communicates that points on the boundary are excluded.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$y > 2x - 1 \implies \text{solid line } y = 2x - 1 \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$y > 2x - 1 \implies \text{dashed line } y = 2x - 1 \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: The point $(1, 1)$ lies on the boundary line $y = 2x - 1$, and substituting $(1, 1)$ into $y > 2x - 1$ gives $1 > 1$, which is false.

#### Question `10_2_1_Q5` [Medium] *(Budget Constraint Modelling)*
A Ballarat plumber charges a $\$90$ call-out fee plus $\$65$ per hour. If a homeowner has a maximum budget of $\$350$, write and solve a linear inequality to find the maximum number of hours $h$ the plumber can work.

#### Question `10_2_1_Q6` [Medium] *(Minimum Test Score Modelling)*
Amira scored $74$, $81$, and $79$ on her first three Year 10 Mathematics tests. What minimum score $x$ must she achieve on her fourth test to have an average of at least $80$ across all four tests?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Write the average inequality: $\frac{74 + 81 + 79 + x}{4} \ge 80$.
2. Multiply both sides by $4$: $234 + x \ge 320$.
3. Subtract $234$: $x \ge 86$.

**Final Answer**: At least $86$ ($x \ge 86$)
</details>

#### Question `10_2_1_Q7` [Medium] *(Two-Variable Revenue Inequality)*
A school drama production sells student tickets for $\$10$ ($x$) and adult tickets for $\$25$ ($y$). To cover hall hire costs, total revenue must be at least $\$500$. Write a two-variable inequality modelling this requirement and determine whether selling $18$ student tickets and $12$ adult tickets meets the target.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The revenue inequality is $10x + 25y \ge 500$ (where $x, y \ge 0$ are integers).
2. Substitute $x = 18$ and $y = 12$: $10(18) + 25(12) = 180 + 300 = 480$.
3. Since $480 < 500$, the point $(18, 12)$ does not satisfy $10x + 25y \ge 500$ ($\$20$ short of the target).

**Final Answer**: $10x + 25y \ge 500$; yes, since $10(18) + 25(12) = \$480 < \$500$ is false—actually $\$480$ is $\$20$ short, so it does NOT meet the target
</details>

#### Question `10_2_1_Q8` [Challenging] *(Area of a Bounded Region)*
A triangle in the first quadrant is bounded by $x \ge 0$, $y \ge 0$, and $3x + 4y \le 12$. Find the vertices of this triangular region and calculate its exact area.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The boundary line $3x + 4y = 12$ meets the $x$-axis ($y = 0$) at $(4, 0)$ and the $y$-axis ($x = 0$) at $(0, 3)$.
2. With $x \ge 0$ and $y \ge 0$, the region is a right-angled triangle with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(4, 0)$, and $(0, 3)$.
3. Area $= \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \frac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 3 = 6\text{ square units}$.

**Final Answer**: Vertices $(0, 0)$, $(4, 0)$, and $(0, 3)$; area $6\text{ square units}$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `10_2_1_Q9` [Challenging] *(Proof of Sign Reversal Property)*
Use subtraction to prove algebraically why $a < b$ implies $-a > -b$ for any real numbers $a$ and $b$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Start with $a < b$. Adding or subtracting any real number from both sides preserves the inequality order.
2. Subtract $a$ from both sides: $0 < b - a$.
3. Subtract $b$ from both sides: $-b < -a$, which is identical to $-a > -b$.

**Final Answer**: Subtracting $(a + b)$ from both sides of $a < b$ preserves the inequality direction and yields $-b < -a$, i.e. $-a > -b$
</details>

#### Question `10_2_1_Q10` [Medium] *(Choosing a Valid Test Point)*
Explain why the test point $(0, 0)$ cannot be used to decide which side of the boundary line $y \ge 3x$ to shade, and choose a valid alternative test point to determine the correct half-plane.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. When $x = 0$ and $y = 0$, $0 = 3(0)$, so $(0, 0)$ lies directly ON the boundary line $y = 3x$ and cannot distinguish between the two open half-planes.
2. Choose a point not on the line, such as $(1, 0)$: substituting into $y \ge 3x$ gives $0 \ge 3$ (False), so we shade the opposite side (containing $(0, 1)$, where $1 \ge 0$ is True).

**Final Answer**: $(0, 0)$ lies on the boundary line $y = 3x$; testing $(1, 0)$ gives $0 \ge 3$ (False), so shade the half-plane above/left of $y = 3x$
</details>

#### Question `10_2_1_Q11` [Challenging] *(Intersection of Two Inequalities)*
Find all values of $x$ that satisfy BOTH $3x - 5 < 10$ and $1 - 2x \le 7$, and state the number of integers in the intersection.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Solve the first inequality: $3x < 15 \implies x < 5$.
2. Solve the second inequality: $-2x \le 6 \implies x \ge -3$.
3. Combining both gives $-3 \le x < 5$, which contains the $8$ integers $\{-3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}$.

**Final Answer**: $-3 \le x < 5$ ($8$ integers)
</details>

#### Question `10_2_1_Q12` [Extension] *(Sign of Unknown Variable in Denominator)*
A student attempts to solve $\frac{6}{x} < 2$ by multiplying both sides by $x$ to write $6 < 2x \implies x > 3$. Explain why this method misses an entire interval of solutions, and state the complete solution set.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Because the sign of $x$ is unknown, multiplying by $x$ is only valid without flipping the sign when $x > 0$ (which gives $x > 3$).
2. Whenever $x < 0$, the left-hand side $\frac{6}{x}$ is negative and therefore automatically less than $+2$, so the complete solution is $x < 0$ or $x > 3$.

**Final Answer**: If $x < 0$, multiplying by $x$ reverses the inequality sign (and $\frac{6}{x}$ is negative, which is always $< 2$); complete solution is $x < 0$ or $x > 3$
</details>
