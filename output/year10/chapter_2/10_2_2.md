### Key Vocabulary
- **Simultaneous linear equations**: A system of two or more linear equations in the same variables that must be satisfied at the same time.
- **Point of intersection**: The coordinate pair $(x, y)$ where the graphs of two linear equations cross, representing the unique simultaneous solution to both equations.
- **Substitution method**: An algebraic method where one variable is expressed in terms of the other from one equation and substituted into the second equation.
- **Elimination method**: An algebraic method where equations are added or subtracted (after multiplying by suitable constants if needed) to eliminate one variable.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Graphical Interpretation and the Method of Substitution

Geometrically, each linear equation in two variables represents a straight line in the Cartesian plane. Solving a pair of simultaneous equations finds the point of intersection $(x, y)$ that lies on both lines. The substitution method is most efficient when at least one equationalready has $x$ or $y$ isolated as the subject (e.g. $y = 3x - 2$): replace that variable in the other equation with its algebraic expression to obtain a single equation in one unknown.

$$\begin{cases} y = mx + c \\ ax + by = d \end{cases} \implies ax + b(mx + c) = d$$

> **💡 Use Brackets When Substituting an Expression**
> When substituting $y = 2x - 5$ into $3x - 4y = 10$, always enclose the expression in brackets: $3x - 4(2x - 5) = 10$, so that $-4$ multiplies both $2x$ and $-5$.

### The Method of Elimination

When both simultaneous equations are written in standard form $ax + by = c$, the elimination method is usually faster. If one variable has coefficients of equal magnitude in both equations, add the equations (when the signs are opposite, such as $+3y$ and $-3y$) or subtract the equations (when the signs are the same, such as $+2x$ and $+2x$). If neither variable has matching coefficient magnitudes, multiply one or both equations by constants first to create matching coefficients.

$$\begin{cases} a_1 x + b y = c_1 \\ a_2 x - b y = c_2 \end{cases} \xrightarrow{\text{Add (1) + (2)}} (a_1 + a_2)x = c_1 + c_2$$

> **💡 Same Signs Subtract, Opposite Signs Add**
> To eliminate a variable with coefficients $+4y$ and $-4y$ (opposite signs), ADD the two equations. To eliminate $+4y$ and $+4y$ (same signs), SUBTRACT one equation from the other.

### Parallel Lines, Coincident Lines, and Break-Even Modelling

Two lines in a plane can intersect at a single point (unique solution, when gradients $m_1 \neq m_2$), be parallel and distinct (no solution, when $m_1 = m_2$ and $c_1 \neq c_2$), or be the exact same line (infinitely many solutions, when $m_1 = m_2$ and $c_1 = c_2$). In business modelling, solving the cost equation $C = F + vx$ simultaneously with the revenue equation $R = px$ finds the break-even point where revenue equals cost ($R = C$).

$$\text{Break-even: } R(x) = C(x) \iff px = F + vx \implies x = \frac{F}{p - v}$$

> **💡 Always Define Both Variables and Check Your Solution in Both Equations**
> In word problems, start by writing 'Let $x$ be ... and let $y$ be ...' with units. Once you find $(x, y)$, substitute both values into BOTH original equations to guarantee zero arithmetic errors.

## Worked Examples

### WE10_2_2_1: Solving Simultaneous Equations by Substitution

**Problem**: Solve the simultaneous equations $y = 2x - 1$ and $3x + 2y = 19$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Substitute the expression $y = 2x - 1$ from the first equation into the second equation. | $$3x + 2(2x - 1) = 19$$ |
| 2 | Expand the brackets and solve for $x$. | $$3x + 4x - 2 = 19 \implies 7x = 21 \implies x = 3$$ |
| 3 | Substitute $x = 3$ back into $y = 2x - 1$ to find $y$, and check in the second equation ($3(3) + 2(5) = 19$). | $$y = 2(3) - 1 = 5 \implies (x, y) = (3, 5)$$ |

**Answer**: $x = 3, y = 5$ or $(3, 5)$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Solve the simultaneous equations $y = 3x - 4$ and $2x + 3y = 21$.
> *Hint*: Replace $y$ with $(3x - 4)$ in the second equation: $2x + 3(3x - 4) = 21$.
> **Working**: $2x + 9x - 12 = 21 \implies 11x = 33 \implies x = 3$, and $y = 3(3) - 4 = 5$.
> **Answer**: $x = 3, y = 5$

### WE10_2_2_2: Solving Simultaneous Equations by Elimination

**Problem**: Solve the simultaneous equations $3x + 2y = 16$ and $5x - 3y = 14$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | To eliminate $y$, multiply equation (1) by $3$ and equation (2) by $2$ so that the $y$-coefficients become $+6y$ and $-6y$. | $$\begin{aligned} 9x + 6y &= 48 \quad (1') \\ 10x - 6y &= 28 \quad (2') \end{aligned}$$ |
| 2 | Since $+6y$ and $-6y$ have opposite signs, add $(1')$ and $(2')$ to eliminate $y$ and solve for $x$. | $$19x = 76 \implies x = 4$$ |
| 3 | Substitute $x = 4$ into $3x + 2y = 16$ to find $y$. | $$3(4) + 2y = 16 \implies 12 + 2y = 16 \implies 2y = 4 \implies y = 2$$ |

**Answer**: $x = 4, y = 2$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Solve the simultaneous equations $2x + 5y = 19$ and $3x - 2y = 0$.
> *Hint*: Multiply the first equation by $2$ and the second equation by $5$, then add.
> **Working**: $4x + 10y = 38$ and $15x - 10y = 0$; adding gives $19x = 38 \implies x = 2, y = 3$.
> **Answer**: $x = 2, y = 3$

### WE10_2_2_3: Modelling with Simultaneous Equations

**Problem**: At a Melbourne bakery, $3$ meat pies and $2$ coffees cost $\$23$, while $4$ meat pies and $3$ coffees cost $\$32$. Find the price of one meat pie and one coffee.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Define variables: let $p$ be the price of a meat pie ($\$$) and $c$ be the price of a coffee ($\$$). Form two simultaneous equations. | $$\begin{aligned} 3p + 2c &= 23 \quad (1) \\ 4p + 3c &= 32 \quad (2) \end{aligned}$$ |
| 2 | Multiply (1) by $3$ and (2) by $2$ so both have $+6c$, then subtract $(2')$ from $(1')$. | $$\begin{aligned} 9p + 6c &= 69 \quad (1') \\ 8p + 6c &= 64 \quad (2') \\ \hline p &= 5 \end{aligned}$$ |
| 3 | Substitute $p = 5$ into equation (1) to find $c$. | $$3(5) + 2c = 23 \implies 2c = 8 \implies c = 4$$ |

**Answer**: Meat pie: $\$5$; coffee: $\$4$

> **✏️ Now You Try**

> **Working**: $6a + 9c = 207$ and $6a + 4c = 152 \implies 5c = 55 \implies c = 11$, so $a = 18$.

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Adding Instead of Subtracting When Eliminating Terms with the Same Sign
- **The Misconception**: Adding $2x + 3y = 13$ and $5x + 3y = 22$ to get $7x = 35$, forgetting that $+3y + (+3y) = +6y \neq 0$.
- **The Correct Concept**: When the coefficients of the variable to be eliminated have the SAME sign (both $+3y$ or both $-3y$), you must SUBTRACT one equation from the other so that $3y - 3y = 0$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$(2x + 3y) + (5x + 3y) = 13 + 22 \implies 7x = 35 \quad (\text{Incorrect: } 3y + 3y = 6y, \text{ not } 0)$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$(5x + 3y) - (2x + 3y) = 22 - 13 \implies 3x = 9 \implies x = 3, y = 7/3 \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: If $x = 5$ (from the incorrect addition), $2(5) + 3y = 13 \implies y = 1$, which fails in the second equation: $5(5) + 3(1) = 28 \neq 22$.

### Forgetting to Multiply the Right-Hand Side Constant When Scaling an Equation
- **The Misconception**: Multiplying the left-hand side of $2x + 3y = 7$ by $4$ to get $8x + 12y = 7$ instead of $8x + 12y = 28$.
- **The Correct Concept**: To preserve equality, every term on BOTH sides of the equation—including the constant on the right-hand side—must be multiplied by the scaling factor.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$2x + 3y = 7 \xrightarrow{\times 4} 8x + 12y = 7 \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$2x + 3y = 7 \xrightarrow{\times 4} 8x + 12y = 28 \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: The point $(2, 1)$ satisfies $2(2) + 3(1) = 7$ and $8(2) + 12(1) = 28$, not $7$.

### Fluency & Understanding

#### Question `10_2_2_Q1` [Easy] *(Substitution Method)*
Solve the simultaneous equations $y = 4x - 7$ and $2x + y = 11$ using substitution.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute $y = 4x - 7$ into $2x + y = 11$: $2x + (4x - 7) = 11 \implies 6x = 18 \implies x = 3$.
2. Substitute $x = 3$ into $y = 4x - 7$: $y = 4(3) - 7 = 5$.

**Final Answer**: $x = 3, y = 5$
</details>

#### Question `10_2_2_Q2` [Easy] *(Elimination by Addition)*
Solve the simultaneous equations $4x + 3y = 18$ and $2x - 3y = 0$ using elimination.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Add the two equations to eliminate $y$: $(4x + 2x) + (3y - 3y) = 18 + 0 \implies 6x = 18 \implies x = 3$.
2. Substitute $x = 3$ into $2x - 3y = 0$: $6 - 3y = 0 \implies y = 2$.

**Final Answer**: $x = 3, y = 2$
</details>

#### Question `10_2_2_Q3` [Easy] *(Elimination by Subtraction)*
Solve the simultaneous equations $5x + 2y = 11$ and $3x + 2y = 9$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Subtract the second equation from the first: $(5x - 3x) + (2y - 2y) = 11 - 9 \implies 2x = 2 \implies x = 1$.
2. Substitute $x = 1$ into $3x + 2y = 9$: $3 + 2y = 9 \implies 2y = 6 \implies y = 3$.

**Final Answer**: $x = 1, y = 3$
</details>

#### Question `10_2_2_Q4` [Medium] *(Scaling Both Equations)*
Solve the simultaneous equations $4x - 3y = 18$ and $3x + 5y = -1$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Multiply the first equation by $5$ ($20x - 15y = 90$) and the second equation by $3$ ($9x + 15y = -3$).
2. Add the scaled equations: $29x = 87 \implies x = 3$.
3. Substitute $x = 3$ into $3x + 5y = -1$: $9 + 5y = -1 \implies 5y = -10 \implies y = -2$.

**Final Answer**: $x = 3, y = -2$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `10_2_2_Q5` [Medium] *(Break-Even Analysis)*
A Bendigo craftsperson makes wooden chopping boards. Setup costs are $\$360$ plus $\$12$ in timber per board ($C = 12n + 360$). Each board sells for $\$30$ ($R = 30n$). Find the break-even number of boards $n$ and the revenue at the break-even point.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. At break-even, $R = C$: $30n = 12n + 360$.
2. Subtract $12n$: $18n = 360 \implies n = 20\text{ boards}$.
3. Break-even revenue $= 30 \times 20 = \$600$.

**Final Answer**: $n = 20\text{ boards}$; break-even revenue $\$600$
</details>

#### Question `10_2_2_Q6` [Medium] *(Geometric Perimeter Modelling)*
A rectangle has a perimeter of $46\text{ cm}$. Its length $L$ is $5\text{ cm}$ greater than twice its width $W$. Set up and solve a pair of simultaneous equations to find $L$ and $W$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. From the perimeter, $2L + 2W = 46 \implies L + W = 23$. From the length relation, $L = 2W + 5$.
2. Substitute $L = 2W + 5$ into $L + W = 23$: $(2W + 5) + W = 23 \implies 3W = 18 \implies W = 6\text{ cm}$.
3. Then $L = 2(6) + 5 = 17\text{ cm}$.

**Final Answer**: Width $W = 6\text{ cm}$, length $L = 17\text{ cm}$
</details>

#### Question `10_2_2_Q7` [Challenging] *(Chemical Mixture Problem)*
A chemist needs $100\text{ mL}$ of a $28\%$ acid solution by mixing $x\text{ mL}$ of a $20\%$ acid solution with $y\text{ mL}$ of a $40\%$ acid solution. Write and solve simultaneous equations to find $x$ and $y$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Total volume equation: $x + y = 100$. Pure acid volume equation: $0.20x + 0.40y = 0.28(100) = 28$ (or $2x + 4y = 280$).
2. Substitute $y = 100 - x$ into $2x + 4y = 280$: $2x + 4(100 - x) = 280 \implies -2x = -120 \implies x = 60\text{ mL}$.
3. Then $y = 100 - 60 = 40\text{ mL}$.

**Final Answer**: $x = 60\text{ mL}$ of $20\%$ solution and $y = 40\text{ mL}$ of $40\%$ solution
</details>

#### Question `10_2_2_Q8` [Challenging] *(Upstream and Downstream Kinematics)*
A river boat on the Murray River travels $36\text{ km}$ downstream in $2\text{ hours}$ and returns the same $36\text{ km}$ upstream in $3\text{ hours}$. Find the speed of the boat in still water ($b\text{ km/h}$) and the speed of the river current ($c\text{ km/h}$).

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Downstream speed is $b + c = \frac{36}{2} = 18\text{ km/h}$; upstream speed is $b - c = \frac{36}{3} = 12\text{ km/h}$.
2. Add the two equations: $2b = 30 \implies b = 15\text{ km/h}$.
3. Substitute $b = 15$: $15 + c = 18 \implies c = 3\text{ km/h}$.

**Final Answer**: Boat speed $15\text{ km/h}$; current speed $3\text{ km/h}$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `10_2_2_Q9` [Medium] *(Inconsistent Parallel System)*
Explain both algebraically and geometrically why the simultaneous system $2x - y = 4$ and $4x - 2y = 10$ has no solution.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Algebraically, multiplying $2x - y = 4$ by $2$ gives $4x - 2y = 8$, which contradicts $4x - 2y = 10$ (subtracting gives $0 = -2$, a contradiction).
2. Geometrically, rearranging into gradient-intercept form gives $y = 2x - 4$ and $y = 2x - 5$: parallel distinct lines with gradient $2$ that never intersect.

**Final Answer**: Multiplying the first equation by $2$ gives $4x - 2y = 8 \neq 10$; geometrically both lines have gradient $m = 2$ but different $y$-intercepts ($-4$ and $-5$), so they are parallel and never intersect
</details>

#### Question `10_2_2_Q10` [Challenging] *(Coincident Lines Parameter)*
For the system $3x + ky = 6$ and $6x + 8y = 12$, find the value of $k$ for which the system has infinitely many solutions, and explain what happens geometrically for this value of $k$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Notice that the $x$-coefficient and constant in $6x + 8y = 12$ are exactly twice those in $3x + ky = 6$.
2. For the two equations to be identical multiples of each other, $2k = 8 \implies k = 4$, making the two lines coincident so every point on the line is a solution.

**Final Answer**: $k = 4$; the two equations represent the exact same (coincident) straight line
</details>

#### Question `10_2_2_Q11` [Challenging] *(Symmetric Linear System Shortcut)*
Without solving for $x$ and $y$ individually, find the value of $x + y$ given that $7x + 5y = 31$ and $5x + 7y = 17$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Add the two equations directly: $(7x + 5y) + (5x + 7y) = 31 + 17 \implies 12x + 12y = 48$.
2. Factor out $12$ and divide both sides by $12$: $12(x + y) = 48 \implies x + y = 4$.

**Final Answer**: $x + y = 4$
</details>

#### Question `10_2_2_Q12` [Extension] *(General Sum and Difference System)*
Prove that for any constants $p$ and $q$, the system $x + y = p$ and $x - y = q$ always has the unique solution $x = \frac{p + q}{2}$ and $y = \frac{p - q}{2}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Add the two equations: $(x + y) + (x - y) = p + q \implies 2x = p + q \implies x = \frac{p + q}{2}$.
2. Subtract the second equation from the first: $(x + y) - (x - y) = p - q \implies 2y = p - q \implies y = \frac{p - q}{2}$.

**Final Answer**: Adding gives $2x = p + q \implies x = \frac{p+q}{2}$; subtracting gives $2y = p - q \implies y = \frac{p-q}{2}$
</details>
