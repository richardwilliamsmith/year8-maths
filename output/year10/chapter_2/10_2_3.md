**VCAA Version 2.0 Alignment**: `VC2M10A03` (Algebra)
> Recognise the connection between algebraic and graphical representations of relations including parallel and perpendicular lines.

**Target Mastery**: Use m1 = m2 for parallel lines and m1 * m2 = -1 for perpendicular lines to find equations of straight lines and perpendicular bisectors.

**Prerequisite Skills**:
- Gradient, midpoint, and distance formulas (Year 9 VC2M9A03)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Parallel lines**: Distinct coplanar straight lines that never intersect; non-vertical parallel lines have the exact same gradient ($m_1 = m_2$).
- **Perpendicular lines**: Two straight lines that intersect at a right angle ($90^\circ$); for non-vertical, non-horizontal perpendicular lines, the product of their gradients is $-1$ ($m_1 m_2 = -1$).
- **Negative reciprocal**: The number obtained by flipping a fraction and reversing its sign: the negative reciprocal of $\frac{a}{b}$ is $-\frac{b}{a}$, so that $\frac{a}{b} \times \left(-\frac{b}{a}\right) = -1$.
- **Perpendicular bisector**: The straight line that passes through the midpoint of a line segment $AB$ at a $90^\circ$ angle to $AB$; every point on the perpendicular bisector is equidistant from $A$ and $B$.

---

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Parallel Lines and Point-Gradient Form

Two non-vertical straight lines $y = m_1 x + c_1$ and $y = m_2 x + c_2$ are parallel if and only if they rise at the exact same rate, meaning $m_1 = m_2$ (with $c_1 \neq c_2$ for distinct lines). Once you know the gradient $m$ of a line and a point $(x_1, y_1)$ on the line, you can write its equation immediately using the point-gradient formula $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.

$$L_1 \parallel L_2 \iff m_1 = m_2; \quad y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$$

> **💡 Rearrange to $y = mx + c$ Before Reading the Gradient**
> If a line is given in standard form such as $2x + 5y = 10$, do NOT say the gradient is $2$! Rearrange to make $y$ the subject first: $y = -\frac{2}{5}x + 2$, so $m = -\frac{2}{5}$.

### Perpendicular Lines and Negative Reciprocal Gradients

If a line has gradient $m_1 = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{b}{a}$, rotating its gradient triangle through $90^\circ$ swaps the horizontal run and vertical rise and reverses the direction of one of them, giving gradient $m_2 = -\frac{a}{b}$. Therefore, two non-vertical, non-horizontal lines are perpendicular if and only if the product of their gradients is $-1$, or equivalently $m_2 = -\frac{1}{m_1}$. Note that any horizontal line ($y = k$, gradient $0$) is perpendicular to any vertical line ($x = h$, undefined gradient).

$$L_1 \perp L_2 \iff m_1 m_2 = -1 \iff m_2 = -\frac{1}{m_1}$$

> **💡 Flip the Fraction and Flip the Sign**
> To find the perpendicular gradient $m_{\perp}$: if $m = \frac{3}{4}$, then $m_{\perp} = -\frac{4}{3}$; if $m = -5 = -\frac{5}{1}$, then $m_{\perp} = +\frac{1}{5}$.

### Finding the Equation of a Perpendicular Bisector

The perpendicular bisector of the line segment joining $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$ is found in three systematic steps: (1) find the midpoint $M$ of $AB$, (2) calculate the gradient $m_{AB}$ of $AB$ and take its negative reciprocal $m_{\perp} = -\frac{1}{m_{AB}}$, and (3) substitute $m_{\perp}$ and the coordinates of $M$ into the point-gradient formula $y - y_M = m_{\perp}(x - x_M)$.

$$M = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2},\, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right), \quad m_{AB} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}, \quad m_{\perp} = -\frac{1}{m_{AB}}$$

> **💡 Use the Midpoint $M$, Not Endpoint $A$ or $B$**
> A perpendicular bisector passes through the MIDPOINT $M$ of segment $AB$, so substitute $M(x_M, y_M)$ into $y - y_1 = m_{\perp}(x - x_1)$.

## Worked Examples

### WE10_2_3_1: Finding the Equation of a Parallel Line

**Problem**: Find the equation of the straight line passing through $(3, -2)$ that is parallel to $4x - 2y = 9$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Rearrange $4x - 2y = 9$ into gradient-intercept form $y = mx + c$ to find its gradient. | $$2y = 4x - 9 \implies y = 2x - \frac{9}{2} \implies m_1 = 2$$ |
| 2 | Since the required line is parallel, its gradient is $m_2 = m_1 = 2$. Substitute $m = 2$ and $(3, -2)$ into $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$. | $$y - (-2) = 2(x - 3) \implies y + 2 = 2x - 6 \implies y = 2x - 8$$ |

**Answer**: $y = 2x - 8$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Find the equation of the straight line passing through $(-2, 5)$ that is parallel to $6x + 2y = 7$.
> *Hint*: Rearranging $6x + 2y = 7$ gives $y = -3x + \frac{7}{2}$, so $m = -3$.
> **Working**: $y - 5 = -3(x - (-2)) = -3x - 6 \implies y = -3x - 1$.
> **Answer**: $y = -3x - 1$

### WE10_2_3_2: Finding the Equation of a Perpendicular Line

**Problem**: Find the equation of the line passing through $(6, 1)$ that is perpendicular to $y = -\frac{3}{2}x + 4$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Identify the gradient of the given line $m_1 = -\frac{3}{2}$ and take its negative reciprocal to find $m_{\perp}$. | $$m_{\perp} = -\frac{1}{-3/2} = \frac{2}{3}$$ |
| 2 | Substitute $m_{\perp} = \frac{2}{3}$ and $(x_1, y_1) = (6, 1)$ into $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$ and simplify. | $$y - 1 = \frac{2}{3}(x - 6) \implies y - 1 = \frac{2}{3}x - 4 \implies y = \frac{2}{3}x - 3$$ |

**Answer**: $y = \frac{2}{3}x - 3$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Find the equation of the line passing through $(4, -1)$ that is perpendicular to $y = \frac{2}{5}x - 3$.
> *Hint*: The negative reciprocal of $\frac{2}{5}$ is $-\frac{5}{2}$.
> **Working**: $y - (-1) = -\frac{5}{2}(x - 4) = -\frac{5}{2}x + 10 \implies y = -\frac{5}{2}x + 9$.
> **Answer**: $y = -\frac{5}{2}x + 9$

### WE10_2_3_3: Finding the Perpendicular Bisector of a Line Segment

**Problem**: Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of the line segment joining $A(1, 2)$ and $B(5, 10)$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Calculate the midpoint $M$ of $A(1, 2)$ and $B(5, 10)$. | $$M = \left(\frac{1 + 5}{2},\, \frac{2 + 10}{2}\right) = (3, 6)$$ |
| 2 | Find the gradient of segment $AB$ and then the perpendicular gradient $m_{\perp}$. | $$m_{AB} = \frac{10 - 2}{5 - 1} = \frac{8}{4} = 2 \implies m_{\perp} = -\frac{1}{2}$$ |
| 3 | Substitute $m_{\perp} = -\frac{1}{2}$ and the midpoint $M(3, 6)$ into $y - y_M = m_{\perp}(x - x_M)$. | $$y - 6 = -\frac{1}{2}(x - 3) \implies y = -\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{3}{2} + 6 = -\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{15}{2}$$ |

**Answer**: $y = -\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{15}{2}$ (or $x + 2y = 15$)

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of the line segment joining $P(2, 1)$ and $Q(6, 5)$.
> *Hint*: Find the midpoint $M(4, 3)$ and the gradient $m_{PQ} = 1$, so $m_{\perp} = -1$.
> **Working**: $M = (4, 3)$, $m_{PQ} = \frac{5 - 1}{6 - 2} = 1 \implies m_{\perp} = -1$; $y - 3 = -1(x - 4) \implies y = -x + 7$.
> **Answer**: $y = -x + 7$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Taking the Reciprocal Without Flipping the Sign for Perpendicular Gradients
- **The Misconception**: Saying that a line perpendicular to $y = \frac{2}{3}x + 1$ has gradient $+\frac{3}{2}$ instead of $-\frac{3}{2}$.
- **The Correct Concept**: Two positive gradients both slope upwards from left to right and can never meet at $90^\circ$. Perpendicular gradients must have opposite signs so that $m_1 m_2 = -1$ ($\frac{2}{3} \times \left(-\frac{3}{2}\right) = -1$).
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$m_1 = \frac{2}{3} \implies m_{\perp} = \frac{3}{2} \quad (\text{Incorrect: product is } +1, \text{ not } -1)$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$m_1 = \frac{2}{3} \implies m_{\perp} = -\frac{3}{2} \quad (\text{Correct: product is } -1)$$
- **Counter-Example**: The lines $y = x$ ($m = 1$) and $y = -x$ ($m = -1$) bisect the quadrants at $90^\circ$, and $1 \times (-1) = -1$.

### Reading the Coefficient of $x$ in $ax + by = c$ as the Gradient
- **The Misconception**: Assuming the gradient of $3x + 4y = 12$ is $m = 3$.
- **The Correct Concept**: The coefficient of $x$ is the gradient ONLY when the equation is solved for $y$ in the form $y = mx + c$. Rearranging $3x + 4y = 12$ gives $4y = -3x + 12 \implies y = -\frac{3}{4}x + 3$, so $m = -\frac{3}{4}$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$3x + 4y = 12 \implies m = 3 \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$3x + 4y = 12 \implies y = -\frac{3}{4}x + 3 \implies m = -\frac{3}{4} \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: The line $3x + 4y = 12$ passes through $(0, 3)$ and $(4, 0)$, giving gradient $\frac{0 - 3}{4 - 0} = -\frac{3}{4} \neq 3$.

### Fluency & Understanding

#### Question `10_2_3_Q1` [Easy] *(Parallel Line Equation)*
Find the equation of the line passing through $(2, 7)$ that is parallel to $y = 3x - 4$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The parallel gradient is $m = 3$.
2. Substitute $m = 3$ and $(2, 7)$ into $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$: $y - 7 = 3(x - 2) \implies y = 3x + 1$.

**Final Answer**: $y = 3x + 1$
</details>

#### Question `10_2_3_Q2` [Easy] *(Perpendicular Line Equation)*
Find the equation of the line passing through $(-4, 3)$ that is perpendicular to $y = 2x + 5$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The gradient of $y = 2x + 5$ is $m_1 = 2$, so $m_{\perp} = -\frac{1}{2}$.
2. Substitute $m_{\perp} = -\frac{1}{2}$ and $(-4, 3)$: $y - 3 = -\frac{1}{2}(x + 4) = -\frac{1}{2}x - 2 \implies y = -\frac{1}{2}x + 1$.

**Final Answer**: $y = -\frac{1}{2}x + 1$
</details>

#### Question `10_2_3_Q3` [Medium] *(Classifying Line Pairs)*
Determine whether the lines $2x - 3y = 6$ and $6x + 4y = 12$ are parallel, perpendicular, or neither.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Rearrange the first line: $3y = 2x - 6 \implies y = \frac{2}{3}x - 2$, so $m_1 = \frac{2}{3}$.
2. Rearrange the second line: $4y = -6x + 12 \implies y = -\frac{3}{2}x + 3$, so $m_2 = -\frac{3}{2}$.
3. Since $m_1 m_2 = \frac{2}{3} \times \left(-\frac{3}{2}\right) = -1$, the lines are perpendicular.

**Final Answer**: Perpendicular ($m_1 = \frac{2}{3}$, $m_2 = -\frac{3}{2}$, and $m_1 m_2 = -1$)
</details>

#### Question `10_2_3_Q4` [Medium] *(Unknown Coordinate from Perpendicularity)*
Find the value of $k$ if the line joining $(1, 3)$ and $(4, k)$ is perpendicular to a line with gradient $-\frac{3}{5}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The gradient perpendicular to $-\frac{3}{5}$ is $m_{\perp} = \frac{5}{3}$.
2. Set the gradient between $(1, 3)$ and $(4, k)$ equal to $\frac{5}{3}$: $\frac{k - 3}{4 - 1} = \frac{5}{3} \implies \frac{k - 3}{3} = \frac{5}{3} \implies k = 8$.

**Final Answer**: $k = 8$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `10_2_3_Q5` [Medium] *(Voronoi Boundary / Perpendicular Bisector)*
Two emergency mobile towers are located at $A(-2, 4)$ and $B(4, -2)$ on a Cartesian grid (in kilometres). The boundary line where mobile phones switch from Tower A to Tower B is the perpendicular bisector of $AB$. Find its equation.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find the midpoint $M$ of $A(-2, 4)$ and $B(4, -2)$: $M = \left(\frac{-2 + 4}{2}, \frac{4 - 2}{2}\right) = (1, 1)$.
2. Find $m_{AB} = \frac{-2 - 4}{4 - (-2)} = \frac{-6}{6} = -1$, so $m_{\perp} = -\frac{1}{-1} = 1$.
3. Substitute $m_{\perp} = 1$ and $M(1, 1)$: $y - 1 = 1(x - 1) \implies y = x$.

**Final Answer**: $y = x$
</details>

#### Question `10_2_3_Q6` [Medium] *(Proving a Right Angle via Gradients)*
Triangle $ABC$ has vertices $A(1, 1)$, $B(4, 5)$, and $C(9, 2)$. By calculating the gradients of $AB$ and $BC$, determine whether $\triangle ABC$ is a right-angled triangle at $B$. Wait—let us check $C(8, 2)$: for $A(1, 1)$, $B(4, 5)$, and $C(8, 2)$, calculate $m_{AB}$ and $m_{BC}$ and prove that $\angle ABC = 90^\circ$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate the gradient of $AB$: $m_{AB} = \frac{5 - 1}{4 - 1} = \frac{4}{3}$.
2. Calculate the gradient of $BC$: $m_{BC} = \frac{2 - 5}{8 - 4} = -\frac{3}{4}$.
3. Multiply the gradients: $m_{AB} \times m_{BC} = \frac{4}{3} \times \left(-\frac{3}{4}\right) = -1$, proving $AB \perp BC$ and $\angle ABC = 90^\circ$.

**Final Answer**: $m_{AB} = \frac{4}{3}$ and $m_{BC} = -\frac{3}{4}$; since $m_{AB} \times m_{BC} = -1$, $AB \perp BC$ and $\angle ABC = 90^\circ$
</details>

#### Question `10_2_3_Q7` [Challenging] *(Shortest Distance from a Point to a Line)*
A gas pipeline follows the line $y = 2x + 1$. A farmhouse at $F(5, 1)$ needs to be connected to the pipeline by the shortest possible straight pipe. Find: (a) the equation of the connecting pipe, and (b) the coordinates of the junction point $J$ on the pipeline.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The shortest path from a point to a line is perpendicular to the line, so the connecting pipe has gradient $m_{\perp} = -\frac{1}{2}$.
2. Using $F(5, 1)$: $y - 1 = -\frac{1}{2}(x - 5) \implies y = -\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{7}{2}$.
3. Solve simultaneously with $y = 2x + 1$: $2x + 1 = -\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{7}{2} \implies \frac{5}{2}x = \frac{5}{2} \implies x = 1$, and $y = 2(1) + 1 = 3$, so $J = (1, 3)$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $y = -\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{7}{2}$, (b) $J(1, 3)$
</details>

#### Question `10_2_3_Q8` [Challenging] *(Exact Perpendicular Distance)*
Using the junction point $J(1, 3)$ from Question `10_2_3_Q7`, calculate the exact shortest distance from the farmhouse $F(5, 1)$ to the pipeline $y = 2x + 1$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Apply the distance formula between $F(5, 1)$ and $J(1, 3)$: $d = \sqrt{(5 - 1)^2 + (1 - 3)^2}$.
2. $d = \sqrt{16 + 4} = \sqrt{20} = 2\sqrt{5}$ units.

**Final Answer**: $2\sqrt{5}$ units
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `10_2_3_Q9` [Medium] *(Horizontal and Vertical Perpendicularity)*
Explain why the condition $m_1 m_2 = -1$ cannot be used to test whether the lines $x = 4$ and $y = -2$ are perpendicular, and explain why they are nevertheless perpendicular.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The horizontal line $y = -2$ has gradient $m_1 = 0$, while the vertical line $x = 4$ has an undefined gradient (division by zero).
2. Because $m_2$ is undefined, the product $m_1 m_2$ is not defined, but geometrically any horizontal line (parallel to the $x$-axis) and vertical line (parallel to the $y$-axis) meet at $90^\circ$.

**Final Answer**: $y = -2$ has gradient $0$ and $x = 4$ has an undefined gradient, so $m_1 m_2$ cannot be evaluated; however, a vertical line and a horizontal line always intersect at $90^\circ$ at $(4, -2)$
</details>

#### Question `10_2_3_Q10` [Medium] *(Coordinate Proof of a Parallelogram)*
Four points in the plane are $A(1, 2)$, $B(5, 4)$, $C(7, 8)$, and $D(3, 6)$. Use gradients to prove that $ABCD$ is a parallelogram.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate the gradients of opposite sides $AB$ and $DC$: $m_{AB} = \frac{4 - 2}{5 - 1} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$ and $m_{DC} = \frac{8 - 6}{7 - 3} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$, so $AB \parallel DC$.
2. Calculate the gradients of opposite sides $BC$ and $AD$: $m_{BC} = \frac{8 - 4}{7 - 5} = \frac{4}{2} = 2$ and $m_{AD} = \frac{6 - 2}{3 - 1} = \frac{4}{2} = 2$, so $BC \parallel AD$.
3. Since both pairs of opposite sides are parallel, $ABCD$ is a parallelogram.

**Final Answer**: $m_{AB} = m_{DC} = \frac{1}{2}$ and $m_{BC} = m_{AD} = 2$; since both pairs of opposite sides are parallel, $ABCD$ is a parallelogram
</details>

#### Question `10_2_3_Q11` [Challenging] *(Perpendicular Diagonals of a Rhombus)*
For the parallelogram $ABCD$ in Question `10_2_3_Q10` with $A(1, 2)$, $B(5, 4)$, $C(7, 8)$, and $D(3, 6)$, find the gradients of the diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ and prove that the diagonals are perpendicular (showing $ABCD$ is a rhombus).

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Gradient of diagonal $AC$: $m_{AC} = \frac{8 - 2}{7 - 1} = \frac{6}{6} = 1$.
2. Gradient of diagonal $BD$: $m_{BD} = \frac{6 - 4}{3 - 5} = \frac{2}{-2} = -1$.
3. Since $m_{AC} \times m_{BD} = 1 \times (-1) = -1$, the diagonals intersect at $90^\circ$, proving $ABCD$ is a rhombus.

**Final Answer**: $m_{AC} = 1$ and $m_{BD} = -1$; since $m_{AC} \times m_{BD} = -1$, $AC \perp BD$ and $ABCD$ is a rhombus
</details>

#### Question `10_2_3_Q12` [Extension] *(General Standard-Form Perpendicularity Proof)*
Prove algebraically that for any non-zero constants $a$ and $b$, the straight lines $ax + by = c_1$ and $bx - ay = c_2$ are always perpendicular.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Rearrange $ax + by = c_1$ to solve for $y$: $y = -\frac{a}{b}x + \frac{c_1}{b}$, which has gradient $m_1 = -\frac{a}{b}$.
2. Rearrange $bx - ay = c_2$ to solve for $y$: $y = \frac{b}{a}x - \frac{c_2}{a}$, which has gradient $m_2 = \frac{b}{a}$.
3. Multiply the gradients: $m_1 m_2 = \left(-\frac{a}{b}\right)\left(\frac{b}{a}\right) = -1$, proving the lines are always perpendicular.

**Final Answer**: Their gradients are $m_1 = -\frac{a}{b}$ and $m_2 = \frac{b}{a}$, whose product is $m_1 m_2 = -1$
</details>
