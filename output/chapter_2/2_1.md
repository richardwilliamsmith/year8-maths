### Key Vocabulary
- **Rational number**: Any number that can be written in the form $\frac{a}{b}$, where $a$ and $b$ are integers and $b \neq 0$. Denoted by $\mathbb{Q}$.
- **Lowest Common Denominator (LCD)**: The Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) of the denominators of two or more fractions.
- **Reciprocal (multiplicative inverse)**: For any non-zero fraction $\frac{a}{b}$, its reciprocal is $\frac{b}{a}$ such that $\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{b}{a} = 1$. A number and its reciprocal always share the same sign.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Negative Signs in Fractions and Mixed Numerals

A negative rational number can be written with the negative sign in front of the fraction, in the numerator, or in the denominator: $-\frac{a}{b} = \frac{-a}{b} = \frac{a}{-b}$. Standard mathematical convention places the negative sign either in front of the fraction or in the numerator. For a negative mixed numeral such as $-2\frac{3}{5}$, the negative sign applies to the entire quantity: $-2\frac{3}{5} = -\left(2 + \frac{3}{5}\right) = -\frac{13}{5}$.

$$-a\frac{b}{c} = -\left(a + \frac{b}{c}\right) = -\frac{ac + b}{c}$$

> **💡 Converting Negative Mixed Numerals**
> When converting $-2\frac{3}{5}$ to an improper fraction, ignore the negative sign first ($2 \times 5 + 3 = 13$), write $\frac{13}{5}$, and then place the negative sign in front: $-\frac{13}{5}$.

### Adding and Subtracting Signed Fractions

To add or subtract positive and negative fractions, first convert any mixed numerals into improper fractions and simplify adjacent signs. Next, find the Lowest Common Denominator (LCD) using $\text{LCM}(b, d)$, rewrite each fraction as an equivalent fraction over the LCD, and combine the signed numerators using integer addition and subtraction rules.

$$\frac{a}{b} + \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ad + bc}{bd}$$

> **💡 Put Negative Signs in the Numerator**
> Once both fractions share a positive common denominator, attach each fraction's sign to its numerator so you simply add or subtract integers across the top.

### Multiplying Signed Fractions

To multiply signed fractions, convert any mixed numerals to improper fractions first. Determine the sign of the product using the integer sign rules (same signs give $+$, different signs give $-$), cancel any common factors between numerators and denominators, and multiply the remaining numerators and denominators.

$$\left(-\frac{a}{b}\right) \times \left(-\frac{c}{d}\right) = +\frac{ac}{bd}, \quad \left(-\frac{a}{b}\right) \times \frac{c}{d} = -\frac{ac}{bd}$$

> **💡 Cross-Cancel Before Multiplying**
> Divide out common factors diagonally or vertically before multiplying numerators and denominators to keep the numbers small and avoid simplifying large fractions at the end.

### Dividing Signed Fractions Using the Reciprocal

Dividing by a non-zero fraction is equivalent to multiplying by its reciprocal. Keep the first fraction unchanged, change the division sign ($\div$) to multiplication ($\times$), and invert the second fraction (keeping its negative sign if it is negative).

$$\frac{a}{b} \div \left(-\frac{c}{d}\right) = \frac{a}{b} \times \left(-\frac{d}{c}\right) = -\frac{ad}{bc}$$

> **💡 Keep, Change, Flip (Preserve the Sign)**
> The reciprocal of $-\frac{3}{4}$ is $-\frac{4}{3}$, NOT $+\frac{4}{3}$. Taking a reciprocal flips the numerator and denominator but never changes the sign.

## Worked Examples

### WE2_1_1: Adding and Subtracting Signed Fractions

**Problem**: Evaluate $-\dfrac{5}{6} + \dfrac{3}{8}$ in simplest form.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Find the Lowest Common Denominator of $6$ and $8$: $\text{LCM}(6, 8) = 24$. | $$\text{LCD} = \text{LCM}(6, 8) = 24$$ |
| 2 | Convert both fractions to equivalent fractions with denominator $24$, placing the negative sign in the numerator of the first fraction. | $$-\frac{5}{6} + \frac{3}{8} = \frac{-5 \times 4}{24} + \frac{3 \times 3}{24} = \frac{-20}{24} + \frac{9}{24}$$ |
| 3 | Add the signed numerators $-20 + 9 = -11$ over the common denominator $24$. | $$= \frac{-20 + 9}{24} = -\frac{11}{24}$$ |

**Answer**: $-\dfrac{11}{24}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Evaluate $-\dfrac{7}{10} + \dfrac{3}{4}$ in simplest form.
> *Hint*: Use $\text{LCD} = \text{LCM}(10, 4) = 20$.
> **Working**: $\frac{-14}{20} + \frac{15}{20} = \frac{-14 + 15}{20} = \frac{1}{20}$.
> **Answer**: $\dfrac{1}{20}$

### WE2_1_2: Subtracting Negative Mixed Numerals

**Problem**: Evaluate $-1\dfrac{3}{4} - \left(-2\dfrac{5}{6}\right)$ as a mixed numeral in simplest form.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Simplify the two adjacent minus signs $-(-2\frac{5}{6})$ into $+2\frac{5}{6}$, and convert both mixed numerals to improper fractions. | $$-1\frac{3}{4} + 2\frac{5}{6} = -\frac{7}{4} + \frac{17}{6}$$ |
| 2 | Rewrite both fractions over the lowest common denominator $\text{LCM}(4, 6) = 12$. | $$= \frac{-21}{12} + \frac{34}{12} = \frac{-21 + 34}{12}$$ |
| 3 | Evaluate the numerator $-21 + 34 = 13$ and convert $\frac{13}{12}$ back to a mixed numeral. | $$= \frac{13}{12} = 1\frac{1}{12}$$ |

**Answer**: $1\dfrac{1}{12}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Evaluate $-2\dfrac{2}{3} - \left(-1\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$ as a mixed numeral in simplest form.
> *Hint*: Convert to improper fractions: $-\frac{8}{3} + \frac{3}{2}$.
> **Working**: $-\frac{8}{3} + \frac{3}{2} = \frac{-16 + 9}{6} = -\frac{7}{6} = -1\frac{1}{6}$.
> **Answer**: $-1\dfrac{1}{6}$

### WE2_1_3: Multiplying and Dividing Signed Mixed Numerals

**Problem**: Evaluate $-2\dfrac{1}{4} \div \left(-1\dfrac{7}{8}\right)$ in simplest form.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Convert both mixed numerals into improper fractions: $-2\frac{1}{4} = -\frac{9}{4}$ and $-1\frac{7}{8} = -\frac{15}{8}$. | $$-2\frac{1}{4} \div \left(-1\frac{7}{8}\right) = -\frac{9}{4} \div \left(-\frac{15}{8}\right)$$ |
| 2 | Since both fractions are negative, the quotient is positive. Multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor. | $$= +\left(\frac{9}{4} \times \frac{8}{15}\right)$$ |
| 3 | Cross-cancel common factors: divide $9$ and $15$ by $3$, and divide $8$ and $4$ by $4$. | $$= \frac{3}{1} \times \frac{2}{5} = \frac{6}{5} = 1\frac{1}{5}$$ |

**Answer**: $\dfrac{6}{5}$ (or $1\dfrac{1}{5}$)

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Evaluate $-3\dfrac{1}{3} \div 2\dfrac{1}{2}$ in simplest form.
> *Hint*: Write as $-\frac{10}{3} \div \frac{5}{2} = -\frac{10}{3} \times \frac{2}{5}$.
> **Working**: $-\frac{10}{3} \times \frac{2}{5} = -\frac{2 \times 2}{3 \times 1} = -\frac{4}{3} = -1\frac{1}{3}$.
> **Answer**: $-\dfrac{4}{3}$ (or $-1\dfrac{1}{3}$)

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Misinterpreting a Negative Mixed Numeral $-a\frac{b}{c}$ as $-a + \frac{b}{c}$
- **The Misconception**: When converting $-3\frac{1}{4}$ to an improper fraction, students sometimes compute $(-3) \times 4 + 1 = -11$, writing $-\frac{11}{4}$ instead of $-\frac{13}{4}$.
- **The Correct Concept**: In $-3\frac{1}{4}$, both the whole number part and the fractional part are negative: $-3\frac{1}{4} = -3 - \frac{1}{4} = -\frac{12}{4} - \frac{1}{4} = -\frac{13}{4}$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$-3\frac{1}{4} = \frac{-3 \times 4 + 1}{4} = -\frac{11}{4} \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$-3\frac{1}{4} = -\frac{3 \times 4 + 1}{4} = -\frac{13}{4} \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: On a number line, $-3\frac{1}{4}$ lies between $-3$ and $-4$, whereas $-\frac{11}{4} = -2\frac{3}{4}$ lies between $-2$ and $-3$.

### Making Both Numerator and Denominator Negative for a Single Negative Fraction
- **The Misconception**: Rewriting $-\frac{3}{5}$ as $\frac{-3}{-5}$, believing the negative sign distributes to both top and bottom.
- **The Correct Concept**: A fraction represents division: $\frac{-3}{-5} = (-3) \div (-5) = +\frac{3}{5}$. A single negative sign belongs to EITHER the numerator OR the denominator, never both.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$-\frac{3}{5} = \frac{-3}{-5} \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$-\frac{3}{5} = \frac{-3}{5} = \frac{3}{-5} \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: Dividing $-3$ by $-5$ gives $+0.6$, whereas $-\frac{3}{5} = -0.6$.

#### Question `2_1_Q1` [Easy] *(Signed Fraction Subtraction)*
Evaluate $-\dfrac{3}{4} - \dfrac{5}{6}$ in simplest form.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find $\text{LCD} = \text{LCM}(4, 6) = 12$.
2. Rewrite over $12$: $\frac{-9}{12} - \frac{10}{12} = \frac{-9 - 10}{12} = -\frac{19}{12} = -1\frac{7}{12}$.

**Final Answer**: $-\dfrac{19}{12}$ (or $-1\dfrac{7}{12}$)

#### Question `2_1_Q2` [Easy] *(Adjacent Signs with Fractions)*
Evaluate $-\dfrac{7}{12} - \left(-\dfrac{5}{8}\right)$ in simplest form.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Simplify adjacent minus signs: $-\frac{7}{12} + \frac{5}{8}$.
2. Use $\text{LCD} = 24$: $\frac{-14}{24} + \frac{15}{24} = \frac{1}{24}$.

**Final Answer**: $\dfrac{1}{24}$
</details>

#### Question `2_1_Q3` [Medium] *(Multiplying Signed Fractions)*
Evaluate $-\dfrac{9}{14} \times \left(-\dfrac{7}{12}\right)$ in simplest form.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Two negatives multiply to give a positive result: $+\left(\frac{9}{14} \times \frac{7}{12}\right)$.
2. Cross-cancel $9$ and $12$ by $3$, and $7$ and $14$ by $7$: $\frac{3}{2} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{8}$.

**Final Answer**: $\dfrac{3}{8}$
</details>

#### Question `2_1_Q4` [Medium] *(Dividing Signed Fractions)*
Evaluate $-\dfrac{15}{16} \div \dfrac{9}{20}$ in simplest form.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Multiply by the reciprocal of $\frac{9}{20}$: $-\frac{15}{16} \times \frac{20}{9}$.
2. Cancel $15$ and $9$ by $3$, and $20$ and $16$ by $4$: $-\frac{5}{4} \times \frac{5}{3} = -\frac{25}{12}$.

**Final Answer**: $-\dfrac{25}{12}$ (or $-2\dfrac{1}{12}$)
</details>

#### Question `2_1_Q5` [Medium] *(Mixed Numerals)*
Evaluate $-3\dfrac{1}{5} + 1\dfrac{3}{4}$ as an improper fraction and as a mixed numeral.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Convert to improper fractions: $-\frac{16}{5} + \frac{7}{4}$.
2. Rewrite over $\text{LCD} = 20$: $\frac{-64 + 35}{20} = -\frac{29}{20} = -1\frac{9}{20}$.

**Final Answer**: $-\dfrac{29}{20} = -1\dfrac{9}{20}$
</details>

#### Question `2_1_Q6` [Challenging] *(Powers of Signed Fractions)*

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `2_1_Q7` [Medium] *(Victorian Agriculture (Goulburn Valley))*
The water level in a Goulburn Valley irrigation channel changes by $-\dfrac{3}{8}\text{ m}$ per day during a dry week. After $4$ days, farmers release water from Goulburn Weir, raising the level by $1\dfrac{1}{6}\text{ m}$. What is the net change in the channel's water level after these events?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate the change over $4$ dry days: $4 \times \left(-\frac{3}{8}\right) = -\frac{12}{8} = -\frac{3}{2}\text{ m}$.
2. Add the release of $1\frac{1}{6} = \frac{7}{6}\text{ m}$: $-\frac{3}{2} + \frac{7}{6} = \frac{-9 + 7}{6} = -\frac{2}{6} = -\frac{1}{3}\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: $-\dfrac{1}{3}\text{ m}$
</details>

#### Question `2_1_Q8` [Challenging] *(Port Phillip Bay Marine Science)*
A scuba diver exploring Port Phillip Bay is at a depth of $-4\dfrac{1}{2}\text{ m}$ and descends to a reef at $-15\dfrac{3}{4}\text{ m}$ in $3\dfrac{3}{4}\text{ minutes}$. Find the diver's average rate of change of elevation in metres per minute.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate the change in elevation: $-15\frac{3}{4} - \left(-4\frac{1}{2}\right) = -\frac{63}{4} + \frac{9}{2} = \frac{-63 + 18}{4} = -\frac{45}{4}\text{ m}$.
2. Divide by the time $3\frac{3}{4} = \frac{15}{4}\text{ minutes}$: $-\frac{45}{4} \div \frac{15}{4} = -\frac{45}{4} \times \frac{4}{15} = -3\text{ m/min}$.

**Final Answer**: $-3\text{ m/min}$
</details>

#### Question `2_1_Q9` [Challenging] *(Australian School Transport)*
In a school sibling survey in Bendigo, $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of the students walk to school, $\dfrac{1}{3}$ catch a bus, $\dfrac{1}{6}$ are driven by car, and the remaining $18$ students ride bicycles. How many students are in the survey in total?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Add the known fractions over $\text{LCD}(5, 3, 6) = 30$: $\frac{12}{30} + \frac{10}{30} + \frac{5}{30} = \frac{27}{30} = \frac{9}{10}$.
2. The remaining fraction who ride bicycles is $1 - \frac{9}{10} = \frac{1}{10}$.
3. Since $\frac{1}{10}$ of the students equals $18$, the total number of students is $18 \div \frac{1}{10} = 18 \times 10 = 180$.

**Final Answer**: $180\text{ students}$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `2_1_Q10` [Challenging] *(Number Sense & Inequalities)*
If $-1 < x < 0$, arrange $x$, $x^2$, and $\dfrac{1}{x}$ in ascending order (from smallest to largest). Justify your ordering by testing $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Let $x = -\frac{1}{2}$. Then $x^2 = \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = +\frac{1}{4}$, which is positive.
2. The reciprocal is $\frac{1}{x} = -2$, which is less than $-1$.
3. Comparing the three values gives $-2 < -\frac{1}{2} < \frac{1}{4}$, so $\frac{1}{x} < x < x^2$ for all $-1 < x < 0$.

**Final Answer**: $\dfrac{1}{x} < x < x^2$
</details>

#### Question `2_1_Q11` [Medium] *(Reciprocal Justification)*
Explain why dividing any number by $-\dfrac{1}{4}$ produces the exact same result as multiplying that number by $-4$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. By definition of fraction division, $a \div \left(-\frac{1}{4}\right) = a \times \left(\text{reciprocal of } -\frac{1}{4}\right)$.
2. Since $\left(-\frac{1}{4}\right) \times (-4) = 1$, the reciprocal of $-\frac{1}{4}$ is $-4$.
3. Therefore, $a \div \left(-\frac{1}{4}\right) = a \times (-4)$ for every real number $a$.

**Final Answer**: The reciprocal of $-\frac{1}{4}$ is $-\frac{4}{1} = -4$, and division by a fraction is multiplication by its reciprocal
</details>

#### Question `2_1_Q12` [Extension] *(Algebraic Fraction Proof)*
Prove that for any positive integer $n$, $\dfrac{1}{n(n+1)} = \dfrac{1}{n} - \dfrac{1}{n+1}$, and use this identity ('telescoping sum') to evaluate $\dfrac{1}{1 \times 2} + \dfrac{1}{2 \times 3} + \dfrac{1}{3 \times 4} + \dfrac{1}{4 \times 5}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Express the right-hand side over the common denominator $n(n+1)$: $\frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{n+1} = \frac{(n+1) - n}{n(n+1)} = \frac{1}{n(n+1)}$.
2. Rewrite each term in the sum: $\left(1 - \frac{1}{2}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{3}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{4}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{5}\right)$.
3. All intermediate fractions cancel in pairs, leaving $1 - \frac{1}{5} = \frac{4}{5}$.

**Final Answer**: $\dfrac{4}{5}$
</details>
