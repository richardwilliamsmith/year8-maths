### Key Vocabulary
- **Linear equation**: A mathematical statement containing an equals sign ($=$) in which the highest power of the pronumeral is $1$ (e.g., $4x - 7 = 13$).
- **Inverse operations**: Operations that undo each other: addition ($+$) and subtraction ($-$) are inverses; multiplication ($\times$) and division ($\div$) are inverses.
- **Equivalent equations**: Equations that have the exact same solution, obtained by performing the same operation on both sides of the equals sign.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Maintaining Balance and Inverse Operations

An equation is like a balanced set of scales: whatever operation you perform on the Left-Hand Side (LHS), you must perform the exact same operation on the Right-Hand Side (RHS). To isolate a pronumeral $x$, undo the operations applied to $x$ in the reverse order of operations (undo addition/subtraction outside fractions before undoing multiplication/division).

$$3x - 7 = 14 \implies 3x = 21 \implies x = 7$$

> **💡 Unwrap Operations from the Outside In**
> Think of $x$ as a parcel wrapped in layers. In $3x - 7 = 14$, $x$ was multiplied by $3$ first and then $7$ was subtracted; to unwrap it, add $7$ first, then divide by $3$.

### Equations with Brackets and Algebraic Fractions

When an equation contains brackets, either expand the brackets first using the distributive law (and collect like terms) or divide both sides by the front multiplier if it divides the other side cleanly. When an entire expression is divided by a denominator (such as $\frac{2x + 5}{3} = 7$), multiply both sides by the denominator first to clear the fraction.

$$\frac{2x + 5}{3} = 7 \implies 2x + 5 = 21 \implies 2x = 16 \implies x = 8$$

> **💡 Distinguish Full Fractions from Separate Terms**
> In $\frac{x + 4}{3} = 5$, multiply by $3$ first ($x + 4 = 15$). In $\frac{x}{3} + 4 = 5$, subtract $4$ first ($\frac{x}{3} = 1$).

### Equations with Pronumerals on Both Sides

When the pronumeral appears on both the LHS and RHS (e.g., $7x - 4 = 3x + 16$), collect all pronumeral terms on one side by adding or subtracting the smaller pronumeral term from both sides. Then solve the resulting two-step equation and verify your solution by substituting it into both the original LHS and RHS.

$$7x - 4 = 3x + 16 \implies 4x - 4 = 16 \implies 4x = 20 \implies x = 5$$

> **💡 Eliminate the Smaller Pronumeral Term First**
> Subtracting the smaller coefficient (e.g., subtracting $3x$ rather than $7x$) keeps the remaining pronumeral coefficient positive and avoids sign errors.

## Worked Examples

### WE6_1_1: Solving Equations with Algebraic Fractions

**Problem**: Solve the following equations and verify each solution: (a) $\dfrac{4x - 3}{5} = 5$ and (b) $\dfrac{x}{4} - 6 = -2$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), the entire numerator $4x - 3$ is divided by $5$. Multiply both sides by $5$ first, then add $3$ and divide by $4$. | $$\frac{4x - 3}{5} = 5 \implies 4x - 3 = 25 \implies 4x = 28 \implies x = 7$$
Check: $\text{LHS} = \frac{4(7) - 3}{5} = \frac{25}{5} = 5 = \text{RHS}$. |
| 2 | In part (b), only $x$ is divided by $4$, and $6$ is subtracted afterwards. Add $6$ to both sides first, then multiply by $4$. | $$\frac{x}{4} - 6 = -2 \implies \frac{x}{4} = 4 \implies x = 16$$
Check: $\text{LHS} = \frac{16}{4} - 6 = 4 - 6 = -2 = \text{RHS}$. |

**Answer**: (a) $x = 7$, (b) $x = 16$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Solve (a) $\dfrac{3x + 4}{2} = 11$ and (b) $\dfrac{x}{5} + 7 = 4$.
> *Hint*: In (a) multiply by $2$ first; in (b) subtract $7$ first.
> **Working**: (a) $3x + 4 = 22 \implies 3x = 18 \implies x = 6$; (b) $\frac{x}{5} = -3 \implies x = -15$.
> **Answer**: (a) $x = 6$, (b) $x = -15$

### WE6_1_2: Solving Equations with Brackets and Pronumerals on Both Sides

**Problem**: Solve $5(2x - 3) = 3(x + 4) + 8$ and verify the solution by substitution.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Expand the brackets on both sides and simplify the RHS by collecting constant terms. | $$10x - 15 = 3x + 12 + 8$$
$$10x - 15 = 3x + 20$$ |
| 2 | Subtract $3x$ from both sides so all $x$-terms are on the LHS, then add $15$ to both sides and divide by $7$. | $$7x - 15 = 20 \implies 7x = 35 \implies x = 5$$
Check: $\text{LHS} = 5(10 - 3) = 35$; $\text{RHS} = 3(5 + 4) + 8 = 27 + 8 = 35$. |

**Answer**: $x = 5$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Solve $4(3x - 2) = 2(2x + 5) + 6$.
> *Hint*: Expand to $12x - 8 = 4x + 10 + 6 = 4x + 16$.
> **Working**: $12x - 8 = 4x + 16 \implies 8x = 24 \implies x = 3$.
> **Answer**: $x = 3$

### WE6_1_3: Forming and Solving a Linear Equation from a Context

**Problem**: Two Melbourne electricians quote for a rewiring job. SparkRight charges a $\$95$ call-out fee plus $\$80$ per hour. VoltPro charges a $\$155$ call-out fee plus $\$65$ per hour. For how many hours of work $h$ do both electricians charge the exact same total amount, and what is that cost?

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Write algebraic expressions for each electrician's total cost for $h$ hours and equate them. | $$80h + 95 = 65h + 155$$ |
| 2 | Subtract $65h$ from both sides, subtract $95$ from both sides, and solve for $h$. Then substitute $h$ to find the cost. | $$15h + 95 = 155 \implies 15h = 60 \implies h = 4\text{ hours}$$
$$\text{Cost} = 80(4) + 95 = \$415$$ |

**Answer**: $4\text{ hours}$ (at a total cost of $\$415$)

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Gym A charges a $\$120$ joining fee plus $\$18$ per week. Gym B charges a $\$40$ joining fee plus $\$26$ per week. After how many weeks $w$ have two members paid the same total amount?
> *Hint*: Solve $18w + 120 = 26w + 40$.
> **Working**: $80 = 8w \implies w = 10\text{ weeks}$ (total paid $= \$300$).
> **Answer**: $10\text{ weeks}$ (total $\$300$)

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Detaching a Minus Sign from Its Term ($15 - 2x = 7$)
- **The Misconception**: Adding instead of subtracting when moving a negative coefficient or misreading the sign attached to a term.
- **The Correct Concept**: In $15 - 2x = 7$, the $15$ is positive ($+15$) and the $2x$ is negative ($-2x$). To eliminate $15$, subtract $15$ from both sides to leave $-2x = -8$, not $2x = 22$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$15 - 2x = 7 \implies 2x = 7 + 15 = 22 \implies x = 11 \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$15 - 2x = 7 \implies -2x = 7 - 15 = -8 \implies x = \frac{-8}{-2} = 4$$
- **Counter-Example**: Substitute $x = 11$ into $15 - 2x$: $15 - 22 = -7 \neq 7$. Substituting $x = 4$ gives $15 - 8 = 7$.

### Multiplying Only One Term When Clearing a Fraction
- **The Misconception**: Multiplying only the fraction term by the denominator instead of every term on both sides.
- **The Correct Concept**: If you clear a fraction by multiplying by its denominator, every separate term on both sides must be multiplied, or you should subtract the non-fraction term first.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\frac{x}{3} + 4 = 9 \implies x + 4 = 27 \implies x = 23 \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\frac{x}{3} + 4 = 9 \implies \frac{x}{3} = 5 \implies x = 15$$
- **Counter-Example**: With $x = 23$, $\frac{23}{3} + 4 = 11\frac{2}{3} \neq 9$. With $x = 15$, $\frac{15}{3} + 4 = 5 + 4 = 9$.

#### Question `6_1_Q1` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Solve $6x - 11 = 25$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

**Final Answer**: $x = 6$

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

2. Divide both sides by $-4$: $x = \frac{-16}{-4} = 4$.

**Final Answer**: $x = 4$

#### Question `6_1_Q6` [Challenging] *(Understanding)*
Solve $\dfrac{2x - 1}{3} = \dfrac{x + 4}{2}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Multiply both sides by the lowest common denominator $\text{LCM}(3, 2) = 6$: $2(2x - 1) = 3(x + 4)$.
2. Expand both sides: $4x - 2 = 3x + 12$.
3. Subtract $3x$ and add $2$ to both sides: $x = 14$.

**Final Answer**: $x = 14$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `6_1_Q7` [Medium] *(Australian Sport Dimensions)*
The perimeter of a rectangular netball court at Waverley Netball Centre is $91\text{ m}$. Its length is $0.5\text{ m}$ more than twice its width $w$. Form and solve a linear equation to find the width $w$ and length $l$ of the court.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Express length in terms of width $w$: $l = 2w + 0.5$.
2. Substitute into the perimeter formula $2(l + w) = 91$: $2(2w + 0.5 + w) = 91 \implies 2(3w + 0.5) = 91$.
3. Expand and solve: $6w + 1 = 91 \implies 6w = 90 \implies w = 15\text{ m}$, and $l = 2(15) + 0.5 = 30.5\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: Width $= 15\text{ m}$, length $= 30.5\text{ m}$
</details>

#### Question `6_1_Q8` [Medium] *(Number Modelling)*
The sum of three consecutive odd integers is $117$. Let the smallest odd integer be $n$. Write a linear equation in terms of $n$ and find the three integers.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Consecutive odd integers differ by $2$, so they are $n$, $n + 2$, and $n + 4$.
2. Form the equation: $n + (n + 2) + (n + 4) = 117 \implies 3n + 6 = 117$.
3. Solve for $n$: $3n = 111 \implies n = 37$. The three odd integers are $37, 39$, and $41$.

**Final Answer**: $37, 39, 41$
</details>

#### Question `6_1_Q9` [Challenging] *(Australian Renewable Energy)*
A Bendigo solar battery starts at $18\text{ kWh}$ and charges at $3.5\text{ kWh}$ per hour, while a Geelong battery starts at $42\text{ kWh}$ and discharges at $2.5\text{ kWh}$ per hour. After how many hours $t$ do both batteries hold the exact same energy, and how many kilowatt-hours is that?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Equate the two energy expressions after $t$ hours: $18 + 3.5t = 42 - 2.5t$.
2. Add $2.5t$ to both sides and subtract $18$: $6t = 24 \implies t = 4\text{ hours}$.
3. Energy at $t = 4$: $18 + 3.5(4) = 18 + 14 = 32\text{ kWh}$.

**Final Answer**: $4\text{ hours}$ ($32\text{ kWh}$)
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `6_1_Q10` [Medium] *(Existence of Solutions)*
Noah attempts to solve $3(x + 4) = 3x + 8$. After expanding, he gets $3x + 12 = 3x + 8$, and subtracting $3x$ gives $12 = 8$. Explain what this result means about the solutions to the equation.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Subtracting $3x$ from both sides leaves the false statement $12 = 8$, which is never true for any value of $x$.
2. Therefore, there is no real number $x$ for which $3(x + 4)$ equals $3x + 8$; the equation has no solution.

**Final Answer**: The equation has no solution (it is a contradiction)
</details>

#### Question `6_1_Q11` [Challenging] *(Identities vs Equations)*
Compare the equation in Q10 with $2(3x - 5) + 4 = 6x - 6$. Expand the LHS and explain how many solutions this equation has.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Expand the LHS: $6x - 10 + 4 = 6x - 6$, which is identical to the RHS ($6x - 6 = 6x - 6$).
2. Because the LHS and RHS simplify to the exact same expression, the equation is an identity and is true for every real value of $x$ (infinitely many solutions).

**Final Answer**: Infinitely many solutions (all real numbers $x$, because it is an algebraic identity)
</details>

#### Question `6_1_Q12` [Extension] *(Parameter Determination)*
Find the value of the constant $k$ so that $x = -3$ is the solution to the linear equation $4(x - k) = 2x + 14$. Show your algebraic reasoning.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute $x = -3$ into the equation: $4(-3 - k) = 2(-3) + 14$.
2. Simplify both sides: $-12 - 4k = -6 + 14 = 8$.
3. Add $12$ to both sides and divide by $-4$: $-4k = 20 \implies k = -5$.

**Final Answer**: $k = -5$
</details>
