### Key Vocabulary
- **Gradient ($m$)**: A measure of the steepness and direction of a straight line, defined as $\text{gradient } m = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$.
- **$y$-intercept ($c$) and $x$-intercept**: The $y$-intercept is the value of $y$ where a graph crosses the vertical $y$-axis (at $x = 0$). The $x$-intercept is the value of $x$ where a graph crosses the horizontal $x$-axis (at $y = 0$).
- **Gradient-intercept form**: The equation of a non-vertical straight line written in the form $y = mx + c$, where $m$ is the gradient and $(0, c)$ is the $y$-intercept.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Gradient ($m$) as Rise Over Run

Between any two points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ on a straight line, the ratio of the vertical change (rise) to the horizontal change (run) is constant. Moving from left to right: a line that slopes upwards has a positive gradient ($m > 0$), a line that slopes downwards has a negative gradient ($m < 0$), a horizontal line has zero gradient ($m = 0$), and a vertical line has an undefined gradient.

$$m = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

> **💡 Read Graphs from Left to Right**
> Always take run as positive (moving left to right). If the line goes up, rise is positive ($+$); if the line goes down, rise is negative ($-$).

### Gradient-Intercept Form ($y = mx + c$) and Axis Intercepts

Every non-vertical straight line can be written as $y = mx + c$, where $m$ is the gradient (rate of change of $y$ per $1$-unit increase in $x$) and $c$ is the $y$-intercept (the value of $y$ when $x = 0$). To find the $x$-intercept of a line, substitute $y = 0$ and solve for $x$.

$$y = mx + c: \quad y\text{-intercept at } (0, c), \quad x\text{-intercept at } \left(-\frac{c}{m}, 0\right)$$

> **💡 Opposite Variable Equals Zero for Intercepts**
> To find the $y$-intercept, set $x = 0$. To find the $x$-intercept, set $y = 0$.

### Linear Modelling in Practical Contexts

Many real-world situations with a fixed starting value and a constant rate of change are modelled by a linear relation $y = mx + c$. In applied contexts, the $y$-intercept $c$ represents the initial value (when $x = 0$), and the gradient $m$ represents the constant rate of increase ($m > 0$) or decrease ($m < 0$) per unit of $x$.

$$\text{Total Value} = (\text{Rate of change}) \times x + (\text{Initial value})$$

> **💡 Match Rate to $m$ and Initial Value to $c$**
> When a tank starts with $500\text{ L}$ and drains at $20\text{ L/min}$, initial value $c = 500$ and rate $m = -20$, giving $V = -20t + 500$.

## Worked Examples

### WE6_2_1: Finding the Gradient and Equation of a Line Through Two Points

**Problem**: A straight line passes through the points $(0, -4)$ and $(3, 5)$. Find (a) the gradient $m$ of the line and (b) its equation in the form $y = mx + c$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Use the gradient formula $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$ with $(x_1, y_1) = (0, -4)$ and $(x_2, y_2) = (3, 5)$. | $$m = \frac{5 - (-4)}{3 - 0} = \frac{9}{3} = 3$$ |
| 2 | Identify the $y$-intercept $c$ from the point $(0, -4)$, which gives $c = -4$, and substitute $m = 3$ and $c = -4$ into $y = mx + c$. | $$y = 3x - 4$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $m = 3$, (b) $y = 3x - 4$

### WE6_2_2: Finding the $x$- and $y$-Intercepts of a Linear Relation

**Problem**: Find the coordinates of the $y$-intercept and the $x$-intercept of the line $3x + 4y = 24$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | To find the $y$-intercept, substitute $x = 0$ into $3x + 4y = 24$ and solve for $y$. | $$3(0) + 4y = 24 \implies 4y = 24 \implies y = 6 \implies (0, 6)$$ |
| 2 | To find the $x$-intercept, substitute $y = 0$ into $3x + 4y = 24$ and solve for $x$. | $$3x + 4(0) = 24 \implies 3x = 24 \implies x = 8 \implies (8, 0)$$ |

**Answer**: $y$-intercept $(0, 6)$, $x$-intercept $(8, 0)$

### WE6_2_3: Linear Modelling of a Draining Water Tank

**Problem**: A rural firefighting water tank near Horsham starts with $2400\text{ L}$ of water and is pumped out at a constant rate of $150\text{ L}$ per minute. (a) Write a linear rule for the volume $V\text{ (L)}$ remaining after $t$ minutes. (b) Find the volume remaining after $9$ minutes. (c) How many minutes does it take for the tank to empty completely?

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | The initial volume at $t = 0$ is $c = 2400$ and the rate of change is $m = -150\text{ L/min}$. Write the rule $V = mt + c$ and evaluate at $t = 9$. | $$\text{(a) } V = -150t + 2400$$
$$\text{(b) At } t = 9: \quad V = -150(9) + 2400 = -1350 + 2400 = 1050\text{ L}$$ |
| 2 | In part (c), the tank is empty when $V = 0$. Substitute $V = 0$ and solve for $t$. | $$0 = -150t + 2400 \implies 150t = 2400 \implies t = \frac{2400}{150} = 16\text{ minutes}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $V = -150t + 2400$, (b) $1050\text{ L}$, (c) $16\text{ minutes}$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Inverting Gradient as Run Over Rise
- **The Misconception**: Calculating gradient as run over rise ($\frac{x_2 - x_1}{y_2 - y_1}$) instead of rise over run ($\frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$).
- **The Correct Concept**: Gradient measures vertical change per unit of horizontal change, so the difference in $y$-coordinates ($\text{rise}$) is always in the numerator and the difference in $x$-coordinates ($\text{run}$) is always in the denominator.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\text{Through } (1, 2) \text{ and } (3, 8): \quad m = \frac{3 - 1}{8 - 2} = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3} \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$m = \frac{8 - 2}{3 - 1} = \frac{6}{2} = 3$$
- **Counter-Example**: As $x$ increases by $2$ (from $1$ to $3$), $y$ increases by $6$ (from $2$ to $8$), which is $3$ units of rise for every $1$ unit of run.

### Confusing Term Order with $m$ and $c$ in $y = c + mx$
- **The Misconception**: Assuming the first number written on the RHS of a linear equation is always the gradient $m$.
- **The Correct Concept**: The gradient $m$ is always the coefficient of $x$, and the $y$-intercept $c$ is the constant term, regardless of the order in which the terms are written.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\text{In } y = 12 - 5x, \text{ gradient } m = 12 \text{ and } y\text{-intercept } c = -5 \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$y = 12 - 5x = -5x + 12 \implies m = -5, \quad c = 12$$
- **Counter-Example**: When $x = 0$, $y = 12 - 5(0) = 12$, confirming the $y$-intercept is $12$, not $-5$.

State the gradient $m$ and the $y$-intercept $c$ of the line $y = 15 - 4x$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

**Final Answer**: $m = -4, c = 15$

#### Question `6_2_Q2` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Find the gradient of the straight line passing through the points $(-2, 5)$ and $(4, 23)$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute $(x_1, y_1) = (-2, 5)$ and $(x_2, y_2) = (4, 23)$ into $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$.
2. Evaluate: $m = \frac{23 - 5}{4 - (-2)} = \frac{18}{6} = 3$.

**Final Answer**: $m = 3$
</details>

Find the gradient and the equation of the line passing through $(0, 9)$ and $(5, -6)$.

<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

2. Since the line passes through $(0, 9)$, the $y$-intercept is $c = 9$, giving $y = -3x + 9$.

**Final Answer**: $m = -3$, $y = -3x + 9$

#### Question `6_2_Q4` [Medium] *(Fluency)*
Find the $y$-intercept and the $x$-intercept of the line $y = 4x - 20$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute $x = 0$: $y = 4(0) - 20 = -20$, so the $y$-intercept is $(0, -20)$.
2. Substitute $y = 0$: $0 = 4x - 20 \implies 4x = 20 \implies x = 5$, so the $x$-intercept is $(5, 0)$.

**Final Answer**: $y$-intercept $(0, -20)$, $x$-intercept $(5, 0)$
</details>

#### Question `6_2_Q5` [Medium] *(Understanding)*
A table of values for a linear relation shows $(x, y)$ pairs: $(1, 11), (2, 15), (3, 19), (4, 23)$. Find the linear rule in the form $y = mx + c$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. As $x$ increases by $1$, $y$ increases by $4$, so the gradient is $m = 4$.
2. Substitute $(1, 11)$ into $y = 4x + c$: $11 = 4(1) + c \implies c = 7$. Thus $y = 4x + 7$.

**Final Answer**: $y = 4x + 7$
</details>

#### Question `6_2_Q6` [Challenging] *(Understanding)*
Find the equation of the straight line passing through the two points $(2, 1)$ and $(6, 13)$ in the form $y = mx + c$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate the gradient: $m = \frac{13 - 1}{6 - 2} = \frac{12}{4} = 3$.
2. Substitute $m = 3$ and the point $(2, 1)$ into $y = mx + c$: $1 = 3(2) + c \implies c = -5$, giving $y = 3x - 5$.

**Final Answer**: $y = 3x - 5$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `6_2_Q7` [Easy] *(Melbourne Public Transport)*
A Melbourne Myki card holds a balance of $\$50$ at the start of the month. Each daily full-fare Zone 1+2 commute deducts $\$10.60$ from the balance. (a) Write a linear rule for the balance $B\text{ (\$)}$ after $d$ days of travel. (b) What is the balance after $4$ days of travel?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Initial balance is $c = 50$ and daily change is $m = -10.6$, so $B = -10.6d + 50$.
2. Substitute $d = 4$: $B = -10.6(4) + 50 = -42.4 + 50 = \$7.60$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $B = -10.6d + 50$, (b) $\$7.60$
</details>

#### Question `6_2_Q8` [Medium] *(Victorian Alpine Weather)*
At Mount Buller, the depth of snow $S\text{ (cm)}$ at the base station starts at $65\text{ cm}$ at midnight and increases during a blizzard at a constant rate of $3.5\text{ cm}$ per hour for $t$ hours. (a) Write a rule for $S$ in terms of $t$. (b) After how many hours does the snow depth reach $93\text{ cm}$?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. With initial depth $c = 65$ and rate $m = 3.5$, the linear rule is $S = 3.5t + 65$.
2. Substitute $S = 93$: $93 = 3.5t + 65 \implies 3.5t = 28 \implies t = \frac{28}{3.5} = 8\text{ hours}$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $S = 3.5t + 65$, (b) $8\text{ hours}$
</details>

#### Question `6_2_Q9` [Challenging] *(Coordinate Geometry & Area)*
A straight line has $x$-intercept $(6, 0)$ and $y$-intercept $(0, 8)$. Calculate the area of the right-angled triangle enclosed by this line, the $x$-axis, and the $y$-axis.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The line intersects the positive $x$-axis at $6$ (base $= 6$ units) and the positive $y$-axis at $8$ (perpendicular height $= 8$ units).
2. Calculate the area of the right-angled triangle: $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 8 = 24\text{ square units}$.

**Final Answer**: $24\text{ square units}$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `6_2_Q10` [Medium] *(Collinearity & Verification)*
Determine algebraically whether the point $(12, -29)$ lies on the straight line $y = -3x + 7$. Justify your conclusion.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute $x = 12$ into the right-hand side of $y = -3x + 7$: $y = -3(12) + 7 = -36 + 7 = -29$.
2. Since the calculated $y$-value ($-29$) matches the $y$-coordinate of the point $(12, -29)$, the point lies on the line.

**Final Answer**: Yes, $(12, -29)$ lies on the line because substituting $x = 12$ gives $y = -29$
</details>

#### Question `6_2_Q11` [Challenging] *(Mathematical Reasoning)*
Explain why a vertical line (such as $x = 4$) has an undefined gradient, whereas a horizontal line (such as $y = 4$) has a gradient of $0$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. On a horizontal line $y = 4$, any two points have the same $y$-coordinate, so $\text{rise} = 0$ and $m = \frac{0}{\text{run}} = 0$.
2. On a vertical line $x = 4$, any two points have the same $x$-coordinate, so $\text{run} = 0$. Because division by zero is undefined, $m = \frac{\text{rise}}{0}$ is undefined.

**Final Answer**: Horizontal line has $\text{rise} = 0$ ($m = \frac{0}{\text{run}} = 0$); vertical line has $\text{run} = 0$ ($m = \frac{\text{rise}}{0}$, which is undefined)
</details>

#### Question `6_2_Q12` [Extension] *(Collinear Points)*
Three points $A(1, 3)$, $B(4, 15)$, and $C(7, k)$ are collinear (they lie on the same straight line). Use gradients to find the value of $k$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find the gradient of segment $AB$: $m_{AB} = \frac{15 - 3}{4 - 1} = \frac{12}{3} = 4$.
2. Since $A, B, C$ are collinear, the gradient of $BC$ must also equal $4$: $\frac{k - 15}{7 - 4} = 4 \implies \frac{k - 15}{3} = 4 \implies k - 15 = 12 \implies k = 27$.

**Final Answer**: $k = 27$
</details>
