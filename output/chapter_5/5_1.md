### Key Vocabulary
- **Pronumeral (Variable)**: A letter or symbol (such as $x, y, n$) used to represent an unknown or varying number.
- **Coefficient and Constant term**: The coefficient is the signed numerical factor multiplying a pronumeral (e.g., in $-5x$, the coefficient is $-5$). A constant term contains no pronumerals.
- **Like terms**: Terms that have the exact same pronumeral factors raised to the exact same powers (e.g., $4a^2b$ and $-7ba^2$).

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Algebraic Notation, Coefficients, and Substitution with Negative Values

In algebra, multiplication signs are omitted ($3 \times a \times b = 3ab$) and division is written as a fraction ($a \div b = \frac{a}{b}$). The sign in front of a term belongs to its coefficient. When substituting a negative number for a pronumeral, always enclose the negative number in brackets so that powers and multiplications apply to the negative sign correctly.

$$\text{If } x = -4, \quad x^2 - 3x = (-4)^2 - 3(-4) = 16 + 12 = 28$$

> **💡 Use Brackets When Substituting Negative Numbers**
> Whenever you replace a pronumeral with a negative number, wrap it in brackets: $(-4)^2 = +16$, not $-16$.

### Collecting Like Terms

Like terms contain the exact same pronumerals raised to the exact same powers (regardless of the order in which the pronumerals are written, since $ab = ba$). By the distributive law, you add or subtract like terms by adding or subtracting their signed coefficients while keeping the pronumeral part unchanged.

$$ma + na = (m + n)a, \quad 5xy - 8yx = (5 - 8)xy = -3xy$$

> **💡 Pronumeral Exponents Never Change When Adding**
> When collecting like terms, $3x + 4x = 7x$, NOT $7x^2$. Terms with different powers such as $x$ and $x^2$ are UNLIKE terms and cannot be combined by addition.

### Multiplying and Dividing Algebraic Terms

Unlike addition, any two algebraic terms can be multiplied or divided. Multiply or divide the numerical coefficients using directed number rules, and combine identical pronumerals using the First and Second Index Laws ($x \times x = x^2$ and $\frac{x^2}{x} = x$).

$$(-4a^2b) \times (3ab) = -12a^3b^2, \quad \frac{-18x^2y}{6xy} = -3x$$

> **💡 Coefficients First, Then Each Pronumeral Alphabetically**
> Write the simplified product with the signed coefficient at the front, followed by pronumerals in alphabetical order.

## Worked Examples

### WE5_1_1: Substituting Directed Numbers into Algebraic Expressions

**Problem**: Given $a = -3$ and $b = 4$, evaluate (a) $2a^2 - 5b$ and (b) $\dfrac{b + 2 - a^3}{11}$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), substitute $a = (-3)$ in brackets and $b = 4$: evaluate $(-3)^2 = 9$ first. | $$2a^2 - 5b = 2(-3)^2 - 5(4) = 2(9) - 20 = 18 - 20 = -2$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), substitute $b = 4$ and $a = (-3)$: since $(-3)^3 = -27$, subtracting $(-27)$ adds $27$. | $$\frac{b + 2 - a^3}{11} = \frac{4 + 2 - (-3)^3}{11} = \frac{6 - (-27)}{11} = \frac{33}{11} = 3$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $-2$, (b) $3$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Given $x = -4$ and $y = 5$, evaluate (a) $3x^2 - 4y$ and (b) $\dfrac{2y - x}{7}$.
> *Hint*: Remember $(-4)^2 = +16$ and $2(5) - (-4) = 10 + 4 = 14$.
> **Working**: (a) $3(16) - 20 = 48 - 20 = 28$; (b) $\frac{10 - (-4)}{7} = \frac{14}{7} = 2$.
> **Answer**: (a) $28$, (b) $2$

### WE5_1_2: Simplifying by Collecting Like Terms

**Problem**: Simplify $7x^2 - 4xy + 3y - 10x^2 + 9yx - 8y$ by collecting like terms.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Recognise that $-4xy$ and $+9yx$ are like terms because $yx = xy$. Group the like terms together with their attached signs. | $$(7x^2 - 10x^2) + (-4xy + 9xy) + (3y - 8y)$$ |
| 2 | Combine the coefficients of each group. | $$= -3x^2 + 5xy - 5y$$ |

**Answer**: $-3x^2 + 5xy - 5y$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Simplify $5a^2 - 6ab - 2a^2 + 10ba - 7$.
> *Hint*: Group $(5a^2 - 2a^2)$ and $(-6ab + 10ab)$.
> **Working**: $(5 - 2)a^2 + (-6 + 10)ab - 7 = 3a^2 + 4ab - 7$.
> **Answer**: $3a^2 + 4ab - 7$

### WE5_1_3: Multiplying and Dividing Algebraic Terms

**Problem**: Simplify: (a) $-6m^2n \times (-4mn^3)$ and (b) $\dfrac{-24a^3b^2}{16ab^3}$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), multiply the coefficients $(-6) \times (-4) = +24$, then add exponents of like pronumerals: $m^{2+1} = m^3$ and $n^{1+3} = n^4$. | $$-6m^2n \times (-4mn^3) = 24m^3n^4$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), divide $-24$ and $16$ by $\text{HCF}(24, 16) = 8$ to get $-\frac{3}{2}$. Cancel $a$ to leave $a^2$ in the numerator, and cancel $b^2$ to leave $b$ in the denominator. | $$\frac{-24a^3b^2}{16ab^3} = -\frac{3a^2}{2b}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $24m^3n^4$, (b) $-\dfrac{3a^2}{2b}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Simplify: (a) $-5pq^2 \times 7p^3q$ and (b) $\dfrac{-30x^4y}{-18x^2y^2}$.
> *Hint*: In (b), two negatives divide to give a positive, and $\text{HCF}(30, 18) = 6$.
> **Working**: (a) $-35p^4q^3$; (b) $\frac{5x^2}{3y}$.
> **Answer**: (a) $-35p^4q^3$, (b) $\dfrac{5x^2}{3y}$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Adding Exponents When Collecting Like Terms ($x + x \neq x^2$)
- **The Misconception**: Writing $3x + 4x = 7x^2$ by confusing addition of like terms with multiplication ($3x \times 4x = 12x^2$).
- **The Correct Concept**: Adding like terms counts how many of that unit you have: $3$ lots of $x$ plus $4$ lots of $x$ equals $7$ lots of $x$ ($7x$). Exponents only change when multiplying or dividing.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$3x + 4x = 7x^2 \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$3x + 4x = 7x \quad \text{and} \quad 3x \times 4x = 12x^2 \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: If $x = 10$, then $3(10) + 4(10) = 30 + 40 = 70 = 7(10)$, whereas $7(10)^2 = 700$.

### Combining Unlike Powers Such as $x$ and $x^2$
- **The Misconception**: Simplifying $2x + 5x^2$ into $7x^3$ or $7x^2$.
- **The Correct Concept**: Because $x$ and $x^2$ have different exponents, they are unlike terms and cannot be simplified further under addition.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$2x + 5x^2 = 7x^3 \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$2x + 5x^2 \text{ cannot be simplified further} \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: Geometrically, $2x$ is a length whereas $5x^2$ is an area; you cannot add lengths and areas into a single term.

Evaluate $3p^2 - 2pq$ when $p = -4$ and $q = 5$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute $p = -4$ and $q = 5$: $3(-4)^2 - 2(-4)(5)$.

Simplify $9a - 4b - 12a + 7b - 3$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Group like terms: $(9a - 12a) + (-4b + 7b) - 3$.

#### Question `5_1_Q4` [Medium] *(Collecting Like Terms)*
Simplify $8m^2n - 5mn^2 - 3nm^2 + 2n^2m$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Identify like terms: $8m^2n$ and $-3nm^2$ both have $m^2n$; $-5mn^2$ and $+2n^2m$ both have $mn^2$.
2. Combine each pair: $(8 - 3)m^2n + (-5 + 2)mn^2 = 5m^2n - 3mn^2$.

**Final Answer**: $5m^2n - 3mn^2$
</details>

#### Question `5_1_Q5` [Medium] *(Multiplying & Dividing Terms)*
Simplify $-4x^2y \times 5xy^3 \div (-2xy^2)$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Multiply the first two terms: $-4x^2y \times 5xy^3 = -20x^3y^4$.
2. Divide by $-2xy^2$: $\frac{-20x^3y^4}{-2xy^2} = 10x^{3-1}y^{4-2} = 10x^2y^2$.

**Final Answer**: $10x^2y^2$
</details>

#### Question `5_1_Q6` [Challenging] *(Fractional Substitution)*
Evaluate $\dfrac{x^2 - y^2}{x - y}$ when $x = -3$ and $y = -7$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Evaluate the numerator: $(-3)^2 - (-7)^2 = 9 - 49 = -40$.
2. Evaluate the denominator: $-3 - (-7) = -3 + 7 = 4$.
3. Divide: $-40 \div 4 = -10$.

**Final Answer**: $-10$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `5_1_Q7` [Medium] *(Australian Sport (AFL Grand Final))*
In Australian Rules Football (AFL), a team scores $g$ goals ($6$ points each) and $b$ behinds ($1$ point each). (a) Write an algebraic expression for the team's total score $S$. (b) In the 2023 AFL Grand Final at the MCG, Collingwood scored $12$ goals and $18$ behinds, while Brisbane scored $13$ goals and $8$ behinds. Use your formula to find each team's score and the winning margin.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Each goal is worth $6$ points and each behind is worth $1$ point, so $S = 6g + b$.
2. Collingwood: $S = 6(12) + 18 = 72 + 18 = 90$ points.
3. Brisbane: $S = 6(13) + 8 = 78 + 8 = 86$ points.
4. Winning margin: $90 - 86 = 4$ points.

**Final Answer**: (a) $S = 6g + b$; (b) Collingwood $90$, Brisbane $86$ (Collingwood by $4$ points)
</details>

#### Question `5_1_Q8` [Medium] *(Geometric Perimeter Expression)*
An L-shaped garden bed has exterior sides of length $5x$ and $4y$, with an inner cutout leaving perimeter segments of $2x$, $y$, $3x$, and $3y$. Write and simplify an expression for (a) the total perimeter of the rectangle of width $3x + 2y$ and length $5x - y$, and (b) evaluate the perimeter when $x = 4\text{ m}$ and $y = 3\text{ m}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Add twice the width and twice the length: $P = 2(3x + 2y) + 2(5x - y) = 6x + 4y + 10x - 2y = 16x + 2y$.
2. Substitute $x = 4$ and $y = 3$: $P = 16(4) + 2(3) = 64 + 6 = 70\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $16x + 2y$, (b) $70\text{ m}$
</details>

#### Question `5_1_Q9` [Challenging] *(3D Mensuration in Algebra)*
A rectangular prism has length $4a$, width $3a$, and height $2b$. Find simplified algebraic expressions for (a) the volume $V$ of the prism and (b) the total surface area $SA$ of all $6$ faces.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Volume $V = l \times w \times h = 4a \times 3a \times 2b = 24a^2b$.
2. Surface area $SA = 2(4a \times 3a) + 2(4a \times 2b) + 2(3a \times 2b) = 24a^2 + 16ab + 12ab = 24a^2 + 28ab$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $V = 24a^2b$, (b) $SA = 24a^2 + 28ab$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `5_1_Q10` [Medium] *(Fraction Cancellation Error Analysis)*
Show by substitution with $x = 6$ and $y = 3$ that $\dfrac{6x + 3y}{3} \neq 2x + 3y$, and state the correct simplified form of $\dfrac{6x + 3y}{3}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. When $x = 6, y = 3$, $\frac{6(6) + 3(3)}{3} = \frac{36 + 9}{3} = \frac{45}{3} = 15$.
2. Meanwhile, $2x + 3y = 2(6) + 3(3) = 12 + 9 = 21 \neq 15$, because the $3y$ term was not divided by $3$.
3. Dividing BOTH terms in the numerator by $3$ gives $\frac{6x}{3} + \frac{3y}{3} = 2x + y$ (which evaluates to $2(6) + 3 = 15$).

**Final Answer**: Correct simplified form is $2x + y$
</details>

#### Question `5_1_Q11` [Challenging] *(Algebraic Divisibility Proof)*
Prove that the sum of any three consecutive integers $n, n+1, n+2$ is always divisible by $3$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Let the three consecutive integers be $n$, $n + 1$, and $n + 2$.
2. Add them and collect like terms: $n + (n + 1) + (n + 2) = 3n + 3$.
3. Since $3n + 3 = 3(n + 1)$ and $n + 1$ is an integer, the sum is always an exact multiple of $3$.

**Final Answer**: $n + (n + 1) + (n + 2) = 3n + 3 = 3(n + 1)$, which is a multiple of $3$
</details>

#### Question `5_1_Q12` [Extension] *(Place Value & Number Theory Proof)*
A two-digit number has tens digit $a$ and units digit $b$, so its value is $10a + b$. Prove that if you reverse its digits to form $10b + a$, the sum of the original number and the reversed number is always a multiple of $11$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Write the sum of the two numbers: $(10a + b) + (10b + a)$.
2. Collect like terms in $a$ and $b$: $(10a + a) + (b + 10b) = 11a + 11b$.
3. Factor out $11$: $11(a + b)$, which is always divisible by $11$ for any digits $a$ and $b$.

**Final Answer**: $(10a + b) + (10b + a) = 11a + 11b = 11(a + b)$
</details>
