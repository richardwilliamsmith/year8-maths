**Target Mastery**: Calculate and convert unit rates, compare Australian supermarket products using unit pricing ($/100 g or $/L), and solve distance-speed-time and fuel consumption problems.

**Prerequisite Skills**:

### Key Vocabulary
- **Rate**: A comparison of two related quantities measured in different units, written with 'per' or a slash `/` (e.g., $\text{km/h}$, $\text{\$/kg}$, $\text{L/min}$).
- **Unit rate**: A simplified rate in which the second quantity is $1\text{ unit}$ (e.g., $95\text{ km}$ per $1\text{ hour}$).
- **Unit pricing (Best Buy)**: Comparing the cost of different package sizes by calculating the price per standard unit (such as per $100\text{ g}$, per $\text{kg}$, or per $\text{L}$).

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Simplifying Rates and Supermarket Unit Pricing (Best Buys)

Unlike ratios, rates always retain their units because they compare two different types of measurement (such as dollars and grams). To simplify a rate $A\text{ units}_1 \text{ per } B\text{ units}_2$, divide $A$ by $B$ to get the amount per $1\text{ unit}_2$. Australian supermarkets display unit prices (typically per $100\text{ g}$ or per $1\text{ L}$) so shoppers can identify the 'best buy' (lowest unit cost).

$$\text{Unit Rate} = \frac{\text{First Quantity}}{\text{Second Quantity}} \text{ (in units}_1\text{/unit}_2\text{)}$$

> **💡 Order of Division matches the Slash**
> The word 'per' (or `/`) means DIVIDE! Dollars per kilogram ($\text{\$/kg}$) means $\text{Dollars} \div \text{Kilograms}$.

### Average Speed, Distance, and Time

Average speed is the rate of distance travelled per unit of time. The three quantities Distance ($D$), Average Speed ($S$), and Time ($T$) are linked by three equivalent formulas.

$$S = \frac{D}{T}, \quad D = S \times T, \quad T = \frac{D}{S}$$

> **💡 Converting Between $\text{m/s}$ and $\text{km/h}$**
> Since $1\text{ km} = 1000\text{ m}$ and $1\text{ h} = 3600\text{ s}$, $1\text{ m/s} = \frac{3600}{1000}\text{ km/h} = 3.6\text{ km/h}$. Multiply $\text{m/s}$ by $3.6$ to get $\text{km/h}$; divide $\text{km/h}$ by $3.6$ to get $\text{m/s}$.

### Fuel Consumption Rate ($\text{L/100 km}$)

In Australia, motor vehicle fuel efficiency is rated in litres of fuel consumed per $100\text{ km}$ driven ($\text{L/100 km}$).

$$\text{Fuel Rate (L/100 km)} = \frac{\text{Litres Used}}{\text{Distance in km}} \times 100$$

> **💡 Hundreds of Kilometres Method**
> If a car uses $7.5\text{ L/100 km}$, then for a $400\text{ km}$ trip ($4$ lots of $100\text{ km}$), it uses $4 \times 7.5 = 30\text{ L}$ of fuel.

## Worked Examples

### WE4_2_1: Supermarket Best Buy Comparison

**Problem**: At a supermarket in Box Hill, Australian rolled oats are sold in two sizes: a $750\text{ g}$ box for $\$4.50$, or a $1.2\text{ kg}$ bag for $\$6.60$. Calculate the cost per $100\text{ g}$ for each option and state which is the better buy.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | For the $750\text{ g}$ box ($7.5$ lots of $100\text{ g}$), divide $\$4.50$ by $7.5$. | $$\text{Option A} = \frac{4.50}{7.5} = \$0.60\text{ per } 100\text{ g}$$ |
| 2 | Convert $1.2\text{ kg}$ to $1200\text{ g}$ ($12$ lots of $100\text{ g}$) and divide $\$6.60$ by $12$. | $$\text{Option B} = \frac{6.60}{12} = \$0.55\text{ per } 100\text{ g}$$ |
| 3 | Compare the unit prices: $\$0.55 < \$0.60$, so the $1.2\text{ kg}$ bag is cheaper per $100\text{ g}$. | $$\$0.55/100\text{ g} < \$0.60/100\text{ g} \implies 1.2\text{ kg bag is the best buy}$$ |

**Answer**: Option A: $\$0.60/100\text{ g}$; Option B: $\$0.55/100\text{ g}$. The $1.2\text{ kg}$ bag is the better buy.

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Olive oil is sold in a $500\text{ mL}$ bottle for $\$7.50$ or a $750\text{ mL}$ bottle for $\$10.50$. Find the cost per $100\text{ mL}$ for each and identify the best buy.
> *Hint*: Divide $\$7.50$ by $5$ and $\$10.50$ by $7.5$.
> **Working**: $500\text{ mL}$: $7.50 \div 5 = \$1.50/100\text{ mL}$; $750\text{ mL}$: $10.50 \div 7.5 = \$1.40/100\text{ mL}$.
> **Answer**: The $750\text{ mL}$ bottle ($\$1.40/100\text{ mL}$) is the better buy.

### WE4_2_2: Speed, Distance, Time and $\text{m/s} \leftrightarrow \text{km/h}$ Conversion

**Problem**: (a) Convert a sprinter's speed of $10\text{ m/s}$ into $\text{km/h}$. (b) A V/Line train travels $180\text{ km}$ from Melbourne to Shepparton in $2\text{ hours } 15\text{ minutes}$. Find its average speed in $\text{km/h}$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | To convert $\text{m/s}$ to $\text{km/h}$, multiply by $3.6$. | $$10\text{ m/s} = 10 \times 3.6 = 36\text{ km/h}$$ |
| 2 | Convert $2\text{ hours } 15\text{ minutes}$ to hours: $15\text{ minutes} = \frac{15}{60} = 0.25\text{ hours}$, so $T = 2.25\text{ hours}$. Divide $D = 180\text{ km}$ by $T = 2.25\text{ h}$. | $$S = \frac{D}{T} = \frac{180}{2.25} = 80\text{ km/h}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $36\text{ km/h}$, (b) $80\text{ km/h}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: (a) Convert $90\text{ km/h}$ into $\text{m/s}$. (b) How far does a car travelling at an average speed of $96\text{ km/h}$ travel in $1\text{ hour } 45\text{ minutes}$?
> *Hint*: Divide $90$ by $3.6$ for (a), and write $1\text{ h } 45\text{ min} = 1.75\text{ h}$ for (b).
> **Working**: (a) $90 \div 3.6 = 25\text{ m/s}$; (b) $D = 96 \times 1.75 = 168\text{ km}$.
> **Answer**: (a) $25\text{ m/s}$, (b) $168\text{ km}$

### WE4_2_3: Australian Highway Fuel Consumption Modelling

**Problem**: A family drives $650\text{ km}$ along the Hume Highway from Melbourne to Canberra. Their car has an average fuel consumption of $6.8\text{ L/100 km}$, and unleaded petrol costs $\$2.00\text{ per litre}$. Find (a) the total volume of fuel used and (b) the total fuel cost for the trip.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Express $650\text{ km}$ as $6.5$ lots of $100\text{ km}$ and multiply by $6.8\text{ L/100 km}$. | $$\text{Fuel used} = \frac{650}{100} \times 6.8 = 6.5 \times 6.8 = 44.2\text{ L}$$ |
| 2 | Multiply the $44.2\text{ L}$ of fuel by $\$2.00\text{/L}$. | $$\text{Cost} = 44.2 \times 2.00 = \$88.40$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $44.2\text{ L}$, (b) $\$88.40$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: A ute uses $49.5\text{ L}$ of diesel to travel $550\text{ km}$ across outback Victoria. Calculate its fuel consumption rate in $\text{L/100 km}$.
> *Hint*: Divide $49.5\text{ L}$ by $5.5$ (the number of hundreds of kilometres).
> **Working**: $49.5 \div 5.5 = 9\text{ L/100 km}$.
> **Answer**: $9\text{ L/100 km}$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Writing Hours and Minutes Directly as a Base-10 Decimal
- **The Misconception**: Writing $2\text{ hours } 30\text{ minutes}$ as $2.3\text{ hours}$, or $1\text{ hour } 15\text{ minutes}$ as $1.15\text{ hours}$, when calculating $D = S \times T$.
- **The Correct Concept**: There are $60\text{ minutes}$ in $1\text{ hour}$, not $100$. Always divide the minutes by $60$: $30\text{ min} = \frac{30}{60} = 0.5\text{ h}$ (so $2.5\text{ h}$), and $15\text{ min} = \frac{15}{60} = 0.25\text{ h}$ (so $1.25\text{ h}$).
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$2\text{ h } 30\text{ min} = 2.3\text{ h} \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$2\text{ h } 30\text{ min} = 2 + \frac{30}{60} = 2.5\text{ h} \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: At $100\text{ km/h}$ for $2\text{ hours } 30\text{ minutes}$, you travel $250\text{ km}$ ($100 \times 2.5$), not $230\text{ km}$.

### Averaging Two Speeds Instead of Dividing Total Distance by Total Time
- **The Misconception**: If a cyclist rides $30\text{ km}$ uphill at $15\text{ km/h}$ and $30\text{ km}$ back downhill at $30\text{ km/h}$, assuming the average speed for the whole trip is $\frac{15 + 30}{2} = 22.5\text{ km/h}$.
- **The Correct Concept**: Because the cyclist spends more time travelling at the slower uphill speed ($2\text{ h}$ uphill vs $1\text{ h}$ downhill), average speed is always $\frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}} = \frac{60\text{ km}}{3\text{ h}} = 20\text{ km/h}$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\text{Average Speed} = \frac{15 + 30}{2} = 22.5\text{ km/h} \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\text{Average Speed} = \frac{30 + 30}{2 + 1} = \frac{60}{3} = 20\text{ km/h} \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: The total trip of $60\text{ km}$ took $3\text{ hours}$ in total, which is exactly $20\text{ km}$ per hour.

#### Question `4_2_Q6` [Medium] *(Unit Pricing)*

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `4_2_Q7` [Medium] *(Victorian Regional Driving)*
A motorist drives from Melbourne to Warrnambool: the first $180\text{ km}$ takes $2\text{ hours}$, and the remaining $80\text{ km}$ takes $1\text{ hour } 15\text{ minutes}$. Find the motorist's overall average speed for the entire journey in $\text{km/h}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Total distance $= 180 + 80 = 260\text{ km}$.
2. Total time $= 2 + 1.25 = 3.25\text{ hours}$.
3. Overall average speed $= 260 \div 3.25 = 80\text{ km/h}$.

**Final Answer**: $80\text{ km/h}$
</details>

#### Question `4_2_Q8` [Medium] *(Combined Flow Rates)*
Two taps are used together to fill an $1800\text{ L}$ spa pool. Tap A flows at $18\text{ L/min}$ and Tap B flows at $12\text{ L/min}$. How many minutes will it take to fill the pool when both taps are turned on simultaneously?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Add the two flow rates to find the combined rate: $18 + 12 = 30\text{ L/min}$.
2. Divide the total volume by the combined rate: $1800 \div 30 = 60\text{ minutes}$.

**Final Answer**: $60\text{ minutes}$ ($1\text{ hour}$)
</details>

#### Question `4_2_Q9` [Challenging] *(Fuel Efficiency Savings)*
A hybrid SUV uses $5.2\text{ L/100 km}$ while a conventional petrol SUV uses $8.7\text{ L/100 km}$. If a Melbourne driver travels $15\,000\text{ km}$ per year and petrol costs $\$2.00\text{/L}$, how much money in fuel costs does the hybrid SUV save per year?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Fuel saved per $100\text{ km}$: $8.7 - 5.2 = 3.5\text{ L/100 km}$.
2. Over $15\,000\text{ km}$ ($150$ lots of $100\text{ km}$), fuel saved $= 150 \times 3.5 = 525\text{ L}$.
3. Dollar savings at $\$2.00\text{/L}$: $525 \times 2.00 = \$1050$.

**Final Answer**: $\$1050$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `4_2_Q10` [Medium] *(Dimensional Analysis Proof)*
Prove from first principles (using $1\text{ km} = 1000\text{ m}$ and $1\text{ h} = 3600\text{ s}$) why converting from $\text{m/s}$ to $\text{km/h}$ requires multiplying by $3.6$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. An object moving at $v\text{ m/s}$ travels $v\text{ metres}$ in $1\text{ second}$.
2. In $1\text{ hour}$ ($3600\text{ seconds}$), it travels $3600v\text{ metres}$.
3. Dividing by $1000$ to convert metres to kilometres gives $\frac{3600v}{1000} = 3.6v\text{ km/h}$.

**Final Answer**: $1\text{ m/s} = 3600\text{ m/h} = \frac{3600}{1000}\text{ km/h} = 3.6\text{ km/h}$
</details>

#### Question `4_2_Q11` [Challenging] *(Relative Speed & Pursuit)*
Train A leaves Southern Cross Station at $9\text{:00 am}$ travelling west at $80\text{ km/h}$. Train B leaves the same station at $9\text{:30 am}$ on a parallel track travelling west at $100\text{ km/h}$. At what time does Train B catch up to Train A, and how far from the station are they?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. By $9\text{:30 am}$ ($0.5\text{ h}$), Train A has a head start of $80 \times 0.5 = 40\text{ km}$.
2. Train B closes the gap at a relative speed of $100 - 80 = 20\text{ km/h}$.
3. Time to close the $40\text{ km}$ gap: $40 \div 20 = 2\text{ hours}$ after $9\text{:30 am}$, which is $11\text{:30 am}$.
4. Distance from station: $2 \times 100 = 200\text{ km}$.

**Final Answer**: $11\text{:30 am}$ at $200\text{ km}$ from the station
</details>

#### Question `4_2_Q12` [Extension] *(Harmonic Work Rates)*
Painter A can paint a fence in $6\text{ hours}$ alone, and Painter B can paint the same fence in $4\text{ hours}$ alone. By expressing their work rates in 'fences per hour', find how many hours and minutes it takes them to paint $1$ fence working together.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Painter A's rate is $\frac{1}{6}$ fence/hour and Painter B's rate is $\frac{1}{4}$ fence/hour.
2. Combined rate $= \frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{2 + 3}{12} = \frac{5}{12}$ fences/hour.
3. Time to paint $1$ fence $= 1 \div \frac{5}{12} = \frac{12}{5} = 2\frac{2}{5}\text{ hours} = 2\text{ hours } 24\text{ minutes}$.

**Final Answer**: $2\dfrac{2}{5}\text{ hours}$ ($2\text{ hours } 24\text{ minutes}$)
</details>
