**Target Mastery**: Factorise monic quadratic trinomials x^2 + bx + c by finding factor pairs of c that sum to b.

**Prerequisite Skills**:

- Directed number factors

### Key Vocabulary
- **Monic quadratic trinomial**: A quadratic expression of the form $x^2 + bx + c$ whose leading coefficient (the coefficient of $x^2$) is equal to $1$.
- **Sum-and-product factorisation**: Factorising $x^2 + bx + c$ into $(x + m)(x + n)$ by finding two numbers $m$ and $n$ whose product is $mn = c$ and whose sum is $m + n = b$.
- **Perfect square trinomial**: A quadratic trinomial of the form $x^2 \pm 2ax + a^2$ that factorises into the repeated linear factor $(x \pm a)^2$.
- **Algebraic fraction simplification**: Cancelling common binomial factors between the numerator and denominator of a rational expression after both have been fully factorised.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### The Sum-and-Product Method for Monic Quadratics

Since expanding $(x + m)(x + n)$ yields $x^2 + (m + n)x + mn$, factorising a monic quadratic trinomial $x^2 + bx + c$ is the reverse puzzle: find two integers $m$ and $n$ that multiply to give the constant term $c$ ($mn = c$) and add to give the middle coefficient $b$ ($m + n = b$). Always list factor pairs of $c$ systematically rather than guessing at random.

$$x^2 + bx + c = (x + m)(x + n) \quad \text{where } m \times n = c \text{ and } m + n = b$$

> **💡 Test Factor Pairs of c First, Not Pairs That Sum to b**
> There are infinitely many integer pairs that add to $b$, but only a few integer factor pairs that multiply to $c$. Always start from the factors of $c$.

### Using the Signs of b and c to Determine Factor Signs

The signs of $c$ and $b$ immediately tell you the signs of the two numbers $m$ and $n$: (1) If $c > 0$ and $b > 0$, both $m$ and $n$ are positive. (2) If $c > 0$ and $b < 0$, both $m$ and $n$ are negative (since a negative times a negative is positive, and their sum is negative). (3) If $c < 0$, one factor is positive and the other is negative, and the factor with the larger absolute value has the same sign as $b$.

$$x^2 - 7x + 12 = (x - 3)(x - 4), \quad x^2 - 4x - 12 = (x - 6)(x + 2)$$

> **💡 Sign Shortcut for Factor Pairs (m, n)**
> If $c > 0$, $m$ and $n$ have the SAME sign (both match the sign of $b$). If $c < 0$, $m$ and $n$ have OPPOSITE signs (the larger one matches the sign of $b$).

### Extracting a Common Factor Before Trinomial Factorisation

When a quadratic trinomial $ax^2 + bx + c$ has a common numerical or algebraic factor across all three terms (such as $2x^2 - 10x - 28$), factor out the HCF first. If the remaining bracketed quadratic is monic, apply the sum-and-product method to complete the factorisation.

$$2x^2 - 10x - 28 = 2(x^2 - 5x - 14) = 2(x - 7)(x + 2)$$

> **💡 Factor Out a Negative Coefficient When x^2 Is Negative**
> If the $x^2$ term is negative, such as $-x^2 + 3x + 10$, factor out $-1$ first to make the bracketed trinomial monic: $-(x^2 - 3x - 10) = -(x - 5)(x + 2)$.

### WE9_3_3_1: Factorising Monic Trinomials with a Positive Constant Term

**Problem**: Factorise: (a) $x^2 + 9x + 20$ and (b) $x^2 - 11x + 24$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), since $c = +20 > 0$ and $b = +9 > 0$, look for two positive factors of $20$ that add to $9$: $4 \times 5 = 20$ and $4 + 5 = 9$. | $$x^2 + 9x + 20 = (x + 4)(x + 5)$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), since $c = +24 > 0$ and $b = -11 < 0$, look for two negative factors of $24$ that add to $-11$: $(-3) \times (-8) = +24$ and $(-3) + (-8) = -11$. | $$x^2 - 11x + 24 = (x - 3)(x - 8)$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $(x + 4)(x + 5)$, (b) $(x - 3)(x - 8)$

> **Working**: (a) $(x + 3)(x + 7)$; (b) $(x - 3)(x - 6)$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), $c = -32 < 0$, so the factors have opposite signs with the larger one positive: $+8 \times (-4) = -32$ and $+8 + (-4) = +4$. | $$x^2 + 4x - 32 = (x + 8)(x - 4)$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), $c = -36 < 0$ and $b = -5 < 0$, so the larger factor is negative: $(-9) \times (+4) = -36$ and $(-9) + 4 = -5$. | $$x^2 - 5x - 36 = (x - 9)(x + 4)$$ |

### WE9_3_3_3: Multi-Step Factorisation and Simplifying Algebraic Fractions

**Problem**: (a) Factorise completely $3x^2 - 6x - 45$. (b) Hence simplify the algebraic fraction $\frac{3x^2 - 6x - 45}{x^2 - 25}$ (for $x \neq \pm 5$).

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), extract the HCF of $3$ from all three terms, then factorise the monic trinomial $x^2 - 2x - 15$ using $(-5) \times (+3) = -15$ and $-5 + 3 = -2$. | $$3x^2 - 6x - 45 = 3(x^2 - 2x - 15) = 3(x - 5)(x + 3)$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), factorise the denominator $x^2 - 25 = (x - 5)(x + 5)$ by DOTS and cancel the common binomial factor $(x - 5)$. | $$\frac{3x^2 - 6x - 45}{x^2 - 25} = \frac{3(x - 5)(x + 3)}{(x - 5)(x + 5)} = \frac{3(x + 3)}{x + 5}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $3(x - 5)(x + 3)$, (b) $\frac{3(x + 3)}{x + 5}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: (a) Factorise completely $2x^2 + 4x - 30$. (b) Hence simplify $\frac{2x^2 + 4x - 30}{x^2 - 9}$ (for $x \neq \pm 3$).
> *Hint*: Extract $2$ first to get $2(x^2 + 2x - 15)$.
> **Working**: (a) $2(x + 5)(x - 3)$; (b) $\frac{2(x + 5)(x - 3)}{(x - 3)(x + 3)} = \frac{2(x + 5)}{x + 3}$.
> **Answer**: (a) $2(x + 5)(x - 3)$, (b) $\frac{2(x + 5)}{x + 3}$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Reversing the Signs of the Factor Pair When c Is Negative
- **The Misconception**: Factorising $x^2 - 4x - 21$ as $(x + 7)(x - 3)$ instead of $(x - 7)(x + 3)$.
- **The Correct Concept**: Because the middle coefficient $b = -4$ is negative, the factor with the larger magnitude ($7$) must be negative: $(-7) + 3 = -4$, whereas $+7 + (-3) = +4$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$x^2 - 4x - 21 = (x + 7)(x - 3) \quad (\text{Incorrect — expands to } x^2 + 4x - 21)$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$x^2 - 4x - 21 = (x - 7)(x + 3) \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: If $x = 7$, $7^2 - 4(7) - 21 = 49 - 28 - 21 = 0$, matching $(7 - 7)(7 + 3) = 0$, whereas $(7 + 7)(7 - 3) = 56 \neq 0$.

### Cancelling Individual Terms Across Addition in Algebraic Fractions
- **The Misconception**: Cancelling $x^2$ from top and bottom of $\frac{x^2 + 5x + 6}{x^2 - 4}$ to get $\frac{5x + 6}{-4}$.
- **The Correct Concept**: You can only cancel common FACTORS that multiply the entire numerator and denominator, never individual terms added or subtracted. Always factorise numerator and denominator first.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\frac{x^2 + 5x + 6}{x^2 - 4} = \frac{5x + 6}{-4} \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\frac{x^2 + 5x + 6}{x^2 - 4} = \frac{(x + 2)(x + 3)}{(x + 2)(x - 2)} = \frac{x + 3}{x - 2} \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: For $x = 3$, $\frac{9 + 15 + 6}{9 - 4} = \frac{30}{5} = 6$, which matches $\frac{3 + 3}{3 - 2} = 6$, whereas $\frac{15 + 6}{-4} = -5.25$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find two negative integers that multiply to $+40$ and add to $-13$: $-5$ and $-8$.

#### Question `9_3_3_Q2` [Easy] *(Perfect Square Trinomial)*
Factorise $x^2 - 14x + 49$ as a perfect square.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Note that $(-7) \times (-7) = 49$ and $(-7) + (-7) = -14$.
2. Therefore $x^2 - 14x + 49 = (x - 7)(x - 7) = (x - 7)^2$.

**Final Answer**: $(x - 7)^2$
</details>

Factorise completely $4x^2 + 12x - 72$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Extract the common numerical factor $4$: $4(x^2 + 3x - 18)$.
2. Find factors of $-18$ that add to $+3$ ($+6$ and $-3$): $4(x + 6)(x - 3)$.

**Final Answer**: $4(x + 6)(x - 3)$
</details>

**Final Answer**: $-(x - 7)(x + 5)$

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `9_3_3_Q5` [Medium] *(Rectangular Area and Perimeter)*
A rectangular tennis court enclosure in Geelong has an area modelled by $(x^2 + 14x + 45)\text{ m}^2$. Factorise this expression to find binomial expressions for the length and width of the enclosure, and calculate the perimeter when $x = 15\text{ m}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Since $9 \times 5 = 45$ and $9 + 5 = 14$, $x^2 + 14x + 45 = (x + 9)(x + 5)$.
2. Perimeter $P = 2((x + 9) + (x + 5)) = 2(2x + 14) = 4x + 28\text{ m}$.
3. When $x = 15$, $P = 4(15) + 28 = 60 + 28 = 88\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: Dimensions $(x + 9)\text{ m}$ by $(x + 5)\text{ m}$; Perimeter $= 88\text{ m}$
</details>

#### Question `9_3_3_Q6` [Medium] *(3D Prism Dimensions from Cubic Polynomial)*
A rectangular prism water tank has volume $V = (x^3 - 3x^2 - 10x)\text{ m}^3$ for $x > 5$. Factorise $V$ completely to find algebraic expressions for its three dimensions (height, width, and length), and evaluate the volume when $x = 8$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Extract the common pronumeral factor $x$: $V = x(x^2 - 3x - 10)$.
2. Factorise the trinomial using $-5$ and $+2$: $V = x(x - 5)(x + 2)$.
3. When $x = 8$, $V = 8(8 - 5)(8 + 2) = 8 \times 3 \times 10 = 240\text{ m}^3$.

**Final Answer**: $x(x - 5)(x + 2)$; Volume $= 240\text{ m}^3$ when $x = 8$
</details>

#### Question `9_3_3_Q7` [Challenging] *(Multiplying Rational Expressions)*
Simplify the product of algebraic fractions $\frac{x^2 - x - 12}{x^2 - 9} \times \frac{x^2 + 5x + 6}{x^2 - 2x - 8}$ by factorising all four quadratics first.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Factorise each numerator and denominator: $\frac{(x - 4)(x + 3)}{(x - 3)(x + 3)} \times \frac{(x + 2)(x + 3)}{(x - 4)(x + 2)}$.
2. Cancel the common factors $(x - 4)$, $(x + 2)$, and one pair of $(x + 3)$ to obtain $\frac{x + 3}{x - 3}$.

**Final Answer**: $\frac{x + 3}{x - 3}$
</details>

#### Question `9_3_3_Q8` [Challenging] *(Perfect Square Trinomial Plus DOTS)*
A square courtyard has area $(x^2 + 16x + 64)\text{ m}^2$. A smaller square fountain of area $9\text{ m}^2$ is built inside it. Write the remaining unpaved area $(x^2 + 16x + 64) - 9$ as a difference of two squares and factorise it completely.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Recognise $x^2 + 16x + 64 = (x + 8)^2$ as a perfect square trinomial.
2. Rewrite the remaining area as $(x + 8)^2 - 3^2$ and apply DOTS: $(x + 8 - 3)(x + 8 + 3) = (x + 5)(x + 11)$.

**Final Answer**: $(x + 5)(x + 11)$
</details>

#### Question `9_3_3_Q9` [Medium] *(Exploring Factorisable Trinomials)*
Find all possible integer values of the middle coefficient $b$ such that $x^2 + bx + 12$ can be factorised into $(x + m)(x + n)$ with integer constants $m$ and $n$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. List all positive integer factor pairs of $12$: $(1, 12)$, $(2, 6)$, and $(3, 4)$, which sum to $13$, $8$, and $7$ respectively.
2. Including the corresponding negative factor pairs $(-1, -12)$, $(-2, -6)$, and $(-3, -4)$ gives $b = \pm 7, \pm 8, \pm 13$.

**Final Answer**: $b \in \{\pm 7, \pm 8, \pm 13\}$
</details>

#### Question `9_3_3_Q10` [Easy] *(Completing the Perfect Square)*
Find the positive constant $k$ that makes $x^2 - 18x + k$ a perfect square trinomial, and write its factorised form.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. In a monic perfect square $x^2 - 2ax + a^2 = (x - a)^2$, half the coefficient of $x$ is $\frac{-18}{2} = -9$.
2. Square $-9$ to find $k = (-9)^2 = 81$, giving $(x - 9)^2$.

**Final Answer**: $k = 81$; $(x - 9)^2$
</details>

#### Question `9_3_3_Q11` [Easy] *(Prime Quadratics Over the Integers)*
Explain why the quadratic trinomial $x^2 + 3x + 5$ cannot be factorised into $(x + m)(x + n)$ using integers $m$ and $n$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Since $5$ is prime, the only integer pairs $(m, n)$ with $mn = 5$ are $(1, 5)$ and $(-1, -5)$.
2. Their sums are $1 + 5 = 6 \neq 3$ and $-1 + (-5) = -6 \neq 3$, so no integer factorisation exists.

**Final Answer**: The only integer factor pairs of $+5$ are $(1, 5)$ and $(-1, -5)$, which sum to $+6$ and $-6$, never $+3$
</details>

#### Question `9_3_3_Q12` [Extension] *(Quartic Reducible to a Quadratic)*
By letting $u = x^2$, factorise the quartic (degree-$4$) polynomial $x^4 - 13x^2 + 36$ completely into four linear factors.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute $u = x^2$ to obtain the monic quadratic $u^2 - 13u + 36 = (u - 4)(u - 9)$.
2. Replace $u$ with $x^2$: $(x^2 - 4)(x^2 - 9)$.
3. Apply DOTS to each bracket: $(x - 2)(x + 2)(x - 3)(x + 3)$.

**Final Answer**: $(x - 2)(x + 2)(x - 3)(x + 3)$
</details>
