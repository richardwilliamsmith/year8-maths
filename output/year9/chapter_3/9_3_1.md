### Key Vocabulary
- **Binomial product**: The product of two two-term algebraic expressions, such as $(x + a)(x + b)$ or $(ax + b)(cx + d)$.
- **Quadratic trinomial**: A degree-$2$ polynomial with three terms of the form $ax^2 + bx + c$ ($a \neq 0$), called monic when the leading coefficient is $a = 1$.
- **Perfect square expansion**: The square of a binomial $(a \pm b)^2 = a^2 \pm 2ab + b^2$, which always produces a middle term equal to twice the product of the two terms.
- **Difference of two squares (DOTS)**: The product of a sum and difference of the same two terms, $(a - b)(a + b) = a^2 - b^2$, where the opposite middle terms $-ab$ and $+ab$ cancel to zero.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Expanding Binomial Products Using the Distributive Law

Multiplying two binomials $(a + b)(c + d)$ applies the distributive law twice: multiply every term in the first bracket by every term in the second bracket, producing four partial products (First, Outer, Inner, Last — FOIL), and then collect like terms. For monic binomials $(x + a)(x + b)$, the middle $x$-coefficient is the sum $(a + b)$ and the constant term is the product $ab$.

$$(a + b)(c + d) = a(c + d) + b(c + d) = ac + ad + bc + bd, \quad (x + a)(x + b) = x^2 + (a + b)x + ab$$

> **💡 Sum for the Middle Coefficient, Product for the Constant**
> In $(x + 5)(x - 3)$, the middle coefficient is $5 + (-3) = +2$ and the constant term is $5 \times (-3) = -15$, giving $x^2 + 2x - 15$ directly.

### Perfect Square Identities

Geometrically, a square of side length $(a + b)$ splits into two squares of areas $a^2$ and $b^2$ plus two congruent rectangles each of area $ab$. Therefore, squaring a binomial $(a \pm b)^2$ always produces THREE terms: the square of the first term, plus or minus twice the product of the two terms, plus the square of the last term (which is always positive because $(-b)^2 = +b^2$).

$$(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2, \quad (a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$$

> **💡 Never Forget the Middle Term 2ab**
> $(a + b)^2$ means $(a + b)(a + b) = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$. The last term $+b^2$ is always positive whether the bracket is $(a + b)^2$ or $(a - b)^2$.

### The Difference of Two Squares Identity

When expanding $(a - b)(a + b)$, the outer product $+ab$ and inner product $-ab$ are additive inverses and cancel completely, leaving only the square of the first term minus the square of the second term. This identity also enables rapid mental arithmetic for products symmetric about a round number, such as $47 \times 53 = (50 - 3)(50 + 3) = 2500 - 9 = 2491$.

$$(a - b)(a + b) = (a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2$$

> **💡 Conjugate Binomials Cancel the Middle Term**
> Whenever two brackets have the exact same first terms and opposite signs on the second terms, use $(a - b)(a + b) = a^2 - b^2$ immediately.

## Worked Examples

### WE9_3_1_1: Expanding Monic and Non-Monic Binomial Products

**Problem**: Expand and simplify: (a) $(x - 7)(x + 4)$ and (b) $(3x - 2)(2x + 5)$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), multiply each term in $(x - 7)$ by each term in $(x + 4)$ and collect the middle like terms. | $$(x - 7)(x + 4) = x^2 + 4x - 7x - 28 = x^2 - 3x - 28$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), apply the distributive law $3x(2x + 5) - 2(2x + 5)$ and collect like terms in $x$. | $$(3x - 2)(2x + 5) = 6x^2 + 15x - 4x - 10 = 6x^2 + 11x - 10$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $x^2 - 3x - 28$, (b) $6x^2 + 11x - 10$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Expand and simplify: (a) $(x + 6)(x - 9)$ and (b) $(4x - 3)(2x - 5)$.
> *Hint*: In (b), note that $(-3) \times (-5) = +15$.
> **Working**: (a) $x^2 - 9x + 6x - 54 = x^2 - 3x - 54$; (b) $8x^2 - 20x - 6x + 15 = 8x^2 - 26x + 15$.
> **Answer**: (a) $x^2 - 3x - 54$, (b) $8x^2 - 26x + 15$

### WE9_3_1_2: Expanding Perfect Squares and Differences of Two Squares

**Problem**: Expand using special identities: (a) $(3x - 5)^2$ and (b) $(4m + 7n)(4m - 7n)$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), apply $(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$ with $a = 3x$ and $b = 5$. | $$(3x - 5)^2 = (3x)^2 - 2(3x)(5) + 5^2 = 9x^2 - 30x + 25$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), apply $(a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2$ with $a = 4m$ and $b = 7n$. | $$(4m + 7n)(4m - 7n) = (4m)^2 - (7n)^2 = 16m^2 - 49n^2$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $9x^2 - 30x + 25$, (b) $16m^2 - 49n^2$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Expand: (a) $(2y + 7)^2$ and (b) $(5a - 3b)(5a + 3b)$.
> *Hint*: Square the entire term $(2y)^2 = 4y^2$ and $(5a)^2 = 25a^2$.
> **Working**: (a) $(2y)^2 + 2(2y)(7) + 7^2 = 4y^2 + 28y + 49$; (b) $(5a)^2 - (3b)^2 = 25a^2 - 9b^2$.
> **Answer**: (a) $4y^2 + 28y + 49$, (b) $25a^2 - 9b^2$

### WE9_3_1_3: Composite Expressions with Subtracted Binomial Products

**Problem**: Expand and simplify $(x + 4)^2 - (x - 3)(x + 5)$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Expand both products, keeping the second expansion inside brackets so the subtraction sign applies to every term of $(x - 3)(x + 5)$. | $$(x + 4)^2 - (x - 3)(x + 5) = (x^2 + 8x + 16) - (x^2 + 2x - 15)$$ |
| 2 | Distribute the minus sign across the second bracket and collect like terms. | $$= x^2 + 8x + 16 - x^2 - 2x + 15 = 6x + 31$$ |

**Answer**: $6x + 31$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Expand and simplify $(x - 5)^2 - (x - 2)(x + 2)$.
> *Hint*: Keep $(x^2 - 4)$ in brackets before subtracting: $-(x^2 - 4) = -x^2 + 4$.
> **Working**: $(x^2 - 10x + 25) - (x^2 - 4) = x^2 - 10x + 25 - x^2 + 4 = -10x + 29$.
> **Answer**: $-10x + 29$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Omitting the Middle Term 2ab When Squaring a Binomial
- **The Misconception**: Writing $(x + 5)^2 = x^2 + 25$ or $(x - 5)^2 = x^2 - 25$ (the 'freshman's dream' error).
- **The Correct Concept**: Exponents do not distribute over addition or subtraction. Expanding $(x + 5)(x + 5)$ gives two cross-product rectangles of area $5x$ each, so $(x + 5)^2 = x^2 + 10x + 25$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$(x + 5)^2 = x^2 + 25 \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$(x + 5)^2 = x^2 + 2(x)(5) + 5^2 = x^2 + 10x + 25 \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: If $x = 3$, $(3 + 5)^2 = 8^2 = 64$, whereas $3^2 + 5^2 = 9 + 25 = 34 \neq 64$.

### Failing to Bracket a Subtracted Binomial Product
- **The Misconception**: Expanding $x^2 - (x + 2)(x + 3)$ as $x^2 - x^2 + 5x + 6 = 5x + 6$, negating only the first term of the product.
- **The Correct Concept**: The subtraction sign applies to the entire expanded trinomial $(x^2 + 5x + 6)$, flipping every sign inside: $x^2 - (x^2 + 5x + 6) = -5x - 6$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$x^2 - (x + 2)(x + 3) = x^2 - x^2 + 5x + 6 = 5x + 6 \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$x^2 - (x + 2)(x + 3) = x^2 - (x^2 + 5x + 6) = -5x - 6 \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: For $x = 1$, $1^2 - (3)(4) = 1 - 12 = -11$, which matches $-5(1) - 6 = -11$, not $5(1) + 6 = 11$.

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `9_3_1_Q5` [Medium] *(Path Border Area Modelling)*
A square lawn in a suburban Melbourne park has side length $x\text{ m}$. It is surrounded on all four sides by a uniform gravel path of width $3\text{ m}$. Write an expanded expression for the area of the gravel path alone, and find this area when $x = 10\text{ m}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The outer square including the path on both sides has side length $(x + 6)\text{ m}$.
2. Subtract the inner lawn area: $A_{\text{path}} = (x + 6)^2 - x^2 = (x^2 + 12x + 36) - x^2 = 12x + 36\text{ m}^2$.
3. When $x = 10$, $A_{\text{path}} = 12(10) + 36 = 156\text{ m}^2$.

**Final Answer**: $12x + 36\text{ m}^2$; $156\text{ m}^2$ when $x = 10$
</details>

#### Question `9_3_1_Q6` [Medium] *(Area Comparison via DOTS)*
A square vegetable patch of side length $x\text{ m}$ is redesigned into a rectangle by increasing its length by $5\text{ m}$ and decreasing its width by $5\text{ m}$. Prove algebraically that the new rectangular patch always has $25\text{ m}^2$ less area than the original square patch.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The original square has area $x^2\text{ m}^2$, and the new rectangle has dimensions $(x + 5)\text{ m}$ by $(x - 5)\text{ m}$.
2. By the difference of two squares, $A_{\text{new}} = (x + 5)(x - 5) = x^2 - 25\text{ m}^2$, which is exactly $25\text{ m}^2$ less than $x^2$.

**Final Answer**: $A_{\text{new}} = (x + 5)(x - 5) = x^2 - 25$, which is always $25\text{ m}^2$ less than $x^2$
</details>

#### Question `9_3_1_Q7` [Medium] *(Open-Top Box Base Area)*
A rectangular sheet of cardboard measures $20\text{ cm}$ by $14\text{ cm}$. Equal squares of side length $x\text{ cm}$ are cut from each of the four corners so the sides can be folded up to form an open-top box. Find an expanded quadratic expression for the base area of the box in terms of $x$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Cutting $x\text{ cm}$ from both ends reduces the length to $(20 - 2x)\text{ cm}$ and the width to $(14 - 2x)\text{ cm}$.
2. Expand the base area: $A = (20 - 2x)(14 - 2x) = 280 - 40x - 28x + 4x^2 = 4x^2 - 68x + 280\text{ cm}^2$.

**Final Answer**: $4x^2 - 68x + 280\text{ cm}^2$
</details>

#### Question `9_3_1_Q8` [Challenging] *(Geometric Area Difference)*
A right-angled triangle has legs of length $(x + 3)\text{ cm}$ and $(x - 4)\text{ cm}$, and a second rectangle has dimensions $x\text{ cm}$ by $(x - 6)\text{ cm}$. Find an expanded expression for the difference between the rectangle's area and twice the triangle's area: $x(x - 6) - (x + 3)(x - 4)$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Expand both expressions: $x(x - 6) = x^2 - 6x$ and $(x + 3)(x - 4) = x^2 - x - 12$.
2. Subtract carefully using brackets: $(x^2 - 6x) - (x^2 - x - 12) = x^2 - 6x - x^2 + x + 12 = -5x + 12\text{ cm}^2$.

**Final Answer**: $-5x + 12\text{ cm}^2$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `9_3_1_Q9` [Easy] *(Mental Arithmetic via DOTS)*
Show how the difference of two squares identity $(a - b)(a + b) = a^2 - b^2$ can be used to evaluate $96 \times 104$ mentally.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Rewrite $96$ and $104$ symmetrically around $100$: $96 \times 104 = (100 - 4)(100 + 4)$.
2. Apply $a^2 - b^2$: $100^2 - 4^2 = 10\,000 - 16 = 9984$.

**Final Answer**: $(100 - 4)(100 + 4) = 10\,000 - 16 = 9984$
</details>

#### Question `9_3_1_Q10` [Medium] *(Binomial Products with Surds)*
Expand and simplify $(\sqrt{5} - \sqrt{2})(\sqrt{5} + \sqrt{2})$ and $(2\sqrt{3} - 1)^2$, explaining which expansion produces a rational number and why.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Using DOTS, $(\sqrt{5} - \sqrt{2})(\sqrt{5} + \sqrt{2}) = (\sqrt{5})^2 - (\sqrt{2})^2 = 5 - 2 = 3$, which is rational because the surd cross-terms cancel.
2. Using the perfect square identity, $(2\sqrt{3} - 1)^2 = (2\sqrt{3})^2 - 2(2\sqrt{3})(1) + 1^2 = 12 - 4\sqrt{3} + 1 = 13 - 4\sqrt{3}$, which remains irrational.

**Final Answer**: $(\sqrt{5} - \sqrt{2})(\sqrt{5} + \sqrt{2}) = 3 \in \mathbb{Q}$; $(2\sqrt{3} - 1)^2 = 13 - 4\sqrt{3} \notin \mathbb{Q}$
</details>

#### Question `9_3_1_Q11` [Challenging] *(Number Theory Proof with Perfect Squares)*
Prove that the difference between the squares of any two consecutive integers, $(n + 1)^2 - n^2$, is always an odd integer and equals the sum of the two consecutive integers.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Expand $(n + 1)^2 - n^2 = (n^2 + 2n + 1) - n^2 = 2n + 1$.
2. Since $2n$ is always even for any integer $n$, $2n + 1$ is always odd, and $2n + 1 = n + (n + 1)$ is the sum of the two consecutive integers.

**Final Answer**: $(n + 1)^2 - n^2 = 2n + 1 = n + (n + 1)$, which is always odd
</details>

#### Question `9_3_1_Q12` [Extension] *(Legendre/Euler Perfect Square Identity)*
Verify Euler's identity $(a + b)^2 - (a - b)^2 = 4ab$ by expanding both perfect squares, and use it to find the exact value of $ab$ if $a + b = 9$ and $a - b = 5$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Expand both squares: $(a^2 + 2ab + b^2) - (a^2 - 2ab + b^2) = 4ab$.
2. Substitute $a + b = 9$ and $a - b = 5$: $9^2 - 5^2 = 4ab \implies 81 - 25 = 56 = 4ab \implies ab = 14$.

**Final Answer**: $ab = 14$
</details>
