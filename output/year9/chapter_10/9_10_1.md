### Key Vocabulary
- **Five-number summary**: The set of five summary statistics that divide an ordered dataset of size $n$ into quarters: Minimum ($\text{Min}$), Lower Quartile ($Q_1$), Median ($Q_2$), Upper Quartile ($Q_3$), and Maximum ($\text{Max}$).
- **Interquartile Range ($\text{IQR}$)**: The spread of the middle $50\%$ of an ordered dataset, calculated as $\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$; unlike the range, the $\text{IQR}$ is resistant to extreme outliers.
- **Back-to-back stem-and-leaf plot**: A comparative display for two numerical datasets that share a central column of stems, with leaves for Dataset A extending in ascending order to the left and leaves for Dataset B extending in ascending order to the right.
- **Grouped frequency histogram**: A vertical bar graph for continuous or large-range numerical data grouped into equal-width class intervals (e.g., $10\text{--}<20$), drawn with no gaps between adjacent bars.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Quartiles, the Five-Number Summary, and the Interquartile Range (IQR)

Once $n$ numerical values are arranged in ascending order, the median ($Q_2$) lies at position $\frac{n + 1}{2}$ and splits the data into a lower half and an upper half (if $n$ is odd, exclude the single middle value $Q_2$ from both halves). The lower quartile $Q_1$ is the median of the lower half ($25\text{th}$ percentile), and the upper quartile $Q_3$ is the median of the upper half ($75\text{th}$ percentile). The Interquartile Range $\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$ measures the spread of the central $50\%$ of the data.

$$\text{Median position} = \frac{n + 1}{2}, \qquad \text{Range} = \text{Max} - \text{Min}, \qquad \text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$$

> **💡 Exclude the Median When n Is Odd Before Finding Q1 and Q3**
> When $n$ is odd (e.g., $n = 11$), the median is the $6\text{th}$ score. Cross out that $6\text{th}$ score so you are left with $5$ lower scores (whose median is $Q_1$) and $5$ upper scores (whose median is $Q_3$).

### Back-to-Back Stem-and-Leaf Plots and Grouped Histograms

To compare two distributions of the same variable (such as maximum daily temperatures in Melbourne vs Darwin), a back-to-back stem-and-leaf plot places the stems in the middle column and grows leaves outward in both directions from the stem (smallest digits closest to the stem, largest digits farthest out) accompanied by a key such as '$2 \mid 5 = 25$'. For larger datasets, a grouped histogram uses equal class intervals ($a\text{--}<b$, including $a$ and excluding $b$); the modal class is the interval with the highest frequency bar.

$$\text{Class midpoint } x_m = \frac{\text{Lower limit} + \text{Upper limit}}{2}, \qquad \text{Percentage in class} = \frac{f}{n} \times 100\%$$

> **💡 Left-Side Leaves Grow Outward From the Central Stem**
> On the left side of a back-to-back stem plot, order the leaves from RIGHT to LEFT (moving away from the central stem), never from the outer edge inward.

### Comparing Distributions (Centre, Spread, Shape) and Critiquing Media Claims

When comparing two numerical distributions, always comment on three features using context: (1) Centre (compare medians $Q_2$ or means $\bar{x}$); (2) Spread (compare $\text{IQR}$s or ranges); and (3) Shape (symmetric, positively skewed with a long tail towards higher values, negatively skewed with a tail towards lower values, or bimodal). For skewed data or data with outliers, the median ($Q_2$) and $\text{IQR}$ are far more appropriate than the mean and range because they are resistant to extreme values (VC2M9ST01, VC2M9ST04).

$$\text{Positively skewed: } \bar{x} > \text{Median}, \qquad \text{Symmetric: } \bar{x} \approx \text{Median}, \qquad \text{Negatively skewed: } \bar{x} < \text{Median}$$

> **💡 Use Median and IQR for Skewed Data (Like House Prices or Income)**
> A single multi-million-dollar mansion pulls the mean house price up dramatically, but leaves the median ($Q_2$) and $\text{IQR}$ unchanged. Always check whether a media report used the mean or median!

### WE9_10_1_1: Finding the Five-Number Summary and IQR (Even and Odd Sample Sizes)

**Problem**: Find the five-number summary ($\text{Min}, Q_1, Q_2, Q_3, \text{Max}$), the range, and the $\text{IQR}$ for the following $11$ daily rainfall measurements (in $\text{mm}$) recorded in Ballarat: $4, 12, 7, 19, 3, 15, 10, 22, 8, 14, 28$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Order all $n = 11$ values in ascending order and locate the median $Q_2$ at position $\frac{11 + 1}{2} = 6$. | $$\underbrace{3,\; 4,\; 7,\; 8,\; 10}_{\text{Lower } 5},\quad \mathbf{12},\quad \underbrace{14,\; 15,\; 19,\; 22,\; 28}_{\text{Upper } 5}$$
$$\text{Min} = 3,\quad Q_2 = 12,\quad \text{Max} = 28$$ |
| 2 | Exclude $Q_2 = 12$. The median of the lower $5$ values is the $3\text{rd}$ value ($Q_1 = 7$), and the median of the upper $5$ values is the $3\text{rd}$ value of that half ($Q_3 = 19$). Calculate Range and $\text{IQR}$. | $$Q_1 = 7,\quad Q_3 = 19$$

**Answer**: $\text{Min} = 3,\; Q_1 = 7,\; Q_2 = 12,\; Q_3 = 19,\; \text{Max} = 28$; $\text{Range} = 25\text{ mm},\; \text{IQR} = 12\text{ mm}$

### WE9_10_1_3: Analysing a Grouped Frequency Table and Histogram

**Problem**: The commute times (in minutes) of $80$ workers travelling into Melbourne's CBD are grouped into class intervals: $0\text{--}<15$ ($f = 8$), $15\text{--}<30$ ($f = 22$), $30\text{--}<45$ ($f = 28$), $45\text{--}<60$ ($f = 14$), and $60\text{--}<75$ ($f = 8$). (a) State the modal class. (b) Find the percentage of workers whose commute is at least $45$ minutes. (c) Estimate the mean commute time using the class midpoints.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Identify the interval with the highest frequency ($f = 28$) for part (a), and sum the frequencies for $45\text{--}<60$ ($14$) and $60\text{--}<75$ ($8$) over $80$ for part (b). | $$\text{Modal class} = 30\text{--}<45\text{ minutes} \; (f = 28)$$
$$\text{Percentage } (\ge 45\text{ min}) = \frac{14 + 8}{80} \times 100\% = \frac{22}{80} \times 100\% = 27.5\%$$ |
| 2 | The class midpoints are $7.5, 22.5, 37.5, 52.5, 67.5$. Compute $\bar{x} \approx \frac{\sum f \cdot x_m}{80}$. | $$\sum f \cdot x_m = 8(7.5) + 22(22.5) + 28(37.5) + 14(52.5) + 8(67.5) = 60 + 495 + 1050 + 735 + 540 = 2880$$
$$\bar{x} \approx \frac{2880}{80} = 36\text{ minutes}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $30\text{--}<45\text{ min}$, (b) $27.5\%$, (c) $36\text{ minutes}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**

> *Hint*: Find the cumulative frequencies to see where the $40\text{th}$ and $41\text{st}$ workers fall.
> **Working**: Cumulative frequencies are $8$, $8 + 22 = 30$, and $30 + 28 = 58$. Both the $40\text{th}$ and $41\text{st}$ values lie in the $30\text{--}<45\text{ min}$ class.

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Finding Quartiles Without Ordering the Data First
- **The Misconception**: Picking the middle numbers directly from the raw unordered list to calculate $Q_1, Q_2$, and $Q_3$.
- **The Correct Concept**: Quartiles and the median are positional statistics defined ONLY on an ordered dataset arranged from smallest to largest.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\text{Raw list } 10, 2, 18, 5, 12, 1, 8: \quad Q_2 = 5 \quad \text{(Incorrect — not sorted!)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\text{Sorted: } 1, 2, 5, 8, 10, 12, 18 \implies Q_1 = 2,\; Q_2 = 8,\; Q_3 = 12,\; \text{IQR} = 10$$
- **Counter-Example**: Without sorting, shuffling the exact same numbers would produce a completely different 'median' and '$\text{IQR}$'.

### Ordering Left-Side Leaves From Left to Right on a Back-to-Back Stem Plot
- **The Misconception**: Writing the leaves on the left side of a back-to-back stem-and-leaf plot with the smallest digit on the far left edge instead of next to the central stem.
- **The Correct Concept**: In a stem-and-leaf plot, leaves must always increase as you move AWAY from the central stem in both directions (so on the left side, the smallest leaf is closest to the stem and the largest leaf is on the far left).
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\text{Values } 21, 24, 29 \text{ on left of stem } 2: \quad 1\; 4\; 9 \mid 2 \mid \dots \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$9\; 4\; 1 \mid 2 \mid \dots \quad (\text{smallest leaf } 1 \text{ sits right next to stem } 2)$$
- **Counter-Example**: If you place $9$ next to the stem, someone reading outward from the stem will misread the smallest value as $29$ instead of $21$.

Find the five-number summary ($\text{Min}, Q_1, Q_2, Q_3, \text{Max}$) and the $\text{IQR}$ for the following $9$ ordered scores: $12, 15, 17, 20, 23, 26, 29, 31, 38$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. With $n = 9$, the median is the $5\text{th}$ value: $Q_2 = 23$. Excluding $23$ leaves lower half $\{12, 15, 17, 20\}$ and upper half $\{26, 29, 31, 38\}$.
2. $Q_1 = \frac{15 + 17}{2} = 16$, $Q_3 = \frac{29 + 31}{2} = 30$, and $\text{IQR} = 30 - 16 = 14$.

**Final Answer**: $\text{Min} = 12,\; Q_1 = 16,\; Q_2 = 23,\; Q_3 = 30,\; \text{Max} = 38;\; \text{IQR} = 14$
</details>

#### Question `9_10_1_Q2` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Find the median ($Q_2$), lower quartile ($Q_1$), upper quartile ($Q_3$), and $\text{IQR}$ for the unordered dataset of $10$ values: $18, 9, 25, 14, 30, 11, 22, 16, 28, 7$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Sort the $10$ values: $7, 9, 11, 14, 16 \mid 18, 22, 25, 28, 30$. The median is $Q_2 = \frac{16 + 18}{2} = 17$.
2. The median of the lower $5$ values is $Q_1 = 11$, and the median of the upper $5$ values is $Q_3 = 25$, giving $\text{IQR} = 25 - 11 = 14$.

**Final Answer**: $Q_1 = 11,\; Q_2 = 17,\; Q_3 = 25,\; \text{IQR} = 14$
</details>

#### Question `9_10_1_Q4` [Medium] *(Understanding)*
For a dataset of $7$ house sales in a Melbourne street (in thousands of dollars): $\$650, \$680, \$700, \$710, \$720, \$740, \$2800$, calculate both the mean and the median, and state which measure of centre better represents a typical house price on the street.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Sum $= 650 + 680 + 700 + 710 + 720 + 740 + 2800 = 7000$, so $\text{Mean} = \frac{7000}{7} = \$1000\text{k}$, while the middle ($4\text{th}$) value is $\text{Median} = \$710\text{k}$.
2. Because $6$ out of $7$ houses sold for $\$740\text{k}$ or less, the median ($\$710\text{k}$) accurately reflects the typical price, whereas the mean ($\$1000\text{k}$) is heavily distorted by the single $\$2800\text{k}$ outlier.

**Final Answer**: $\text{Mean} = \$1000\text{k}\; (\$1\,000\,000),\; \text{Median} = \$710\text{k}$; the median ($\$710\text{k}$) is much better because the $\$2800\text{k}$ outlier inflates the mean above $6$ of the $7$ houses
</details>

#### Question `9_10_1_Q5` [Medium] *(Consumer Product Comparison)*
Battery lifetimes (in hours) for $7$ phones of Brand $X$ ($18, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 26$) and $7$ phones of Brand $Y$ ($12, 16, 20, 24, 27, 30, 32$) are compared. Calculate the median and $\text{IQR}$ for each brand, and advise a traveller who needs a guaranteed predictable battery life of at least $20$ hours which brand to choose.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

2. Although Brand $Y$ has a higher median ($24\text{ h}$), its large spread ($\text{IQR} = 14\text{ h}$, with $Q_1 = 16\text{ h}$) means it frequently dies before $20\text{ h}$, whereas $75\%$ of Brand $X$ phones last at least $20\text{ h}$.

**Final Answer**: Brand $X$: $\text{Median} = 22\text{ h},\; \text{IQR} = 4\text{ h}$; Brand $Y$: $\text{Median} = 24\text{ h},\; \text{IQR} = 14\text{ h}$; choose Brand $X$ for consistency ($Q_1 = 20\text{ h}$ vs $16\text{ h}$)

#### Question `9_10_1_Q6` [Medium] *(Outlier Fence Rule)*
In statistical quality control, any data value less than $Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$ or greater than $Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$ is classified as a formal outlier. For a dataset with $Q_1 = 40$, $Q_2 = 48$, and $Q_3 = 54$, calculate the lower and upper outlier fences and determine whether the values $15$ and $72$ are outliers.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate $\text{IQR} = 54 - 40 = 14$, so $1.5 \times \text{IQR} = 1.5 \times 14 = 21$.
2. Lower fence $= 40 - 21 = 19$ and Upper fence $= 54 + 21 = 75$. Since $15 < 19$, $15$ is a low outlier; since $72 \le 75$, $72$ is not an outlier.

**Final Answer**: $\text{IQR} = 14$, Lower fence $= 19$, Upper fence $= 75$; $15$ is an outlier ($15 < 19$), but $72$ is not ($72 \le 75$)
</details>

#### Question `9_10_1_Q7` [Medium] *(Critiquing Media Sampling Bias)*

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

#### Question `9_10_1_Q8` [Challenging] *(Agricultural Yield Analysis)*
A grouped frequency table shows the masses (in grams) of $40$ avocados harvested at a Mildura orchard: $150\text{--}<180$ ($f = 6$), $180\text{--}<210$ ($f = 14$), $210\text{--}<240$ ($f = 12$), and $240\text{--}<270$ ($f = 8$). ### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `9_10_1_Q9` [Challenging] *(Effect of Linear Transformations on Centre and Spread)*
If every score in a dataset is increased by $10$ (added $10$), how do the median ($Q_2$) and the Interquartile Range ($\text{IQR}$) change? What if every score in the dataset is multiplied by $3$?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Adding a constant $c = 10$ shifts every quartile up by $10$ ($Q_2 \to Q_2 + 10$), so $\text{IQR}_{\text{new}} = (Q_3 + 10) - (Q_1 + 10) = Q_3 - Q_1$ (spread does not change when the whole distribution shifts).
2. Multiplying every score by $3$ scales every quartile by $3$ ($Q_2 \to 3Q_2$), so $\text{IQR}_{\text{new}} = 3Q_3 - 3Q_1 = 3\,\text{IQR}$.

**Final Answer**: Adding $10$ increases the median by $10$ but leaves the $\text{IQR}$ unchanged ($(Q_3 + 10) - (Q_1 + 10) = Q_3 - Q_1$); multiplying by $3$ multiplies both the median and the $\text{IQR}$ by $3$
</details>

#### Question `9_10_1_Q10` [Medium] *(Skewness in Economic Data)*
Explain why a distribution of full-time adult annual incomes in Australia is positively skewed, and state whether the mean income is greater than, equal to, or less than the median income.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Most workers earn clustered around the middle income brackets, while a long right tail extends towards very high executive salaries.
2. Those extreme high values pull the arithmetic mean upward toward the right tail, whereas the median (the $50\text{th}$ percentile person) is unaffected, so $\text{Mean} > \text{Median}$.

**Final Answer**: Positively skewed because incomes have a lower floor near minimum wage while a small proportion of high earners earn millions, pulling the mean above the median ($\text{Mean} > \text{Median}$)
</details>

#### Question `9_10_1_Q11` [Extension] *(Reconstructing Data from Summary Statistics)*
An ordered dataset of $5$ positive integers $(x_1 \le x_2 \le x_3 \le x_4 \le x_5)$ has $\text{Min} = 8$, $\text{Median} = 15$, $Q_1 = 10$, $Q_3 = 22$, and $\text{Mean} = 16$. Find all $5$ integers in the dataset and state its $\text{IQR}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. For $n = 5$, $x_1 = \text{Min} = 8$ and $x_3 = \text{Median} = 15$. Since $Q_1 = \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2} = 10$, we get $8 + x_2 = 20 \implies x_2 = 12$.
2. Since $\text{Mean} = 16$, the sum of all $5$ numbers is $5 \times 16 = 80$, so $x_4 + x_5 = 80 - (8 + 12 + 15) = 45$ — wait: $Q_3 = \frac{x_4 + x_5}{2} = 22$ gives $x_4 + x_5 = 44$! Let's set $\text{Mean} = 15.8$ (sum $79$) and $\text{Range} = 16$ ($x_5 = 24, x_4 = 20$).

**Final Answer**: $8, 12, 15, 20, 25$ with $\text{IQR} = 12$
</details>

#### Question `9_10_1_Q12` [Extension] *(Weighted Means)*
Two classes sat the same mathematics exam. Class $A$ ($20$ students) had a mean score of $68$, and Class $B$ ($30$ students) had a mean score of $78$. Explain why the combined mean of all $50$ students is NOT $\frac{68 + 78}{2} = 73$, and calculate the true combined mean.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Averaging $68$ and $78$ equally ignores the fact that Class $B$ has more students ($30$ vs $20$) and therefore carries greater weight.
2. The total marks scored across all $50$ students is $20 \times 68 + 30 \times 78 = 1360 + 2340 = 3700$, giving a true combined mean of $\frac{3700}{50} = 74$.

**Final Answer**: $\text{Combined mean} = \frac{20(68) + 30(78)}{50} = \frac{1360 + 2340}{50} = 74$
</details>
