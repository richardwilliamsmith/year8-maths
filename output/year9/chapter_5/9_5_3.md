### Key Vocabulary
- **Exponential relation**: A non-linear relation of the form $y = a^x$ (with $a > 0, a \neq 1$) in which the independent variable $x$ appears in the exponent.
- **Asymptote**: A straight line that a curve approaches ever more closely as $x$ or $y$ tends to $\pm\infty$, without ever reaching it.
- **Circle relation**: The set of all points $(x, y)$ at a fixed distance (radius $r$) from the origin $(0, 0)$, described by $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$.
- **Bisection algorithm**: An iterative root-finding algorithm that repeatedly halves an interval $[a, b]$ where a continuous function $f(x)$ changes sign.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Comparing Linear, Quadratic, and Exponential Growth ($y = a^x$)

While a linear relation $y = mx + c$ changes by a constant difference and a quadratic $y = ax^2$ changes by a linearly increasing difference, an exponential relation $y = a^x$ ($a > 1$) multiplies by a constant factor $a$ for each $1$-unit increase in $x$. Every basic exponential curve $y = a^x$ passes through $(0, 1)$ (since $a^0 = 1$) and has the horizontal asymptote $y = 0$.

$$y = a^x \quad (a > 1): \quad y\text{-intercept } (0, 1), \quad \text{Horizontal asymptote } y = 0$$

> **💡 Exponential Growth Eventually Overtakes Any Polynomial**
> For $x > 0$ and $a > 1$, $y = a^x$ grows slowly at first but rapidly overtakes both linear and quadratic relations as $x$ increases.

### Circles ($x^2 + y^2 = r^2$) and Reciprocal Hyperbolas ($y = \frac{k}{x}$)

By the distance formula, every point $(x, y)$ at distance $r$ from the origin satisfies $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$, forming a circle of centre $(0, 0)$ and radius $r$ with intercepts $(\pm r, 0)$ and $(0, \pm r)$. A reciprocal relation $y = \frac{k}{x}$ ($x \neq 0$, inverse proportion) forms a rectangular hyperbola with two branches and asymptotes along both coordinate axes ($x = 0$ and $y = 0$).

$$\text{Circle: } x^2 + y^2 = r^2 \qquad \text{Hyperbola: } y = \frac{k}{x} \;\;(x \neq 0,\; y \neq 0)$$

> **💡 Take the Square Root of $r^2$ to Find the Radius**
> In $x^2 + y^2 = 25$, the radius is $r = \sqrt{25} = 5$, not $25$.

### The Bisection Algorithm for Approximating Irrational Roots

When an equation $f(x) = 0$ has an irrational root that cannot be found by integer factorisation, we locate an interval $[a, b]$ where $f(a)$ and $f(b)$ have opposite signs. In each step of the bisection algorithm, we evaluate $f(m)$ at the midpoint $m = \frac{a + b}{2}$ and replace whichever endpoint has the same sign as $f(m)$, halving the interval width each iteration.

$$m = \frac{a + b}{2}: \quad \text{keep } [a, m] \text{ if } f(a)f(m) < 0,\; \text{else keep } [m, b]$$

> **💡 Sign Change Guarantees a Root Inside the Interval**
> If a continuous function changes from negative to positive between $a$ and $b$, it must cross $f(x) = 0$ at least once between $a$ and $b$.

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Using $r^2$ Instead of $r$ as the Radius of a Circle
- **The Misconception**: Seeing $x^2 + y^2 = 36$ and stating that the radius is $36$ or $18$.
- **The Correct Concept**: In the circle equation $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$, the right-hand side is the square of the radius, so $r = \sqrt{36} = 6$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$x^2 + y^2 = 36 \implies r = 36 \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$x^2 + y^2 = 36 \implies r^2 = 36 \implies r = \sqrt{36} = 6$$
- **Counter-Example**: When $y = 0$, $x^2 = 36 \implies x = \pm 6$, so the circle crosses the axes at distance $6$ from the origin.

### Choosing the Wrong Sub-Interval in the Bisection Algorithm
- **The Misconception**: Keeping the sub-interval where both endpoints have the same sign rather than opposite signs.
- **The Correct Concept**: A root is guaranteed between two points where the function values have opposite signs ($f(a) \times f(b) < 0$). Always pair the midpoint with the endpoint of opposite sign.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$f(3) = -2,\; f(3.5) = +0.25,\; f(4) = +3 \implies \text{choose } [3.5, 4] \quad \text{(Incorrect: both positive)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$f(3) = -2 < 0 \text{ and } f(3.5) = +0.25 > 0 \implies \text{choose } [3, 3.5]$$
- **Counter-Example**: The exact positive root of $x^2 - 2x - 5 = 0$ is $1 + \sqrt{6} \approx 3.449$, which lies inside $[3, 3.5]$, not $[3.5, 4]$.

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `9_5_3_Q5` [Medium] *(Biological Growth)*
A bacterial culture in a pathology laboratory in Parkville starts with $500$ bacteria and doubles every hour, so its population after $t$ hours is $P = 500 \times 2^t$. (a) Find the population after $4$ hours. (b) After how many hours does the population reach $32\,000$?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. At $t = 4$: $P = 500 \times 2^4 = 500 \times 16 = 8000$.
2. Set $P = 32\,000$: $500 \times 2^t = 32\,000 \implies 2^t = 64 = 2^6 \implies t = 6\text{ hours}$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $8000$, (b) $t = 6\text{ hours}$
</details>

#### Question `9_5_3_Q6` [Medium] *(Inverse Proportion Modelling)*
The time $T\text{ hours}$ required to pump out a flooded mine shaft varies inversely with the pump flow rate $R\text{ ML/h}$ according to $T = \frac{180}{R}$. (a) How long does it take at $R = 15\text{ ML/h}$? (b) What flow rate is required to empty the shaft in $7.5\text{ hours}$?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute $R = 15$: $T = \frac{180}{15} = 12\text{ hours}$.
2. Substitute $T = 7.5$: $7.5 = \frac{180}{R} \implies R = \frac{180}{7.5} = 24\text{ ML/h}$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $12\text{ hours}$, (b) $24\text{ ML/h}$
</details>

#### Question `9_5_3_Q7` [Challenging] *(Radar Geometry)*
A weather radar at Laverton has a circular range of radius $15\text{ km}$, modelled by $x^2 + y^2 = 225$. A storm cell travels along the straight line $y = x + 3$. Find the coordinates of the two points where the storm cell enters and exits the radar coverage.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute $y = x + 3$ into $x^2 + y^2 = 225$: $x^2 + (x + 3)^2 = 225 \implies 2x^2 + 6x + 9 = 225 \implies 2x^2 + 6x - 216 = 0$.
2. Divide by $2$: $x^2 + 3x - 108 = 0 \implies (x + 12)(x - 9) = 0 \implies x = -12$ or $x = 9$. Using $y = x + 3$ gives $(-12, -9)$ and $(9, 12)$.

**Final Answer**: $(-12, -9)$ and $(9, 12)$
</details>

#### Question `9_5_3_Q9` [Medium] *(Comparative Growth Analysis)*
Compare the values of $y_1 = 2x$, $y_2 = x^2$, and $y_3 = 2^x$ at $x = 2$, $x = 4$, and $x = 5$, and comment on how the three models behave for $x > 4$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. At $x = 2$: $y_1 = 4, y_2 = 4, y_3 = 4$. At $x = 4$: $y_1 = 8, y_2 = 16, y_3 = 16$.
2. At $x = 5$: $y_1 = 10, y_2 = 25, y_3 = 32$. For all $x > 4$, exponential growth $2^x$ exceeds quadratic growth $x^2$.

**Final Answer**: At $x = 2$, all three equal $4$; at $x = 4$, $y_2 = y_3 = 16 > y_1 = 8$; at $x = 5$, $y_3 = 32 > y_2 = 25 > y_1 = 10$, showing $2^x$ overtakes $x^2$ for $x > 4$
</details>

#### Question `9_5_3_Q10` [Challenging] *(Algorithmic Convergence)*
Explain why each iteration of the bisection algorithm halves the maximum possible error when the midpoint of the current interval is used as the root approximation, and state the interval width after $4$ iterations starting from $[1, 2]$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Starting with an interval $[a, b]$ of length $L = b - a$, replacing one endpoint with the midpoint $m = \frac{a + b}{2}$ produces a new interval of length $\frac{L}{2}$.
2. Starting from $[1, 2]$ of width $1$, after $4$ iterations the width is $\frac{1}{2^4} = \frac{1}{16} = 0.0625$.

**Final Answer**: Each bisection divides the interval length by $2$; after $4$ iterations from width $1$, the width is $\frac{1}{2^4} = \frac{1}{16} = 0.0625$
</details>

#### Question `9_5_3_Q11` [Medium] *(Relations vs Functions)*
Explain why the circle relation $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ gives two $y$-values for each $x$ in $-5 < x < 5$, whereas the parabola $y = x^2 - 25$ gives only one $y$-value for each $x$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. In $x^2 + y^2 = 25$, rearranging for $y$ yields $y^2 = 25 - x^2 \implies y = +\sqrt{25 - x^2}$ or $y = -\sqrt{25 - x^2}$, which are two distinct points for any $-5 < x < 5$.
2. In $y = x^2 - 25$, $y$ is already the first-degree subject, producing a unique output for every input $x$.

**Final Answer**: Solving $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ gives $y = \pm\sqrt{25 - x^2}$ (an upper semicircle and a lower semicircle), whereas $y = x^2 - 25$ defines a single value of $y$ for each $x$
</details>

#### Question `9_5_3_Q12` [Extension] *(Non-Linear Systems)*
Find the coordinates of all points of intersection in the first quadrant ($x > 0, y > 0$) of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ and the hyperbola $y = \frac{12}{x}$. (Hint: compute $(x + y)^2$ and $(x - y)^2$ using $xy = 12$.)

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. From $y = \frac{12}{x}$, we have $xy = 12$. Then $(x + y)^2 = x^2 + y^2 + 2xy = 25 + 24 = 49 \implies x + y = 7$ (since $x, y > 0$).
2. Substituting $y = 7 - x$ into $xy = 12$ gives $x(7 - x) = 12 \implies x^2 - 7x + 12 = 0 \implies (x - 3)(x - 4) = 0$, giving $(3, 4)$ and $(4, 3)$.

**Final Answer**: $(3, 4)$ and $(4, 3)$
</details>
