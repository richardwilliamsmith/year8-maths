### Key Vocabulary
- **Parabola**: The smooth, symmetrical U-shaped curve formed by graphing a quadratic relation $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ ($a \neq 0$).
- **Turning point (Vertex)**: The minimum point (when $a > 0$, upright/concave up) or maximum point (when $a < 0$, inverted/concave down) of a parabola.
- **Axis of symmetry**: The vertical line $x = h$ passing through the turning point that divides a parabola into two mirror-image halves.
- **Turning point form**: A quadratic relation written as $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$, which directly displays the vertex at $(h, k)$ and axis of symmetry $x = h$.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Transformations and Turning Point Form $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$

Compared to the basic parabola $y = x^2$ with vertex $(0, 0)$, the graph of $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$ is dilated vertically by factor $|a|$ (reflected in the $x$-axis if $a < 0$), translated $h$ units horizontally, and translated $k$ units vertically. Its turning point is $(h, k)$ and its axis of symmetry is $x = h$.

$$y = a(x - h)^2 + k \implies \text{Vertex } (h, k), \quad \text{Axis of symmetry } x = h$$

> **💡 Opposite Sign Inside the Bracket for $h$**
> In $y = (x - 3)^2 + 5$, the vertex is $(+3, 5)$; in $y = (x + 4)^2 - 2$, the vertex is $(-4, -2)$.

### Factorised (Intercept) Form $y = a(x - p)(x - q)$

When a quadratic relation is factorised as $y = a(x - p)(x - q)$, setting $y = 0$ immediately gives the $x$-intercepts at $(p, 0)$ and $(q, 0)$. By symmetry, the axis of symmetry lies halfway between the $x$-intercepts at $x = \frac{p + q}{2}$. Substituting this $x$-value back into the equation gives the $y$-coordinate of the turning point.

$$x\text{-intercepts: } (p, 0),\; (q, 0) \qquad \text{Axis of symmetry: } x = \frac{p + q}{2}$$

> **💡 Vertex Lies Halfway Between the $x$-Intercepts**
> Average the two $x$-intercepts to find the axis of symmetry $x_v = \frac{p + q}{2}$, then evaluate $y_v$ at $x = x_v$.

### General Form $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ and Key Features

For a quadratic in general form $y = ax^2 + bx + c$, substituting $x = 0$ gives the $y$-intercept $(0, c)$, and the axis of symmetry is $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$. Factorising the right-hand side and setting $y = 0$ yields the $x$-intercepts via the Null Factor Law.

$$y = ax^2 + bx + c \implies y\text{-intercept } (0, c), \quad \text{Axis of symmetry } x = -\frac{b}{2a}$$

> **💡 Checklist of Four Key Features for Every Parabola Sketch**
> Always label: (1) concavity (up/down), (2) $y$-intercept $(0, c)$, (3) $x$-intercepts (if any), and (4) turning point coordinates.

## Worked Examples

### WE9_5_2_1: Identifying Key Features from Turning Point Form

**Problem**: For the parabola $y = 2(x - 3)^2 - 8$, find (a) the coordinates of the turning point and whether it is a minimum or maximum, (b) the $y$-intercept, and (c) the $x$-intercepts.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Read $(h, k) = (3, -8)$ from $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$. Since $a = 2 > 0$, the parabola opens upwards (minimum). Substitute $x = 0$ for the $y$-intercept. | $$\text{Minimum turning point: } (3, -8)$$
$$\text{At } x = 0: \quad y = 2(0 - 3)^2 - 8 = 2(9) - 8 = 10 \implies (0, 10)$$ |
| 2 | Set $y = 0$ and solve for $x$ to find the $x$-intercepts. | $$2(x - 3)^2 - 8 = 0 \implies (x - 3)^2 = 4 \implies x - 3 = \pm 2$$
$$x = 5 \;\text{ or }\; x = 1 \implies (1, 0) \text{ and } (5, 0)$$ |

**Answer**: (a) Minimum at $(3, -8)$, (b) $y$-intercept $(0, 10)$, (c) $x$-intercepts $(1, 0)$ and $(5, 0)$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: For $y = (x + 2)^2 - 9$, find (a) the turning point, (b) the $y$-intercept, and (c) the $x$-intercepts.

> **Working**: (a) Minimum at $(-2, -9)$; (b) $y = 4 - 9 = -5 \implies (0, -5)$; (c) $(x + 2)^2 = 9 \implies x + 2 = \pm 3 \implies x = 1$ or $x = -5$.
> **Answer**: (a) $(-2, -9)$, (b) $(0, -5)$, (c) $(-5, 0)$ and $(1, 0)$

### WE9_5_2_2: Finding Key Features of a Monic Quadratic in General Form

**Problem**: Consider the quadratic relation $y = x^2 - 6x + 5$. Find (a) the $y$-intercept, (b) the $x$-intercepts, (c) the equation of the axis of symmetry, and (d) the coordinates of the turning point.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Set $x = 0$ for the $y$-intercept, then factorise $x^2 - 6x + 5 = 0$ to find the $x$-intercepts. | $$y\text{-intercept: } (0, 5)$$
$$x^2 - 6x + 5 = 0 \implies (x - 1)(x - 5) = 0 \implies (1, 0) \text{ and } (5, 0)$$ |

$$y_v = 3^2 - 6(3) + 5 = 9 - 18 + 5 = -4 \implies \text{Vertex } (3, -4)$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $(0, 5)$, (b) $(1, 0)$ and $(5, 0)$, (c) $x = 3$, (d) $(3, -4)$

Find (a) the maximum height of the arch, (b) the height of the arch at the western bank ($x = 0$), and (c) the total horizontal span between the two points where the arch meets water level ($h = 0$).

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Reading the Sign of $h$ Directly Without Reversing in $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$
- **The Misconception**: Stating that the turning point of $y = (x - 4)^2 + 7$ is $(-4, 7)$ instead of $(4, 7)$.
- **The Correct Concept**: The squared term $(x - 4)^2$ reaches its minimum value of $0$ when $x - 4 = 0$, which occurs at $x = +4$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$y = (x - 4)^2 + 7 \implies \text{Vertex } (-4, 7) \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$x - 4 = 0 \implies x = 4 \implies \text{Vertex } (4, 7)$$
- **Counter-Example**: At $x = 4$, $y = 0^2 + 7 = 7$; at $x = -4$, $y = (-8)^2 + 7 = 71$.

### Assuming the Constant Term in Turning Point Form Is the $y$-Intercept
- **The Misconception**: Claiming that $k$ in $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$ is the $y$-intercept.
- **The Correct Concept**: The constant $k$ is the $y$-coordinate of the turning point $(h, k)$. To find the $y$-intercept, you must substitute $x = 0$, which gives $y = ah^2 + k$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$y = (x - 3)^2 - 4 \implies y\text{-intercept is } -4 \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\text{At } x = 0: \quad y = (0 - 3)^2 - 4 = 9 - 4 = 5 \implies y\text{-intercept is } (0, 5)$$
- **Counter-Example**: The point $(0, -4)$ does not satisfy $y = (x - 3)^2 - 4$.

<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

**Final Answer**: Turning point $(5, -9)$, axis of symmetry $x = 5$, $y$-intercept $(0, 16)$

#### Question `9_5_2_Q2` [Medium] *(Fluency)*
For the parabola $y = -(x + 3)^2 + 16$, find the coordinates of the maximum turning point and the two $x$-intercepts.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Since $a = -1 < 0$, the parabola has a maximum turning point at $(-3, 16)$.
2. Set $y = 0$: $(x + 3)^2 = 16 \implies x + 3 = \pm 4 \implies x = 1$ or $x = -7$.

**Final Answer**: Maximum turning point $(-3, 16)$, $x$-intercepts $(-7, 0)$ and $(1, 0)$
</details>

#### Question `9_5_2_Q3` [Medium] *(Fluency)*
Find the $x$-intercepts, $y$-intercept, and turning point of $y = x^2 - 8x + 12$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. When $x = 0, y = 12 \implies (0, 12)$. Factorise: $(x - 2)(x - 6) = 0 \implies (2, 0)$ and $(6, 0)$.
2. The axis of symmetry is $x = \frac{2 + 6}{2} = 4$, and $y_v = (4 - 2)(4 - 6) = -4$, giving turning point $(4, -4)$.

**Final Answer**: $x$-intercepts $(2, 0)$ and $(6, 0)$, $y$-intercept $(0, 12)$, turning point $(4, -4)$
</details>

#### Question `9_5_2_Q4` [Medium] *(Understanding)*
A parabola has turning point $(2, -5)$ and passes through the point $(4, 7)$. Find its equation in turning point form $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute $(h, k) = (2, -5)$ into $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$: $y = a(x - 2)^2 - 5$.
2. Substitute $(4, 7)$: $7 = a(4 - 2)^2 - 5 \implies 12 = 4a \implies a = 3$, giving $y = 3(x - 2)^2 - 5$.

**Final Answer**: $y = 3(x - 2)^2 - 5$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `9_5_2_Q5` [Medium] *(Marine Biology Modelling)*
A dolphin leaps out of the water in Port Phillip Bay. Its height $h\text{ m}$ above the water at horizontal distance $x\text{ m}$ from its launch point is modelled by $h = -x^2 + 6x$. Find (a) the horizontal distance where the dolphin re-enters the water and (b) the maximum height of the leap.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Set $h = 0$: $-x(x - 6) = 0 \implies x = 0$ (launch) or $x = 6\text{ m}$ (re-entry).
2. The axis of symmetry is $x = \frac{0 + 6}{2} = 3\text{ m}$, so maximum height is $h = -(3)^2 + 6(3) = 9\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $6\text{ m}$, (b) $9\text{ m}$
</details>

#### Question `9_5_2_Q6` [Challenging] *(Optimisation)*
A farmer in Gippsland has $40\text{ m}$ of fencing to enclose a rectangular sheep pen against an existing straight barn wall (so only three sides require fencing: two widths of $x\text{ m}$ and one length of $(40 - 2x)\text{ m}$). (a) Show that the area $A\text{ m}^2$ is $A = 2x(20 - x)$. (b) Find the value of $x$ that maximises the area and state the maximum area.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Area $= \text{width} \times \text{length} = x(40 - 2x) = 2x(20 - x)$.
2. The $x$-intercepts are $x = 0$ and $x = 20$, so the maximum occurs halfway at $x = 10\text{ m}$, giving $A_{\max} = 2(10)(20 - 10) = 200\text{ m}^2$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $A = x(40 - 2x) = 2x(20 - x)$, (b) $x = 10\text{ m}$, maximum area $= 200\text{ m}^2$
</details>

#### Question `9_5_2_Q7` [Medium] *(Structural Engineering)*
A suspension bridge cable hangs in a parabolic curve $y = \frac{1}{10}(x - 30)^2 + 8$, where $y\text{ m}$ is the height above the roadway and $x\text{ m}$ is the distance from the left tower ($0 \le x \le 60$). Find (a) the height of the cable at the towers ($x = 0$ and $x = 60$) and (b) the minimum height of the cable above the roadway.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. At $x = 0$ (and by symmetry $x = 60$): $y = \frac{1}{10}(-30)^2 + 8 = 90 + 8 = 98\text{ m}$.
2. From turning point form, the minimum height is $k = 8\text{ m}$ at $x = 30\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $98\text{ m}$, (b) $8\text{ m}$
</details>

#### Question `9_5_2_Q8` [Challenging] *(Algebraic Reconstruction)*
A monic parabola crosses the $x$-axis at $(-3, 0)$ and $(7, 0)$. Find its equation in expanded form $y = x^2 + bx + c$ and determine the coordinates of its turning point.

<details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `9_5_2_Q9` [Medium] *(Graphical Reasoning)*
Without solving for $x$-intercepts, explain how you can tell from the turning point form $y = 3(x - 2)^2 + 5$ that its graph has no $x$-intercepts.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Because $(x - 2)^2 \ge 0$ for all real $x$ and $a = 3 > 0$, the minimum value of $y$ is $5$ at $(2, 5)$.
2. A parabola that starts at $(2, 5)$ above the $x$-axis and opens upwards never crosses $y = 0$.

**Final Answer**: The minimum turning point is $(2, 5)$, which lies above the $x$-axis, and the parabola opens upwards ($a = 3 > 0$), so $y \ge 5 > 0$ for all $x$
</details>

#### Question `9_5_2_Q10` [Challenging] *(Equivalent Representations)*
Show by expanding and completing the square that $y = x^2 - 10x + 16$ can be written in both factorised form $y = (x - 2)(x - 8)$ and turning point form $y = (x - 5)^2 - 9$, and explain which feature each form reveals most directly.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Expanding $(x - 2)(x - 8) = x^2 - 10x + 16$, and expanding $(x - 5)^2 - 9 = x^2 - 10x + 25 - 9 = x^2 - 10x + 16$.
2. Each form highlights a different geometric feature: expanded form gives the $y$-intercept $(0, 16)$, factorised form gives the $x$-intercepts $(2, 0)$ and $(8, 0)$, and turning point form gives the vertex $(5, -9)$.

**Final Answer**: Factorised form directly shows $x$-intercepts $(2, 0)$ and $(8, 0)$; turning point form directly shows vertex $(5, -9)$; expanded form directly shows $y$-intercept $(0, 16)$
</details>

#### Question `9_5_2_Q11` [Medium] *(Symmetry Properties)*
A parabola has axis of symmetry $x = 4$. If the point $(1, 11)$ lies on the parabola, find the coordinates of another point on the parabola with $y$-coordinate $11$. Justify your answer using symmetry.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The point $(1, 11)$ lies $4 - 1 = 3$ units to the left of the axis of symmetry $x = 4$.
2. By reflection symmetry across $x = 4$, the mirror-image point lies $3$ units to the right at $x = 4 + 3 = 7$, giving $(7, 11)$.

**Final Answer**: $(7, 11)$
</details>

#### Question `9_5_2_Q12` [Extension] *(Simultaneous Linear-Quadratic Systems)*
Find the coordinates of the two points of intersection of the parabola $y = x^2 - 4x + 3$ and the straight line $y = 2x - 2$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Equate the two expressions for $y$: $x^2 - 4x + 3 = 2x - 2 \implies x^2 - 6x + 5 = 0$.
2. Factorise: $(x - 1)(x - 5) = 0 \implies x = 1$ or $x = 5$. Substituting into $y = 2x - 2$ gives $(1, 0)$ and $(5, 8)$.

**Final Answer**: $(1, 0)$ and $(5, 8)$
</details>
