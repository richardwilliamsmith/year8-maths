### Key Vocabulary
- **Simple interest**: Interest calculated as a fixed percentage of the original principal $P$ for each time period: $I = Prt$, where $r$ is the decimal interest rate per period and $t$ is the number of periods.
- **Hire-purchase agreement**: buying an item by paying an initial deposit followed by regular equal instalments; the total paid minus the cash price equals the total interest charged.
- **Direct proportion (direct variation)**: A relationship between two variables $x$ and $y$ where their ratio $\frac{y}{x} = k$ is constant, written $y = kx$ (or $y \propto x$). Its graph is a straight line through the origin $(0, 0)$.
- **Constant of proportionality**: The fixed multiplier $k = \frac{y}{x}$ in a direct variation relationship $y = kx$, representing the rate of change of $y$ per unit of $x$.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Transposing the Simple Interest Formula for P, r, and t

When simple interest $I$ is earned or charged on a principal $P$ at an annual rate $r$ (expressed as a decimal or fraction $\frac{R}{100}$) over $t$ years, $I = Prt$ and the total amount is $A = P + I = P(1 + rt)$. By dividing both sides of $I = Prt$ by the known factors, we can transpose the formula to find an unknown principal $P$, rate $r$, or time $t$. Remember that $r$ and $t$ must always use matching time units (for example, p.a. rate with $t$ in years).

$$I = Prt \implies P = \frac{I}{rt}, \quad r = \frac{I}{Pt}, \quad t = \frac{I}{Pr}$$

> **💡 Convert Months or Days to Years Before Using a p.a. Rate**
> If the interest rate is given per annum (p.a.) and the loan duration is $m$ months, substitute $t = \frac{m}{12}$ years into $I = Prt$.

### Hire-Purchase and Loan Repayment Modelling

Under a hire-purchase or flat-rate repayment plan, a buyer pays an upfront deposit and settles the balance Paid Financed $= \text{Cash Price} - \text{Deposit}$ plus interest via equal instalments. The total interest paid is the difference between the total of all instalments and the balance financed (or equivalently, $\text{Total Paid} - \text{Cash Price}$).

$$\text{Balance Financed} = P = \text{Cash Price} - \text{Deposit}, \quad I = \text{Total Instalments} - P$$

> **💡 Interest Rate Applies to the Balance Financed, Not the Full Cash Price**
> Once a deposit is paid upfront, only the remaining unpaid balance $P = \text{Cash Price} - \text{Deposit}$ is borrowed, so the flat rate $r$ is calculated on $P$.

### Direct Proportion and the Constant of Proportionality

Two quantities $y$ and $x$ are in direct proportion ($y \propto x$) if multiplying $x$ by any factor multiplies $y$ by the exact same factor. Equivalently, $y = kx$, where $k$ is the constant of proportionality. Note that simple interest $I = Prt$ is simultaneously directly proportional to $P$, to $r$, and to $t$ when the other two variables are held fixed.

$$y \propto x \iff y = kx \iff \frac{y_1}{x_1} = \frac{y_2}{x_2} = k$$

> **💡 Three-Step Method for Direct Variation Problems**
> 1. Write $y = kx$. 2. Substitute the given pair $(x_1, y_1)$ to solve for $k = \frac{y_1}{x_1}$. 3. Write the specific law $y = kx$ and substitute to find the unknown value.

## Worked Examples

### WE9_2_2_1: Finding Unknown Principal or Rate Using I = Prt

**Problem**: (a) What principal must be invested at $4.5\%$ p.a. simple interest to earn $\$810$ interest in $4$ years? (b) At what annual simple interest rate will $\$6000$ earn $\$540$ interest in $18$ months?

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), substitute $I = 810$, $r = 0.045$, and $t = 4$ into $P = \frac{I}{rt}$. | $$P = \frac{810}{0.045 \times 4} = \frac{810}{0.18} = \$4500$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), convert $18$ months to $t = \frac{18}{12} = 1.5\text{ years}$, then substitute $I = 540$, $P = 6000$, and $t = 1.5$ into $r = \frac{I}{Pt}$. | $$r = \frac{540}{6000 \times 1.5} = \frac{540}{9000} = 0.06 = 6\%\text{ p.a.}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $\$4500$, (b) $6\%$ p.a.

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: (a) Find the principal that earns $\$630$ simple interest in $3$ years at $3.5\%$ p.a. (b) Find the annual simple interest rate if $\$4000$ earns $\$250$ in $15$ months.
> *Hint*: In (b), $15\text{ months} = \frac{15}{12} = 1.25\text{ years}$.
> **Working**: (a) $P = \frac{630}{0.035 \times 3} = \$6000$; (b) $r = \frac{250}{4000 \times 1.25} = 0.05 = 5\%$ p.a.
> **Answer**: (a) $\$6000$, (b) $5\%$ p.a.

### WE9_2_2_2: Analysing a Hire-Purchase Repayment Agreement

**Problem**: A musical instrument in Melbourne has a cash price of $\$3200$. Under a hire-purchase agreement, Ruby pays a $15\%$ deposit and repays the balance via $24$ monthly instalments of $\$135.70$. Find: (a) the balance financed, (b) the total interest charged, and (c) the flat annual simple interest rate charged on the balance financed.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Calculate the $15\%$ deposit ($0.15 \times 3200 = \$480$) and subtract it from the cash price to find the principal financed $P$. Then compare total instalments ($24 \times 135.70$) with $P$ to find interest $I$. | $$P = 3200 - 480 = \$2720, \quad I = 24 \times 135.70 - 2720 = 3256.80 - 2720 = \$536.80$$ |
| 2 | Substitute $I = 536.80$, $P = 2720$, and $t = \frac{24}{12} = 2\text{ years}$ into $r = \frac{I}{Pt}$. | $$r = \frac{536.80}{2720 \times 2} = \frac{536.80}{5440} = 0.098676\dots \approx 9.87\%\text{ p.a.}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $\$2720$, (b) $\$536.80$, (c) $9.87\%$ p.a. (or exact $\frac{671}{68}\%$ p.a.)

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: A laptop with cash price $\$2000$ is bought on hire-purchase with a $\$400$ deposit and $18$ monthly payments of $\$98$. Find: (a) the balance financed, (b) the interest charged, and (c) the flat annual simple interest rate.
> *Hint*: Here $P = \$1600$ and $t = 1.5\text{ years}$.
> **Working**: (a) $P = \$1600$; (b) $I = 18(98) - 1600 = 1764 - 1600 = \$164$; (c) $r = \frac{164}{1600 \times 1.5} = \frac{41}{600} \approx 6.83\%$ p.a.
> **Answer**: (a) $\$1600$, (b) $\$164$, (c) $6.83\%$ p.a.

### WE9_2_2_3: Modelling Direct Proportion

**Problem**: The electrical energy $E$ (in kilowatt-hours, $\text{kWh}$) generated by a rooftop solar array in Mildura is directly proportional to the hours of peak sunlight $t$. If the array generates $19.5\text{ kWh}$ in $3$ hours of peak sunlight: (a) find the equation connecting $E$ and $t$, (b) find the energy generated in $5.5$ hours, and (c) find how long it takes to generate $45.5\text{ kWh}$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Write $E = kt$ and substitute $E = 19.5$ when $t = 3$ to find the constant of proportionality $k$. | $$19.5 = 3k \implies k = \frac{19.5}{3} = 6.5\text{ kW} \implies E = 6.5t$$ |
| 2 | Substitute $t = 5.5$ to find $E$, and substitute $E = 45.5$ to solve for $t$. | $$\text{(b) } E = 6.5(5.5) = 35.75\text{ kWh}, \quad \text{(c) } 45.5 = 6.5t \implies t = \frac{45.5}{6.5} = 7\text{ hours}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $E = 6.5t$, (b) $35.75\text{ kWh}$, (c) $7\text{ hours}$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Substituting Months Directly for t When the Interest Rate Is Per Annum
- **The Misconception**: Calculating $I = 2000 \times 0.06 \times 9 = \$1080$ for a $9$-month loan at $6\%$ p.a., treating $9$ months as $9$ years.
- **The Correct Concept**: The time period $t$ must match the rate unit. For a per annum (yearly) rate, $9$ months must be converted to $t = \frac{9}{12} = 0.75\text{ years}$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$I = 2000 \times 0.06 \times 9 = \$1080 \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$I = 2000 \times 0.06 \times \frac{9}{12} = \$90 \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: $9$ months is less than $1$ year, so the interest must be less than $1$ year's interest ($0.06 \times 2000 = \$120$).

### Confusing Any Increasing Linear Relationship y = mx + c with Direct Proportion
- **The Misconception**: Claiming that a plumber's charge $C = 90 + 60h$ is directly proportional to hours worked $h$ because $C$ increases linearly with $h$.
- **The Correct Concept**: Direct proportion requires $y = kx$ with zero y-intercept ($c = 0$), so doubling $x$ doubles $y$. Because of the $\$90$ call-out fee, doubling $h$ from $1$ to $2$ hours increases $C$ from $\$150$ to $\$210$ (not $\$300$).
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$C = 90 + 60h \text{ is linear, so } C \propto h. \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\frac{C}{h} = \frac{90}{h} + 60 \text{ is not constant, so } C \text{ is NOT directly proportional to } h. \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: At $h = 1$, $\frac{C}{h} = 150$; at $h = 2$, $\frac{C}{h} = 105 \neq 150$.

### Fluency & Understanding

#### Question `9_2_2_Q1` [Easy] *(Finding Time in Simple Interest)*
How many years will it take for an investment of $\$8000$ at $5.5\%$ p.a. simple interest to earn $\$1980$ in interest?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Use $t = \frac{I}{Pr}$ with $I = 1980$, $P = 8000$, and $r = 0.055$.
2. Calculate $t = \frac{1980}{8000 \times 0.055} = \frac{1980}{440} = 4.5\text{ years}$.

**Final Answer**: $4.5\text{ years}$
</details>

#### Question `9_2_2_Q2` [Medium] *(Finding Principal from Maturity Value)*
An investor deposits a principal $P$ at $4\%$ p.a. simple interest. After $5$ years, the total amount in the account (principal plus interest) is $\$9000$. Find the original principal $P$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Use $A = P(1 + rt)$ with $A = 9000$, $r = 0.04$, and $t = 5$.
2. Since $1 + 0.04(5) = 1.20$, solve $1.20P = 9000 \implies P = \frac{9000}{1.20} = \$7500$.

**Final Answer**: $\$7500$
</details>

#### Question `9_2_2_Q3` [Easy] *(Direct Proportion Law)*
Given that $y$ is directly proportional to $x$ and $y = 42$ when $x = 12$, find the constant of proportionality $k$ and the value of $y$ when $x = 20$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find $k = \frac{y}{x} = \frac{42}{12} = 3.5$, so $y = 3.5x$.
2. Substitute $x = 20$: $y = 3.5 \times 20 = 70$.

**Final Answer**: $k = 3.5$ (or $\frac{7}{2}$); $y = 70$
</details>

#### Question `9_2_2_Q4` [Medium] *(Fractional Year Rate Calculation)*
A loan of $\$3600$ is repaid over $9$ months with simple interest of $\$216$. Calculate the annual simple interest rate (% p.a.).

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Convert $9$ months to years: $t = \frac{9}{12} = 0.75\text{ years}$.
2. Calculate $r = \frac{I}{Pt} = \frac{216}{3600 \times 0.75} = \frac{216}{2700} = 0.08 = 8\%$ p.a.

**Final Answer**: $8\%$ p.a.
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `9_2_2_Q5` [Medium] *(Hire-Purchase Monthly Instalments)*
An electric bike in Brisbane has a cash price of $\$2500$. Sam buys it on hire-purchase by paying a $20\%$ deposit and borrowing the balance at $9\%$ p.a. simple interest over $2$ years, repaid in $24$ equal monthly instalments. Calculate the amount of each monthly instalment.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Deposit $= 0.20 \times 2500 = \$500$, so balance financed $P = 2500 - 500 = \$2000$.
2. Interest over $2$ years: $I = 2000 \times 0.09 \times 2 = \$360$. Total to repay $= 2000 + 360 = \$2360$.
3. Monthly instalment $= \frac{2360}{24} = \frac{295}{3} \approx \$98.33$.

**Final Answer**: $\$98.33$ (exact $\$\frac{295}{3}$ per month)
</details>

#### Question `9_2_2_Q6` [Medium] *(Hooke's Law Direct Variation)*
By Hooke's Law, the extension $e\text{ (cm)}$ of a steel spring is directly proportional to the load mass $m\text{ (kg)}$ suspended from it. A mass of $6\text{ kg}$ extends the spring by $4.5\text{ cm}$. What mass will extend the spring by $12\text{ cm}$?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Write $e = km$ and substitute $e = 4.5, m = 6$ to get $k = \frac{4.5}{6} = 0.75\text{ cm/kg}$.
2. Substitute $e = 12$: $12 = 0.75m \implies m = \frac{12}{0.75} = 16\text{ kg}$.

**Final Answer**: $16\text{ kg}$
</details>

#### Question `9_2_2_Q7` [Medium] *(Electric Vehicle Range Modelling)*
An electric vehicle travelling along the Hume Highway uses $27\text{ kWh}$ of battery energy to travel $180\text{ km}$ at a constant speed. Assuming energy consumption $E$ is directly proportional to distance $d$, how far can the vehicle travel on a full $72\text{ kWh}$ battery charge?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Set up the direct proportion ratio $\frac{d}{E} = \frac{180}{27} = \frac{20}{3}\text{ km/kWh}$.
2. Multiply by $72\text{ kWh}$: $d = \frac{20}{3} \times 72 = 480\text{ km}$.

**Final Answer**: $480\text{ km}$
</details>

#### Question `9_2_2_Q8` [Challenging] *(Dual-Account Simple Interest Allocation)*
Ava invests a total of $\$10\,000$ split across two accounts for $1$ year: Account A pays $4\%$ p.a. simple interest and Account B pays $6.5\%$ p.a. simple interest. If her combined annual interest from both accounts is $\$550$, how much principal did she invest in each account?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Let $x$ be the amount in Account A and $10\,000 - x$ be the amount in Account B.
2. Write the total interest equation: $0.04x + 0.065(10\,000 - x) = 550$.
3. Simplify: $0.04x + 650 - 0.065x = 550 \implies -0.025x = -100 \implies x = 4000$. Thus $\$4000$ is in Account A and $\$6000$ is in Account B.

**Final Answer**: $\$4000$ in Account A and $\$6000$ in Account B
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `9_2_2_Q9` [Medium] *(Doubling Time Under Simple Interest)*
At what annual simple interest rate (% p.a.) will any non-zero principal $P$ double in value ($A = 2P$) in exactly $8$ years? Explain why the answer does not depend on the starting amount $P$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. For the investment to double, the interest earned must equal the original principal ($I = P$).
2. Substitute $I = P$ into $I = Prt$: $P = P \times r \times 8$. Dividing both sides by $P > 0$ cancels $P$, leaving $8r = 1 \implies r = \frac{1}{8} = 12.5\%$ p.a.

**Final Answer**: $12.5\%$ p.a.; $P$ cancels from both sides of $I = P = Prt$
</details>

#### Question `9_2_2_Q10` [Easy] *(Testing for Direct Proportionality)*
Two variables $x$ and $y$ are recorded in a table: $(x, y) = (2, 9), (4, 18), (6, 27), (8, 34)$. Determine whether $y$ is directly proportional to $x$ across the entire table, justifying your conclusion.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Check the ratio $\frac{y}{x}$ for each pair: $\frac{9}{2} = 4.5$, $\frac{18}{4} = 4.5$, $\frac{27}{6} = 4.5$.
2. For $(8, 34)$, $\frac{34}{8} = 4.25 \neq 4.5$ (it would need to be $36$), so $y$ is not directly proportional to $x$ across the whole table.

**Final Answer**: No; the ratio $\frac{y}{x}$ is $4.5$ for the first three pairs, but $\frac{34}{8} = 4.25 \neq 4.5$ for the fourth pair
</details>

#### Question `9_2_2_Q11` [Challenging] *(Flat Rate vs Effective Interest Rate)*
In a hire-purchase agreement, a borrower repays the principal gradually each month, yet flat-rate simple interest is charged on the initial balance financed for the entire loan term. Explain why the true effective interest rate is roughly double the advertised flat rate.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Under flat-rate interest, interest $I = Prt$ is charged as if the borrower kept the full principal $P$ for the entire $t$ years.
2. In reality, paying equal monthly instalments reduces the principal owed linearly from $P$ to $0$, so the average principal owed during the loan is approximately $\frac{P}{2}$, making the true annual cost rate close to $\frac{I}{(P/2)t} = 2r$.

**Final Answer**: Because regular monthly repayments steadily reduce the owing balance from $P$ down to $\$0$, the average amount owed over the loan term is approximately $\frac{1}{2}P$, making the effective rate on the average balance owed roughly $2r$.
</details>

#### Question `9_2_2_Q12` [Extension] *(Transitivity of Direct Proportion)*
Suppose $y$ is directly proportional to $x$ with $y = k_1 x$, and $z$ is directly proportional to $y$ with $z = k_2 y$. Prove that $z$ is directly proportional to $x$ and state the constant of proportionality in terms of $k_1$ and $k_2$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute $y = k_1 x$ into $z = k_2 y$: $z = k_2(k_1 x) = (k_1 k_2)x$.
2. Since $k_1$ and $k_2$ are constants, their product $k = k_1 k_2$ is constant, proving $z \propto x$.

**Final Answer**: $z = (k_1 k_2)x$, with constant of proportionality $k = k_1 k_2$
</details>
