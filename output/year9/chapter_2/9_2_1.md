### Key Vocabulary
- **Overtime and penalty rates**: Higher hourly pay rates paid for hours worked beyond standard full-time hours, commonly time-and-a-half ($1.5 \times \text{normal rate}$) or double time ($2.0 \times \text{normal rate}$).
- **Commission and retainer**: Income earned as a percentage of the value of goods sold, often combined with a fixed base payment called a retainer.
- **Leave loading**: An extra allowance of $17.5\%$ added to $4$ weeks of normal pay during annual holiday leave in many Australian awards.
- **Taxable income and progressive tax**: Taxable income equals gross income minus allowable tax deductions. Under Australia's progressive PAYG tax system, higher marginal rates apply only to the portion of taxable income above each bracket threshold.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Wages, Salaries, Overtime, and Holiday Leave Loading

A wage earner is paid an hourly rate $r$ for each hour worked, whereas a salary earner receives a fixed annual amount divided across $52$ weeks or $26$ fortnights (for accounting accuracy, $1\text{ year} = 52\text{ weeks} = 26\text{ fortnights} = 12\text{ months}$). Overtime hours worked beyond standard hours are paid at time-and-a-half ($1.5r$) or double time ($2r$). Under many Australian employment awards, $4$-week annual holiday pay includes a $17.5\%$ leave loading bonus.

$$\text{Gross Wage} = h_{\text{norm}} r + h_{1.5}(1.5r) + h_{2.0}(2r), \quad \text{Holiday Pay (4 wks)} = 4w \times 1.175$$

> **💡 Convert Between Annual Salary and Weekly Pay Carefully**
> To convert an annual salary to weekly pay, divide by $52$ (or by $26$ for fortnightly pay). Multiply by $1.175$ to include the $17.5\%$ holiday leave loading on $4$ weeks of pay.

### Retainers and Tiered Sales Commission

Salespeople such as real-estate agents and car dealers often earn a fixed retainer plus a percentage commission on sales. In a tiered (sliding-scale) commission scheme, one percentage rate applies to sales up to a specified threshold, and a different percentage rate applies only to the sales amount exceeding that threshold.

$$\text{Total Earnings} = \text{Retainer} + r_1 T + r_2 (\text{Sales} - T) \quad (\text{for } \text{Sales} > T)$$

> **💡 Tiered Rates Apply Only to the Excess Above the Threshold**
> If commission is $2\%$ on the first $\$500\,000$ and $1.5\%$ on the balance above $\$500\,000$, a sale of $\$700\,000$ earns $0.02(\$500\,000) + 0.015(\$200\,000)$.

### Australian Progressive Income Tax and the Medicare Levy

Taxable income is calculated by subtracting work-related tax deductions from gross annual income. Under the 2024–25 Australian Resident Income Tax rates: $\$0\text{–}\$18\,200$ is tax-free; $\$18\,201\text{–}\$45\,000$ is taxed at $16\text{c}$ per $\$1$ over $\$18\,200$; $\$45\,001\text{–}\$135\,000$ is $\$4288$ plus $30\text{c}$ per $\$1$ over $\$45\,000$; $\$135\,001\text{–}\$190\,000$ is $\$31\,288$ plus $37\text{c}$ per $\$1$ over $\$135\,000$; and over $\$190\,000$ is $\$51\,638$ plus $45\text{c}$ per $\$1$ over $\$190\,000$. Most taxpayers also pay a $2\%$ Medicare levy on their entire taxable income.

$$\text{Taxable Income} = \text{Gross Income} - \text{Deductions}, \quad \text{Medicare Levy} = 0.02 \times \text{Taxable Income}$$

> **💡 Crossing Into a Higher Tax Bracket Never Reduces Net Pay**
> Only the dollars earned ABOVE a bracket threshold are taxed at the higher marginal rate — all income below the threshold stays taxed at the lower rates.

## Worked Examples

### WE9_2_1_1: Calculating Weekly Gross Pay with Overtime

**Problem**: Chloe works as an apprentice electrician in Ballarat at a normal rate of $\$28\text{/h}$. In one week she works $38$ normal hours, $4$ hours at time-and-a-half, and $3$ hours on Sunday at double time. Calculate her gross weekly pay.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Calculate her normal pay for $38$ hours at $\$28\text{/h}$, time-and-a-half pay for $4$ hours at $1.5 \times \$28 = \$42\text{/h}$, and double-time pay for $3$ hours at $2 \times \$28 = \$56\text{/h}$. | $$\text{Normal} = 38 \times 28 = \$1064, \quad \text{Time-and-a-half} = 4 \times 42 = \$168, \quad \text{Double time} = 3 \times 56 = \$168$$ |
| 2 | Sum the three components to find Chloe's total gross pay for the week. | $$\text{Gross Pay} = 1064 + 168 + 168 = \$1400$$ |

**Answer**: $\$1400$

> **✏️ Now You Try**

> **Working**: $35(24) + 6(36) + 2(48) = 840 + 216 + 96 = \$1152$.

### WE9_2_1_2: Tiered Commission with a Weekly Retainer

**Problem**: A software sales representative in Melbourne receives a weekly retainer of $\$650$, plus $4\%$ commission on the first $\$20\,000$ of weekly sales and $6.5\%$ on all sales exceeding $\$20\,000$. Calculate her total earnings in a week where her sales total $\$34\,000$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Split the $\$34\,000$ sales into the first $\$20\,000$ (at $4\%$) and the excess $\$34\,000 - \$20\,000 = \$14\,000$ (at $6.5\%$). | $$\text{Commission} = 0.04 \times 20\,000 + 0.065 \times 14\,000 = 800 + 910 = \$1710$$ |
| 2 | Add the weekly retainer of $\$650$ to the total commission. | $$\text{Total Earnings} = 650 + 1710 = \$2360$$ |

**Answer**: $\$2360$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: A real-estate agent earns $2.5\%$ commission on the first $\$600\,000$ of a property's sale price and $1.5\%$ on the remainder above $\$600\,000$. Find the total commission on a house sold for $\$840\,000$.
> *Hint*: The excess above $\$600\,000$ is $\$240\,000$.
> **Working**: $0.025(600\,000) + 0.015(240\,000) = 15\,000 + 3600 = \$18\,600$.
> **Answer**: $\$18\,600$

### WE9_2_1_3: Calculating Australian Income Tax and Medicare Levy

**Problem**: An engineer in Adelaide has a gross annual salary of $\$98\,400$ and allowable work-related deductions of $\$3400$. Using the 2024–25 resident tax bracket ($\$45\,001\text{–}\$135\,000$: $\$4288$ plus $30\text{c}$ for each $\$1$ over $\$45\,000$), calculate: (a) her taxable income, (b) her income tax payable, and (c) her $2\%$ Medicare levy.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Subtract allowable deductions from gross income to find taxable income. | $$\text{Taxable Income} = 98\,400 - 3400 = \$95\,000$$ |
| 2 | Since $\$95\,000$ lies in the $\$45\,001\text{–}\$135\,000$ bracket, add $\$4288$ to $30\%$ of the excess above $\$45\,000$ ($\$95\,000 - \$45\,000 = \$50\,000$), and compute $2\%$ of $\$95\,000$ for the Medicare levy. | $$\text{Income Tax} = 4288 + 0.30(95\,000 - 45\,000) = 4288 + 15\,000 = \$19\,288, \quad \text{Medicare} = 0.02 \times 95\,000 = \$1900$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $\$95\,000$, (b) $\$19\,288$, (c) $\$1900$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: A teacher has a gross income of $\$84\,200$ and tax deductions of $\$2200$. Using the same bracket ($\$4288 + 30\%$ of excess over $\$45\,000$), find their taxable income and income tax payable.
> *Hint*: Taxable income is $\$82\,000$, which is $\$37\,000$ over $\$45\,000$.
> **Working**: Taxable income $= \$82\,000$; Tax $= 4288 + 0.30(37\,000) = 4288 + 11\,100 = \$15\,388$.
> **Answer**: Taxable income $= \$82\,000$; Income tax $= \$15\,388$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Applying the Top Marginal Tax Rate to the Entire Taxable Income
- **The Misconception**: Thinking that a worker with a taxable income of $\$50\,000$ in the $30\%$ bracket pays $0.30 \times \$50\,000 = \$15\,000$ in income tax.
- **The Correct Concept**: In a progressive tax system, the first $\$18\,200$ is taxed at $0\%$, the slice from $\$18\,200$ to $\$45\,000$ is taxed at $16\%$ ($\$4288$), and only the $\$5000$ above $\$45\,000$ is taxed at $30\%$ ($\$1500$), giving $\$5788$ total tax.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\text{Tax} = 0.30 \times 50\,000 = \$15\,000 \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\text{Tax} = 4288 + 0.30(50\,000 - 45\,000) = 4288 + 1500 = \$5788 \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: At $\$50\,000$ taxable income, the effective average tax rate is $\frac{5788}{50\,000} = 11.576\%$, far below $30\%$.

### Dividing Annual Salary by 48 Instead of 52 to Find Weekly Pay
- **The Misconception**: Assuming $1\text{ month} = 4\text{ weeks}$ and dividing an annual salary by $48$ (or multiplying weekly pay by $4$ to get monthly pay).
- **The Correct Concept**: A standard year has $52$ weeks ($365 \div 7 \approx 52.14$), not $48$. Always convert between weekly and monthly via the annual figure.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\text{Weekly Pay} = \frac{\text{Annual Salary}}{48} \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\text{Weekly Pay} = \frac{\text{Annual Salary}}{52}, \quad \text{Monthly Pay} = \frac{\text{Annual Salary}}{12} \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: For a $\$62\,400$ annual salary, true weekly pay is $\$62\,400 \div 52 = \$1200$, whereas dividing by $48$ falsely gives $\$1300$.

Noah earns a normal wage of $\$25\text{/h}$. In one week he works $36$ normal hours and $6$ hours of overtime at time-and-a-half. Calculate his gross pay for the week.

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `9_2_1_Q5` [Medium] *(Solving for Unknown Overtime Hours)*
A nurse in Hobart earns $\$40\text{/h}$ for a standard $38$-hour week. Overtime on Saturdays is paid at time-and-a-half, and overtime on Sundays is paid at double time. In one week she works $38$ normal hours, $5$ hours on Saturday, and $h$ hours on Sunday, earning a gross pay of $\$2140$. Find $h$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Normal pay $= 38 \times 40 = \$1520$; Saturday pay $= 5 \times (1.5 \times 40) = 5 \times 60 = \$300$.
2. Sunday double-time rate is $2 \times 40 = \$80\text{/h}$. Set up the equation: $1520 + 300 + 80h = 2140$.
3. Solve $1820 + 80h = 2140 \implies 80h = 320 \implies h = 4\text{ hours}$.

**Final Answer**: $h = 4\text{ hours}$
</details>

#### Question `9_2_1_Q6` [Medium] *(Target Sales Quota)*
An agricultural equipment agent in Shepparton receives a monthly retainer of $\$2200$ plus $5\%$ commission on all sales above a monthly quota of $\$30\,000$. What total value of sales must she achieve in a month to earn a gross monthly income of $\$6450$?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Subtract the retainer to find the required commission: $6450 - 2200 = \$4250$.
2. Let $S$ be total sales above $\$30\,000$: $0.05(S - 30\,000) = 4250 \implies S - 30\,000 = 85\,000 \implies S = \$115\,000$.

**Final Answer**: $\$115\,000$
</details>

#### Question `9_2_1_Q7` [Challenging] *(Net Income After Tax and Medicare)*
Marcus has a taxable income of $\$150\,000$. Using the 2024–25 bracket ($\$135\,001\text{–}\$190\,000$: $\$31\,288$ plus $37\text{c}$ for each $\$1$ over $\$135\,000$) and the $2\%$ Medicare levy, calculate his total tax + Medicare levy, and hence find his net annual after-tax income.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Income tax $= 31\,288 + 0.37(150\,000 - 135\,000) = 31\,288 + 0.37(15\,000) = 31\,288 + 5550 = \$36\,838$.
2. Medicare levy $= 0.02 \times 150\,000 = \$3000$, giving total deduction $36\,838 + 3000 = \$39\,838$.
3. Net annual income $= 150\,000 - 39\,838 = \$110\,162$.

**Final Answer**: Total tax + Medicare $= \$39\,838$; Net income $= \$110\,162$
</details>

#### Question `9_2_1_Q8` [Challenging] *(PAYG Tax Refund vs Balance Payable)*
During the year, an employer withheld $\$410$ per week in PAYG tax instalments for $52$ weeks from a worker whose final taxable income was $\$85\,000$. Using the 2024–25 tax rule ($\$4288 + 30\%$ of excess over $\$45\,000$) plus the $2\%$ Medicare levy, determine whether the worker receives a tax refund or owes a tax bill, and state the exact dollar amount.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Total PAYG tax withheld over $52$ weeks $= 52 \times 410 = \$21\,320$.
2. Actual tax liability $= 4288 + 0.30(85\,000 - 45\,000) + 0.02(85\,000) = 4288 + 12\,000 + 1700 = \$17\,988$.
3. Since $\$21\,320 > \$17\,988$, the worker receives a tax refund of $21\,320 - 17\,988 = \$3332$.

**Final Answer**: Tax refund of $\$3332$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `9_2_1_Q9` [Easy] *(Continuity of Progressive Tax Brackets)*
Verify mathematically where the base tax figure of $\$4288$ at the start of the $\$45\,001\text{–}\$135\,000$ bracket comes from in the 2024–25 Australian tax table.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. In the previous bracket ($\$18\,201\text{–}\$45\,000$), the width of the bracket is $45\,000 - 18\,200 = \$26\,800$.
2. Taxing that entire $\$26\,800$ slice at $16\text{c}$ per dollar gives $0.16 \times 26\,800 = \$4288$.

**Final Answer**: $0.16 \times (45\,000 - 18\,200) = 0.16 \times 26\,800 = \$4288$
</details>

#### Question `9_2_1_Q10` [Medium] *(How Tax Deductions Work)*
A worker whose taxable income is $\$75\,000$ (in the $30\%$ marginal tax bracket, plus $2\%$ Medicare levy) buys a $\$500$ textbook and professional tool kit that is an allowable work-related deduction. Explain why this deduction reduces their total tax + Medicare bill by $\$160$, not $\$500$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. A tax deduction is subtracted from gross income before tax is calculated, reducing taxable income from $\$75\,000$ to $\$74\,500$.
2. Since each marginal dollar in this bracket is taxed at $30\%$ income tax plus $2\%$ Medicare levy ($32\%$ total), the tax saved is $0.32 \times 500 = \$160$.

**Final Answer**: A deduction reduces taxable income by $\$500$, saving the combined marginal tax and Medicare rate of $32\%$ ($0.32 \times \$500 = \$160$)
</details>

#### Question `9_2_1_Q11` [Medium] *(Comparing Remuneration Packages)*
Job Offer A pays a flat salary of $\$1250$ per week. Job Offer B pays a weekly retainer of $\$550$ plus $7\%$ commission on all weekly sales $S$. Write an equation to find the weekly sales $S$ at which both offers pay the same amount, and state when Offer B is better.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Equate the two weekly pay expressions: $550 + 0.07S = 1250$.
2. Solve $0.07S = 700 \implies S = \$10\,000$. Whenever weekly sales exceed $\$10\,000$, Offer B pays more.

**Final Answer**: $S = \$10\,000$; Offer B is better whenever weekly sales exceed $\$10\,000$
</details>

#### Question `9_2_1_Q12` [Extension] *(Effective vs Marginal Tax Rate)*
Define the effective (average) income tax rate as $\frac{\text{Income Tax Payable}}{\text{Taxable Income}} \times 100\%$ (excluding Medicare). Find the exact taxable income $T$ in the $\$45\,001\text{–}\$135\,000$ bracket at which a resident's effective income tax rate equals exactly $20\%$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. For $45\,000 < T \le 135\,000$, income tax is $4288 + 0.30(T - 45\,000) = 0.30T - 9212$.
2. Set this equal to $0.20T$: $0.30T - 9212 = 0.20T \implies 0.10T = 9212 \implies T = \$92\,120$.

**Final Answer**: $\$92\,120$
</details>
