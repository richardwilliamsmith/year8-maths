### Key Vocabulary
- **Line segment (Interval)**: The part of a straight line bounded by two distinct endpoints $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$.
- **Gradient ($m$)**: The rate of change of $y$ with respect to $x$ along a line segment, given by $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$.
- **Midpoint ($M$)**: The point on a line segment that is equidistant from both endpoints, whose coordinates are the averages of the endpoint coordinates.
- **Euclidean distance ($d$)**: The straight-line length between two points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$, derived from Pythagoras' theorem.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### The Gradient Formula

For any two points $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$ with $x_1 \neq x_2$, the gradient $m$ measures the vertical rise per unit of horizontal run. Subtracting coordinates in either order gives the same gradient, provided the $x$- and $y$-coordinates are subtracted in the same direction.

$$m = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} \quad (x_1 \neq x_2)$$

> **💡 Keep Coordinate Subtraction Order Consistent**
> Label your points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ before substituting, and place brackets around negative coordinates.

### The Midpoint Formula

The midpoint $M$ of the line segment joining $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$ lies halfway horizontally and halfway vertically between $A$ and $B$. Its $x$-coordinate is the mean of $x_1$ and $x_2$, and its $y$-coordinate is the mean of $y_1$ and $y_2$.

$$M = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2},\; \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$$

> **💡 Midpoint Means Average the Coordinates**
> Add the $x$-coordinates and divide by $2$; add the $y$-coordinates and divide by $2$. Never subtract when finding a midpoint.

### The Distance Formula via Pythagoras' Theorem

Constructing a right-angled triangle with horizontal leg $|x_2 - x_1|$ and vertical leg $|y_2 - y_1|$ between $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$ allows us to apply Pythagoras' theorem $d^2 = (x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2$ to find the exact straight-line distance $d$.

$$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$

> **💡 Squares of Differences Are Always Non-Negative**
> Even when $x_2 - x_1$ or $y_2 - y_1$ is negative, its square is positive: $(-6)^2 = +36$, never $-36$.

### WE9_4_2_1: Calculating Gradient and Midpoint of a Line Segment

**Problem**: For the line segment joining $A(-3, 7)$ and $B(5, -9)$, find (a) the gradient $m$ of $AB$ and (b) the coordinates of the midpoint $M$ of $AB$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Substitute $(x_1, y_1) = (-3, 7)$ and $(x_2, y_2) = (5, -9)$ into the gradient formula $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$. | $$m = \frac{-9 - 7}{5 - (-3)} = \frac{-16}{8} = -2$$ |
| 2 | Average the $x$-coordinates and the $y$-coordinates to find the midpoint $M$. | $$M = \left(\frac{-3 + 5}{2},\; \frac{7 + (-9)}{2}\right) = \left(\frac{2}{2},\; \frac{-2}{2}\right) = (1, -1)$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $m = -2$, (b) $M(1, -1)$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Subtracting Instead of Adding in the Midpoint Formula
- **The Misconception**: Confusing the midpoint formula with the gradient/distance formulas and calculating $\left(\frac{x_2 - x_1}{2}, \frac{y_2 - y_1}{2}\right)$.
- **The Correct Concept**: A midpoint is an average (mean) of two positions, so the coordinates are added before dividing by $2$: $\left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\text{Midpoint of } (2, 6) \text{ and } (8, 10): \quad \left(\frac{8 - 2}{2}, \frac{10 - 6}{2}\right) = (3, 2) \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$M = \left(\frac{2 + 8}{2}, \frac{6 + 10}{2}\right) = (5, 8)$$
- **Counter-Example**: The number halfway between $2$ and $8$ is $5$, not $3$.

### Squaring Negative Numbers Incorrectly on a Calculator
- **The Misconception**: Typing $-8^2$ into a calculator and obtaining $-64$ inside the distance formula.
- **The Correct Concept**: In Python and scientific calculators, exponentiation binds tighter than negation, so $-8^2 = -(8^2) = -64$, whereas $(-8)^2 = (-8) \times (-8) = +64$. Distance components squared are always positive.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$d = \sqrt{6^2 + (-8)^2} = \sqrt{36 - 64} \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$d = \sqrt{6^2 + (-8)^2} = \sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10$$
- **Counter-Example**: A right-angled triangle with legs $6$ and $8$ has hypotenuse $10$.

Find the gradient and midpoint of the line segment joining $A(2, -5)$ and $B(10, 11)$.

**Final Answer**: $m = 2$, $M(6, 3)$

#### Question `9_4_2_Q2` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Find the exact distance between the points $(-5, -2)$ and $(7, 3)$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Apply the distance formula: $d = \sqrt{(7 - (-5))^2 + (3 - (-2))^2}$.
2. Simplify: $d = \sqrt{12^2 + 5^2} = \sqrt{144 + 25} = \sqrt{169} = 13$.

**Final Answer**: $13$
</details>

#### Question `9_4_2_Q3` [Medium] *(Fluency)*
Find the exact length of the line segment joining $P(-3, 4)$ and $Q(1, -4)$ in simplified surd form.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute into the distance formula: $d = \sqrt{(1 - (-3))^2 + (-4 - 4)^2} = \sqrt{4^2 + (-8)^2}$.
2. Simplify the surd: $d = \sqrt{16 + 64} = \sqrt{80} = \sqrt{16 \times 5} = 4\sqrt{5}$.

**Final Answer**: $4\sqrt{5}$
</details>

#### Question `9_4_2_Q4` [Medium] *(Understanding)*
The line segment joining $A(-2, k)$ and $B(4, 13)$ has a gradient of $3$. Find the value of $k$ and the midpoint of $AB$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Use the gradient formula: $\frac{13 - k}{4 - (-2)} = 3 \implies \frac{13 - k}{6} = 3 \implies 13 - k = 18 \implies k = -5$.
2. With $A(-2, -5)$ and $B(4, 13)$, the midpoint is $M = \left(\frac{-2 + 4}{2}, \frac{-5 + 13}{2}\right) = (1, 4)$.

**Final Answer**: $k = -5$, $M(1, 4)$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `9_4_2_Q5` [Medium] *(Marine Navigation)*
On a Cartesian grid of Melbourne (where $1\text{ unit} = 1\text{ km}$), a rescue helicopter base is at $H(-3, 2)$ and a vessel in Port Phillip Bay is at $V(6, -10)$. (a) Find the straight-line distance from the base to the vessel. (b) A refuelling buoy is located at the midpoint of $HV$. State its coordinates.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate the distance: $d = \sqrt{(6 - (-3))^2 + (-10 - 2)^2} = \sqrt{9^2 + (-12)^2} = \sqrt{81 + 144} = \sqrt{225} = 15\text{ km}$.
2. Calculate the midpoint: $M = \left(\frac{-3 + 6}{2}, \frac{2 + (-10)}{2}\right) = (1.5, -4)$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $15\text{ km}$, (b) $(1.5, -4)$
</details>

#### Question `9_4_2_Q6` [Medium] *(Circle Geometry on the Plane)*
A circle has diameter $AB$, where $A(-1, 2)$ and $B(7, 8)$. Find (a) the coordinates of the centre $C$ of the circle and (b) the exact radius $r$ of the circle.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The centre $C$ is the midpoint of diameter $AB$: $C = \left(\frac{-1 + 7}{2}, \frac{2 + 8}{2}\right) = (3, 5)$.
2. The radius is the distance from $C(3, 5)$ to $B(7, 8)$: $r = \sqrt{(7 - 3)^2 + (8 - 5)^2} = \sqrt{16 + 9} = \sqrt{25} = 5$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $C(3, 5)$, (b) $r = 5$
</details>

#### Question `9_4_2_Q7` [Challenging] *(Geometric Perimeter)*
A triangle has vertices $P(0, 0)$, $Q(6, 8)$, and $R(12, 0)$. Calculate the perimeter of $\triangle PQR$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find each side length: $PQ = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = 10$, $QR = \sqrt{(12 - 6)^2 + (0 - 8)^2} = \sqrt{36 + 64} = 10$, and $PR = 12 - 0 = 12$.
2. Add the three side lengths: $\text{Perimeter} = 10 + 10 + 12 = 32\text{ units}$.

**Final Answer**: $32\text{ units}$
</details>

#### Question `9_4_2_Q8` [Challenging] *(Coordinate Equations)*
The distance between the points $A(1, 3)$ and $B(x, 11)$ is $10\text{ units}$. Find the two possible values of $x$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Use $d^2 = 10^2$: $(x - 1)^2 + (11 - 3)^2 = 100 \implies (x - 1)^2 + 64 = 100 \implies (x - 1)^2 = 36$.
2. Take the square root of both sides: $x - 1 = 6$ or $x - 1 = -6$, giving $x = 7$ or $x = -5$.

**Final Answer**: $x = 7$ or $x = -5$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `9_4_2_Q9` [Challenging] *(Geometric Proof)*
Prove that the triangle with vertices $A(1, 1)$, $B(4, 5)$, and $C(8, 2)$ is an isosceles right-angled triangle.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate the squared lengths of the sides: $AB^2 = (4 - 1)^2 + (5 - 1)^2 = 9 + 16 = 25$, $BC^2 = (8 - 4)^2 + (2 - 5)^2 = 16 + 9 = 25$, and $AC^2 = (8 - 1)^2 + (2 - 1)^2 = 49 + 1 = 50$.
2. Since $AB = BC = 5$, the triangle is isosceles. Furthermore, $AB^2 + BC^2 = 25 + 25 = 50 = AC^2$, which satisfies the converse of Pythagoras' theorem with a right angle at $B$.

**Final Answer**: $AB = BC = 5$ and $AC = 5\sqrt{2}$, so $AB^2 + BC^2 = 25 + 25 = 50 = AC^2$, proving $\triangle ABC$ is isosceles and right-angled at $B$
</details>

#### Question `9_4_2_Q10` [Challenging] *(Parallelogram Diagonals)*
A quadrilateral $ABCD$ has vertices $A(1, 2)$, $B(6, 4)$, $C(9, 10)$, and $D(4, 8)$. Use the midpoint formula to show that the diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ bisect each other, and state what type of quadrilateral $ABCD$ must be.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find the midpoint of diagonal $AC$: $M_{AC} = \left(\frac{1 + 9}{2}, \frac{2 + 10}{2}\right) = (5, 6)$.
2. Find the midpoint of diagonal $BD$: $M_{BD} = \left(\frac{6 + 4}{2}, \frac{4 + 8}{2}\right) = (5, 6)$. Because the midpoints coincide, the diagonals bisect each other and $ABCD$ is a parallelogram.

**Final Answer**: Both diagonals share the same midpoint $(5, 6)$, so $ABCD$ is a parallelogram
</details>

#### Question `9_4_2_Q11` [Medium] *(Algebraic Justification)*
Explain why $\frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$ and $\frac{y_1 - y_2}{x_1 - x_2}$ always produce the same gradient for any non-vertical line segment.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Note that $y_1 - y_2 = -(y_2 - y_1)$ and $x_1 - x_2 = -(x_2 - x_1)$.
2. Dividing the two gives $\frac{-(y_2 - y_1)}{-(x_2 - x_1)} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$ because the factors of $-1$ cancel.

**Final Answer**: Both numerator and denominator are multiplied by $-1$, and $\frac{-1}{-1} = +1$
</details>

#### Question `9_4_2_Q12` [Extension] *(Equidistant Locus)*
Point $P(x, 0)$ lies on the $x$-axis and is equidistant from $A(2, 4)$ and $B(8, 6)$. Find the coordinates of $P$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Equate the squared distances $PA^2 = PB^2$: $(x - 2)^2 + (0 - 4)^2 = (x - 8)^2 + (0 - 6)^2$.
2. Expand and solve: $x^2 - 4x + 4 + 16 = x^2 - 16x + 64 + 36 \implies -4x + 20 = -16x + 100 \implies 12x = 80 \implies x = \frac{80}{12} = \frac{20}{3}$, so $P\left(\frac{20}{3}, 0\right)$.

**Final Answer**: $P\left(\frac{20}{3}, 0\right)$
</details>
