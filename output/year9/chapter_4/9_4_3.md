**Prerequisite Skills**:

- Gradient-intercept form $y = mx + c$ (Year 8 VC2M8A03)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Gradient-intercept form**: The equation of a non-vertical line expressed as $y = mx + c$, where $m$ is the gradient and $c$ is the $y$-intercept.
- **Point-gradient form**: The equation of a line with gradient $m$ passing through a known point $(x_1, y_1)$, written as $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.
- **Parallel lines**: Lines in the same plane that never intersect; non-vertical parallel lines have identical gradients ($m_1 = m_2$).
- **Perpendicular lines**: Lines that intersect at a right angle ($90^\circ$); two oblique lines are perpendicular if and only if $m_1 m_2 = -1$.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Point-Gradient Form and Finding the Equation of a Line

When the gradient $m$ and any point $(x_1, y_1)$ on a line are known, the equation of the line can be found immediately using the point-gradient formula $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$. If two points are given, first calculate $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$ and then substitute either point.

$$y - y_1 = m(x - x_1) \iff y = mx + (y_1 - mx_1)$$

> **💡 Watch Signs When $(x_1, y_1)$ Has Negative Coordinates**
> Substituting $(-2, -5)$ into $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$ gives $y - (-5) = m(x - (-2))$, which simplifies to $y + 5 = m(x + 2)$.

### Horizontal, Vertical, Parallel, and Perpendicular Lines

A horizontal line passing through $(a, k)$ has equation $y = k$ ($m = 0$), and a vertical line passing through $(h, b)$ has equation $x = h$ (undefined gradient). Two non-vertical lines with gradients $m_1$ and $m_2$ are parallel if $m_1 = m_2$, and perpendicular if $m_2 = -\frac{1}{m_1}$ (the negative reciprocal).

$$\text{Parallel: } m_1 = m_2 \qquad \text{Perpendicular: } m_1 m_2 = -1 \implies m_2 = -\frac{1}{m_1}$$

> **💡 Negative Reciprocal for Perpendicular Gradients**
> To find a perpendicular gradient, flip the fraction and reverse the sign: if $m_1 = \frac{3}{4}$, then $m_2 = -\frac{4}{3}$.

### General Form $Ax + By = C$ and Dual-Intercept Sketching

When a linear relation is given in general (or intercept) form $Ax + By = C$, the fastest way to sketch its graph is the dual-intercept method: set $x = 0$ to find the $y$-intercept, set $y = 0$ to find the $x$-intercept, plot both points, and draw the straight line through them.

$$Ax + By = C \implies y\text{-intercept } \left(0, \frac{C}{B}\right),\quad x\text{-intercept } \left(\frac{C}{A}, 0\right)$$

> **💡 Two Points Uniquely Determine a Line**
> Only lines that pass through the origin $(0, 0)$ have a single intercept point; for those, substitute a second value such as $x = 1$.

## Worked Examples

### WE9_4_3_1: Finding the Equation of a Line Passing Through Two Points

**Problem**: Find the equation of the straight line passing through the points $A(-2, 7)$ and $B(4, -5)$ in the form $y = mx + c$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Calculate the gradient $m$ using $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$. | $$m = \frac{-5 - 7}{4 - (-2)} = \frac{-12}{6} = -2$$ |
| 2 | Substitute $m = -2$ and $(x_1, y_1) = (4, -5)$ into $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$ and rearrange to $y = mx + c$. | $$y - (-5) = -2(x - 4) \implies y + 5 = -2x + 8 \implies y = -2x + 3$$ |

**Answer**: $y = -2x + 3$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Find the equation of the line passing through $(-3, -4)$ and $(3, 14)$ in gradient-intercept form.
> *Hint*: Find $m = \frac{14 - (-4)}{3 - (-3)}$, then use $y - 14 = m(x - 3)$.
> **Working**: $m = \frac{18}{6} = 3$; $y - 14 = 3(x - 3) \implies y = 3x + 5$.
> **Answer**: $y = 3x + 5$

### WE9_4_3_2: Equations of Parallel and Perpendicular Lines

**Problem**: Consider the line $L_1$ with equation $y = \frac{2}{3}x - 4$. Find the equation in gradient-intercept form of (a) the line parallel to $L_1$ passing through $(6, 1)$ and (b) the line perpendicular to $L_1$ passing through $(4, -3)$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | A line parallel to $L_1$ has the same gradient $m = \frac{2}{3}$. Substitute $m = \frac{2}{3}$ and $(6, 1)$ into $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$. | $$y - 1 = \frac{2}{3}(x - 6) \implies y - 1 = \frac{2}{3}x - 4 \implies y = \frac{2}{3}x - 3$$ |
| 2 | A line perpendicular to $L_1$ has gradient $m_{\perp} = -\frac{3}{2}$. Substitute $m_{\perp} = -\frac{3}{2}$ and $(4, -3)$ into $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$. | $$y - (-3) = -\frac{3}{2}(x - 4) \implies y + 3 = -\frac{3}{2}x + 6 \implies y = -\frac{3}{2}x + 3$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $y = \frac{2}{3}x - 3$, (b) $y = -\frac{3}{2}x + 3$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Find the equation of the line perpendicular to $y = -4x + 9$ that passes through $(8, 5)$.
> *Hint*: The negative reciprocal of $-4$ is $m_{\perp} = \frac{1}{4}$.
> **Working**: $y - 5 = \frac{1}{4}(x - 8) \implies y - 5 = \frac{1}{4}x - 2 \implies y = \frac{1}{4}x + 3$.
> **Answer**: $y = \frac{1}{4}x + 3$

### WE9_4_3_3: Linear Modelling from Two Data Points

**Problem**: A solar battery in Mildura charges at a constant linear rate during the morning. At $t = 2\text{ hours}$ after sunrise, the stored energy is $E = 4.6\text{ kWh}$. At $t = 5\text{ hours}$ after sunrise, the stored energy is $E = 10.0\text{ kWh}$. (a) Find the linear rule for $E$ in terms of $t$. (b) State the initial energy at sunrise ($t = 0$) and the charging rate in $\text{kW}$. (c) Find when the battery reaches its $13.6\text{ kWh}$ capacity.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Treat the data as two points $(2, 4.6)$ and $(5, 10.0)$. Find the gradient $m$ and substitute into $E - E_1 = m(t - t_1)$. | $$m = \frac{10.0 - 4.6}{5 - 2} = \frac{5.4}{3} = 1.8\text{ kW}$$
$$E - 10.0 = 1.8(t - 5) \implies E = 1.8t + 1.0$$ |
| 2 | Read off the initial energy $c = 1.0\text{ kWh}$ at $t = 0$, and solve $1.8t + 1.0 = 13.6$ for part (c). | $$1.8t + 1.0 = 13.6 \implies 1.8t = 12.6 \implies t = \frac{12.6}{1.8} = 7\text{ hours}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $E = 1.8t + 1.0$, (b) Initial energy $1.0\text{ kWh}$, charging rate $1.8\text{ kW}$, (c) $t = 7\text{ hours}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: A commercial oven heats linearly. After $3\text{ minutes}$ its temperature is $85^\circ\text{C}$, and after $8\text{ minutes}$ its temperature is $195^\circ\text{C}$. (a) Find the linear rule for temperature $T$ after $t\text{ minutes}$. (b) What was its initial temperature at $t = 0$?
> *Hint*: Find $m = \frac{195 - 85}{8 - 3}$, then solve for $c$.
> **Working**: $m = \frac{110}{5} = 22^\circ\text{C/min}$; $T = 22t + 19$, so initial temperature is $19^\circ\text{C}$.
> **Answer**: (a) $T = 22t + 19$, (b) $19^\circ\text{C}$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Taking the Reciprocal Without Changing the Sign for Perpendicular Lines
- **The Misconception**: Assuming the gradient perpendicular to $m = \frac{2}{5}$ is $\frac{5}{2}$ instead of $-\frac{5}{2}$.
- **The Correct Concept**: One of two perpendicular oblique lines must slope upwards ($m > 0$) while the other slopes downwards ($m < 0$), so their gradients must have opposite signs ($m_1 m_2 = -1$).
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$m_1 = \frac{2}{5} \implies m_{\perp} = \frac{5}{2} \quad \text{(Incorrect: product is } +1\text{, not } -1\text{)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$m_1 = \frac{2}{5} \implies m_{\perp} = -\frac{5}{2}$$
- **Counter-Example**: $\frac{2}{5} \times \left(-\frac{5}{2}\right) = -1$.

### Swapping Horizontal ($y = k$) and Vertical ($x = h$) Line Equations
- **The Misconception**: Thinking $x = 5$ is a horizontal line because the $x$-axis is horizontal.
- **The Correct Concept**: The equation $x = 5$ consists of all points $(5, y)$ whose $x$-coordinate is fixed at $5$, which forms a vertical line parallel to the $y$-axis.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\text{Horizontal line through } (3, 7): \quad x = 3 \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\text{Horizontal line through } (3, 7): \quad y = 7 \qquad \text{Vertical line through } (3, 7): \quad x = 3$$
- **Counter-Example**: The points $(0, 7)$, $(3, 7)$, and $(10, 7)$ all lie on a horizontal line at height $y = 7$.

### Fluency & Understanding

#### Question `9_4_3_Q1` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Find the equation of the straight line with gradient $m = -4$ passing through the point $(3, -2)$ in the form $y = mx + c$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute $m = -4$ and $(x_1, y_1) = (3, -2)$ into $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$: $y - (-2) = -4(x - 3)$.
2. Expand and rearrange: $y + 2 = -4x + 12 \implies y = -4x + 10$.

**Final Answer**: $y = -4x + 10$
</details>

#### Question `9_4_3_Q2` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Find the $x$-intercept and $y$-intercept of the line $5x - 2y = 20$, and write its equation in gradient-intercept form.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. For the $x$-intercept ($y = 0$): $5x = 20 \implies x = 4 \implies (4, 0)$. For the $y$-intercept ($x = 0$): $-2y = 20 \implies y = -10 \implies (0, -10)$.
2. Rearrange $5x - 2y = 20$: $2y = 5x - 20 \implies y = \frac{5}{2}x - 10$.

**Final Answer**: $x$-intercept $(4, 0)$, $y$-intercept $(0, -10)$, $y = \frac{5}{2}x - 10$
</details>

#### Question `9_4_3_Q3` [Medium] *(Fluency)*
Find the equation of the straight line passing through $(-4, 9)$ and $(2, -6)$ in gradient-intercept form.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find the gradient: $m = \frac{-6 - 9}{2 - (-4)} = \frac{-15}{6} = -\frac{5}{2}$.
2. Substitute $(2, -6)$ into $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$: $y + 6 = -\frac{5}{2}(x - 2) = -\frac{5}{2}x + 5 \implies y = -\frac{5}{2}x - 1$.

**Final Answer**: $y = -\frac{5}{2}x - 1$
</details>

#### Question `9_4_3_Q4` [Medium] *(Understanding)*
Find the equation of the line that passes through $(-6, 2)$ and is perpendicular to the line $2x + 3y = 12$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Rearrange $2x + 3y = 12$ into gradient-intercept form: $3y = -2x + 12 \implies y = -\frac{2}{3}x + 4$, so $m_1 = -\frac{2}{3}$ and $m_{\perp} = \frac{3}{2}$.
2. Use point-gradient form with $(-6, 2)$: $y - 2 = \frac{3}{2}(x + 6) = \frac{3}{2}x + 9 \implies y = \frac{3}{2}x + 11$.

**Final Answer**: $y = \frac{3}{2}x + 11$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `9_4_3_Q5` [Medium] *(Water Resource Modelling)*
A desalination storage reservoir holds $V\text{ ML}$ of water $d$ days after maintenance begins. On day $4$, $V = 320\text{ ML}$, and on day $10$, $V = 470\text{ ML}$. (a) Assuming a constant inflow rate, find the linear equation relating $V$ and $d$. (b) How much water was in the reservoir initially ($d = 0$)?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate the rate of change: $m = \frac{470 - 320}{10 - 4} = \frac{150}{6} = 25\text{ ML/day}$.
2. Use $(4, 320)$: $V - 320 = 25(d - 4) \implies V = 25d + 220$, so the initial volume at $d = 0$ was $220\text{ ML}$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $V = 25d + 220$, (b) $220\text{ ML}$
</details>

#### Question `9_4_3_Q6` [Challenging] *(Coordinate Geometry)*
Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of the line segment joining $A(1, -2)$ and $B(5, 6)$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find the midpoint $M$ and gradient $m_{AB}$ of $AB$: $M = \left(\frac{1 + 5}{2}, \frac{-2 + 6}{2}\right) = (3, 2)$ and $m_{AB} = \frac{6 - (-2)}{5 - 1} = \frac{8}{4} = 2$.
2. The perpendicular bisector has gradient $m_{\perp} = -\frac{1}{2}$ and passes through $M(3, 2)$: $y - 2 = -\frac{1}{2}(x - 3) \implies y = -\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{7}{2}$.

**Final Answer**: $y = -\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{7}{2}$
</details>

#### Question `9_4_3_Q7` [Medium] *(Financial Comparison)*
Two country taxis in Warrnambool charge linear fares for a trip of $x\text{ km}$. Taxi A charges $C_A = 2.4x + 4.5$ and Taxi B charges $C_B = 1.8x + 8.1$ (in dollars). Find the trip distance $x$ for which both taxis charge the same fare, and state that fare.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Equate the two cost rules: $2.4x + 4.5 = 1.8x + 8.1 \implies 0.6x = 3.6 \implies x = 6\text{ km}$.
2. Substitute $x = 6$: $C = 2.4(6) + 4.5 = 14.4 + 4.5 = \$18.90$.

**Final Answer**: $x = 6\text{ km}$, fare $\$18.90$
</details>

#### Question `9_4_3_Q8` [Challenging] *(Coordinate Geometry & Mensuration)*
The line $3x + 4y = 24$ intersects the $x$-axis at $A$ and the $y$-axis at $B$. Find (a) the exact length of segment $AB$ and (b) the perpendicular distance from the origin $O(0, 0)$ to the line $AB$ using the area of $\triangle OAB$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The intercepts are $A(8, 0)$ and $B(0, 6)$, so $AB = \sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = 10$.
2. The area of $\triangle OAB$ is $\frac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 6 = 24$. Using $AB = 10$ as the base with perpendicular height $h$: $\frac{1}{2} \times 10 \times h = 24 \implies 5h = 24 \implies h = \frac{24}{5} = 4.8\text{ units}$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $AB = 10$, (b) $4.8\text{ units}$ (or $\frac{24}{5}$)
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `9_4_3_Q9` [Medium] *(Geometric Classification)*
Determine whether the line passing through $A(-1, 4)$ and $B(2, 10)$ is parallel to, perpendicular to, or neither with respect to the line $6x - 3y = 15$. Justify your answer.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate the gradient of $AB$: $m_{AB} = \frac{10 - 4}{2 - (-1)} = \frac{6}{3} = 2$.
2. Rearrange $6x - 3y = 15 \implies 3y = 6x - 15 \implies y = 2x - 5$, which also has gradient $m = 2$ (and $y$-intercept $-5 \neq 6$), so the lines are parallel.

**Final Answer**: Parallel, because both lines have gradient $m = 2$ and different $y$-intercepts
</details>

#### Question `9_4_3_Q10` [Challenging] *(Coordinate Proof)*
Three vertices of a triangle are $P(1, 2)$, $Q(5, 10)$, and $R(9, 8)$. Use gradients to prove that $\triangle PQR$ is right-angled at $Q$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find the gradient of $PQ$: $m_{PQ} = \frac{10 - 2}{5 - 1} = \frac{8}{4} = 2$.
2. Find the gradient of $QR$: $m_{QR} = \frac{8 - 10}{9 - 5} = \frac{-2}{4} = -\frac{1}{2}$. Since $m_{PQ} \times m_{QR} = 2 \times \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) = -1$, $PQ$ is perpendicular to $QR$.

**Final Answer**: $m_{PQ} = 2$ and $m_{QR} = -\frac{1}{2}$; since $m_{PQ} \times m_{QR} = -1$, $PQ \perp QR$ and $\angle PQR = 90^\circ$
</details>

#### Question `9_4_3_Q11` [Medium] *(Conceptual Communication)*
State the equations of the horizontal line and the vertical line that intersect at the point $(-7, 11)$, and explain why one has gradient $0$ while the other has an undefined gradient.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Every point on the horizontal line through $(-7, 11)$ has $y = 11$ (so $\text{rise} = 0 \implies m = 0$).
2. Every point on the vertical line through $(-7, 11)$ has $x = -7$ (so $\text{run} = 0$, making $\frac{\text{rise}}{0}$ undefined).

**Final Answer**: Horizontal: $y = 11$ ($m = 0$); Vertical: $x = -7$ (undefined gradient)
</details>

#### Question `9_4_3_Q12` [Extension] *(Parameter Determination)*
Find the value of $k$ such that the line $(k + 1)x + 6y = 12$ is perpendicular to the line $3x - 2y = 7$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The line $3x - 2y = 7 \implies y = \frac{3}{2}x - \frac{7}{2}$ has gradient $m_1 = \frac{3}{2}$, so a perpendicular line must have gradient $m_2 = -\frac{2}{3}$.
2. Rearrange $(k + 1)x + 6y = 12 \implies y = -\frac{k + 1}{6}x + 2$. Equate gradients: $-\frac{k + 1}{6} = -\frac{2}{3} \implies k + 1 = 4 \implies k = 3$.

**Final Answer**: $k = 3$
</details>
