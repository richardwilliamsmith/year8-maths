### Key Vocabulary
- **Volume of a cylinder**: The three-dimensional space occupied by a cylinder of radius $r$ and perpendicular height $h$, given by $V = A_{\text{base}} h = \pi r^2 h$.
- **Limits of accuracy**: The lower bound and upper bound within which a true continuous measurement lies, given by $\text{Measured value} \pm 0.5 \times (\text{smallest unit of measurement})$.
- **Absolute error**: The magnitude of the difference between a measured (or approximated) value and the true (or exact) value: $E_{\text{abs}} = |\text{Measured} - \text{True}|$.
- **Percentage error**: The absolute error expressed as a percentage of the true value: $\text{Percentage error} = \frac{|\text{Measured} - \text{True}|}{\text{True}} \times 100\%$.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Volume of Right Prisms, Cylinders, and Annular Pipes

Every right prism or cylinder has a uniform cross-sectional area $A$ along its entire height (or length) $h$, so its volume is $V = A \times h$. For a circular cylinder, $A = \pi r^2$, giving $V = \pi r^2 h$. For a hollow cylindrical pipe (annular prism) with outer radius $R$ and inner radius $r$, the cross-sectional area is $\pi(R^2 - r^2)$.

$$V_{\text{cylinder}} = \pi r^2 h, \qquad V_{\text{hollow pipe}} = \pi(R^2 - r^2)h$$

> **💡 Key Capacity Conversions**
> $1\text{ cm}^3 = 1\text{ mL}$, $\;1000\text{ cm}^3 = 1\text{ L}$, and $1\text{ m}^3 = 1000\text{ L} = 1\text{ kL}$.

### Volume of Composite Solids (Addition and Subtraction)

Unlike surface area—where hidden interfaces are tricky—the volume of a composite solid is simply the sum of the volumes of its component parts (or the difference when a cavity or hole is drilled out).

$$V_{\text{composite}} = V_1 + V_2 \quad \text{(joined)} \qquad V_{\text{excavated}} = V_{\text{outer}} - V_{\text{hole}}$$

> **💡 Volumes Add and Subtract Directly**
> To find the volume of a block with a cylindrical hole drilled through it, calculate $V_{\text{block}} - V_{\text{cylinder}}$.

### Limits of Accuracy, Absolute Error, and Percentage Error

All physical measurements are rounded to a chosen precision (smallest scale unit $u$). The maximum possible error (tolerance) is $\pm 0.5u$, giving lower bound $M - 0.5u$ and upper bound $M + 0.5u$. When comparing a measured value $M$ to a known true value $T$, the absolute error is $|M - T|$, the relative error is $\frac{|M - T|}{T}$, and the percentage error is $\frac{|M - T|}{T} \times 100\%$.

$$\text{Absolute error} = |M - T|, \qquad \text{Percentage error} = \frac{|M - T|}{T} \times 100\%$$

> **💡 Divide by the True Value for Percentage Error**
> Always place the true (exact) value $T$ in the denominator when calculating relative or percentage error.

## Worked Examples

### WE9_6_2_1: Calculating the Volume and Capacity of a Cylindrical Water Tank

**Problem**: A cylindrical rainwater tank on a farm near Echuca has an internal diameter of $4\text{ m}$ and height $2.5\text{ m}$. Find (a) its exact volume in $\text{m}^3$ in terms of $\pi$, and (b) its capacity in litres rounded to the nearest litre.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Halve the diameter to get $r = 2\text{ m}$, then substitute $r = 2$ and $h = 2.5$ into $V = \pi r^2 h$. | $$r = \frac{4}{2} = 2\text{ m} \implies V = \pi(2)^2(2.5) = 10\pi\text{ m}^3$$ |
| 2 | Convert $\text{m}^3$ to litres by multiplying by $1000$ (since $1\text{ m}^3 = 1000\text{ L}$). | $$\text{Capacity} = 10\pi \times 1000 = 10\,000\pi \approx 31\,416\text{ L}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $10\pi\text{ m}^3$, (b) $31\,416\text{ L}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: A cylindrical soup tin has radius $4\text{ cm}$ and height $10\text{ cm}$. Find its exact volume in $\text{cm}^3$ and its capacity in millilitres to one decimal place.
> *Hint*: Use $V = \pi r^2 h$ and recall $1\text{ cm}^3 = 1\text{ mL}$.
> **Working**: $V = \pi(4)^2(10) = 160\pi\text{ cm}^3 \approx 502.7\text{ mL}$.
> **Answer**: $160\pi\text{ cm}^3 \approx 502.7\text{ mL}$

### WE9_6_2_2: Volume of a Composite Archway Block

**Problem**: A concrete bridge support is formed by a rectangular prism of length $10\text{ m}$, width $6\text{ m}$, and height $8\text{ m}$, with a semicircular tunnel of radius $r = 2\text{ m}$ running completely through its $10\text{ m}$ length. Find the exact volume of concrete in the support.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Calculate the volume of the full rectangular prism and the volume of the half-cylinder tunnel. | $$V_{\text{prism}} = 10 \times 6 \times 8 = 480\text{ m}^3$$
$$V_{\text{tunnel}} = \frac{1}{2}\pi r^2 h = \frac{1}{2}\pi(2)^2(10) = 20\pi\text{ m}^3$$ |
| 2 | Subtract the volume of the tunnel from the volume of the rectangular prism. | $$V_{\text{concrete}} = (480 - 20\pi)\text{ m}^3 \approx 417.17\text{ m}^3$$ |

**Answer**: $(480 - 20\pi)\text{ m}^3 \approx 417.17\text{ m}^3$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: A solid grain silo consists of a rectangular prism base ($6\text{ m} \times 4\text{ m} \times 5\text{ m}$) topped by a half-cylinder roof of radius $r = 2\text{ m}$ and length $6\text{ m}$. Find the exact total volume.
> *Hint*: Add $V_{\text{prism}} = 6 \times 4 \times 5$ and $V_{\text{half-cylinder}} = \frac{1}{2}\pi(2)^2(6)$.
> **Working**: $V = 120 + 12\pi\text{ m}^3$.
> **Answer**: $(120 + 12\pi)\text{ m}^3$

### WE9_6_2_3: Limits of Accuracy, Absolute Error, and Percentage Error

**Problem**: A carpenter measures the length of a timber beam as $240\text{ cm}$, rounded to the nearest centimetre, whereas laser surveying shows the true length is $241.2\text{ cm}$. (a) State the lower and upper bounds (limits of accuracy) implied by the $240\text{ cm}$ measurement. (b) Calculate the absolute error and percentage error (to $2$ decimal places) of the carpenter's measurement compared to the true length.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | For a measurement to the nearest $1\text{ cm}$, the maximum rounding error is $\pm 0.5\text{ cm}$. | $$\text{Lower bound} = 240 - 0.5 = 239.5\text{ cm}, \qquad \text{Upper bound} = 240 + 0.5 = 240.5\text{ cm}$$ |
| 2 | Calculate $E_{\text{abs}} = \|240 - 241.2\|$ and divide by the true length $241.2\text{ cm}$ to find the percentage error. | $$E_{\text{abs}} = \|240 - 241.2\| = 1.2\text{ cm}$$
$$\text{Percentage error} = \frac{1.2}{241.2} \times 100\% = \frac{100}{201}\% \approx 0.50\%$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $239.5\text{ cm}$ to $240.5\text{ cm}$, (b) Absolute error $= 1.2\text{ cm}$, percentage error $\approx 0.50\%$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: A chemist measures out $48\text{ mL}$ of solution when the true required volume is $50\text{ mL}$. Find (a) the absolute error and (b) the percentage error.
> *Hint*: Divide the absolute error $|48 - 50|$ by the true value $50$.
> **Working**: (a) $|48 - 50| = 2\text{ mL}$; (b) $\frac{2}{50} \times 100\% = 4\%$.
> **Answer**: (a) $2\text{ mL}$, (b) $4\%$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Squaring the Difference of Radii $(R - r)^2$ Instead of $R^2 - r^2$ for a Hollow Pipe
- **The Misconception**: Calculating the cross-sectional area of a hollow pipe as $\pi(R - r)^2$ instead of $\pi(R^2 - r^2)$.
- **The Correct Concept**: The volume of a hollow pipe is the outer cylinder volume $\pi R^2 h$ minus the inner cylinder hole volume $\pi r^2 h$, which factorises to $\pi(R^2 - r^2)h$. Because $(R - r)^2 \neq R^2 - r^2$, subtract the squares of the radii, not the square of their difference.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$R = 5,\; r = 3,\; h = 10 \implies V = \pi(5 - 3)^2(10) = 40\pi \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$V = \pi(5^2 - 3^2)(10) = \pi(25 - 9)(10) = 160\pi$$
- **Counter-Example**: $25 - 9 = 16$, whereas $(5 - 3)^2 = 4$.

### Dividing by the Measured Value Instead of the True Value in Percentage Error
- **The Misconception**: Computing percentage error as $\frac{|M - T|}{M} \times 100\%$ instead of $\frac{|M - T|}{T} \times 100\%$.
- **The Correct Concept**: Percentage error measures how large the error is relative to the true (exact) reference value $T$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$M = 80,\; T = 100 \implies \frac{|80 - 100|}{80} \times 100\% = 25\% \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\text{Percentage error} = \frac{|80 - 100|}{100} \times 100\% = 20\%$$
- **Counter-Example**: A measurement of $80$ when the true value is $100$ is $20\%$ below the true value.

#### Question `9_6_2_Q1` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Calculate the exact volume of a cylinder with radius $6\text{ cm}$ and height $15\text{ cm}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute $r = 6$ and $h = 15$ into $V = \pi r^2 h$.
2. Evaluate: $V = \pi(6)^2(15) = 36 \times 15\pi = 540\pi\text{ cm}^3$.

**Final Answer**: $540\pi\text{ cm}^3$
</details>

#### Question `9_6_2_Q2` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Calculate the volume of a right trapezoidal prism whose cross-section has parallel sides $8\text{ cm}$ and $14\text{ cm}$, perpendicular height $6\text{ cm}$, and prism length $12\text{ cm}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find the area of the trapezium base: $A = \frac{1}{2}(8 + 14)(6) = 66\text{ cm}^2$.
2. Multiply by the prism length $H = 12\text{ cm}$: $V = 66 \times 12 = 792\text{ cm}^3$.

**Final Answer**: $792\text{ cm}^3$
</details>

#### Question `9_6_2_Q3` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
A length is recorded as $16.4\text{ cm}$, correct to one decimal place ($0.1\text{ cm}$). State the absolute rounding tolerance and the lower and upper bounds of the true length.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Half of the smallest unit ($0.1\text{ cm}$) is $0.5 \times 0.1 = 0.05\text{ cm}$.
2. Subtract and add $0.05\text{ cm}$: lower bound $= 16.35\text{ cm}$ and upper bound $= 16.45\text{ cm}$.

**Final Answer**: Tolerance $\pm 0.05\text{ cm}$; lower bound $16.35\text{ cm}$, upper bound $16.45\text{ cm}$
</details>

#### Question `9_6_2_Q4` [Medium] *(Understanding)*
A hollow brass tube has length $25\text{ cm}$, outer diameter $10\text{ cm}$, and inner diameter $6\text{ cm}$. Find the exact volume of brass in the tube.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The outer radius is $R = 5\text{ cm}$ and the inner radius is $r = 3\text{ cm}$.
2. Use $V = \pi(R^2 - r^2)h = \pi(5^2 - 3^2)(25) = 16\pi \times 25 = 400\pi\text{ cm}^3$.

**Final Answer**: $400\pi\text{ cm}^3$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `9_6_2_Q5` [Medium] *(Inverse Mensuration)*
A cylinder has a volume of $245\pi\text{ cm}^3$ and a height of $5\text{ cm}$. Find the radius $r$ of the cylinder.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Substitute into $\pi r^2 h = V$: $\pi r^2 (5) = 245\pi \implies r^2 = \frac{245}{5} = 49$.
2. Since $r > 0$, $r = \sqrt{49} = 7\text{ cm}$.

**Final Answer**: $r = 7\text{ cm}$
</details>

#### Question `9_6_2_Q6` [Medium] *(Agricultural Capacity)*
A horse drinking trough in the Goulburn Valley is shaped as a half-cylinder of internal diameter $1.2\text{ m}$ and length $2.5\text{ m}$. Using $\pi \approx 3.1416$, calculate the capacity of the trough in litres, rounded to the nearest litre.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The radius is $r = 0.6\text{ m}$, so the volume of the half-cylinder is $V = \frac{1}{2}\pi r^2 h = \frac{1}{2}\pi(0.6)^2(2.5) = 0.45\pi\text{ m}^3$.
2. Multiply by $1000\text{ L/m}^3$: $\text{Capacity} = 450\pi \approx 450 \times 3.14159265 \approx 1414\text{ L}$.

**Final Answer**: $1414\text{ L}$
</details>

#### Question `9_6_2_Q7` [Medium] *(Manufacturing Quality Control)*
A machinist manufactures a cylindrical steel bolt with a true specification diameter of $12.50\text{ mm}$. Quality control measures the bolt diameter as $12.35\text{ mm}$. Find (a) the absolute error and (b) the percentage error of the bolt diameter.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate the absolute error: $|12.35 - 12.50| = 0.15\text{ mm}$.

#### Question `9_6_2_Q8` [Challenging] *(Civil Engineering)*
A rectangular swimming pool is $25\text{ m}$ long and $10\text{ m}$ wide. Its depth increases linearly from $1.2\text{ m}$ at the shallow end to $2.4\text{ m}$ at the deep end, so its side profile is a trapezium. Calculate (a) the volume of the pool in $\text{m}^3$ and (b) its capacity in kilolitres ($\text{kL}$).

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The trapezoidal cross-section has area $A = \frac{1}{2}(1.2 + 2.4)(25) = 1.8 \times 25 = 45\text{ m}^2$.
2. Multiply by the width $H = 10\text{ m}$: $V = 45 \times 10 = 450\text{ m}^3 = 450\text{ kL}$ (since $1\text{ m}^3 = 1\text{ kL}$).

**Final Answer**: (a) $450\text{ m}^3$, (b) $450\text{ kL}$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `9_6_2_Q9` [Medium] *(Proportional Reasoning)*
Compare the effect on the volume of a cylinder $V = \pi r^2 h$ when (a) its height $h$ is doubled (with $r$ constant) versus when (b) its radius $r$ is doubled (with $h$ constant).

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Replacing $h$ with $2h$ gives $V_{\text{new}} = \pi r^2 (2h) = 2(\pi r^2 h) = 2V$.
2. Replacing $r$ with $2r$ gives $V_{\text{new}} = \pi (2r)^2 h = 4\pi r^2 h = 4V$.

**Final Answer**: Doubling $h$ doubles the volume ($2V$), whereas doubling $r$ quadruples the volume ($4V$) because $r$ is squared
</details>

#### Question `9_6_2_Q10` [Challenging] *(Error Propagation)*
A rectangular tabletop has measured length $20\text{ cm}$ and width $10\text{ cm}$, each measured to the nearest centimetre ($\pm 0.5\text{ cm}$). Find the lower bound and upper bound of the true area of the tabletop, and explain why the maximum error in the area is not simply $0.5 \times 0.5 = 0.25\text{ cm}^2$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The length lies in $[19.5, 20.5]$ and the width lies in $[9.5, 10.5]$. Thus $A_{\min} = 19.5 \times 9.5 = 185.25\text{ cm}^2$ and $A_{\max} = 20.5 \times 10.5 = 215.25\text{ cm}^2$.
2. Expanding $(20 + 0.5)(10 + 0.5) = 200 + 20(0.5) + 10(0.5) + 0.25 = 215.25$ shows that the $\pm 0.5\text{ cm}$ errors multiply by the full side lengths $20\text{ cm}$ and $10\text{ cm}$, producing $\approx \pm 15\text{ cm}^2$ of variation.

**Final Answer**: Lower bound $185.25\text{ cm}^2$, upper bound $215.25\text{ cm}^2$; error strips along the full length and width dominate
</details>

#### Question `9_6_2_Q11` [Challenging] *(Historical Approximations)*
Archimedes used $\frac{22}{7}$ as a rational approximation for $\pi$. Calculate the percentage error of $\frac{22}{7}$ compared to $\pi$, rounded to $3$ decimal places.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The absolute error is $\left|\frac{22}{7} - \pi\right| \approx 3.142857 - 3.141593 = 0.0012645$.
2. Divide by $\pi$ and multiply by $100\%$: $\frac{0.0012645}{3.1415927} \times 100\% \approx 0.040\%$.

**Final Answer**: $0.040\%$
</details>

#### Question `9_6_2_Q12` [Extension] *(Conservation of Volume)*
A solid metal cylinder of radius $6\text{ cm}$ and height $14\text{ cm}$ is melted down and recast into a right square-based prism of height $11\text{ cm}$ (using $\pi \approx \frac{22}{7}$). Find the side length $s$ of the square base.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Using $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$, the volume of the cylinder is $V = \frac{22}{7} \times 6^2 \times 14 = 22 \times 36 \times 2 = 1584\text{ cm}^3$.
2. Equate to the volume of the square prism $s^2 h = 11s^2$: $11s^2 = 1584 \implies s^2 = 144 \implies s = 12\text{ cm}$ (since $s > 0$).

**Final Answer**: $s = 12\text{ cm}$
</details>
