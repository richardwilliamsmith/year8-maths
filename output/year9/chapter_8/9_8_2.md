### Key Vocabulary
- **Inverse trigonometric functions ($\sin^{-1}, \cos^{-1}, \tan^{-1}$)**: The inverse operations (also written $\arcsin, \arccos, \arctan$) that recover an unknown acute angle $\theta$ from a known side ratio: e.g., if $\sin\theta = \frac{O}{H}$, then $\theta = \sin^{-1}\!\left(\frac{O}{H}\right)$.
- **Angle of elevation**: The upward angle through which an observer's line of sight is raised from the horizontal line when looking up at an elevated object.
- **Angle of depression**: The downward angle through which an observer's line of sight is lowered from the horizontal line when looking down at an object below.
- **True bearing ($\text{NNN}^\circ\text{T}$)**: A direction angle measured clockwise from True North ($000^\circ\text{T}$), always written using three digits from $000^\circ\text{T}$ to $360^\circ\text{T}$ (e.g., due East is $090^\circ\text{T}$ and South-West is $225^\circ\text{T}$).

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Finding Unknown Acute Angles Using Inverse Trigonometry

When two side lengths of a right-angled triangle are known, we find an unknown acute angle $\theta$ by first writing the appropriate trigonometric ratio ($\sin\theta = \frac{O}{H}$, $\cos\theta = \frac{A}{H}$, or $\tan\theta = \frac{O}{A}$) and then applying the corresponding inverse trigonometric function ($\sin^{-1}$, $\cos^{-1}$, or $\tan^{-1}$). Note that the superscript $-1$ in $\sin^{-1}$ denotes the inverse function, NOT the reciprocal $\frac{1}{\sin\theta}$.

$$\sin\theta = \frac{O}{H} \implies \theta = \sin^{-1}\!\left(\frac{O}{H}\right), \quad \cos\theta = \frac{A}{H} \implies \theta = \cos^{-1}\!\left(\frac{A}{H}\right), \quad \tan\theta = \frac{O}{A} \implies \theta = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{O}{A}\right)$$

> **💡 Check that Your Calculator Is in DEGREES Mode**
> Before evaluating any trigonometric or inverse trigonometric expression in Year 9, check that your calculator screen displays 'DEG' (or 'D'). For example, $\sin^{-1}(0.5)$ must return $30^\circ$.

### Angles of Elevation and Depression

Both the angle of elevation (looking UP) and the angle of depression (looking DOWN) are ALWAYS measured from a horizontal reference line, never from a vertical wall or cliff. Because the horizontal line at the top observer and the horizontal ground at the bottom object are parallel, the angle of depression from $A$ down to $B$ equals the angle of elevation from $B$ up to $A$ (alternate angles in a 'Z' configuration on parallel lines).

$$\text{Angle of depression from } A \text{ to } B = \text{Angle of elevation from } B \text{ to } A \; (\text{alternate angles})$$

> **💡 Always Draw the Horizontal Dotted Line First**
> The angle of depression is between the top HORIZONTAL line and the line of sight. If a question gives an angle of depression $\theta$, draw a horizontal dotted line at the top or transfer $\theta$ into the bottom corner as the equal alternate angle of elevation.

### Compass and True Bearings in Navigation

A three-figure true bearing ($\text{NNN}^\circ\text{T}$) measures direction clockwise from North ($000^\circ\text{T}$), with East $= 090^\circ\text{T}$, South $= 180^\circ\text{T}$, and West $= 270^\circ\text{T}$. When solving navigation problems, draw a North-South-East-West compass rose at EVERY turning point or vertex. To find a back-bearing (the direction from $B$ back to $A$), add $180^\circ$ if the forward bearing is less than $180^\circ$, or subtract $180^\circ$ if the forward bearing is greater than or equal to $180^\circ$.

$$\text{Back-bearing} = \begin{cases} \theta + 180^\circ & \text{if } \theta < 180^\circ \\ \theta - 180^\circ & \text{if } \theta \ge 180^\circ \end{cases}$$

> **💡 Read carefully: 'From' Marks the Compass Centre**
> In the phrase 'the bearing of $B$ FROM $A$', the word FROM tells you where to draw the North arrow: stand at $A$, face North ($000^\circ\text{T}$), and turn clockwise until you point towards $B$.

## Worked Examples

### WE9_8_2_1: Finding Unknown Acute Angles Using Inverse Trigonometric Functions

**Problem**: Find the acute angle $\theta$ in each right-angled triangle: (a) Opposite side $O = 9\text{ cm}$ and Hypotenuse $H = 18\text{ cm}$, and (b) Adjacent side $A = 7\text{ m}$ and Opposite side $O = 7\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), $O$ and $H$ are given, so use $\sin\theta = \frac{O}{H}$ and apply $\sin^{-1}$. | $$\sin\theta = \frac{9}{18} = \frac{1}{2} \implies \theta = \sin^{-1}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = 30^\circ$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), $O$ and $A$ are given, so use $\tan\theta = \frac{O}{A}$ and apply $\tan^{-1}$. | $$\tan\theta = \frac{7\sqrt{3}}{7} = \sqrt{3} \implies \theta = \tan^{-1}(\sqrt{3}) = 60^\circ$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $\theta = 30^\circ$, (b) $\theta = 60^\circ$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: A right-angled triangle has an adjacent side of $11\text{ cm}$ and an opposite side of $11\text{ cm}$. Find the acute angle $\theta$.
> *Hint*: Use $\tan\theta = \frac{O}{A} = \frac{11}{11} = 1$.
> **Working**: $\tan\theta = 1 \implies \theta = \tan^{-1}(1) = 45^\circ$.
> **Answer**: $45^\circ$

### WE9_8_2_2: Cliff Lighthouse Angle of Depression Problem

**Problem**: The lantern room of the Split Point Lighthouse at Aireys Inlet is $60\text{ m}$ vertically above sea level. The keeper spots a yacht at an angle of depression of $30^\circ$. Find the exact horizontal distance $d$ from the base of the cliff directly below the lighthouse to the yacht (and round to $1$ decimal place).

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | By alternate angles between the parallel horizontal sea level and the keeper's horizontal line of sight, the angle of elevation from the yacht to the lantern room is also $30^\circ$. | $$\tan 30^\circ = \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Adjacent}} = \frac{60}{d}$$ |
| 2 | Swap $d$ and $\tan 30^\circ$ (where $\tan 30^\circ = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$) to solve for $d$. | $$d = \frac{60}{\tan 30^\circ} = 60\sqrt{3}\text{ m} \approx 103.9\text{ m}$$ |

**Answer**: $60\sqrt{3}\text{ m} \approx 103.9\text{ m}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: From the top of a $45\text{ m}$ observation tower, a ranger sights a campfire at a horizontal ground distance of $45\text{ m}$ from the base of the tower. What is the angle of depression from the ranger to the campfire?
> *Hint*: The angle of depression equals the angle of elevation $\theta$, where $\tan\theta = \frac{45}{45}$.
> **Working**: $\tan\theta = \frac{45}{45} = 1 \implies \theta = \tan^{-1}(1) = 45^\circ$.
> **Answer**: $45^\circ$

### WE9_8_2_3: Navigation and True Bearings in Right-Angled Triangles

**Problem**: A search-and-rescue helicopter leaves base $O$ and flies $40\text{ km}$ due North to point $A$, then turns and flies $40\sqrt{3}\text{ km}$ due East to point $B$. (a) Find the direct distance $OB$ from base $O$ to $B$. (b) Find the three-figure true bearing of $B$ from $O$, and the back-bearing of $O$ from $B$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Since North and East meet at $\angle OAB = 90^\circ$, apply Pythagoras' theorem to find $OB$. | $$OB = \sqrt{40^2 + (40\sqrt{3})^2} = \sqrt{1600 + 4800} = \sqrt{6400} = 80\text{ km}$$ |
| 2 | In $\triangle OAB$, the angle $\theta = \angle AOB$ is measured clockwise from North ($OA$). Use $\tan\theta = \frac{AB}{OA}$ to find $\theta$, write it as a 3-digit bearing, and add $180^\circ$ for the back-bearing. | $$\tan\theta = \frac{40\sqrt{3}}{40} = \sqrt{3} \implies \theta = 60^\circ \implies \text{Bearing of } B \text{ from } O = 060^\circ\text{T}$$
$$\text{Back-bearing of } O \text{ from } B = 060^\circ + 180^\circ = 240^\circ\text{T}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $80\text{ km}$, (b) Bearing of $B$ from $O$ is $060^\circ\text{T}$; bearing of $O$ from $B$ is $240^\circ\text{T}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: A yacht sails from port $P$ on a true bearing of $120^\circ\text{T}$ for $50\text{ km}$ to point $Q$. How far South and how far East of port $P$ is the yacht at $Q$?
> *Hint*: A bearing of $120^\circ\text{T}$ is $30^\circ$ South of East (or $60^\circ$ East of South).
> **Working**: Using the $30^\circ$ angle below East: $\text{South} = 50\sin 30^\circ = 25\text{ km}$ and $\text{East} = 50\cos 30^\circ = 25\sqrt{3}\text{ km} \approx 43.30\text{ km}$.
> **Answer**: $25\text{ km}$ South and $25\sqrt{3}\text{ km} \approx 43.30\text{ km}$ East

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Placing the Angle of Depression Against the Vertical Wall or Cliff
- **The Misconception**: Putting the angle of depression inside the triangle between the vertical cliff face and the slanted line of sight.
- **The Correct Concept**: Angles of elevation and depression are ALWAYS measured from the HORIZONTAL. The angle between the vertical cliff face and the line of sight is the complement $(90^\circ - \theta)$, not $\theta$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\text{Cliff } 50\text{ m}, \text{ depression } 30^\circ: \quad \tan 30^\circ = \frac{d}{50} \implies d = 50\tan 30^\circ \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\tan 30^\circ = \frac{50}{d} \implies d = \frac{50}{\tan 30^\circ} = 50\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$$
- **Counter-Example**: A shallow angle of depression like $5^\circ$ means looking almost horizontally at a very distant ship, whereas putting $5^\circ$ against the cliff places the ship almost directly underneath the cliff.

### Measuring True Bearings from the East/West Axis Instead of North
- **The Misconception**: Using an interior triangle angle measured from East or South directly as the true bearing without converting to clockwise from North ($000^\circ\text{T}$).
- **The Correct Concept**: A true bearing must ALWAYS start at North ($000^\circ\text{T}$), turn clockwise, and be written with three digits. For example, $30^\circ$ South of West is $270^\circ - 30^\circ = 240^\circ\text{T}$, not $030^\circ\text{T}$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$30^\circ \text{ West of South} \implies \text{Bearing } = 030^\circ\text{T} \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\text{South is } 180^\circ\text{T}, \text{ so } 30^\circ \text{ clockwise past South is } 180^\circ + 30^\circ = 210^\circ\text{T}.$$
- **Counter-Example**: $030^\circ\text{T}$ points North-North-East, which is in the opposite hemisphere from South-South-West ($210^\circ\text{T}$).

### Fluency & Understanding

#### Question `9_8_2_Q1` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Find the acute angle $\theta$ in a right-angled triangle if the side adjacent to $\theta$ is $12\text{ cm}$ and the hypotenuse is $24\text{ cm}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Write the cosine ratio: $\cos\theta = \frac{A}{H} = \frac{12}{24} = \frac{1}{2}$.
2. Apply inverse cosine: $\theta = \cos^{-1}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = 60^\circ$.

**Final Answer**: $60^\circ$
</details>

#### Question `9_8_2_Q2` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Find the acute angle $\theta$ in a right-angled triangle if the side opposite $\theta$ is $5\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}$ and the hypotenuse is $10\text{ cm}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Write the sine ratio: $\sin\theta = \frac{5\sqrt{2}}{10} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.
2. Apply inverse sine: $\theta = \sin^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right) = 45^\circ$.

**Final Answer**: $45^\circ$
</details>

#### Question `9_8_2_Q3` [Easy] *(Bearings Fluency)*
Write each of the following compass directions as a three-figure true bearing: (a) $35^\circ$ East of North ($\text{N}35^\circ\text{E}$), (b) $25^\circ$ South of East, (c) $40^\circ$ West of South ($\text{S}40^\circ\text{W}$), and (d) $15^\circ$ West of North ($\text{N}15^\circ\text{W}$).

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. (a) Clockwise from $000^\circ\text{T}$ gives $035^\circ\text{T}$; (b) $25^\circ$ past East ($090^\circ\text{T}$) gives $090^\circ + 25^\circ = 115^\circ\text{T}$.
2. (c) $40^\circ$ clockwise past South ($180^\circ\text{T}$) gives $180^\circ + 40^\circ = 220^\circ\text{T}$; (d) $15^\circ$ anticlockwise from $360^\circ\text{T}$ gives $360^\circ - 15^\circ = 345^\circ\text{T}$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $035^\circ\text{T}$, (b) $115^\circ\text{T}$, (c) $220^\circ\text{T}$, (d) $345^\circ\text{T}$
</details>

#### Question `9_8_2_Q4` [Medium] *(Back-Bearings)*
The true bearing of town $B$ from town $A$ is $138^\circ\text{T}$. Find the true bearing of town $A$ from town $B$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Since the forward bearing $138^\circ\text{T}$ is less than $180^\circ$, add $180^\circ$ to find the opposite direction.
2. $\text{Back-bearing} = 138^\circ + 180^\circ = 318^\circ\text{T}$.

**Final Answer**: $318^\circ\text{T}$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `9_8_2_Q5` [Medium] *(Surveying with ClinometerHeight)*
A surveyor stands $50\text{ m}$ horizontally from the base of a vertical communications tower in Canberra. Using a clinometer held at eye level $1.7\text{ m}$ above the ground, she measures the angle of elevation to the top of the tower to be $45^\circ$. Find the total height of the tower above the ground.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find the vertical rise $h$ above eye level: $h = 50 \tan 45^\circ = 50 \times 1 = 50\text{ m}$.
2. Add the surveyor's eye height of $1.7\text{ m}$: $\text{Total height} = 50 + 1.7 = 51.7\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: $51.7\text{ m}$
</details>

#### Question `9_8_2_Q6` [Medium] *(Aerial Bushfire Surveillance)*
An bushfire spotter plane is flying at a constant altitude of $600\text{ m}$ above flat ground in Gippsland. The pilot spots a plume of smoke at an angle of depression of $30^\circ$. What is the direct line-of-sight distance from the plane to the base of the smoke plume?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The vertical altitude $O = 600\text{ m}$ is opposite the $30^\circ$ angle of elevation/depression, and the direct line-of-sight distance is the hypotenuse $H$.
2. Solve $\sin 30^\circ = \frac{600}{H} \implies H = \frac{600}{\sin 30^\circ} = \frac{600}{0.5} = 1200\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: $1200\text{ m}$ ($1.2\text{ km}$)
</details>

#### Question `9_8_2_Q7` [Challenging] *(Bushwalking Navigation)*
A bushwalker hikes $18\text{ km}$ on a true bearing of $210^\circ\text{T}$ from campsite $C$ to lookout $L$. How far South and how far West of campsite $C$ is lookout $L$?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. A bearing of $210^\circ\text{T}$ makes an angle of $210^\circ - 180^\circ = 30^\circ$ West of South.
2. In the right-angled triangle with hypotenuse $18\text{ km}$, $\text{South} = 18\cos 30^\circ = 9\sqrt{3}\text{ km} \approx 15.59\text{ km}$ and $\text{West} = 18\sin 30^\circ = 9\text{ km}$.

**Final Answer**: $9\sqrt{3}\text{ km} \approx 15.59\text{ km}$ South and $9\text{ km}$ West
</details>

#### Question `9_8_2_Q8` [Challenging] *(Maritime Navigation)*
Two ships leave Fremantle harbour at the same time. Ship $A$ sails on a bearing of $040^\circ\text{T}$ for $15\text{ km}$, while Ship $B$ sails on a bearing of $130^\circ\text{T}$ for $20\text{ km}$. Show that their paths are perpendicular ($90^\circ$) and find the direct distance between the two ships.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The angle between bearings $040^\circ\text{T}$ and $130^\circ\text{T}$ is $130^\circ - 40^\circ = 90^\circ$, forming a right-angled triangle.
2. By Pythagoras' theorem, $d = \sqrt{15^2 + 20^2} = \sqrt{225 + 400} = \sqrt{625} = 25\text{ km}$.

**Final Answer**: $25\text{ km}$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `9_8_2_Q9` [Medium] *(Domain of Inverse Trig Functions)*
A student enters $\sin^{-1}(1.4)$ into her calculator and receives a 'MATH ERROR'. Explain geometrically why $\sin^{-1}(x)$ and $\cos^{-1}(x)$ are undefined whenever $|x| > 1$, whereas $\tan^{-1}(x)$ is defined for all real numbers $x$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Because the hypotenuse $H$ is strictly the longest side of any right-angled triangle, neither $\frac{O}{H}$ nor $\frac{A}{H}$ can ever be $1.4 > 1$.
2. By contrast, the ratio of the two legs $\tan\theta = \frac{O}{A}$ can be arbitrarily large as $\theta$ approaches $90^\circ$.

**Final Answer**: In a right-angled triangle, $O < H$ and $A < H$, so $\sin\theta = \frac{O}{H}$ and $\cos\theta = \frac{A}{H}$ can never exceed $1$; however, $\frac{O}{A}$ can take any positive value
</details>

#### Question `9_8_2_Q10` [Challenging] *(Two-Position Elevation Problem)*
From a boat at point $A$, the angle of elevation to the top of a vertical $60\text{ m}$ coastal cliff is $30^\circ$. The boat then sails directly towards the cliff to point $B$, where the angle of elevation to the top of the cliff is now $60^\circ$. Find the exact distance $AB$ that the boat sailed.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. At point $A$, horizontal distance from the cliff base is $d_A = \frac{60}{\tan 30^\circ} = 60\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$. At point $B$, horizontal distance is $d_B = \frac{60}{\tan 60^\circ} = \frac{60}{\sqrt{3}} = 20\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$.
2. Subtracting gives the distance sailed: $AB = d_A - d_B = 60\sqrt{3} - 20\sqrt{3} = 40\sqrt{3}\text{ m} \approx 69.28\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: $40\sqrt{3}\text{ m} \approx 69.28\text{ m}$
</details>

#### Question `9_8_2_Q11` [Extension] *(3D Angle of Elevation)*
A rectangular prism has base dimensions $4\text{ cm} \times 3\text{ cm}$ and vertical height $5\text{ cm}$. Find the angle of elevation $\theta$ that the internal 3D space diagonal makes with the horizontal $4\text{ cm} \times 3\text{ cm}$ base floor.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. First find the horizontal base diagonal using Pythagoras' theorem: $d_{\text{floor}} = \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5\text{ cm}$.
2. In the vertical right-angled triangle formed by $d_{\text{floor}} = 5\text{ cm}$ and vertical height $h = 5\text{ cm}$, $\tan\theta = \frac{5}{5} = 1 \implies \theta = 45^\circ$.

**Final Answer**: $45^\circ$
</details>

#### Question `9_8_2_Q12` [Extension] *(Relative Elevation Between Towers)*
Two observation towers $A$ and $B$ of heights $20\text{ m}$ and $50\text{ m}$ stand on flat ground $30\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$ apart. Find (a) the angle of elevation from the top of tower $A$ to the top of tower $B$, and (b) the direct distance between their tops.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The vertical height difference between the tops is $\Delta h = 50 - 20 = 30\text{ m}$, and the horizontal separation is $30\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$.
2. Thus $\tan\theta = \frac{30}{30\sqrt{3}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \implies \theta = 30^\circ$, and the direct distance is $d = \frac{30}{\sin 30^\circ} = 60\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: (a) $30^\circ$, (b) $60\text{ m}$
</details>
