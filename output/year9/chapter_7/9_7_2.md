### Key Vocabulary
- **Area scale factor ($k^2$)**: If two 2D figures or 3D surfaces are similar with linear scale factor $k$, the ratio of their corresponding areas is $k^2 = \frac{\text{Area of image}}{\text{Area of original}}$.
- **Volume scale factor ($k^3$)**: If two 3D solids are similar with linear scale factor $k$, the ratio of their volumes (or capacities) is $k^3 = \frac{\text{Volume of image}}{\text{Volume of original}}$.
- **Convex polyhedron**: A closed three-dimensional solid bounded entirely by flat polygonal faces ($F$), straight edges ($E$), and sharp corner vertices ($V$), with no indentations.
- **Euler's polyhedron formula**: For any convex polyhedron with $V$ vertices, $E$ edges, and $F$ faces, the alternating sum of vertices, edges, and faces is always $2$: $V - E + F = 2$.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Scaling Length ($k$), Area ($k^2$), and Volume ($k^3$)

When every linear dimension (length, width, height, radius) of a shape or solid is multiplied by a linear scale factor $k$, any two-dimensional area is multiplied by $k \times k = k^2$, and any three-dimensional volume (or liquid capacity) is multiplied by $k \times k \times k = k^3$. If corresponding lengths are in the ratio $a : b$, then corresponding areas are in the ratio $a^2 : b^2$ and corresponding volumes are in the ratio $a^3 : b^3$.

$$k_{\text{length}} = k \implies k_{\text{area}} = k^2 \quad \text{and} \quad k_{\text{volume}} = k^3$$

> **💡 Match the Exponent to the Dimension**
> Length is $1\text{D}$ (multiply by $k^1$), Area is $2\text{D}$ in $\text{cm}^2$ (multiply by $k^2$), and Volume is $3\text{D}$ in $\text{cm}^3$ (multiply by $k^3$).

### Working Backwards from Area or Volume Ratios to Find $k$

If you are given the areas or volumes of two similar objects and need to find a missing length, you must first recover the linear scale factor $k$ before scaling any other quantity. From areas, take the square root: $k = \sqrt{\frac{A_2}{A_1}}$. From volumes, take the cube root: $k = \sqrt[3]{\frac{V_2}{V_1}}$. To convert from an area ratio to a volume ratio, go through $k$ as a bridge: $V_2 / V_1 = k^3 = (\sqrt{A_2 / A_1})^3$.

$$k = \sqrt{\frac{A_{\text{image}}}{A_{\text{original}}}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{V_{\text{image}}}{V_{\text{original}}}}$$

> **💡 Always Pass Through the Linear Bridge $k$**
> Never try to convert directly between area and volume in one step. Always take $\sqrt{\text{area ratio}}$ or $\sqrt[3]{\text{volume ratio}}$ to find $k$ first, then raise $k$ to the target dimension.

### Polyhedra Nets and Euler's Formula ($V - E + F = 2$)

A polyhedron is a 3D solid with flat polygonal faces (e.g., prisms, pyramids, and the five Platonic solids: tetrahedron, cube, octahedron, dodecahedron, and icosahedron). Cylinders, cones, and spheres are not polyhedra because they have curved surfaces. Leonhard Euler proved that for every convex polyhedron with $V$ vertices, $E$ edges, and $F$ faces, the invariant relation $V - E + F = 2$ (equivalently $V + F = E + 2$) always holds.

$$V - E + F = 2 \iff V + F = E + 2$$

> **💡 Vertices Plus Faces Equals Edges Plus Two**
> For a quick mental check without negative signs, add Vertices and Faces together — the result is always exactly $2$ more than the number of Edges ($V + F = E + 2$).

## Worked Examples

### WE9_7_2_1: Calculating Surface Area and Volume of Similar Solids from Length Ratio

**Problem**: Two mathematically similar water tanks have heights of $1.5\text{ m}$ and $4.5\text{ m}$. The smaller tank has an external surface area of $12\text{ m}^2$ and a capacity of $4500\text{ L}$. Find (a) the surface area and (b) the capacity of the larger tank.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Calculate the linear scale factor $k$ from the heights, then find the area scale factor $k^2$ and volume scale factor $k^3$. | $$k = \frac{4.5}{1.5} = 3 \implies k^2 = 3^2 = 9, \qquad k^3 = 3^3 = 27$$ |
| 2 | Multiply the smaller tank's surface area by $k^2 = 9$ and its capacity by $k^3 = 27$. | $$\text{Area}_{\text{large}} = 9 \times 12 = 108\text{ m}^2$$
$$\text{Capacity}_{\text{large}} = 27 \times 4500 = 121\,500\text{ L}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $108\text{ m}^2$, (b) $121\,500\text{ L}$ (or $121.5\text{ kL}$)

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: A model yacht is built to a linear scale of $1 : 5$ compared to a full-size racing yacht. If the model's sail area is $0.8\text{ m}^2$ and its hull volume is $0.04\text{ m}^3$, find the sail area and hull volume of the full-size yacht.
> *Hint*: Here $k = 5$, so multiply area by $5^2 = 25$ and volume by $5^3 = 125$.
> **Working**: $\text{Sail area} = 25 \times 0.8 = 20\text{ m}^2$; $\text{Hull volume} = 125 \times 0.04 = 5\text{ m}^3$.
> **Answer**: Sail area $20\text{ m}^2$, hull volume $5\text{ m}^3$

### WE9_7_2_2: Finding Linear Dimensions and Surface Area Ratio from Volumes

**Problem**: Two similar solid brass statues have volumes of $160\text{ cm}^3$ and $1280\text{ cm}^3$. (a) Find the linear scale factor $k$ from the smaller statue to the larger statue. (b) If the smaller statue is $12\text{ cm}$ tall and requires $\$18$ worth of gold leaf to gild its surface, find the height and the cost of gilding the larger statue.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Divide the volumes to find $k^3$, then take the cube root to find the linear scale factor $k$. | $$k^3 = \frac{1280}{160} = 8 \implies k = \sqrt[3]{8} = 2$$ |
| 2 | Height is a $1\text{D}$ length (multiply by $k = 2$), while gilding cost is proportional to $2\text{D}$ surface area (multiply by $k^2 = 4$). | $$\text{Height}_{\text{large}} = 2 \times 12 = 24\text{ cm}$$
$$\text{Cost}_{\text{large}} = 2^2 \times \$18 = 4 \times \$18 = \$72$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $k = 2$, (b) Height $= 24\text{ cm}$, Gilding cost $= \$72$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Two similar olive oil tins have surface areas of $150\text{ cm}^2$ and $600\text{ cm}^2$. If the smaller tin holds $250\text{ mL}$, how much olive oil does the larger tin hold?
> *Hint*: Find $k = \sqrt{\frac{600}{150}}$ first, then multiply the volume by $k^3$.
> **Working**: $k^2 = \frac{600}{150} = 4 \implies k = 2$. Then $V_{\text{large}} = 2^3 \times 250 = 8 \times 250 = 2000\text{ mL}$ ($2\text{ L}$).
> **Answer**: $2000\text{ mL}$ (or $2\text{ L}$)

### WE9_7_2_3: Verifying and Applying Euler's Formula for Polyhedra

**Problem**: (a) A hexagonal prism has $2$ hexagonal bases and $6$ rectangular side faces. Count its faces $F$, vertices $V$, and edges $E$, and verify Euler's formula $V - E + F = 2$. (b) A convex polyhedron (a regular icosahedron) has $20$ triangular faces and $30$ edges. How many vertices does it have?

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | For a hexagonal prism, count faces ($2 + 6 = 8$), vertices ($6$ top $+ 6$ bottom $= 12$), and edges ($6$ top $+ 6$ bottom $+ 6$ vertical $= 18$), then evaluate $V - E + F$. | $$F = 8,\; V = 12,\; E = 18 \implies V - E + F = 12 - 18 + 8 = 2 \quad \checkmark$$ |
| 2 | Substitute $F = 20$ and $E = 30$ into $V - E + F = 2$ and solve for $V$. | $$V - 30 + 20 = 2 \implies V - 10 = 2 \implies V = 12$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $F = 8, V = 12, E = 18$ ($12 - 18 + 8 = 2$), (b) $V = 12$ vertices

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: A regular dodecahedron has $12$ pentagonal faces and $20$ vertices. Use Euler's formula to find the number of edges $E$.
> *Hint*: Substitute $V = 20$ and $F = 12$ into $V + F = E + 2$.
> **Working**: $20 + 12 = E + 2 \implies E = 32 - 2 = 30$ edges.
> **Answer**: $30$ edges

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Multiplying Area or Volume by the Linear Scale Factor $k$
- **The Misconception**: Assuming that doubling all side lengths of a box doubles its surface area and doubles its volume.
- **The Correct Concept**: Doubling every linear dimension ($k = 2$) multiplies surface area by $k^2 = 2^2 = 4$ and multiplies volume by $k^3 = 2^3 = 8$ because scaling occurs independently along length, width, and height.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$k = 2,\; V_1 = 10\text{ cm}^3 \implies V_2 = 2 \times 10 = 20\text{ cm}^3 \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$k = 2,\; V_1 = 10\text{ cm}^3 \implies V_2 = 2^3 \times 10 = 80\text{ cm}^3$$
- **Counter-Example**: A $1 \times 1 \times 1$ cube has volume $1\text{ cm}^3$, whereas a $2 \times 2 \times 2$ cube fits $8$ unit cubes ($8\text{ cm}^3$), not $2$.

### Confusing the Signs in Euler's Formula ($V - E + F = 2$)
- **The Misconception**: Writing $V + E - F = 2$ or $V + E + F = 2$ because students forget which letter is subtracted.
- **The Correct Concept**: In any polyhedron, the number of Edges ($E$) is always the largest of the three counts ($E > V$ and $E > F$), so $E$ is the quantity subtracted: $V - E + F = 2$, or equivalently $V + F = E + 2$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\text{Cube } (V=8, F=6): \quad 8 + E - 6 = 2 \implies E = 0 \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$V + F = E + 2 \implies 8 + 6 = E + 2 \implies E = 12$$
- **Counter-Example**: A cube has $8$ vertices, $6$ faces, and $12$ edges ($8 + 6 = 12 + 2 = 14$).

### Fluency & Understanding

#### Question `9_7_2_Q1` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Two similar triangles have corresponding base lengths in the ratio $2 : 5$. If the smaller triangle has an area of $16\text{ cm}^2$, find the area of the larger triangle.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The linear scale factor is $k = \frac{5}{2}$, so the area scale factor is $k^2 = \left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{25}{4}$.
2. Multiply the smaller area by $k^2$: $A = \frac{25}{4} \times 16 = 100\text{ cm}^2$.

**Final Answer**: $100\text{ cm}^2$
</details>

#### Question `9_7_2_Q2` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Two similar cylinders have radii of $4\text{ cm}$ and $6\text{ cm}$. If the smaller cylinder has a volume of $240\text{ cm}^3$, find the volume of the larger cylinder.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The linear scale factor is $k = \frac{6}{4} = \frac{3}{2}$, so the volume scale factor is $k^3 = \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^3 = \frac{27}{8}$.
2. Multiply the smaller volume by $k^3$: $V = \frac{27}{8} \times 240 = 810\text{ cm}^3$.

**Final Answer**: $810\text{ cm}^3$
</details>

#### Question `9_7_2_Q3` [Medium] *(Understanding)*
Two similar polygons have areas of $45\text{ cm}^2$ and $180\text{ cm}^2$. If the perimeter of the smaller polygon is $30\text{ cm}$, find the perimeter of the larger polygon.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find $k^2 = \frac{180}{45} = 4 \implies k = \sqrt{4} = 2$.
2. Since perimeter is a $1\text{D}$ length measurement, multiply by $k = 2$: $P = 2 \times 30 = 60\text{ cm}$.

**Final Answer**: $60\text{ cm}$
</details>

#### Question `9_7_2_Q4` [Easy] *(Polyhedra)*
A pentagonal pyramid has $1$ pentagonal base and $5$ triangular lateral faces. Find its number of faces $F$, vertices $V$, and edges $E$, and verify Euler's formula.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. A pentagonal pyramid has $F = 1 + 5 = 6$ faces, $V = 5 + 1 = 6$ vertices ($5$ base $+ 1$ apex), and $E = 5 + 5 = 10$ edges.
2. Check Euler's formula: $V - E + F = 6 - 10 + 6 = 2$.

**Final Answer**: $F = 6,\; V = 6,\; E = 10$ ($6 - 10 + 6 = 2$)
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `9_7_2_Q5` [Medium] *(Hospitality & Packaging)*
A Melbourne café sells coffee in two mathematically similar takeaway cups of heights $9\text{ cm}$ (regular) and $12\text{ cm}$ (jumbo). If the regular cup holds $270\text{ mL}$, how many millilitres does the jumbo cup hold?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate the linear scale factor: $k = \frac{12}{9} = \frac{4}{3}$.
2. Multiply the volume by $k^3 = \left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^3 = \frac{64}{27}$: $V = \frac{64}{27} \times 270 = 640\text{ mL}$.

**Final Answer**: $640\text{ mL}$
</details>

#### Question `9_7_2_Q6` [Medium] *(Architectural Modelling)*
An architectural scale model of a stadium in Brisbane is built at a linear scale of $1 : 200$. The model roof has an area of $0.75\text{ m}^2$. What is the actual roof area of the stadium in square metres ($\text{m}^2$) and in hectares ($1\text{ ha} = 10\,000\text{ m}^2$)?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. With $k = 200$, the area scale factor is $k^2 = 200^2 = 40\,000$.
2. Multiply the model area by $40\,000$: $A = 40\,000 \times 0.75 = 30\,000\text{ m}^2 = 3\text{ ha}$.

**Final Answer**: $30\,000\text{ m}^2$ ($3\text{ ha}$)
</details>

#### Question `9_7_2_Q7` [Challenging] *(Manufacturing & Mass-Volume Scaling)*
Two mathematically similar solid chocolate bunnies are made from the same chocolate. The smaller bunny weighs $125\text{ g}$ and is wrapped in $100\text{ cm}^2$ of gold foil. If the larger bunny weighs $1000\text{ g}$, what area of gold foil is needed to wrap it?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Mass is proportional to volume for uniform density, so $k^3 = \frac{1000}{125} = 8 \implies k = \sqrt[3]{8} = 2$.
2. Foil area scales by $k^2 = 2^2 = 4$, giving $A = 4 \times 100 = 400\text{ cm}^2$.

**Final Answer**: $400\text{ cm}^2$
</details>

#### Question `9_7_2_Q8` [Challenging] *(Sports Equipment Geometry)*
A standard soccer ball (a truncated icosahedron) is stitched from $12$ regular pentagons and $20$ regular hexagons ($F = 32$ faces in total). Every edge is shared by exactly $2$ faces, and every vertex is where $3$ edges meet. Find the number of edges $E$ and vertices $V$, and verify that $V - E + F = 2$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The $12$ pentagons and $20$ hexagons have $12 \times 5 + 20 \times 6 = 60 + 120 = 180$ polygon sides in total. Since each edge joins $2$ faces, $E = \frac{180}{2} = 90$ edges.
2. Since $3$ edges meet at each vertex, $V = \frac{180}{3} = 60$ vertices. Checking Euler's formula: $V - E + F = 60 - 90 + 32 = 2$.

**Final Answer**: $E = 90,\; V = 60$ ($60 - 90 + 32 = 2$)
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `9_7_2_Q9` [Challenging] *(Frustum Volume Reasoning)*
A right circular cone of total height $H$ and volume $V$ is sliced horizontally halfway down from its apex (at depth $\frac{1}{2}H$). What fraction of the total volume $V$ is contained in the top small cone, and what fraction is in the bottom frustum?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The top cone is similar to the full cone with linear scale factor $k = \frac{1}{2}$, so its volume is $k^3 V = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^3 V = \frac{1}{8}V$.
2. Subtracting from the full cone leaves $V - \frac{1}{8}V = \frac{7}{8}V$ in the bottom frustum.

**Final Answer**: Top cone is $\frac{1}{8}V$; bottom frustum is $\frac{7}{8}V$
</details>

#### Question `9_7_2_Q10` [Challenging] *(Truncated Polyhedra Invariance)*
If a cube of side length $4\text{ cm}$ has one small corner sliced off by a flat triangular cut (truncating one vertex without reaching the adjacent vertices), determine the new numbers of faces $F$, vertices $V$, and edges $E$, and show that Euler's formula $V - E + F = 2$ still holds.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. A standard cube has $F = 6, V = 8, E = 12$. Slicing off one corner adds $1$ new triangular face ($F = 6 + 1 = 7$), replaces $1$ old vertex with $3$ new vertices ($V = 8 - 1 + 3 = 10$), and adds $3$ new edges around the triangle ($E = 12 + 3 = 15$).
2. Evaluating Euler's formula gives $V - E + F = 10 - 15 + 7 = 2$.

**Final Answer**: $F = 7,\; V = 10,\; E = 15$ ($10 - 15 + 7 = 2$)
</details>

#### Question `9_7_2_Q11` [Extension] *(Biomechanics & Square-Cube Law)*
Galileo's Square-Cube Law in biology states that an animal's bone cross-sectional strength is proportional to its area ($k^2$), whereas its body weight is proportional to its volume ($k^3$). If a giant creature were scaled up by a linear factor of $k = 10$ compared to a human while keeping the exact same shape and material density, by what factor would the stress (weight divided by bone cross-sectional area) on its leg bones increase?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Body weight increases by $k^3 = 10^3 = 1000$, while bone cross-sectional area increases only by $k^2 = 10^2 = 100$.
2. Therefore the weight-to-area ratio (stress) increases by $\frac{k^3}{k^2} = k = \frac{1000}{100} = 10$ times, which would crush the bones unless the legs are made proportionally thicker.

**Final Answer**: Bone stress increases by a factor of $10$ ($\frac{k^3}{k^2} = k = 10$)
</details>

#### Question `9_7_2_Q12` [Extension] *(General Algebraic Proof of Euler's Formula for Prisms)*
A prism has an $n$-sided polygon as its cross-section. Write algebraic expressions in terms of $n$ for its number of faces $F$, vertices $V$, and edges $E$, and prove algebraically that $V - E + F = 2$ for every positive integer $n \ge 3$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. An $n$-gonal prism has $2$ end bases and $n$ rectangular side faces ($F = n + 2$), $n$ vertices on each end ($V = 2n$), and $n$ edges on each end plus $n$ vertical edges ($E = 3n$).
2. Substituting into Euler's expression gives $V - E + F = 2n - 3n + (n + 2) = 2$ for all $n \ge 3$.

**Final Answer**: $F = n + 2,\; V = 2n,\; E = 3n \implies 2n - 3n + (n + 2) = 2$
</details>
