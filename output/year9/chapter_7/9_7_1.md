### Key Vocabulary
- **Similar figures ($\sim$)**: Two geometric figures that have the exact same shape but not necessarily the same size; their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding side lengths are in a constant ratio.
- **Linear scale factor ($k$)**: The constant ratio of any side length in the image (second figure) to the corresponding side length in the original (first figure): $k = \frac{\text{image length}}{\text{original length}}$.
- **Similarity tests for triangles**: Four minimum sufficient conditions to prove two triangles are similar: Angle-Angle-Angle ($\text{AAA}$), Side-Angle-Side ($\text{SAS}$ in ratio), Side-Side-Side ($\text{SSS}$ in ratio), and Right angle-Hypotenuse-Side ($\text{RHS}$ in ratio).
- **Corresponding sides**: Pairs of sides located opposite equal angles in two similar triangles, whose lengths form equal ratios.

## Theory & Key Ideas

### The Four Similarity Tests for Triangles (AAA, SAS, SSS, RHS)

Two triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle DEF$ are similar (written $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$, with vertices listed in matching order) if they satisfy any one of four tests: (1) $\text{AAA}$ (or $\text{AA}$): two pairs of corresponding angles are equal (the third pair is automatically equal by the $180^\circ$ angle sum); (2) $\text{SAS}$: two pairs of corresponding sides are in the same ratio and the included angles between them are equal; (3) $\text{SSS}$: all three pairs of corresponding sides are in the same constant ratio $k$; (4) $\text{RHS}$: both triangles contain a $90^\circ$ right angle, and the hypotenuses and one pair of shorter sides are in the same ratio.

$$\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF \iff \frac{DE}{AB} = \frac{EF}{BC} = \frac{DF}{AC} = k \quad \text{and} \quad \angle A = \angle D,\; \angle B = \angle E,\; \angle C = \angle F$$

> **💡 Vertex Order Locks In the Side Ratios**
> Once you write $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$ in matching angle order, the corresponding side ratios can be read straight from the letters without even looking at the diagram: $\frac{DE}{AB} = \frac{EF}{BC} = \frac{DF}{AC}$.

### Finding Unknown Side Lengths Using Scale Factors and Proportions

Once two triangles are known or proved to be similar, any unknown side length can be found either by calculating the scale factor $k = \frac{\text{image}}{\text{original}}$ from a known pair of corresponding sides, or by setting up a proportion equation with the unknown in the numerator. Always match sides that lie opposite equal angles.

$$\frac{x}{a} = \frac{d}{c} \implies x = \frac{a \times d}{c}$$

> **💡 Put the Unknown Side on Top**
> When writing a similarity proportion, always place the unknown pronumeral $x$ in the numerator on the left-hand side so that solving takes just one multiplication step.

### Overlapping and Parallel-Line Triangles in Geometric Proofs

When a line segment $DE$ is drawn parallel to the base $BC$ of $\triangle ABC$ (with $D$ on $AB$ and $E$ on $AC$), the smaller triangle $\triangle ADE$ and the full triangle $\triangle ABC$ share a common angle at $A$, and have equal corresponding angles $\angle ADE = \angle ABC$ and $\angle AED = \angle ACB$ on the parallel lines. Therefore $\triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC$ by $\text{AAA}$. Similarly, a bowtie (hourglass) configuration formed by parallel lines creates similar triangles via vertically opposite angles and alternate angles.

$$DE \parallel BC \implies \triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC \; (\text{AAA}) \implies \frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{AE}{AC} = \frac{DE}{BC}$$

> **💡 Use Full Side Lengths in Nested Triangles**
> In a nested triangle $\triangle ADE$ inside $\triangle ABC$, the side of the large triangle is the FULL length $AB = AD + DB$, never just the lower piece $DB$ when comparing with the parallel bases $DE$ and $BC$.

## Worked Examples

### WE9_7_1_1: Proving Two Triangles Are Similar and Finding a Scale Factor

**Problem**: In $\triangle ABC$, $AB = 6\text{ cm}$, $BC = 9\text{ cm}$, and $\angle ABC = 52^\circ$. In $\triangle PQR$, $PQ = 10\text{ cm}$, $QR = 15\text{ cm}$, and $\angle PQR = 52^\circ$. (a) Write a formal similarity proof to show that $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR$. (b) If $AC = 7.2\text{ cm}$, find the length of $PR$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Calculate the ratios of the two pairs of given sides around the $52^\circ$ included angle. | $$\frac{PQ}{AB} = \frac{10}{6} = \frac{5}{3}, \qquad \frac{QR}{BC} = \frac{15}{9} = \frac{5}{3}$$ |
| 2 | State the equal included angles and conclude similarity with the correct test and vertex order. | $$\angle ABC = \angle PQR = 52^\circ \text{ (given included angle)}$$
$$\therefore \triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR \; (\text{SAS similarity},\; k = \tfrac{5}{3})$$ |
| 3 | Multiply $AC = 7.2\text{ cm}$ by the linear scale factor $k = \frac{5}{3}$ to find $PR$. | $$PR = k \times AC = \frac{5}{3} \times 7.2 = 12\text{ cm}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR$ by $\text{SAS}$ with $k = \frac{5}{3}$, (b) $PR = 12\text{ cm}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Triangle $DEF$ has side lengths $4\text{ cm}$, $7\text{ cm}$, and $9\text{ cm}$. Triangle $XYZ$ has side lengths $10\text{ cm}$, $17.5\text{ cm}$, and $22.5\text{ cm}$. State the similarity test that proves $\triangle DEF \sim \triangle XYZ$ and find the scale factor $k$.
> *Hint*: Check whether $\frac{10}{4}$, $\frac{17.5}{7}$, and $\frac{22.5}{9}$ simplify to the same number.
> **Working**: $\frac{10}{4} = 2.5$, $\frac{17.5}{7} = 2.5$, and $\frac{22.5}{9} = 2.5$, so $\triangle DEF \sim \triangle XYZ$ by $\text{SSS}$ with $k = 2.5$ (or $\frac{5}{2}$).
> **Answer**: $\text{SSS}$ similarity with $k = 2.5$

### WE9_7_1_2: Finding Unknown Lengths in Overlapping Parallel-Line Triangles

**Problem**: In $\triangle ABC$, point $D$ lies on $AB$ and point $E$ lies on $AC$ such that $DE \parallel BC$. Given $AD = 8\text{ cm}$, $DB = 4\text{ cm}$, and $DE = 10\text{ cm}$, prove that $\triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC$ and calculate the length of the base $BC$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Prove $\triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC$ using the common angle at $A$ and corresponding angles on parallel lines $DE \parallel BC$. | $$\angle DAE = \angle BAC \text{ (common angle)}$$
$$\angle ADE = \angle ABC \text{ (corresponding angles, } DE \parallel BC\text{)}$$
$$\therefore \triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC \; (\text{AAA})$$ |
| 2 | Find the full side length $AB = AD + DB = 8 + 4 = 12\text{ cm}$ and equate ratios of corresponding sides. | $$AB = 8 + 4 = 12\text{ cm}$$
$$\frac{BC}{DE} = \frac{AB}{AD} \implies \frac{BC}{10} = \frac{12}{8} \implies BC = 10 \times 1.5 = 15\text{ cm}$$ |

**Answer**: $BC = 15\text{ cm}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: In $\triangle PQR$, $ST \parallel QR$ with $S$ on $PQ$ and $T$ on $PR$. If $PS = 6\text{ cm}$, $SQ = 9\text{ cm}$, and $QR = 20\text{ cm}$, find the length of $ST$.
> *Hint*: The full side is $PQ = 6 + 9 = 15\text{ cm}$, and $\frac{ST}{QR} = \frac{PS}{PQ}$.
> **Working**: $PQ = 15\text{ cm}$. Since $\triangle PST \sim \triangle PQR$ ($\text{AAA}$), $\frac{ST}{20} = \frac{6}{15} \implies ST = 20 \times \frac{2}{5} = 8\text{ cm}$.
> **Answer**: $8\text{ cm}$

### WE9_7_1_3: Indirect Shadow Measurement Using Similar Right-Angled Triangles

**Problem**: In Melbourne's Royal Botanic Gardens, a vertical $1.8\text{ m}$ sighting pole casts a horizontal shadow of length $2.4\text{ m}$ at the same instant that a tall eucalyptus tree casts a horizontal shadow of length $18\text{ m}$. Assuming the sun's rays are parallel, calculate the height $h$ of the eucalyptus tree.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Both the pole and the tree stand vertically ($90^\circ$ to the horizontal ground), and the parallel sun rays meet the ground at equal angles, so the two right-angled shadow triangles are similar by $\text{AAA}$. | $$\frac{\text{Tree height}}{\text{Pole height}} = \frac{\text{Tree shadow}}{\text{Pole shadow}} \implies \frac{h}{1.8} = \frac{18}{2.4}$$ |
| 2 | Multiply both sides by $1.8$ to solve for $h$. | $$h = \frac{18 \times 1.8}{2.4} = \frac{32.4}{2.4} = 13.5\text{ m}$$ |

**Answer**: $13.5\text{ m}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: A $1.5\text{ m}$ fence post casts a $2.0\text{ m}$ shadow at the same time a stadium light tower casts a $36\text{ m}$ shadow. How tall is the light tower?
> *Hint*: Set up $\frac{h}{1.5} = \frac{36}{2.0}$.
> **Working**: $h = 1.5 \times \frac{36}{2.0} = 1.5 \times 18 = 27\text{ m}$.
> **Answer**: $27\text{ m}$

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Using the Partial Segment Instead of the Full Side in Nested Triangles
- **The Misconception**: When $DE \parallel BC$ inside $\triangle ABC$, students often write $\frac{DE}{BC} = \frac{AD}{DB}$ using the lower side segment $DB$ instead of the full triangle side $AB = AD + DB$.
- **The Correct Concept**: Because $BC$ is a side of the complete triangle $\triangle ABC$, its ratio partner must be the complete side $AB = AD + DB$ of $\triangle ABC$. The trapezoid segment $DB$ is not a side of any triangle.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$AD = 6,\; DB = 3,\; DE = 8: \quad \frac{8}{BC} = \frac{6}{3} \implies BC = 4 \quad \text{(Incorrect — smaller than } DE\text{!)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$AB = 6 + 3 = 9: \quad \frac{BC}{8} = \frac{9}{6} \implies BC = 12$$
- **Counter-Example**: If $AD = 6$ and $DB = 3$, the base $BC$ is clearly wider than $DE = 8$; using $\frac{6}{3}$ wrongly makes $BC = 4$.

### Applying SAS Similarity When the Equal Angle Is Not Included
- **The Misconception**: Concluding two triangles are similar by $\text{SAS}$ when two sides are in ratio and a non-included angle is equal.
- **The Correct Concept**: For $\text{SAS}$ similarity, the equal angle MUST be the included angle sandwiched directly between the two proportional sides.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$\frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{BC}{EF} \text{ and } \angle A = \angle D \implies \triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF \text{ by SAS} \quad \text{(Incorrect)}$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$\frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{BC}{EF} \text{ requires the included angle } \angle B = \angle E \text{ for SAS similarity.}$$
- **Counter-Example**: Just like the ambiguous case in congruence, two side lengths and a non-included acute angle can form two completely different non-similar triangles.

#### Question `9_7_1_Q1` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Given that $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$ with $AB = 5\text{ cm}$, $BC = 8\text{ cm}$, $DE = 15\text{ cm}$, and $EF = x\text{ cm}$, find the linear scale factor $k$ and the value of $x$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Calculate the linear scale factor from the corresponding sides $AB$ and $DE$: $k = \frac{DE}{AB} = \frac{15}{5} = 3$.
2. Multiply $BC = 8\text{ cm}$ by $k = 3$: $x = 3 \times 8 = 24$.

**Final Answer**: $k = 3,\; x = 24$
</details>

#### Question `9_7_1_Q2` [Easy] *(Fluency)*
Determine whether $\triangle ABC$ with sides $6\text{ cm}, 8\text{ cm}, 12\text{ cm}$ and $\triangle DEF$ with sides $9\text{ cm}, 12\text{ cm}, 18\text{ cm}$ are similar. If so, state the similarity test and scale factor $k$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Match shortest, middle, and longest sides: $\frac{9}{6} = 1.5$, $\frac{12}{8} = 1.5$, and $\frac{18}{12} = 1.5$.
2. Since all three pairs of corresponding sides are in the same ratio $k = 1.5$, $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$ by $\text{SSS}$.

**Final Answer**: Yes, $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$ by $\text{SSS}$ with $k = 1.5$ (or $\frac{3}{2}$)
</details>

#### Question `9_7_1_Q3` [Medium] *(Understanding)*
Two right-angled triangles $\triangle ABC$ ($\angle B = 90^\circ$) and $\triangle DEF$ ($\angle E = 90^\circ$) have hypotenuses $AC = 10\text{ cm}$ and $DF = 25\text{ cm}$, and shorter sides $AB = 6\text{ cm}$ and $DE = 15\text{ cm}$. State the similarity test that proves $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$ and find $EF$ if $BC = 8\text{ cm}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Check the ratio of the hypotenuses and shorter legs: $\frac{DF}{AC} = \frac{25}{10} = 2.5$ and $\frac{DE}{AB} = \frac{15}{6} = 2.5$ with $\angle B = \angle E = 90^\circ$, so $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$ by $\text{RHS}$.
2. Multiply $BC = 8\text{ cm}$ by $k = 2.5$: $EF = 8 \times 2.5 = 20\text{ cm}$.

**Final Answer**: $\text{RHS}$ similarity ($k = 2.5$), $EF = 20\text{ cm}$
</details>

#### Question `9_7_1_Q4` [Medium] *(Understanding)*
In $\triangle ABC$, a line $DE$ is parallel to $BC$ with $D$ on $AB$ and $E$ on $AC$. If $AD = 9\text{ cm}$, $DB = 6\text{ cm}$, and $BC = 20\text{ cm}$, find the length of $DE$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. By $\text{AAA}$ similarity, $\triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC$, where the full side is $AB = 9 + 6 = 15\text{ cm}$.
2. Set up $\frac{DE}{20} = \frac{9}{15} = \frac{3}{5} \implies DE = 20 \times \frac{3}{5} = 12\text{ cm}$.

**Final Answer**: $12\text{ cm}$
</details>

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `9_7_1_Q5` [Medium] *(Surveying & Navigation)*
Two straight segments $AB$ and $CD$ intersect at point $X$ between parallel riverbanks $AC \parallel BD$ along the Yarra River. A surveyor measures $AX = 12\text{ m}$, $BX = 30\text{ m}$, and the bank baseline $AC = 18\text{ m}$. Find the width $BD$ on the opposite bank.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Since $AC \parallel BD$, alternate angles $\angle XAC = \angle XBD$ and vertically opposite angles $\angle AXC = \angle BXD$ are equal, so $\triangle AXC \sim \triangle BXD$ ($\text{AAA}$).
2. Equate corresponding sides: $\frac{BD}{AC} = \frac{BX}{AX} \implies \frac{BD}{18} = \frac{30}{12} = 2.5 \implies BD = 45\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: $45\text{ m}$
</details>

#### Question `9_7_1_Q6` [Medium] *(Alpine Architecture)*
An A-frame ski chalet roof at Mount Buller has rafters of total length $7.5\text{ m}$ from the apex $A$ to the eaves $B$ and $C$, where the ground floor width is $BC = 9.0\text{ m}$. A horizontal ceiling joist $DE$ is installed $5.0\text{ m}$ down the rafters from the apex $A$. Find the width of the ceiling joist $DE$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Since $DE \parallel BC$, $\triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC$ ($\text{AAA}$) with $AD = 5.0\text{ m}$ and $AB = 7.5\text{ m}$.
2. Solve $\frac{DE}{9.0} = \frac{5.0}{7.5} = \frac{2}{3} \implies DE = 6.0\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: $6.0\text{ m}$
</details>

#### Question `9_7_1_Q7` [Challenging] *(Optics & Indirect Measurement)*
A student places a small flat mirror horizontally on the ground $3\text{ m}$ from the base of a flagpole and steps back $1.2\text{ m}$ from the mirror until she sees the top of the flagpole centred in the mirror. If her eyes are $1.6\text{ m}$ above the ground, and the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, how tall is the flagpole?

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Both the student and flagpole are vertical ($90^\circ$) and the reflection angles at the mirror are equal, so the two right-angled triangles are similar by $\text{AAA}$.
2. Solve $\frac{h}{1.6} = \frac{3}{1.2} = 2.5 \implies h = 4\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: $4\text{ m}$
</details>

#### Question `9_7_1_Q8` [Challenging] *(Civil Engineering)*
A ramp rises uniformly from ground level to a loading dock. At a horizontal distance of $2.4\text{ m}$ from the start of the ramp, a vertical support strut of height $0.45\text{ m}$ touches the underside of the ramp. If the total horizontal length of the ramp is $6.4\text{ m}$, find the height of the loading dock.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. The vertical strut and the vertical loading dock form similar right-angled triangles with the horizontal ground ($\text{AAA}$).
2. Solve $\frac{H}{0.45} = \frac{6.4}{2.4} = \frac{8}{3} \implies H = 0.45 \times \frac{8}{3} = 1.2\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: $1.2\text{ m}$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `9_7_1_Q9` [Medium] *(Algebraic Similarity Proof)*
In $\triangle ABC$, $DE \parallel BC$ with $AD = x\text{ cm}$, $DB = 5\text{ cm}$, $AE = 6\text{ cm}$, and $EC = 7.5\text{ cm}$. Prove that $\frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{AE}{AC}$ and hence solve for $x$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Since $DE \parallel BC$, $\triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC$ ($\text{AAA}$), so $\frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{AE}{AC}$ where $AB = x + 5$ and $AC = 6 + 7.5 = 13.5$.
2. Solve $\frac{x}{x + 5} = \frac{6}{13.5} = \frac{4}{9} \implies 9x = 4x + 20 \implies 5x = 20 \implies x = 4$.

**Final Answer**: $x = 4$
</details>

#### Question `9_7_1_Q10` [Medium] *(Geometric Reasoning)*
Explain why all equilateral triangles are similar to one another, whereas not all isosceles triangles are similar.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Every equilateral triangle has all three interior angles equal to $60^\circ$, so any two equilateral triangles satisfy the $\text{AAA}$ similarity test.
2. Two isosceles triangles can have completely different interior angles (such as $40^\circ, 70^\circ, 70^\circ$ and $100^\circ, 40^\circ, 40^\circ$), failing $\text{AAA}$.

**Final Answer**: Every equilateral triangle has angles $60^\circ, 60^\circ, 60^\circ$ ($\text{AAA}$), whereas isosceles triangles can have different apex angles (e.g. $40^\circ, 70^\circ, 70^\circ$ vs $100^\circ, 40^\circ, 40^\circ$)
</details>

#### Question `9_7_1_Q11` [Extension] *(Right-Triangle Altitude Theorem)*
In a right-angled triangle $\triangle ABC$ with $\angle ABC = 90^\circ$, an altitude $BD$ is drawn perpendicular to the hypotenuse $AC$. Prove that $\triangle ADB \sim \triangle BDC$ and deduce that $BD^2 = AD \times DC$. If $AD = 4\text{ cm}$ and $DC = 9\text{ cm}$, find $BD$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Let $\angle DAB = \theta$. Then in right-angled $\triangle ADB$, $\angle ABD = 90^\circ - \theta$. Since $\angle ABC = 90^\circ$, $\angle DBC = 90^\circ - (90^\circ - \theta) = \theta = \angle DAB$. With $\angle ADB = \angle BDC = 90^\circ$, $\triangle ADB \sim \triangle BDC$ ($\text{AAA}$).
2. Equating corresponding sides gives $\frac{BD}{DC} = \frac{AD}{BD} \implies BD^2 = AD \times DC = 4 \times 9 = 36 \implies BD = 6\text{ cm}$.

**Final Answer**: $BD = 6\text{ cm}$
</details>

#### Question `9_7_1_Q12` [Extension] *(Crossed Ladders Theorem)*
Two vertical poles of heights $a = 3\text{ m}$ and $b = 6\text{ m}$ stand on horizontal ground. Taut wires connect the top of each pole to the base of the other pole, crossing at height $h$ above the ground. Using similar triangles, show that $\frac{1}{h} = \frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b}$ regardless of the distance between the poles, and calculate $h$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Let the horizontal distance between the poles be $d$, divided into $d_1 + d_2 = d$ at the point directly below the intersection. By similar right-angled triangles, $\frac{h}{a} = \frac{d_2}{d}$ and $\frac{h}{b} = \frac{d_1}{d}$.
2. Adding the two equations gives $\frac{h}{a} + \frac{h}{b} = \frac{d_1 + d_2}{d} = 1 \implies \frac{1}{h} = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{2} \implies h = 2\text{ m}$.

**Final Answer**: $h = 2\text{ m}$
</details>
