### Key Vocabulary
- **Scientific notation (standard form)**: Writing a positive number in the form $a \times 10^n$, where the coefficient $a$ satisfies $1 \le a < 10$ and the order of magnitude $n$ is an integer.
- **Significant figures**: The digits in a measurement that carry meaningful precision, starting from the first non-zero digit on the left.
- **Order of magnitude**: The integer power of ten ($10^n$) in a number's scientific notation representation, used to compare scales across astronomy, physics, and biology.
- **Metric prefixes**: Standard multipliers based on powers of $10^3$, such as tera ($\text{T} = 10^{12}$), giga ($\text{G} = 10^9$), mega ($\text{M} = 10^6$), kilo ($\text{k} = 10^3$), milli ($\text{m} = 10^{-3}$), micro ($\mu = 10^{-6}$), and nano ($\text{n} = 10^{-9}$).

## Theory & Key Ideas

### Scientific Notation for Very Large and Very Small Numbers

Scientists and engineers express extreme measurements in scientific notation $a \times 10^n$, where $1 \le a < 10$ and $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. Numbers greater than or equal to $10$ have a positive integer exponent $n > 0$ (counting how many places the decimal point moved left to sit after the first non-zero digit), whereas small positive numbers between $0$ and $1$ have a negative integer exponent $n < 0$ (counting how many places the decimal point moved right).

$$a \times 10^n \quad (1 \le a < 10,\; n \in \mathbb{Z}): \quad 45\,000 = 4.5 \times 10^4, \quad 0.00032 = 3.2 \times 10^{-4}$$

> **💡 Single Non-Zero Digit Before the Decimal Point**
> The coefficient $a$ in $a \times 10^n$ must have exactly ONE non-zero digit to the left of the decimal point ($1 \le a < 10$). For example, $34.5 \times 10^6$ must be adjusted to $3.45 \times 10^7$.

### Counting and Rounding to Significant Figures

Every non-zero digit is significant. Zeros trapped between non-zero digits (such as the zero in $408$) are always significant. Leading zeros at the start of a small decimal (such as $0.0045$) are only place-holders and are NOT significant. Trailing zeros after a decimal point (such as $4.50$) ARE significant because they indicate measurement precision. Writing a number in scientific notation removes any ambiguity about trailing zeros in whole numbers.

$$0.004050 \text{ has } 4 \text{ significant figures } (4, 0, 5, 0) \implies 4.050 \times 10^{-3}$$

> **💡 Start Counting from the First Non-Zero Digit**
> To round to $k$ significant figures, count $k$ digits from left to right starting at the first non-zero digit, then look at the $(k+1)\text{th}$ digit to decide whether to round up or down.

### Multiplying and Dividing in Scientific Notation

To multiply or divide numbers in scientific notation, apply the commutative law and index laws: multiply or divide the coefficients $a$ and $b$ separately from the powers of $10$, add or subtract the indices of $10$, and finally re-normalise the coefficient so that it lies in the interval $1 \le a < 10$.

$$(a \times 10^m)(b \times 10^n) = (ab) \times 10^{m+n}, \quad \frac{a \times 10^m}{b \times 10^n} = \left(\frac{a}{b}\right) \times 10^{m-n}$$

> **💡 Re-Normalise If the New Coefficient Falls Outside [1, 10)**
> If $(4 \times 10^5)(6 \times 10^3) = 24 \times 10^8$, rewrite $24 = 2.4 \times 10^1$ so the final answer is $2.4 \times 10^9$.

### WE9_1_3_1: Converting to Scientific Notation and Rounding to Significant Figures

**Problem**: Write each number in scientific notation correct to $3$ significant figures: (a) $48\,760\,000$ and (b) $0.0005084$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), move the decimal point $7$ places left to get $4.876 \times 10^7$, then round the coefficient to $3$ significant figures (since the $4\text{th}$ digit is $6 \ge 5$, round up). | $$48\,760\,000 = 4.876 \times 10^7 \approx 4.88 \times 10^7 \; (3\text{ s.f.})$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), move the decimal point $4$ places right past the first non-zero digit $5$ to get $5.084 \times 10^{-4}$, then round to $3$ significant figures. | $$0.0005084 = 5.084 \times 10^{-4} \approx 5.08 \times 10^{-4} \; (3\text{ s.f.})$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $4.88 \times 10^7$, (b) $5.08 \times 10^{-4}$

### WE9_1_3_2: Multiplying and Dividing in Scientific Notation

**Problem**: Evaluate without a calculator, giving your answers in scientific notation: (a) $(3.5 \times 10^6) \times (4 \times 10^{-2})$ and (b) $\frac{2.4 \times 10^{-3}}{8 \times 10^4}$.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | In part (a), multiply the coefficients ($3.5 \times 4 = 14$), add the powers of $10$ ($6 + (-2) = 4$), and re-normalise $14 = 1.4 \times 10^1$. | $$(3.5 \times 10^6)(4 \times 10^{-2}) = 14 \times 10^4 = 1.4 \times 10^5$$ |
| 2 | In part (b), divide the coefficients ($2.4 \div 8 = 0.3$), subtract the powers of $10$ ($-3 - 4 = -7$), and re-normalise $0.3 = 3 \times 10^{-1}$. | $$\frac{2.4 \times 10^{-3}}{8 \times 10^4} = 0.3 \times 10^{-7} = 3 \times 10^{-8}$$ |

**Answer**: (a) $1.4 \times 10^5$, (b) $3 \times 10^{-8}$

> **✏️ Now You Try**
> **Problem**: Evaluate in scientific notation: (a) $(5 \times 10^4) \times (6.2 \times 10^3)$ and (b) $\frac{3.6 \times 10^5}{9 \times 10^{-2}}$.
> *Hint*: Remember to adjust $31 \times 10^7$ and $0.4 \times 10^7$ into standard form.
> **Working**: (a) $31 \times 10^7 = 3.1 \times 10^8$; (b) $0.4 \times 10^{5 - (-2)} = 0.4 \times 10^7 = 4 \times 10^6$.

### WE9_1_3_3: Astronomical Time Scales Using Scientific Notation

**Problem**: Light travels at $3.0 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$. The Parkes radio telescope ('Murriyang') in New South Wales receives a signal from a spacecraft $7.5 \times 10^{11}\text{ m}$ from Earth. How many seconds does the signal take to reach Parkes? Express your answer in both scientific notation and minutes/seconds.

| Step | Thinking | Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Use $t = \frac{d}{s}$ and divide the coefficients and powers of $10$. | $$t = \frac{7.5 \times 10^{11}}{3.0 \times 10^8} = 2.5 \times 10^{11-8} = 2.5 \times 10^3\text{ seconds}$$ |
| 2 | Convert $2.5 \times 10^3 = 2500\text{ s}$ into minutes and seconds by dividing by $60$ ($2500 = 41 \times 60 + 40$). | $$2500\text{ s} = 41\text{ minutes } 40\text{ seconds}$$ |

**Answer**: $2.5 \times 10^3\text{ s}$ ($41\text{ min } 40\text{ s}$)

> **✏️ Now You Try**

> *Hint*: Calculate $\frac{1.5 \times 10^{11}}{3.0 \times 10^8}$ and re-normalise.
> **Working**: $t = 0.5 \times 10^3 = 5.0 \times 10^2\text{ s} = 500\text{ s}$ ($8\text{ min } 20\text{ s}$).
> **Answer**: $5.0 \times 10^2\text{ s}$ ($500\text{ s}$)

## ⚠️ Common Misconceptions Alert

### Counting Leading Zeros as Significant Figures
- **The Misconception**: Stating that $0.0025$ has $4$ or $5$ significant figures because of the zeros at the front.
- **The Correct Concept**: Leading zeros only position the decimal point; they are never significant. In $0.0025 = 2.5 \times 10^{-3}$, only the digits $2$ and $5$ are significant ($2$ significant figures).
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$0.0025 \text{ has } 4 \text{ significant figures.} \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$0.0025 = 2.5 \times 10^{-3} \text{ has } 2 \text{ significant figures.} \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: Converting $0.0025\text{ m}$ to millimetres gives $2.5\text{ mm}$; changing units does not change the precision ($2$ significant figures).

### Shifting the Power of 10 in the Wrong Direction When Re-Normalising
- **The Misconception**: Rewriting $45 \times 10^6$ as $4.5 \times 10^5$ instead of $4.5 \times 10^7$.
- **The Correct Concept**: Because $45$ is divided by $10$ to become $4.5$, the power of $10$ must be multiplied by $10$ to keep the value unchanged: $45 \times 10^6 = (4.5 \times 10^1) \times 10^6 = 4.5 \times 10^7$.
- **❌ Incorrect Working**: $$45 \times 10^6 = 4.5 \times 10^5 \quad (\text{Incorrect})$$
- **✅ Correct Working**: $$45 \times 10^6 = 4.5 \times 10^1 \times 10^6 = 4.5 \times 10^7 \quad (\text{Correct})$$
- **Counter-Example**: $45 \times 10^6 = 45\,000\,000 = 4.5 \times 10^7$, whereas $4.5 \times 10^5 = 450\,000$.

### Problem-Solving & Modelling

#### Question `9_1_3_Q5` [Medium] *(Microbiology Scale Comparison)*
A human red blood cell has a diameter of approximately $7.5\text{ micrometres}$ ($7.5\,\mu\text{m}$, where $1\,\mu\text{m} = 10^{-6}\text{ m}$). How many red blood cells laid side-by-side in a straight line would span a length of $1.5\text{ cm}$ ($1.5 \times 10^{-2}\text{ m}$)? Give your answer in scientific notation.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Divide the total length by the diameter of one cell: $N = \frac{1.5 \times 10^{-2}}{7.5 \times 10^{-6}}$.
2. Calculate $\frac{1.5}{7.5} \times 10^{-2 - (-6)} = 0.2 \times 10^4 = 2.0 \times 10^3$ cells.

**Final Answer**: $2.0 \times 10^3$ ($2000$ cells)
</details>

#### Question `9_1_3_Q7` [Medium] *(Data Storage and Metric Prefixes)*
A research supercomputer in Canberra has $4.5\text{ terabytes}$ ($4.5 \times 10^{12}\text{ bytes}$) of high-speed memory. How many $1.8\text{ megabyte}$ ($1.8 \times 10^6\text{ bytes}$) satellite images can it store simultaneously? Give your answer in scientific notation.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Divide total capacity by image size: $N = \frac{4.5 \times 10^{12}}{1.8 \times 10^6}$.
2. Since $\frac{4.5}{1.8} = 2.5$ and $10^{12-6} = 10^6$, it can store $2.5 \times 10^6$ images.

**Final Answer**: $2.5 \times 10^6$ images
</details>

#### Question `9_1_3_Q8` [Challenging] *(Astrophysics Distance Unit)*
One light-year is the distance light travels in $365.25$ days at $3.0 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$. Given that $1\text{ day} = 86\,400\text{ s}$, calculate the length of one light-year in metres in scientific notation correct to $3$ significant figures.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find the number of seconds in one Julian year: $T = 365.25 \times 86\,400 = 31\,557\,600\text{ s} = 3.15576 \times 10^7\text{ s}$.
2. Multiply by the speed of light: $d = (3.0 \times 10^8) \times (3.15576 \times 10^7) = 9.46728 \times 10^{15}\text{ m} \approx 9.47 \times 10^{15}\text{ m}$ ($3\text{ s.f.}$).

**Final Answer**: $9.47 \times 10^{15}\text{ m}$
</details>

### Reasoning & Communication

#### Question `9_1_3_Q9` [Medium] *(Communicating Precision)*
Explain why reporting a crowd size as $60\,000$ is ambiguous in terms of significant figures, and show how scientific notation distinguishes between $2$ significant figures and $3$ significant figures for this number.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. In the whole number $60\,000$, it is unclear whether the number was rounded to the nearest $10\,000$, $1000$, or $100$.
2. In scientific notation, every digit written in the coefficient is significant: $6.0 \times 10^4$ has $2$ significant figures (nearest $1000$) while $6.00 \times 10^4$ has $3$ significant figures (nearest $100$).

**Final Answer**: In $60\,000$, trailing zeros may be place-holders or measured digits; $6.0 \times 10^4$ shows $2\text{ s.f.}$ whereas $6.00 \times 10^4$ shows $3\text{ s.f.}$
</details>

#### Question `9_1_3_Q10` [Easy] *(Ordering by Order of Magnitude)*
Without converting to standard decimals, explain how to order $A = 8.9 \times 10^{-5}$, $B = 1.2 \times 10^{-4}$, and $C = 9.5 \times 10^{-6}$ from smallest to largest.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. For positive numbers in scientific notation, compare the exponents of $10$ first: $-6 < -5 < -4$.
2. Since a smaller exponent of $10$ gives a smaller positive number regardless of the normalised coefficient, $9.5 \times 10^{-6} < 8.9 \times 10^{-5} < 1.2 \times 10^{-4}$, so $C < A < B$.

**Final Answer**: $C < A < B$
</details>

#### Question `9_1_3_Q11` [Challenging] *(Cubic Unit Conversions)*
A nanometre is $10^{-9}\text{ m}$ and a centimetre is $10^{-2}\text{ m}$. How many cubic nanometres ($\text{nm}^3$) are there in $1\text{ cubic centimetre}$ ($1\text{ cm}^3$)? Justify your answer using index laws.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Find the linear ratio: $\frac{1\text{ cm}}{1\text{ nm}} = \frac{10^{-2}\text{ m}}{10^{-9}\text{ m}} = 10^{-2 - (-9)} = 10^7$, so $1\text{ cm} = 10^7\text{ nm}$.
2. Cube both sides to convert volume: $1\text{ cm}^3 = (10^7\text{ nm})^3 = 10^{21}\text{ nm}^3$.

**Final Answer**: $10^{21}\text{ nm}^3$
</details>

#### Question `9_1_3_Q12` [Extension] *(Bounds on Scientific Notation Products)*
Two positive numbers in scientific notation are $X = a \times 10^m$ and $Y = b \times 10^n$, where $1 \le a, b < 10$. Prove that the product $XY$ has scientific-notation exponent either $m + n$ or $m + n + 1$, and state the exact condition on $ab$ that determines which case occurs.

<details>
<summary><strong>Full Step-by-Step Solution</strong></summary>

1. Multiplying $1 \le a < 10$ and $1 \le b < 10$ gives $1 \le ab < 100$, with $XY = (ab) \times 10^{m+n}$.
2. If $1 \le ab < 10$, the expression is already in scientific notation with exponent $m + n$. If $10 \le ab < 100$, then $1 \le \frac{ab}{10} < 10$, so $XY = \left(\frac{ab}{10}\right) \times 10^{m+n+1}$ has exponent $m + n + 1$.

**Final Answer**: Since $1 \le a, b < 10$, $1 \le ab < 100$. If $1 \le ab < 10$, the exponent is $m + n$; if $10 \le ab < 100$, re-normalising $\frac{ab}{10} \times 10^1$ gives exponent $m + n + 1$.
</details>
